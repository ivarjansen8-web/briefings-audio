Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Tuesday, 10 November.

Qatar carries Iran's Hormuz offer to Trump; Eisenkot tapped to form Israeli government; Khor Mor gas field hit.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 10 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Qatar's prime minister met Trump at the White House on 9 November to carry an Iranian interim proposal, but no deal was announced. Iran reportedly offers to end its Hormuz blockade in return for an end to the US port blockade, partial sanctions relief and a halt to bombing, with nuclear talks to follow; Rubio said negotiations are far from a breakthrough.

Trump said the Iran war is militarily "effectively over" but that the US is ready to strike again. He called renewed strikes "highly unlikely" if Tehran signs a strong nuclear deal and fully reopens Hormuz, while the US naval blockade stays.

Israel's President Herzog tasked Gadi Eisenkot with forming the next government after 67 MKs backed him against 46 for Netanyahu. Eisenkot has 28 days, with a possible two-week extension; Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister.

A drone strike on 8 November halted production at Iraqi Kurdistan's Khor Mor gas field and cut about 2.8 GW of power. No group has claimed it.

Brent fell about 5.9% on 9 November to roughly 92.57 dollarsas Trump held off new strikes on Iran, per one market note.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Netanyahu renewed his call for a unity government of Zionist and Haredi parties without Arab parties, arguing Likud should lead as the largest party. The Joint List says it will back Eisenkot from outside the coalition, and talks with Haredi parties hinge on conscription.

Eisenkot has not ruled out a minority government. This signals he may rely on outside Arab-party support rather than a majority bloc.

Floods swept away tents across Gaza; Hamas-led authorities report at least five dead. The Board of Peace said Israel's winter preparations are insufficient and demanded immediate entry of supplies, while Israel's foreign ministry blamed Hamas and the IDF says over 8,200 trucks have entered.

Israel says it will not allow reconstruction until Hamas disarms, and has banned some equipment. Board of Peace envoy Mladenov and Tony Blair pressed Netanyahu last week to advance the Trump plan.

Settlers reportedly set fire to excavators and vehicles near Beita and moved into eight houses near Jericho. The Jericho governor says the land belongs to the PA; the PA arrested suspects over the sales (single-source, Palestinian and Israeli liveblog reports) (unconfirmed).

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israeli jets reportedly struck five south Lebanon towns on 9 November, including Kfar Tibnit and Mayfadun, with casualties not yet determined. Reports relay a Lebanese portal and carry no official statement (unconfirmed).

Israel is pressing Lebanon, with US mediation, to redeploy the army where Hezbollah operates, fearing a second front if the Iran war resumes. Newscord attributes this to Aljadeed (unconfirmed).

Direct Lebanon-Israel talks are stalled, with Beirut awaiting the impact of US and Israeli elections.

Syria's President al-Sharaa received Jordan's Foreign Minister Safadi in Damascus on 9 November; eight MoUs were signed. Per Daily Beirut the talks covered economic and security cooperation.

A Lebanese delegation in Damascus discussed refugee returns, security cooperation and border control. The Lebanese army separately killed a Syrian woman on 4 November in an anti-smuggling operation near Arsal.

4. Iran.

Tehran told the IAEA in a 10-page report that attacks and the threat of new strikes make normal inspections impossible. This hardens its position as deal hopes fade, per Bloomberg.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Baqaei rejected US claims that Iran is building a nuclear weapon.

The US and Iran are negotiating quietly, with CNN and the NYT reporting Iranian willingness to re-engage. CNN notes this is not independently confirmed.

Hardliners are pushing back on President Pezeshkian's flexibility, and IRGC-linked Tasnim argued for leaving the NPT. Iran International (an exile outlet critical of Tehran) reports Iran rules out nuclear concessions even if the US accepts a Hormuz proposal.

Unexplained explosions hit Qeshm, Sirik, Jask and Bandar Abbas on 7 November. Iranian media linked them to Iran's own military activity, not US strikes, and officials have not confirmed missile strikes.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Dana Gas confirmed the drone attack on Khor Mor and said production remains suspended with no restart date. No staff were hurt, and Kurdistan's authorities called it a "terrorist attack."

The shutdown cut about 2.8 GW, leaving Erbil and Sulaimaniyah without direct supply.

Two Iraqi security sources told Reuters the drone appeared launched from east of Tuz Khurmatu. This is preliminary and unclaimed (unconfirmed).

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Six GCC states signed an agreement in Bahrain on 8 November for a 2,100 km railway from Kuwait to Oman.

Etihad flights from Abu Dhabi to Riyadh and Dammam stay cancelled through November amid Houthi attacks. Gulf News reports no new UAE-wide restrictions.

Qatar, central to Iran mediation, took its proposal to Trump. Qatar is now the main channel between Washington and Tehran.

UAE reported a major oil export surge despite the conflict. This is a Fox News summary (unconfirmed).

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic and a cruise missile fired at Riyadh on 10 November, plus a third missile aimed at the south. No damage or casualties were reported, and the Houthis have not commented.

The coalition reportedly destroyed three Houthi missile-component facilities in Sanaa the day before (single-source) (unconfirmed).

Fighting has escalated since September, when the Houthis moved toward Bab el-Mandeb and stepped up strikes on Saudi airports, ports and oil sites.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

A Jordanian security officer was stabbed to death in an Amman terror attack on 5 November; the attacker was wounded and arrested. Egypt condemned it and affirmed solidarity.

Jordan's Safadi visited Damascus on 9 November, deepening Amman's ties with the new Syrian government.

Sisi said in late September that Egypt lost about 20 dollarsbillion in Suez Canal revenue to regional instability; canal receipts are recovering. August revenue was 567 dollarsmillion.

9. Turkey.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Searches found only older items, including Erdogan's warning to Trump against renewed war with Iran and Fidan's announcement of an Erdogan visit to Syria (dates unconfirmed).

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Eastern Libya ratified the 2019 Turkey-Libya maritime agreement, drawing Greek and US opposition. The House also named Fawzi Abu Khazam central bank governor, per Stimson's 4 November outlook.

Air Algerie resumed flights to Tripoli after 12 years. This points to warming Algeria-Tripoli ties.

Le Monde reports Algeria is deploying troops in Niger to counter a feared Morocco-UAE-Libya axis. Republished by Morocco World News (a Moroccan outlet) and single-source (unconfirmed).

11. Defence and security.

Iran's IRGC Navy said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an "unauthorized route" in Hormuz, and Fars reported a third. Independent confirmation of the mine claim is lacking.

The US military says its aircraft fired on a commercial vessel's stern in the Gulf of Oman, disabling it as it allegedly tried to breach the blockade. The blockade dates to April.

Rep. Thomas Massie filed eight articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Hegseth over the Iran war. A House vote could come by mid-week, with Democrats now controlling Congress after the midterms; outcome uncertain.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent settled about 5.85% lower on 9 November at 92.57 dollarsand WTI at 86.85 dollars, per one daily note. Other outlets cite different intraday figures; prices stayed near 91 dollarson 10 November.

October OPEC output rose 1.38 million b/d to 22.87 million b/d, led by Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq. Washington has no immediate plan to lift its Hormuz blockade.

The Khor Mor outage removes a key gas supply for Kurdistan's power grid.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump says renewed strikes on Iran are "highly unlikely" with a strong deal, while Rubio says talks are far from done.

China's Wang Yi met Iran's Ali Bagheri Kani in Beijing on 2 November, backing dialogue and Iran's sovereignty. Mediators are weighing whether China could help guarantee US sanctions relief.

Iran is courting Beijing and Moscow for leverage in talks with Washington.

Board of Peace envoy Mladenov and Tony Blair pressed Netanyahu to move on the Trump Gaza plan. The EU and Russia produced no significant new Middle East moves in our searches.
