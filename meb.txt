Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Iran talks stall as Hormuz tankers burn and Houthis fire on Riyadh.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 12 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump met Qatar's prime minister on 9 November about ending the eight-month Iran war, but no deal is near. Reuters-sourced reporting quotes US and Gulf officials doubting an imminent agreement, while Trump called new US strikes "quite unlikely" and said the war could end "fairly soon".

Brent rose about 1% to roughly 92.5 dollarson 11 November after the IRGC said more tankers were hit in Hormuz. Prices had dipped below 90 dollarsthe day before on Trump's remarks; the move shows how headline-driven the market remains.

Houthi missiles targeted Riyadh for a second day running, with Saudi air defences intercepting them. The coalition spokesman said three cruise missiles aimed at the Riyadh region were destroyed on 11 November, a sign the Yemen war is now a direct Saudi-Houthi front.

Israel's new Knesset opened with no clear governing majority. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats against 48 for the opposition, and coalition bargaining is expected to run for weeks.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israeli strikes killed three people, including two brothers, in Gaza, according to medics. Arab News also reports little progress on reconstruction under the ceasefire; Gaza's health officials count at least 1,600 Palestinians killed since it began, Israel says four soldiers died.

Settlers burned bulldozers and vehicles in Beita's industrial zone near Nablus on 9 November, per WAFA. A separate report says the army incursion stopped residents and firefighters reaching the site (unconfirmed, Substack source).

Settlers used tear gas on farmers in Beit al-Roush near Hebron on 10 November. The UN (OCHA) recorded 457 West Bank attacks causing casualties or damage in October, the highest monthly count since 2006.

About 30 armed settlers seized eight Palestinian homes near Jericho on 4 November, displacing at least 70 people. The Jericho governor called the purchase a forgery.

Israel's parties have until 18 November to challenge the election results. Netanyahu's Likud accuses Arab-led parties of having "stolen" the vote, and a Netanyahu-Eisenkot unity deal remains possible but unlikely.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israeli strikes and shelling continued in south Lebanon on 9 to 10 November, including Qantara, Kfar Tibnit and Markaba. Lebanese outlets call this a breach of the US-brokered framework deal; Israel says it targets Hezbollah infrastructure.

Lebanon's Health Ministry says Israeli attacks since 2 March have killed 4,390 people (figure as of 5 November). The UN also warns that over one million people face acute food shortages.

Lebanon's PM Nawaf Salam promised a strict probe after five Hezbollah members were arrested near Bsharri-Batroun, and UNIFIL said it came under fire. The US is pressing the army to disarm Hezbollah, which refuses.

The Akkar-Homs border crossing reopened after two years closed. Officials say it could handle 10,000 travellers a day.

Syria denied sending troops to Yemen and signed Saudi agreements during al-Sharaa's 4 November visit, including 30 dollarsmillion for water meters. Enab Baladi also reports October marked a peak in Israeli violations in southern Syria.

4. Iran.

Iran's security council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran will not move "a single atom" of enriched uranium abroad. Russia had offered to take some material; Tehran rejected it, citing plans for nuclear-powered vessels.

The IRGC Navy said two more tankers hit mines and burned in Hormuz on 11 November, after a similar claim on 10 November. The claims are unverified; the IRGC says the ships used an "unauthorized" route with US air cover.

Iran says it has not suspended its NPT commitments, but hardliners openly discuss a bomb. IRGC-linked Tasnim has argued the case for leaving the treaty, which would end what little inspection access remains.

Iran's proposal, sent via Qatar on 5 November, trades lifting its Hormuz blockade for an end to the US port blockade, some sanctions relief and a halt to bombing. Iranian officials dispute that talks are formally under way.

Tehran Times (state-aligned) frames the uranium refusal as Iran's "big no". Read it as signalling, not a neutral account.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

A suspected drone attack hit the Khor Mor gas field in Iraqi Kurdistan on 8 November, and Baghdad formed a joint committee to investigate on 9 November. The field supplies power across the region.

PM Ali al-Zaidi's government says five armed factions have agreed to place weapons under state control. A spokesman did not name them, and talks with two more are expected to end by June 2027.

US envoy Tom Barrack praised al-Zaidi for acting against Iran-backed militias, including cancelling Mosul airport contracts. (unconfirmed, single-source summary)

The KRG urged the PUK to end its cabinet boycott amid KDP-PUK disputes over parliament. Government formation in Erbil remains delayed.

KRG president Nechirvan Barzani met Syria's al-Sharaa on 11 November. The meeting is notable given Kurdish concerns about Damascus.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Qatar, Bahrain and the UAE condemned the Houthi missile attacks on Riyadh as breaches of Saudi sovereignty. A Houthi strike on Riyadh's airport last month reportedly killed 13 people.

The supertanker MT Fujairah Energy was hit by a projectile on 10 November, injuring four crew. The attacker was not named in the report.

Etihad suspended Riyadh and Dammam flights until 19 November, and Qatar Airways also paused Saudi routes. The war is now disrupting regional aviation links.

Anwar Gargash said the UAE does not want a new round of conflict but is braced for the worst.

Iran reportedly attacked navigation infrastructure on an Omani island. (unconfirmed, via WSJ as relayed)

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Yemen's government claims 190 Houthi fighters killed or wounded in intensified fighting, and the Houthis claim heavy blows too. Neither side's figures are verified; over 300,000 people are displaced.

Government forces claim new advances as Houthi border attacks on Saudi Arabia resume.

Saudi air strikes reportedly hit Sanaa (including the state oil company), Hodeidah and Saada, while the Houthis claim attacks on Abqaiq. Claims are unverified.

Evergreen resumed Suez transits on 8 November after nearly three years. I found no confirmed new Red Sea ship attack in the last 72 hours.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

A Jordanian court charged ex-convict Muhannad Al Hmouri with terrorism over the fatal stabbing of a police officer in Amman. It followed an attack on a church on 23 October that killed nine.

Israel's military chief visited the Jordan border on 11 November, calling the Jordan Valley "Israel's eastern defensive wall".

Egypt's foreign minister says Cairo is in talks with Tripoli over a maritime boundary deal with Turkey. (unconfirmed, aggregator headline)

Suez Canal revenue rose 94% year on year to 724 dollarsmillion in October, the highest monthly figure since the Red Sea crisis began. The IMF programme reportedly expires 15 December.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan hosted the COP31 leaders' summit in Antalya on 11 November, urging rich countries to shoulder more climate finance.

Erdogan reportedly withdrew from the receiving line as Cyprus's president arrived. Nicosia called it a sign of weakness (unconfirmed, Cypriot summary).

The death toll from the Izmir steel plant explosion rose to 18.

Erdogan will receive Qatar's emir in Istanbul on 13 November. Iran mediation is likely on the agenda.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Libyan PM Dbeibah discussed the 4+4 talks and the election process with the UN envoy. The east also named Fawzi Abu Khazam central bank governor.

Air Algerie restarted Tripoli flights after 12 years. Stimson reads it as warmer Algiers-Tripoli ties.

Tunisia's October inflation held at 5.6% amid protests and economic strain.

Morocco plans a 815 dollarsmillion Tanger Med expansion. Nothing else significant found for Morocco in 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM said on 11 November it is enforcing an "ironclad" naval blockade of Iran while keeping Hormuz open to shipping. Tanker traffic is still a fraction of normal.

The US launched Task Force Iron Gate on 8 November to cut Iran's land and Caspian supply routes. Analysts say the Caspian element may be symbolic.

US forces disabled the M/V Huda on 7 November for attempting to breach the blockade. At least 23 US service members have died in the war, per Pentagon records cited by media.

Iran says it will target US bases in Europe if US jets use them in war. (unconfirmed, Haaretz headline)

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded near 92.5 dollarsand WTI near 86.7 dollarson 11 November. Analysts note a deal would not restore flows quickly given demining and insurance.

One tracker says only 3 ships crossed Hormuz on 8 November against a normal of about 85 daily. This is an aggregator's estimate.

Egypt's Suez revenue hit 4.67 dollarsbillion in FY2025/26, still under half the pre-crisis peak.

Saudi oil assets (Abqaiq, Aramco Tower) are in Houthi sights, and Saudi strikes hit Yemen's oil company.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump claimed the US has "won" the Iran war and now wants "total victory". He also said the war ends "when we want to end it".

Rubio reportedly judged the Qatar talks had yielded little, contrasting with Trump's "positive momentum".

G7 states stressed avoiding a "security void" in Lebanon after UNIFIL's imminent withdrawal.

Russia and China publicly call for de-escalation yet are accused of materially backing Tehran. China reportedly buys about 90% of Iran's oil, per a US commission.

Russia and Iran stepped up diplomacy on 6 November alongside Doha-led mediation.
