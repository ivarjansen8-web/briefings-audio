Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

Houthis hit Saudi ports and refinery, Kuwait summons Iran envoy over deadly drone strike, Baghdad closes Kurdish crossings.

Compiled from sources dated 22 to 28 November 2026 (most items 25 to 27 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia escalated this week, with a Jazan refinery hit and one worker killed. The Saudi-led coalition says it also intercepted ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh and a drone near Jeddah, pulling Saudi Arabia directly into the wider Iran-linked regional war.

Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador on 27 November after a drone attack killed two Italian Rheinmetall technicians. Tehran denies involvement; Araghchi called the incident "suspicious" and a "precooked recipe for war".

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey vowed to bolster the Kingdom's security after a Makkah Defense Alliance meeting in Islamabad. It is a rare sign of a Turkish-Pakistani security commitment to Riyadh.

Baghdad closed two KRG-run border crossings (Al Waleed with Syria, Bashta with Iran) after a Federal Supreme Court ruling asserting federal control over borders, customs and oil. Kurdish parties reject the move as unconstitutional.

Israel's defence minister threatened a new Gaza offensive if Hamas does not disarm. This cuts across the US-led Board of Peace roadmap.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Katz warned of "Operation Gideon's Chariot II", which he said would displace about a million Gazans unless Hamas disarms voluntarily. Hamas and other factions reportedly agreed a gradual disarmament roadmap with the Board of Peace, but Netanyahu argues it is insufficient.

Netanyahu reportedly signalled to Jared Kushner he is willing to move forward on the Gaza plan. The board has heard similar assurances before and wants follow-through.

The IDF said it killed six militants in Gaza strikes and hit four Hamas weapons depots; Palestinian media reported two dead near Shifa Hospital in Gaza City. The claims come from the IDF and Palestinian media respectively and are not independently verified.

Israeli troops and settlers raided several West Bank locations on 27 November, injuring at least three Palestinians, per WAFA-cited reporting. The UN secretary-general has said the situation is "simply out of control". Briev, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Settlers burned 12 vehicles in the Ramallah and el-Bireh areas on 22 November; a Palestinian body logged 3,133 attacks in October (1,764 by forces, 1,369 by settlers). Figures are the Palestinian side's.

Domestic politics: Yashar and UTJ reportedly agreed key principles on a Haredi draft law, as coalition talks continue ahead of the election. It shapes the next government's Gaza and Iran policy.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Five Hezbollah members face arrest warrants in North Lebanon after security documents and explosives were seized. It signals growing legal pressure on the group inside Lebanon.

Hezbollah says Lebanon cannot be independent while Israel holds more than 500 sq km of its territory. Israeli occupation of the south continues, with large demolitions reported in Wadi Hujeir.

Construction of the Lebanon-Israel border wall is at a standstill, per Haaretz as relayed by L'Orient Today.

Syria's central bank now lets remittances be credited in original foreign currency. The step eases currency restrictions as the government forecasts 12.8% growth, while the World Bank sees 45.8% of Syrians below the poverty line.

Protests in several Syrian governorates backed Quneitra and Daraa against continued Israeli violations. Monitor Sijil counted 89 Israeli violations on 1 to 7 November.

Syria prepares the second anniversary of Assad's fall (8 December) and urged the EU to fast-track a partnership agreement.

4. Iran.

Satellite imagery reported 27 November shows Iran building a hardened underground complex between Isfahan and Natanz. A nuclear purpose cannot be ruled out, per the think tank cited.

Pressure grows inside Iran to leave the NPT: the Assembly of Experts urged examining withdrawal, while Araghchi says Iran remains a member. Iran also keeps refusing IAEA inspector access.

Trump said "we don't need a deal with Iran" while Tehran holds to its demands. Qatari and Omani mediators remain in contact with both sides.

Araghchi said reopening Hormuz is "not difficult" if the US accepts Iran's conditions, and called for new Gulf security structures.

Iran's Foreign Ministry rejected a German demand to halt enrichment.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Iraq closed the Al Waleed (Syria) and Bashta (Iran) crossings after a federal court ruling on border, customs and oil authority.

Baghdad denied sending troops towards Kurdistan; leaders' talks were set for next week.

The KRG prime minister rejected Baghdad's centralisation of oilfields and border control, and Kurdish parties are uniting against it.

A drone strike near Azadi camp was reported on 24 November. Shafaq News, 24 November (unconfirmed)

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Kuwait holds Iran fully responsible for continued attacks after the drone strike that killed two Italians at an air-defence station. UN chief Guterres urged restraint.

Saudi air defences downed two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh, and the Jazan refinery strike forced a partial shutdown. The UAE and Qatar condemned the attacks.

Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE condemned Iran's attack on Bahrain; Bahrain says it intercepted Iranian attacks.

Reports say Qatar and the UAE suspended flights to Saudi Arabia after strikes on Riyadh's airport. Aggregator, 28 November (unconfirmed)

Oman and Qatar remain mediators; Araghchi met Oman's sultan in Muscat on 17 November.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Houthis struck Jazan and targeted Jeddah's port on 25 November, the first time Jeddah has been targeted. They cite alleged Saudi attacks on Hodeidah as the trigger; damage claims come mainly from the Houthis.

Saudi Arabia struck Sanaa as Kuwait summoned Iran's envoy. The New Arab, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Bloomberg reports Houthi attacks on two Saudi Red Sea ports, with claimed hits on the East-West pipeline and refineries. Saudi confirmation of damage is limited.

The Houthis reportedly control Yemen's entire west coast, increasing their leverage over Red Sea shipping. Organiser, 26 November (unconfirmed)

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egypt's Mada Masr faces a court-ordered closure and a EGP 3 million fine; an appeal hearing was set for 25 November, and I found no report of the outcome. The case is a press-freedom test.

Egypt reopened its Tripoli embassy on 7 November after 12 years, amid concern over the Turkey-Libya maritime deal.

A Jordanian court upheld the conviction of a French-Lebanese man linked to Hezbollah.

9. Turkey.

Turkey joined Saudi Arabia and Pakistan in pledging to bolster Saudi security against Houthi attacks.

Erdogan hosted Libya's Khalifa Haftar in Ankara on 26 November, Haftar's first visit. It deepens Turkey's hand in eastern Libya before the Africa-Turkey Summit. Germa Ltd newsletter, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Hurriyet Daily News headlines mention Erdogan's diplomacy over Iran tensions; no dated item found. Hurriyet Daily News (unconfirmed)

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Haftar's Ankara visit led Libya's news on 26 November. Germa Ltd newsletter, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Libya's Investment Authority signed an oil and gas deal with Oman's OQ; Baker Hughes reopened its Tripoli office. Cooking gas shortages persist in Tripoli. Germa Ltd newsletter, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Algeria says its border guards killed two Moroccans near Naama on 20 November, alleging drug smuggling. Algeria cut ties with Morocco in 2021.

Tunisia: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM says a Palau-flagged vessel that left Bandar Abbas and ignored warnings was struck in the Gulf of Oman. Al-Arabiya, 28 November (unconfirmed)

IRGC says an oil tanker hit a naval mine trying to transit Hormuz. The claim is Iranian and unverified. Pomegra, November 2026 (unconfirmed)

US strikes on Iranian missile sites around Qeshm followed Iranian drone attacks on Bahrain on 18 November. CENTCOM said no US damage.

The war began on 28 February 2026; an interim deal collapsed on 8 July.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent settled at 95.44 dollarson Friday 27 November, ending a three-week losing streak. The figure is secondary-sourced. Finimize, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Hormuz traffic is about 9% of pre-war levels, with one transit reported on 22 November versus about 85 a day normally. Tracker figures are modelled. Straits.live, 28 November (unconfirmed)

Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites add supply risk beyond Hormuz. Organiser, 26 November (unconfirmed)

Syria's finance ministry forecasts 12.8% growth despite entrenched poverty.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Iranian commentary questions whether Trump, Xi and Putin struck a deal over Iran at their 19 November Shenzhen summit. Tehran is divided and increasingly disillusioned with Moscow and Beijing.

Araghchi says Russia and China oppose any change to Iran's nuclear doctrine.

Kushner is pressing Netanyahu to back the Board of Peace in Gaza.

Analysts argue the E3 have lost leverage over Iran since issuing joint statements with the US. The piece is opinion.

A Chinese special envoy was due to visit Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia.
