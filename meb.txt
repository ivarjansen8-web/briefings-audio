Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

Houthis hit Riyadh tower as Hormuz stays shut; Baghdad moves on Kurdish oil.

Compiled from sources dated 21 to 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Houthi forces hit a tower in Riyadh's financial district on 24 November, injuring 11 people, per Saudi Civil Defence. Reuters reported the building, which houses the Public Investment Fund's headquarters, had been targeted for the second time in under a week, opening a Saudi-Houthi front inside the wider Iran war.

Iran's army chief said the Strait of Hormuz will stay shut and become "increasingly closed" until Tehran's seven conditions are met; Iran's navy said tankers hit mines east of the strait. Mine claims are Iranian and not independently verified; the US naval blockade also remains in force.

Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi ordered a federal takeover of Kurdistan-region border crossings and oilfields in disputed areas after an oil-smuggling probe. KRG PM Masrour Barzani called the orders "in no way acceptable", setting up a Baghdad-Erbil confrontation.

Israel's defence minister Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons and that Israel should keep a preemptive-strike option, preferably with US participation. Haaretz-cited reporting says the IDF has not independently backed his urgency assessment; Iran denies the claim.

Ukraine's Zelenskyy confirmed Ukrainian naval drones hit a Russian warship and a cargo vessel off Benghazi, Libya. The strike exports the Ukraine war to the Libyan coast and Russia's supply route through Haftar's east.

2. Israel and Palestine.

An Israeli strike in Gaza City killed the commander of Hamas's internal security apparatus on 24 November, per a joint IDF-Shin Bet statement. It is the latest targeted killing despite the nominal ceasefire, and negotiators have not agreed terms for Hamas disarmament or Israeli withdrawal.

A drone strike on a police post west of Khan Younis killed three Palestinians; a local Palestinian report put the day's Gaza toll at nine (unconfirmed). Displaced families in central Gaza are moving again as Israeli forces push west of the withdrawal line.

Settlers burned 12 vehicles near Ramallah and attacked several villages, per WAFA as relayed by Al Jazeera. OCHA counted 377 settler attacks during the olive harvest (1 Sep-2 November), a record, and ACLED says 2026 is the most violent year for settler violence it has tracked.

Israel's post-election deadlock continues: Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats against 48 for Gadi Eisenkot's, and unity-government talks resumed on 23 November. UTJ declined to recommend Netanyahu, and the opposition won the Knesset speakership on 17 November.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel continues strikes in southern Lebanon amid a standoff over withdrawal and Hezbollah disarmament (headline-level report). Haaretz-sourced reporting says Israel occupied more than 600 sq km of south Lebanon after fighting from March to June 2026.

Israel's Lebanon-border wall is on hold, with only 50 km built since 2012, because of budget problems and the recent fighting. It suggests Israel expects a long deployment rather than a fixed boundary.

The US is discussing training Syrian soldiers in demining, five people familiar told Reuters (unconfirmed, single-source). It points to deepening US-Syria security cooperation.

Syria's Finance Ministry raised its 2026 growth forecast to 12.8%, and a Damascus court gave a former Assad-era Political Security officer 15 years for unlawful detention. Growth comes from a very low base; the sentence is part of slow transitional justice.

4. Iran.

US-Iran nuclear talks are deadlocked over enrichment; Araghchi said Iran could negotiate over its 60% stockpile but not ship it abroad. Mediators warn of renewed conflict.

Witkoff and Kushner met Qatari mediators in Berlin on 19 to 20 November; Trump told Axios he would not decide his next step on Iran until the new year. Iran's offer of a two-day ceasefire, Hormuz reopening and talks stays on the table, with demands including lifting the blockade and releasing frozen funds.

Iran says Hormuz could reopen within two days if Washington accepts its seven conditions. The US blockade began in July after the June memorandum collapsed; CENTCOM warns evading vessels may be boarded.

Tehran warned that US bases are within strike range after Katz's chemical-weapons claim.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Baghdad's order will close unofficial crossings and "seasonal roads" between four governorates and the Kurdish region, with Border Forces taking over. The 2027 budget draft would require the KRG to supply at least 400,000 bpd to SOMO.

Masrour Barzani rejected the orders and Masoud Barzani called the decision "troubling". Reuters notes Kurds see the move as risky for stability.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

The UAE and Qatar condemned the Houthi attack on Riyadh; Qatar called it a breach of international law. Houthi attacks have killed 26 people in Saudi Arabia since September, per Saudi authorities.

Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE jointly condemned Iranian attacks on Bahrain; Iranian airlines remain suspended to and from the UAE.

Iranian drones targeted Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base and Bahrain's sites around 17 to 18 November after a US strike on Qeshm Island. A tanker was also hit off Khasab, Oman, by an unidentified projectile.

Saudi Arabia approved a SR1.39 trillion 2027 budget, with growth expected to rebound.

Qatar's emir and Trump discussed mediation, and Doha says it remains hopeful of a US-Iran deal.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Saudi-led coalition air raids hit Houthi-held Hodeidah, with at least eight killed on 23 November; Houthi officials later put an earlier toll at 17. Casualty figures come from the warring sides.

The Houthis warned of strikes on Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports, told port workers to evacuate and urged shipping firms to withdraw vessels. The threat endangers Saudi Red Sea export routes that bypass Hormuz.

Saudi Arabia intercepted a drone and missile on 22 November as the Houthis reported an attack on Sanaa.

The US advised vigilance after Houthi escalation.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

An Egyptian appeals court hears Mada Masr's appeal on 25 November against a ruling closing the site and fining editor Lina Attalah EGP 3 million. Mada Masr's lawyer says appeal suspends enforcement; Amnesty urged reversal.

Amnesty also flagged the detention of six journalists from the fact-checking platform Matsadaash.

Egypt's foreign minister discussed the Benghazi strike with a US presidential adviser, and Egypt and France held the Cleopatra-2026 naval exercise.

Jordan and Uzbekistan upgraded ties to a strategic partnership, signing 19 documents and planning about 40 projects worth 1.5 dollarsbillion.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan called for broader consensus behind the "terror-free Turkiye" process and criticised the main opposition for using it as leverage.

Erdogan urged a swift end to the investment-fund probe, with liquidation and investor repayment under way.

Erdogan will make his first Libya visit since 2011 for the Turkiye-Africa summit, Fidan announced.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Eastern Libyan authorities said a Russian corvette appeared hit by a maritime drone on 22 November; Zelenskyy said seven drones were used and named a second target, the cargo ship MV Angara.

Tripoli's Government of National Unity demanded official clarification from Kyiv, and the UN mission called for an independent investigation. Libya is reportedly weighing an international lawsuit (headline only).

Tripoli hosts the LIBYES 2026 energy conference from 24 to 26 November.

Morocco announced an employment drive for one million young people from 2027 (headline only). Algeria and Tunisia: Quiet.

11. Defence and security.

Katz said Iran cannot yet carry out a chemical attack on Israel but warned of a first strike. A US intelligence report describes a possible Iranian plot against senior US officials (unconfirmed, sources unnamed).

The US struck an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island after an Iranian attack on a US radar installation in Bahrain.

Sanaa and Hodeidah saw coalition raids while Houthi missiles and drones reach Riyadh.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent settled around 91.40 dollarson 24 November, up about 2, dollarsafter touching its lowest since August; WTI closed near 82.05 dollars. Sources differ slightly on the settle.

Hormuz oil flows are about half of normal, while the IEA's November report shows supply exceeding demand for the first time since the war began. The glut is why prices are not higher.

Libya's Sharara pipeline restart added supply; OPEC+ meets on 29 November. Analysts expect Brent at 89 dollars-96.

Iraq's Kurdish oil takeover could disrupt northern exports.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump, Xi and Putin issued a "Declaration toward Peace" at a 19 November Shenzhen summit that omitted specific Iran and Ukraine commitments.

Trump is reportedly waiting until the new year on Iran, and earlier reporting said he rejected a seven-day ceasefire.

Russia's Benghazi exposure and Ukraine's strike raise the stakes for Moscow's Africa logistics.

EU: Quiet, with no EU statement found in the last 72 hours.
