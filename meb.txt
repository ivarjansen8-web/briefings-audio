Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Friday, 20 November.

Iran widens Gulf attacks after Qeshm strike; Israel's Gaza ground raid; US-China-Russia summit skips Iran.

Compiled from sources dated 17 to 20 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Iran and its allies widened attacks on the Gulf on 18 to 19 November, hitting Kuwait, Bahrain and Riyadh, after a US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island. Washington said the 17 November strike answered an Iranian attack on a US radar site in Bahrain, and Iran says it was retaliating for US drone surveillance and strikes on communications towers; the cycle is pulling every Gulf state into the US-Iran war.

Israel launched its first large ground push across the Gaza ceasefire line since the truce began, then said it had concluded the tank raid on 19 November. The Board of Peace separately warned that both Israel and Hamas are failing their commitments, so the October 2025 ceasefire is fraying.

Trump, Putin and Xi issued a joint statement on strategic stability after a Shenzhen summit on 19 November that did not mention Iran or Ukraine. The omission matters because Tehran had counted on Beijing and Moscow for cover.

Netanyahu is reportedly pressing the US to join a new four-to-seven-day strike campaign on Iran, while Trump says he is "not rushing" a decision. Israeli reports say the IDF and CENTCOM have shared plans and only timing is open (unconfirmed, Israeli media sourcing).

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israel's military said on 19 November it had finished a tank raid in central Gaza aimed at a suspected Hamas tunnel. It was the highest-profile incursion beyond the yellow line since the ceasefire, and Hamas called it a flagrant violation.

Gaza hospital officials said three Palestinians were killed as the operation began near Nuseirat and Bureij on 18 November. Al Jazeera reports at least 1,643 Palestinians killed since the ceasefire of 11 October 2025, a figure from Gaza health authorities.

The Board of Peace said on 18 November that the International Stabilisation Force and the Palestinian technocratic committee have still not entered Gaza. It also accused both sides of missing commitments, which leaves the Rafah pilot (about 200 troops, roughly 150 dollarsmillion) hostage to Israeli approvals.

Settlers killed a Palestinian woman and wounded 10 people in a raid in the Jericho area on 17 November, per WAFA. Al Jazeera gives her age as 72 while The Cradle, citing WAFA sources, says 60; a WAFA photographer was also beaten by soldiers.

Israeli authorities advanced 11 West Bank settlement plans covering 895 units, WAFA reported. ACLED says 2026 is on track to be the worst year for settler violence it has recorded.

Israel's politics are unresolved after the 27 October election. Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 seats against 67 for parties backing Eisenkot, and the Knesset elected Yashar's Matan Kahana speaker 66 to 52 after a six-hour filibuster.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel said it struck a former Hezbollah missile production and storage site at Janta in the Beqaa on 19 November, its second such strike that week. The IDF is also demolishing homes in southern villages, so the war that began on 2 March continues despite diplomacy.

US ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz visited Beirut on 15 November to push the "Trilateral Framework" on Hezbollah disarmament and IDF withdrawal. Mediation by Qatar, France and Turkey has reportedly produced no substantive progress (Israeli-government-linked summary).

President al-Sharaa named Ahmad Rawad Ramadan economy and foreign trade minister on 19 November. It is part of a government restructuring that splits some ministries.

Protests over Israeli violations in southern Syria spread to Daraa, Quneitra and Raqqa. Damascus says Israeli actions undermine de-escalation.

Syria and Egypt signed a civil aviation security protocol on 17 November. Homs also began clearing rubble at 75 sites, signalling slow reconstruction.

4. Iran.

Iran's foreign ministry called chemical weapons allegations "fraudulent" on 19 November. Israeli media say Mossad chief Roman Gofman took intelligence to Washington, and Trump told Fox News there were indications of gas and biological work (unconfirmed).

Iran said mines disabled tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, and a tanker was hit by an unknown projectile six nautical miles north of Khasab. UKMTO issued an alert on 19 November; Iran's mine claims are its own and unverified.

Tehran refuses nuclear talks until its conditions are met, and Iran International reports it rejects nuclear concessions even if the US accepts its Hormuz proposal. CBS reports Iran has not agreed to renewed monitoring or removal of highly enriched uranium.

Russia, China and Iran said on 18 November that IAEA work in Iran can resume only after military operations end. The IAEA has had no access to bombed sites since June 2025.

Iran's Assembly of Experts urged leaving the NPT, per Iranian state media reported by CBS. Foreign Minister Araghchi also denounced "foreign interference" on 19 November.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The Kurdistan Regional Government rejected Iraq's draft 2027 budget allocation of 13 trillion dinars (about 8.55 dollarsbillion) and demands 19.74 dollarsbillion. The gap sits on top of unresolved oil management and salary arrears.

Masrour Barzani met Prime Minister al-Zaidi in Baghdad on oilfield management and the budget. A US envoy praised the dialogue.

The KDP-PUK dispute has revived fears of rival administrations like the 1990s. The PUK quit the Kurdistan government on 5 November over a parliament dispute, and some officials floated separate allocations for Erbil and Sulaymaniyah.

Jordan and Iraq blocked Telegram over security concerns; the KRG said it received no federal notice.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Kuwait's air defences responded to drones on 18 to 19 November, the first since early September, and Iran's army said it targeted Patriot systems at Ali Al-Salem Air Base. Kuwait called it a flagrant violation of its sovereignty.

Bahrain intercepted Iranian attacks, its fourth in six days, and the Revolutionary Guards claimed a hit on a radar at Sheikh Isa Air Base. Bahrain hosts the US Fifth Fleet; the GCC secretary-general blamed Iran for the escalation.

Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh were intercepted, with minor damage to an Aramco building and debris damage in the King Abdullah Financial District. Qatar, Bahrain, the UAE and Syria condemned the attack.

The UAE denounced the Somali government's "hostile approach" on 19 November. The state news agency WAM reported it, and the reasons are not given in what I found.

The US reissued security warnings for Americans in Saudi Arabia, Bahrain and Kuwait. The embassy cites Iranian-backed Houthis and Iranian efforts to target US officials.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Saudi-led coalition said it hit about 60 Houthi targets in Sanaa, Hodeidah, Amran and Saada on 18 November, per Al Jazeera's Yemen page. It followed the Houthi missile fire at Riyadh.

Yemen's parliament approved a law designating the Houthis as terrorists on 17 November.

The Houthis claimed control of land overlooking Bab al-Mandab. Analysts told AGBI the shipping impact is largely symbolic because the group already shapes the lanes.

Saudi forces destroyed two Houthi ballistic missile launch platforms, per a coalition statement. Government-Houthi fighting has resumed since September.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egypt's President Sisi hosted Libya's Prime Minister Dbeibah in Cairo on 19 November, and the two signed nine deals. Cairo rejects the Turkey-Libya maritime memorandum, which it calls null.

Jordan sentenced three men to death by hanging over a deadly church attack, per Al Jazeera on 15 November. A stabbing outside the Sri Lankan embassy in Amman killed a corporal.

Jordan expects AI and data-centre projects to add about 5 dollarsbillion to GDP, its minister told Arab News.

Syria-Jordan coordination is moving to a higher council after an earlier airstrike crisis.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan said on 16 November that Turkey is entering a "very different period" with a prominent global position. The remarks came at an AKP student event in Ankara.

Erdogan vowed on 18 November that Turkey will not stay silent over Gaza's suffering. Daily Sabah, a government-aligned outlet, reported it.

Erdogan urged a return to diplomacy to end the US war on Iran on 12 November. Ankara is also among the Lebanon mediators.

Libya's eastern parliament approving the Turkey-Libya maritime deal strengthens Ankara's position against Cairo.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Libya's eastern House of Representatives reversed course and approved the Turkey-Libya maritime agreement. Egypt sent its objections to the UN on 5 November.

Benghazi authorities suspended 16 international NGOs, including MSF, NRC and DRC, over expired permits, per a secondary digest (unconfirmed).

Tunisia saw tear gas in Ettadhamen after a man died in detention, per the Stimson Center on 4 November.

Morocco argues that the UN mission MINURSO should leave once its mandate expires, while Moroccan and Algerian media trade accusations. Morocco World News is a Moroccan outlet.

11. Defence and security.

The Pentagon put the cost of the Iran war above 48 dollarsbillion, per a Times of Israel headline of 19 November. The US blockade, in place since 13 July, continues alongside limited strikes in Hormuz.

Senate Republicans blocked an Iran war powers resolution 48 to 51.

Trump reportedly asked the Pentagon and intelligence agencies whether Iran can be struck without triggering attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure (unconfirmed).

Nine security agency members were killed in a blast west of Tripoli in October. It shows the fragility of Libya's western security.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded around 92 dollars-93 on 19 November, about 15% lower than a month earlier, per Trading Economics. Prices slid despite Iran's mine claims.

Hormuz flows are disputed: Forbes, citing Reuters, says crude shipments are near prewar levels, while Kpler estimates about 8.5 million barrels a day.

Goldman Sachs expects Brent to average 88 dollarsin 2027, and the IEA expects a Q4 surplus.

The Kurdistan Region wants 19.74 dollarsbillion in Iraq's 2027 budget. Salary delays remain a pressure point.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

The US, China and Russia issued a "peace declaration" after their first summit in Shenzhen on 19 November. It covers UN reform, petroleum supply and nuclear-risk dialogue but names neither Iran nor Ukraine.

Washington has deprioritised an Iran deal: Al Jazeera reports Trump told Vance, the lead negotiator, to turn to domestic matters. Trump said he would wait until the new year.

Rubio says sanctions are working and there is "no need" to resume major combat. This sits uneasily with the Israeli push for a strike.

Europe: NewsNow reports the EU is preparing new Iran sanctions (headline only, unconfirmed).

What to watch.

Israeli and US decisions on a joint Iran strike in the coming days; Trump says he will wait until the new year, Netanyahu wants sooner.

Further Iranian drone attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain over the weekend of 21 to 22 November, and any US response around Qeshm.

Gaza: whether Israel repeats ground raids and whether the Board of Peace gets Israeli approval for the Rafah pilot before year-end.

Knesset coalition talks: Eisenkot's attempt to assemble a government in the week of 23 November.

Baghdad's 2027 budget talks with Erbil, and whether the PUK-KDP rift deepens.

Hormuz shipping and Brent on Monday 23 November, plus any Houthi follow-up on Riyadh and coalition strikes.
