Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

US strikes Qeshm after Iran's Bahrain attack, Houthis near Aden, Baghdad sets Kataib Hezbollah deadline.

Compiled from sources dated 15 to 18 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

US jets struck an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island on 17 November after an Iranian drone attack on a US base in Bahrain, the third Iranian drone operation against US facilities in Bahrain in five days. CENTCOM said the drones were intercepted with no US damage; Iran says it was answering US drone incursions.

Houthi forces have closed to within about 60 km of Aden and cut a key government supply route on the Red Sea coast. The advance threatens the Saudi-backed government's last major base.

Baghdad has set Kataib Hezbollah a deadline, reportedly Friday 20 November, to leave Jurf al-Nasr, with the army deployed and allied factions sending reinforcements. A clash between the state and an Iran-aligned militia would be a first of this scale.

Tehran called a CNN report of a dirty-bomb plan against Washington "rubbish"; Trump said the US takes it "very seriously". CNN cited seven unnamed US officials who described it as options-planning, not an imminent attack.

The EU says it is preparing further Iran sanctions and agreed to move ahead with an EU-Syria Association Agreement at the 16 November Foreign Affairs Council.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israeli fire killed at least four Palestinians in Gaza on 17 November, including a 6-year-old boy, according to local medics cited by Reuters; the Israeli military did not immediately comment. Gaza health authorities say at least 1,600 have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire.

The Israeli military dropped leaflets with coffee and cigarettes over Gaza to recruit collaborators, while Palestinian sources say the scenes reflect shortages, not support. It signals an information campaign running alongside a ceasefire that is eroding.

Settlers built a yeshiva outpost in Area A of the West Bank, nominally under full Palestinian control, according to Haaretz, which says it was evacuated once without success.

Settlers reportedly shot dead a Palestinian woman in her 70s near Jericho, which the Palestinian health ministry confirmed; the Israeli army cited a car-ramming attack on an Israeli vehicle. (unconfirmed: the article's date is unclear)

The Knesset elected Yashar's Kahana as speaker, 66 votes to 52, against the Likud candidate. The vote shows the Eisenkot-led bloc controlling the new Knesset while Netanyahu stays in office as caretaker; he has claimed the 27 October election was stolen.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem said the group should continue "the resistance" while hoping it would disarm voluntarily, as US criticism of the pace of disarmament grows. This is reported by the Israeli-linked ITIC, so treat the framing with care.

Israel reportedly struck a weapons shipment in Janta in the Beqaa Valley near the Syrian border. (unconfirmed: single source) The June US-brokered framework on disarmament and phased Israeli withdrawal is under strain.

Naharnet's 17 November headlines include France sending troops to Lebanon for joint patrols with the army. (unconfirmed: headline only)

Syria's UN envoy Ibrahim Olabi accused Israel of abductions of civilians and attacks on journalists in the south, and protests against Israeli incursions were reported in Daraa and Quneitra.

SDF commander Mazloum Abdi said integration of SDF-affiliated forces into state institutions should finish by end-2026. Analysts see it as the next test of whether Damascus-SDF agreements are implemented.

4. Iran.

Iran's IRGC said it targeted a US radar at Sheikh Isa Air Base, Bahrain, on 17 November, and Bahrain and Kuwait issued shelter warnings early on 18 November. Qatar warned the attack threatens peace talks.

Chief negotiator Ghalibaf said any dam, port or power plant near a US or Israeli base is "dual-use", and Bloomberg reports Iran threatens to hit civilian sites if the US does. It is a direct threat to Gulf infrastructure.

Foreign Minister Araghchi called the dirty-bomb report "rubbish", and Iran's UN mission called it disinformation meant to create pretexts for aggression. Araghchi also met Qatari mediators.

Pakistan says it is awaiting a US response to Iran's latest proposals to end the nine-month war, and Trump says a deal is "very close". Talks are stalled and low-intensity attacks continue.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The Iraqi army deployed to Jurf al-Nasr/Jurf al-Sakhar over alleged oil smuggling, and Kataib Hezbollah denies it. The government spokesperson confirmed a strict withdrawal deadline.

Kataib Hezbollah vowed to defend Iran against attacks and accuses Baghdad of passing intelligence to the US.

The KRG rejected Iraq's draft 2027 budget; Masrour Barzani said the region should receive 30 trillion dinars, not the 13 trillion proposed. Oil revenue handover remains the sticking point.

Two explosive drones hit Iranian Kurdish opposition bases near Sulaymaniyah, per Reuters via Middle East Eye, and sirens sounded over the Khor Mor gas field.

Foreign Policy warns a PUK-KDP parliamentary fight could split the Kurdistan Region.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Bahrain said it intercepted and destroyed a number of Iranian aerial attacks, and Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar condemned or criticised them. The attacks are drawing the Gulf closer to the war.

A Saudi-led coalition intercepted a Houthi drone heading for Riyadh, and the Houthis claim drones aimed at the East-West pipeline. (unconfirmed: Houthi claim)

An Indian sailor is missing and 21 Indians were evacuated after a vessel was attacked in Hormuz; Etihad cancelled Abu Dhabi flights to Riyadh and Dammam through 19 November.

Oman discussed maritime security with Iran amid rising attacks in Hormuz. Muscat remains a channel to Tehran.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Houthis are about 60 km from Aden and took Red Sea areas that government forces evacuated. Yemen's government says it is redeploying to protect Aden.

Yemen's army claims 661 attacks on Houthi positions, killing or wounding 173 fighters. That is the army's own figure, not independently verified.

Saudi-backed forces reinforced a key district near Aden, and Saudi Arabia dismissed its coalition commander. (unconfirmed: single source)

The Houthis released 600 government-force prisoners held since July.

Red Sea Tracker lists the last ship attack as 4 October, but Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd and ONE are reportedly sending ships back via Suez. Control of Bab el-Mandeb's approaches is the risk.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Jordan will block Telegram in the kingdom over national security concerns, authorities said.

Jordan's interior minister met Denmark's migration minister on 17 November. A routine item.

Egypt and Jordan's foreign ministers have been coordinating on de-escalation, in an earlier-November call about preventing a return to military operations. Dated before this window.

Egypt: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan praised COP31, hosted in Antalya, as the most successful edition so far.

Turkey backs the EU-Syria association track; Erdogan's son visited Lebanon, which Turkish Minute reads as a widening international profile.

Erdogan reaffirmed support for a Palestinian state on 1967 borders (15 November, via Anadolu).

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

A House of Representatives session in Zintan failed to convene on 16 November; UNSMIL expressed regret and the HoR accuses Dbeibah of obstruction. (unconfirmed: the HoR's account)

Dbeibah pressed Guterres for elections at COP31 in Antalya on 12 November. (unconfirmed: his office's account)

Quiet: Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco had nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

A tracker puts Hormuz at about 3 transits on 8 November against about 85 a day normally, and war-risk insurance is quoted at 6 to 10% of hull value. These are tracker and broker estimates.

Iran's army said it shot down a US LUCAS drone over Hormuz, and the IRGC claimed two tankers hit mines. (unconfirmed: Iranian claims)

Israel's Katz reportedly threatens Beirut's southern suburbs if Hezbollah and Iran attack. (unconfirmed: aggregator headline, undated)

12. Economy and energy.

Brent is about 92 dollarsand WTI about 85, dollarsas expectations of a supply surplus outweigh tanker attacks. The IEA sees a Q4 surplus of about 1.7 million bpd.

Brent averaged 124 dollarsin October, and the EIA sees about 100 dollarsfor November-December. That forecast predates the latest decline.

Iraq resumed oil exports through the rehabilitated Turkish pipeline, per Rudaw, and Kpler puts Middle East crude outages at about 3.8 million bpd.

OPEC+ meets on 29 November to set 2027 output targets.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump is in Asia, and Fox says he will raise the Iran war with Xi and Putin. He said a deal is "very close".

Kaja Kallas said the EU is "preparing further sanctions" on Iran after French accusations of IRGC involvement in attacks on shops, which Iran denies.

The EU agreed to advance the EU-Syria Association Agreement and committed 620 eurosmillion for 2026 to 27.

The Board of Peace has warned the UN Security Council that the Gaza ceasefire faces collapse without full implementation, and no stabilisation force has deployed. (unconfirmed: date unclear)
