Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Qatar-mediated Iran diplomacy meets fresh Gulf shipping and Riyadh missile attacks; Netanyahu's Knesset standoff.

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 11 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump called renewed strikes on Iran "quite unlikely" after meeting Qatar's prime minister on 9 November, while Tehran denied that any talks are planned. Qatar is the main channel for indirect US-Iran contacts, and Vance is reported to speak to the Qatari premier almost daily, but Rubio said no breakthrough was imminent.

An ADNOC Logistics and Services vessel was damaged in an attack in UAE territorial waters on 10 November. ADNOC did not name the vessel, the attacker or any casualties; the attack adds to a string of Gulf shipping strikes while mediation continues.

Saudi forces intercepted Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh on 10 November, hours after reported damage to the East-West pipeline. Saudi-led strikes on Sanaa followed; the Houthis had not claimed the launches at the time of reporting.

Israel's 26th Knesset was sworn in on 10 November, and Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar cancelled coalition talks with Likud after Netanyahu's address. Yashar also filed a motion to replace Speaker Amir Ohana with Matan Kahana.

2. Israel and Palestine.

At least eight Palestinians, including a father and his 13-year-old son, were killed in Israeli attacks on Gaza on 10 November, according to Al Jazeera's count (The National reported at least seven). Gaza's health ministry says more than 1,600 have been killed since the US-brokered truce took effect.

The first strong storm of the season flooded about 70,000 tents in Gaza within 12 hours and killed at least five people, per Gaza's Government Media Office. Negotiators still cannot agree terms for Hamas disarmament or Israeli withdrawal, with Israel controlling about two-thirds of the territory.

The UAE's Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid ordered tents for 50,000 people in Gaza ahead of winter. Arab News also reports the Trump-led Board of Peace saying Israel is too slow on aid and deployment (unconfirmed; headline only, article not read).

Netanyahu's bloc lost ground in the election two weeks ago, and coalition arithmetic is now open. Likud says Netanyahu "stands by" the view that cooperation with supporters of terrorism is impossible; seat figures are provisional.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

The Dabousiya-Abboudieh crossing between Akkar and Homs reopened on 9 to 10 November after two years, since an Israeli strike damaged its bridge in November 2024. Lebanon's transport minister expects up to 10,000 travellers and 150 to 200 trucks daily, and a Syrian official says the Arida crossing opens next month.

Israeli shelling and strikes continued in south Lebanon (for example Bani Haiyyan and Zawtar ash-Sharqiyah on 7 November), with Israel-Lebanon framework talks under way and Hezbollah's leader calling any agreement invalid. Israel's "security zone" blocks army deployment and reconstruction. Israeli monitoring group ITIC, 2 to 9 November (Israeli-aligned source)

Amal is expanding its influence in south Lebanon and taking over positions it previously shared with Hezbollah, per L'Orient-Le Jour on 8 November (unconfirmed; single report relayed by aggregator).

Israeli fire hit a Syrian media crew in Quneitra on 9 November, per the Syrian Journalists Union, and protests over Israeli violations took place in Daraa, Quneitra, Hasakah and Raqqa. Cumulative violations this year stand at 2,427 by the union's count.

Syria denied sending troops to Yemen, saying its defence pact with Saudi Arabia covers training only. The denial follows Houthi-Saudi escalation.

The UN says more than two million people in Syria need winter assistance, and floods hit Latakia, Aleppo and Deir Ezzor (about 115 homes in al-Bukamal).

4. Iran.

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei told Washington to stop "manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks." Foreign Minister Araghchi said there are no plans for direct talks and that Tehran wants the naval blockade lifted and frozen funds released first (citing Axios).

A deal under discussion would reopen the Strait of Hormuz in return for US sanctions relief, with nuclear talks later, according to Times of Israel. Trump says he is not in a hurry to strike but still wants to "take care" of one or two nuclear sites.

Iranian state TV aired a debate signalling cautious openness to nuclear talks via intermediaries, including diluting the 60% stockpile. Iran International (opposition-leaning) reported it on 10 November; hundreds of kilograms of enriched uranium remain unaccounted for per CNN.

The IRGC Navy claimed responsibility for a tanker casualty in the Strait of Hormuz on 10 November (unconfirmed; claim relayed second-hand), and reports described US strikes on Qeshm island overnight, with a provincial official saying vital infrastructure was unaffected.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Iraq reopened the Sheeb border crossing with Iran on 10 November after about a month, amid US Treasury pressure to close all but two crossings. The Mandali crossing is to close for maintenance from about 13 November.

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi and KRG PM Masrour Barzani discussed oil management and the Kurdistan share of the 2027 budget, and US envoy Tom Barrack praised the direct dialogue.

The PUK and New Generation asked the Federal Supreme Court to annul the 4 November Kurdistan Parliament session, and a headline says the PUK quit the Kurdistan government over the dispute (unconfirmed; headline only).

Floods in Erbil killed three and forced the rescue of more than 300 people, per Governor Omed Xoshnaw.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Saudi Arabia: the Aramco Tower in Riyadh's financial district was damaged by falling debris from a 8 November missile attack, and officials inspected it on 10 November. The US Embassy warns staff need special authorisation to use Saudi airports after missile and drone attacks.

UAE: the foreign ministry called attacks on shipping a "grave threat" to navigation and global energy supply. A Qatari Nakilat vessel was reportedly attacked days earlier.

Qatar: the prime minister described "good progress" after talks with Trump and Vance, and Trump said Qatar and Saudi Arabia want a peaceful outcome.

The six GCC states reaffirmed support for Morocco's sovereignty over the Sahara.

Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman: Quiet in the sources found.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The coalition says it intercepted a ballistic and a cruise missile fired at Riyadh, a ballistic missile aimed south and a drone over the Eastern Province, and airstrikes hit Houthi targets in Sanaa. Counts differ by outlet.

Houthis deny hitting the East-West pipeline near Medina and say they struck infrastructure near Riyadh instead. Fighting between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia has run since July.

Houthi forces reportedly captured Perim island in the Bab el-Mandeb about two months ago; analysts caution that control of the strait is unclear.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

The UN human rights office said on 10 November that six journalists from the fact-checking site Matsadash remain detained and urged Egypt to reverse measures against it and Mada Masr.

A court fined Mada Masr editor Lina Attalah EGP 3 million and ordered the site closed on 27 October. An appeal hearing is set for 11 November per Mada Masr or 25 November per Ahram Online; the dates conflict.

Jordan: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours. King Abdullah called West Bank settler incursions a war crime on 22 September.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan announced two new oil fields in the Gabar region with potential reserves of 135 million barrels. He did so ahead of the COP31 climate summit in Antalya (9 to 20 November).

Erdogan met Brazil's Lula and Saudi Arabia's energy minister at COP31, and Turkey and Saudi Arabia signed an agreement; about 102 leaders are expected on 11 to 12 November.

At the 88th Ataturk commemoration on 10 November Erdogan cited progress against terrorism, as he spoke of strengthening the domestic front. anews, 10 November (pro-government outlet)

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Morocco is rallying its Sahara coalition at the UN General Assembly; Hespress counts 128 UN members in a broad support category for its sovereignty claim or autonomy plan (Moroccan outlet's own mapping).

Libya: the UN Security Council extended UNSMIL's mandate to October 2027, and the Libya Observer reported that Boulos warned of sanctions against those obstructing the political transition (unconfirmed; aggregator headlines, dates unclear).

Algeria beat Morocco 1 to 0 in a U17 qualifier in Sousse, Tunisia. Morocco next plays Egypt on 13 November.

Tunisia and Algeria politics: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

UKMTO reported a separate tanker strike near Hormuz on 10 November, and it is unclear whether it is linked to the ADNOC attack.

US military preparations are under way near the Gaza border for international force deployment, per Israeli media (unconfirmed; relayed by The National's roundup).

Arab states have warned Iran of individual and collective retaliation over attacks, per the WSJ as relayed by Iran International (unconfirmed; secondary report).

A Lebanese military court issued arrest warrants for two Hezbollah members caught with explosives at a checkpoint, with 13 suspects indicted. Israeli monitoring group ITIC (Israeli-aligned source)

12. Economy and energy.

Brent briefly fell below 90 dollarson 10 November for the first time since August, closing at 91.68 dollars. It rose to about 92.20 dollarson 11 November as Hormuz attacks offset truce hopes.

LSEG analysts say higher Hormuz flows would still depend on mine clearance, insurance costs and shipowner willingness even if a deal is struck.

Turkey's 135-million-barrel Gabar find is small against regional output but politically useful for Ankara's import bill.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

US: Trump's tone on Iran is softer after last week's midterms, which Iran International says he lost ground in. The Qatar meeting was framed around a new ceasefire and restarting nuclear talks.

China: Wang Yi met Iran's Ali Bagheri Kani in Beijing on 2 November, backing Iran's sovereignty while stressing dialogue, and also met Saudi Arabia's security adviser on protecting energy routes. Chinese MFA, 3 November (state source)

Russia: Lavrov says Iran wants to resume dialogue with the US and the IAEA and that Moscow will help (unconfirmed; undated feed). EU: Quiet in the sources found. TASS (state source)

US envoy Tom Barrack is backing Baghdad-Erbil talks, and a new US ambassador to the UAE is under discussion (unconfirmed; podcast title only).
