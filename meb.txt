Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Houthis and Saudi Arabia trade strikes as Hormuz stays shut; Iraq retakes Jurf al-Sakhar.

Compiled from sources dated 19 to 22 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Saudi Arabia and the Houthis are in open exchange of fire: the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh early on 21 November, a day after it struck Hodeidah port. The Houthis have threatened Jeddah and other Red Sea ports, widening a Yemen war into a Gulf one.

Iraqi forces entered Jurf al-Sakhar on 20 November under a deal with Kataib Hezbollah, a town the Iran-backed militia held for about a decade. PM Ali al-Zaidi calls it the start of a national drive to extend state authority, though the extent of the militia's withdrawal is unclear.

US-Iran talks remain stalled while tankers are hit in Hormuz. A US strike on a Qeshm Island missile site was described by a US official as "self-defense", and Iran says it has held talks with mediator Qatar.

Israel's post-election bargaining continues: Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar bloc were due to meet on 22 November on a possible unity government. Netanyahu's bloc lost its majority in the 27 October vote.

Gaza's ceasefire is fraying: a Board of Peace adviser warned the body could "throw the keys back to the UN" over Israeli obstruction. Strikes killed nine Palestinians over two days.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Nine Palestinians were killed in Israeli strikes across Gaza over two days, including in Bureij, Deir al-Balah, Nuseirat and Gaza City. The October 2025 ceasefire has cut major fighting but not strikes.

An Israeli strike killed three people in a vehicle linked to World Central Kitchen in Gaza City, according to witnesses; the Israeli military said it targeted militants. The competing accounts are unverified.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council that both Israel and Hamas have missed ceasefire commitments, citing 17 dollarsbillion in pledges and 280 dollarsmillion deployed. It says Israel has not authorised the International Stabilization Force, and wants a pilot disarmament programme in Rafah; Hamas accepted the roadmap in July per the board's account.

Likud reportedly wants to keep security zones in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria as a condition for unity talks (unconfirmed, Kan report relayed by a secondary site). This would collide with Eisenkot's terms and with the Board of Peace plan.

Settlers killed an elderly Palestinian woman and wounded 10 in a village attack in the central West Bank on 17 November; WAFA reported a further wave of settler attacks on 21 November. Israel also advanced 11 settlement plans with 895 housing units.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israeli strikes hit Nabatieh, Bint Jbeil and Marjayoun districts in south Lebanon on 21 November. Lebanese figures cited by Al Jazeera put deaths at 4,386 and wounded at 12,407 since the war resumed on 2 March.

President Joseph Aoun demanded Israel withdraw from "every inch" of Lebanese territory and criticised Iranian interference. He said implementation of the June framework has stalled as Hezbollah refuses to disarm.

Israel struck a site near Janta in the Bekaa on 19 November that it said was an underground Hezbollah missile facility. The strikes test the framework linking Israeli withdrawal to disarmament.

Syria's intelligence service said it intercepted a weapons shipment bound for Lebanon (unconfirmed, SANA via a single outlet). It fits a pattern of Damascus policing the border against Hezbollah resupply.

A car bomb in a Damascus suburb on 15 November killed two people, per the CFR tracker. Security in the transitional al-Sharaa government's capital remains fragile.

4. Iran.

US fighter jets struck an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island, which a US official called a "self-defense" strike; Iran says it shot down a US drone over Qeshm on Saturday. The war that began with US-Israeli strikes in late February has no functioning ceasefire.

Iran says it held talks with Qatar, while Trump said "we don't need a deal with Iran". Tehran is demanding confidence-building steps such as lifting the naval blockade and releasing frozen funds.

Witkoff and Kushner met Qatari officials in Berlin on 19 November to explore restarting talks (unconfirmed, secondary outlet). Qatar is the main channel between the two sides.

Avigdor Liberman said there is no security justification for striking Iran now and that a decision to do so would be political. It suggests Israeli debate over renewed action against Iran.

Iran International reported that Tehran rejects nuclear talks despite ongoing mediation. It is an opposition-leaning outlet and the report is dated 1 November.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Iraqi troops took over Jurf al-Sakhar under a deal that provides for militia withdrawal, legal action over oil smuggling and a joint security command. A senior militia commander reportedly said the group would not leave.

Analysts question whether PM al-Zaidi can follow through against Iran-backed groups. The PMF's central area reportedly stays under PMF control.

The KDP-PUK standoff in Kurdistan is deepening: the PUK withdrew its ministers and Bafel Talabani rejected the newly convened parliament, threatening a separate government in Sulaimani. The KDP reached the 51-seat quorum. Middle East Forum (unconfirmed, undated analysis)

Baghdad's draft budget gives the Kurdistan Region 12.7 percent and an oil export target of 445,000 b/d; Erbil wants 14.14 percent and agrees only to 250,000 b/d. Tishwash roundup, 21 November (unconfirmed, aggregator)

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Saudi air defences intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles over Riyadh on 21 November, after a Houthi strike hit Riyadh's tallest skyscraper on Thursday. It was the third consecutive day of Riyadh alerts.

Qatar condemned the Houthi attacks "in the strongest terms" and said Saudi security is integral to its own. The UAE also condemned them.

Qatar says it remains hopeful of a US-Iran deal and warned a Hormuz closure would be a global crisis; UAE officials say they are braced for the worst.

Iran has widened attacks on Gulf states after the US Qeshm strike, per CNN. Detail by country is thin in today's results.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Saudi-led coalition struck Hodeidah port on 20 November, saying it targeted weapons smuggling capabilities. The UN warned that escalation threatens humanitarian supply routes; no independent damage assessment exists.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree told workers to leave Saudi Red Sea ports and urged shippers to pull vessels.

Government forces pulled out of Bab al-Mandeb positions on 15 to 16 November and the Houthis took Dhubab Airport, edging toward Aden.

A Houthi map claiming 7,100 sq km secured in Lahij and Taiz is unverified. Red Sea Futures (unconfirmed)

8. Egypt and Jordan.

The IMF reached a staff-level deal to unlock about 2.3 dollarsbillion for Egypt before the programme expires on 15 December. Total payouts would reach about 9.6 dollarsbillion.

Suez Canal revenue was about 4.67 dollarsbillion in FY2025/26, up 23%, per the canal's chairman as relayed by a secondary source. Transits are still below pre-crisis levels.

Jordan blocked Telegram on 16 November, citing digital security.

A security officer was stabbed to death in Amman on 5 November in what Jordan called a terrorist attack.

9. Turkey.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Searches returned only undated Hurriyet Daily News pages, including a claim that Erdogan announced the Kizilelma drone's entry into service on 19 November, which I could not confirm.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The only dated items were football-related (Libya's CAF complaint after a Tunisia qualifier walk-off threat).

11. Defence and security.

Tankers were hit near Hormuz on 19 November; Iranian state media said two struck mines, while a UK monitor said a projectile hit a tanker's engine room. The accounts differ.

Saudi forces reportedly intercepted five drones and a ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh in an earlier salvo, per AP as cited by CFR.

Iraq set up local headquarters to oversee Jurf al-Sakhar security operations.

Israeli troops now control about two-thirds of Gaza, up from over half at the ceasefire, per press reports. Hamas holds a coastal sliver.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent closed at about 92.56 dollarson 20 November, down roughly 21% from its October high. Prices rose on Houthi attacks on Riyadh and Hormuz tanker incidents.

Hormuz is effectively shut to commercial shipping, with Gulf oil flows estimated at about 48% of pre-war levels (preliminary tracker estimate).

Goldman expects a 2027 supply surplus, with Brent averaging about 88 dollarsand sliding toward 78 dollars.

Kurdistan Region oil export quotas remain disputed with Baghdad (see Iraq).

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump, Xi and Putin held their first trilateral summit in Shenzhen on 19 November and issued a short declaration on nuclear risk reduction. It set no targets and did not name Iran.

Analysts note the summit produced no concrete progress on Ukraine, Iran or Gaza.

The EU-UK summit on 20 November reaffirmed that Ukraine's peace path cannot be decided without Ukraine, after Russia called Europe in "humiliating decline".

The US Board of Peace and Washington are pressing Israel on Gaza while Witkoff and Kushner work the Qatar channel on Iran.
