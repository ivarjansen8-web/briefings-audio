Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

US extends Iran blockade to land routes as nuclear talks revive; Houthis strike Riyadh; Eisenkot tasked to form Israeli government.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The US has opened a land front in its blockade of Iran. CENTCOM announced "Task Force Iron Gate" to disrupt Iranian land and Caspian Sea supply routes, extending the naval blockade reinstated on 14 July; CENTCOM claims the blockade has already denied Iran over 25 dollarsbillion in crude exports. It signals Washington is tightening pressure even as nuclear talks revive.

Iran delivered a formal nuclear proposal to Washington via Qatar on 5 November. Bloomberg reports the sides are edging closer to a deal with Tehran willing to discuss nuclear issues it previously excluded, while Iran International reports a senior official saying Iran will not change its position on the nuclear file. The mixed signals show how contested the file is inside Tehran.

Houthi missiles are hitting Saudi Arabia. Two ballistic missiles were fired at Riyadh and debris hit Aramco offices, per Gulf News; Houthi-affiliated media claim 22 to 27 killed in Saudi strikes in Yemen (Houthi claims, unverified). The Saudi-Houthi front is the sharpest regional escalation this week.

Herzog has tasked Gadi Eisenkot with forming Israel's next government. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats against 48 for Eisenkot's bloc and 19 for Arab-led parties in the 27 October election, and Netanyahu claims the vote was "stolen". Eisenkot has 28 days and needs a majority his 67 recommendations do not by themselves deliver.

Egypt reopened its Tripoli embassy on 7 November. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty inaugurated the mission, closed since 2014 (outlets differ on the length of closure), and raised objections to the Turkey-Libya maritime deal. It marks a regional realignment on Libya.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Trump is reportedly pressing Netanyahu to advance the Gaza plan after the election. Times of Israel carried the report in its 8 November live blog; it is a single-source report (unconfirmed). It matters because the Board of Peace plan has stalled.

Flooding is hitting Gaza's displacement camps. Hamas-run Civil Defense says a sand embankment collapse at a Deir al-Balah camp killed five, including children, and rain flooded tens of thousands of tents. The humanitarian toll compounds the lack of reconstruction.

The IDF says it killed six Hamas operatives in recent Gaza strikes, while a strike on a car in Gaza City reportedly killed one and wounded five, with no IDF comment. The ceasefire is fraying at the edges.

The Board of Peace has not delivered on the ground. Bloomberg-sourced reporting says no stabilization force or Palestinian administration is inside Gaza, and Morocco has trained up to 500 personnel awaiting Israeli approvals. Delays rest partly on outstanding Israeli sign-offs.

Settlers beat three Palestinians near Salfit on 8 November, per WAFA, and attacked olive harvesters in Sinjil. OCHA counted 377 harvest-related settler attacks between 1 September and 2 November, per Middle East Eye.

Tel Aviv protesters rallied on 7 November against a Shin Bet election probe and called on a new government to fire Shin Bet chief Zini. It shows how the transition is politicising the security services.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel is reportedly asking Beirut, via US mediators, to set up roadblocks in Hezbollah areas so the group stays out if the war with Iran resumes, per a Kan report (unconfirmed). It shows Israel expects renewed fighting with Iran.

An Israeli drone strike killed a motorcyclist in Qabrikha on 4 November, per Lebanon's health ministry, the first such death since September; the IDF said it hit "two suspects". NBC counts over 4,300 killed in Lebanon since 2 March.

The US-backed framework ties IDF withdrawal to Hezbollah disarmament, and Hezbollah's refusal is the main obstacle. Reports say the Lebanese army prefers the IDF to stay for now.

Syrian forces entered the SDF-run Roj camp to move foreign women and children ahead of repatriation, as the SDF is dissolved. This shows Damascus asserting control in the northeast.

President al-Sharaa appointed three deputy foreign ministers on 7 November, per SANA via Enab Baladi, and a Syrian source denied sending troops to Yemen. Enab Baladi also reports protests over Israeli violations in southern Syria.

4. Iran.

The US struck Qeshm Island air defences on 6 November, which Trump called "minor strikes", and the IRGC claimed two more US drones, per the GlobalSecurity day-253 update. Strikes continue alongside talks.

Pezeshkian signalled nuclear flexibility in a Fox News interview and drew hardline pushback, per Iran International; IRGC-linked Tasnim set out a case for NPT withdrawal. The IRGC also said it could change its "nuclear doctrine" if ordered.

Tehran conditions talks on seven demands, per GlobalSecurity, and a US Embassy alert on 7 November told US citizens to leave Iran.

Trump says a nuclear deal could avert war and warned the US could destroy 38 bridges in an hour, per Fox News. The threat targets Iran's logistics.

Tehran Times (state-aligned) argues the US elections changed neither Iran's ability to restrict shipping nor its de-escalation conditions. Iran International reports hardline outlets debating a deal after Trump's midterm losses.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

PM Ali al-Zaidi set a 10 million bpd oil output target for 2030. The target is ambitious given a war-disrupted Gulf.

Talks on disarming armed factions are near deadlock, per a government source, and US chargé Steven Fagin warned of sanctions without progress (Shafaq, single-source). Washington is also pressing Baghdad to shut Iran border crossings, per Fox.

Al-Zaidi cancelled three Mosul airport leases tied to armed groups, per an informed source (unconfirmed).

Turkey pledged steps toward handing the Bashiqa camp to Iraq, and Bahrain announced its ambassador's return.

Flash floods killed two people in Erbil on 7 November, per governor Omed Khoshnaw, and Rudaw reports fresh Iranian drone attacks in the Kurdistan Region earlier in the week.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Saudi Arabia intercepted a cruise missile heading for Riyadh, per Gulf News, and Emirates and Air Arabia cancelled selected Saudi flights on 7 November.

Qatar condemned the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and says its US-Iran mediation continues.

Saudi FM Faisal bin Farhan phoned Qatari and Egyptian counterparts on 7 November on de-escalation.

Trump and Qatar's emir discussed mediation on 6 November, per Khaleej Times.

A UAE rescue of 22 crew in the Gulf of Oman accompanied the tightened blockade.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Houthis claim attacks on Aramco and Riyadh airport, and say Saudi strikes killed 27 in Sanaa province (Houthi claim, unverified).

Fox reports Houthis say Saudis killed dozens in air strikes; the US Embassy in Iran warned of higher Houthi attack risk.

The Houthis have seized swathes of the Red Sea coast, and per the WSJ (as relayed by Rio Times) police transit through an email safe-passage system; fighting continues in Taiz and Lahij.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

A stabbing in Amman killed Corporal Jawad al-Salayta of public security on 5 November; the attacker reportedly had prior terrorism charges, and Egypt condemned it.

Egypt and Jordan's information ministers met on 3 November on media cooperation and the Palestinian cause.

Jordan issued flash-flood warnings for Balqa, Madaba and the Dead Sea area.

Egypt reopened its Tripoli embassy, extending its role in Libya.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan told Trump on 7 November that Turkey will keep working for US-Iran diplomacy and help on Ukraine, per his office.

Erdogan is in Shusha with Aliyev for Azerbaijan's Victory Day.

Turkey is a possible host for Ukraine talks, not yet settled.

Ankara objects to Egypt-Cyprus criticism of its Libya maritime deal, a continuing friction.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Egypt reopened its Tripoli embassy with acting FM Taher al-Baour attending.

Dbeibah opened a Sahel-Saharan States meeting in Tripoli, per Libya Observer's homepage (date uncertain).

Morocco's prison population tops 102,000 at 158% capacity.

Tunisia and Algeria: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM disabled the M/V Huda in the Gulf of Oman on 7 November after the crew ignored warnings.

Task Force Iron Gate is a roughly 20-person team in Tampa working with federal agencies.

Israel's defence minister says Iran is trying to renew efforts to obtain nuclear weapons, and the US offers up to 20 dollarsmillion for information (CBS).

Iraq denied a nationwide airspace closure amid an air-defence exercise.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent was about 99.90 dollarson Friday 6 November, up two days after a 9.73% crash on Wednesday, with Hormuz tanker attacks continuing.

Iran's economy is under strain as Tehran says it welcomes US economic pain, per Iran International.

Iraq targets 10 million bpd by 2030.

US port-fee and affiliates-rule suspensions on Chinese-linked vessels expire 9 November, per a secondary weekly briefing (unconfirmed).

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Russia will reportedly supply Iran 90 missile launchers, per Al-Monitor-cited reports (unconfirmed).

Putin met Iran's SNSC secretary and pledged efforts to end the conflict.

Iranian officials met Putin and senior Chinese officials on 2 November, per Critical Threats.

EU: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.
