Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

US-Iran strikes hit Kuwait and Bahrain as Israel pushes into central Gaza and Houthis near Aden.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 19 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The US and Iran traded strikes across the Gulf on 17 to 18 November. US jets hit an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island, and Iran then fired drones at Ali Al-Salem air base in Kuwait and at Bahrain, the fourth reported attack on Bahrain in about six days. Qatar warned the escalation threatens its mediation.

Israel opened a rare ground operation in central Gaza, crossing the Yellow Line. Tanks advanced east of Nuseirat and dozens of displaced people fled; Israel says it targets Hamas infrastructure. It is the sharpest test of the October 2025 ceasefire so far.

The Board of Peace warned the Gaza ceasefire could collapse. Its report to the UN Security Council says both Israel and Hamas have missed commitments, stalling the stabilisation force and a new Palestinian administration.

Houthi forces are about 60 km from Aden. They seized the Kharaz base in Lahj and the Saudi-backed government pulled units back from the Red Sea coast, a major setback for Riyadh.

Netanyahu's bloc lost the Knesset speakership. A 66 to 52 vote after a six-hour filibuster installed Matan Kahana, signalling a shift of power after the 27 October election.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israeli forces launched one of their largest Gaza ground operations since the ceasefire. Gaza health authorities say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the truce began; Hamas called the raid a violation. Israel is said to control about two-thirds of the Strip.

The Board of Peace says the stabilisation force must deploy before Israeli withdrawal and Hamas disarmament can move. It reports 17 billion dollars in recovery pledges, of which 280 million dollars has been deployed, and says daily Israeli strikes delay testing Hamas's agreement to the roadmap.

Netanyahu is courting Naftali Bennett for a unity government. He met Bennett on 18 November, while Lapid and Bennett say they are working on an Eisenkot-led coalition after Netanyahu's bloc lost its majority.

A Palestinian Bedouin woman was shot dead, apparently by settlers, near Jericho on 17 November. The UK and Australia have sanctioned settlement-linked Israeli firms and individuals, and Foreign Minister Sa'ar vowed countermeasures.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel struck a Hezbollah-bound shipment from Syria in the Anti-Lebanon range on 16 to 17 November. The IDF says the strike hit combat equipment and that it dismantled a Hezbollah tunnel; Israeli troops still occupy parts of the south, where bulldozers are demolishing houses in Zawtar al-Sharqiya.

Lebanon will prosecute five suspected Hezbollah members arrested in the north. The charges cover unauthorised armed activity in Koura and Bsharri, and President Aoun again called on Hezbollah to hand over weapons.

A Hezbollah-nominated Lebanese minister has visited Damascus, the first since Assad's fall. The talks covered returns of displaced Syrians; Damascus says Hezbollah arms smuggling must stop (date of visit not confirmed).

President al-Sharaa named a new economy minister and an economic adviser on 18 November. The SDF has formally dissolved into the army after the January offensive that took about 80% of the northeast.

4. Iran.

Iran says it hit US drone and missile sites in Kuwait after the Qeshm strike. Its army claimed the attack on Ali Al-Salem, its first on Kuwait since September; Kuwait says it intercepted everything with no damage. Reports differ on whether the IRGC or the army claimed it.

Qatari and Iranian officials met on 17 to 18 November to narrow gaps between proposals. Washington wants nuclear talks from day one; Tehran wants them on day seven, after the US blockade eases and frozen funds are released.

CENTCOM says its blockade has stopped Iran loading any crude since late August. A third US carrier group has arrived in the region, per CBS reporting.

Iran says it has not suspended its NPT commitments, while hardliners push for a bomb doctrine. Tehran rejects exporting its highly enriched uranium but offers supervised downblending at home.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The Khor Mor gas field in Kurdistan was hit by a drone on 8 November and output halted. About 2.8 GW of regional power generation was lost; Kataib Hezbollah referenced the field the day before, but no group has claimed it (unconfirmed attribution).

Sirens sounded at Khor Mor on 17 November when an unidentified drone flew over. No new strike or casualties were reported.

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi is in a widening dispute with Iran-backed militias. His government sent a revised PMF law to parliament and set a 30 June 2027 deadline to disarm armed groups; Washington opposes the bill as entrenching Iranian influence.

Erbil-Baghdad oil and budget disputes are stalling the 2027 budget.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Bahrain and Kuwait intercepted Iranian attacks on 18 November. Saudi Arabia condemned the strikes and an attack on a UAE (ADNOC) tanker, and Bahrain asked the UN Security Council to act.

Twenty-four crew were evacuated from a burning tanker off Oman's Musandam, one missing. Three Indian sailors were separately reported safe after another tanker attack on 18 November.

Airlines are cutting Saudi routes. Etihad extended Riyadh and Dammam cancellations to 30 November and flydubai Riyadh and Abha to 27 November. The US reissued security warnings for Americans in Saudi Arabia and Bahrain.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Houthis reached within 60 km of Aden after taking Kharaz and the al-Maqatirah road to Taiz. Fighting has displaced over 340,000 people and UNHCR fears for 9,600 refugees at Kharaz camp.

Riyadh is preparing for a roughly six-month campaign against the Houthis (unconfirmed, citing two Western officials). The Saudi-led coalition also said it intercepted a Houthi cruise missile aimed at Riyadh.

The Houthis released 600 government prisoners via the ICRC. I found no confirmed new Red Sea ship attacks dated 18 November.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egypt is pressing for a temporary humanitarian truce in parts of Sudan. Its foreign minister held talks in Abu Dhabi, and Cairo is discussing reviving the Red Sea Forum with Riyadh.

A Jordanian court sentenced three men to death over the Karak attack (16 November listing). This follows a stabbing in Amman on about 5 November that killed a security officer.

Egypt's intelligence chief hosted Libyan talks with Saddam Haftar and Dbeibah (see section 10). Quiet otherwise on Gaza-border issues in what I found.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan said on 16 November that Turkey is entering a "very different period". He cited hosting COP31 and the July NATO summit.

The parliamentary commission to monitor PKK disarmament is still not confirmed as formed. The speaker says reintegration legislation waits on verified disarmament; the PKK says it has pulled its remaining fighters out of Turkey.

Turkey hosted Libya's Dbeibah and Khaled Haftar during COP31 week.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Washington is pushing a Libya unification deal. Under the reported US proposal Dbeibah stays prime minister and Saddam Haftar heads the Presidential Council; adviser Massad Boulos warned of sanctions on spoilers (AFP-reported, unconfirmed details).

UNSMIL's mandate was extended to October 2027, and the mission says sovereign institutions must operate freely. The eastern parliament named a new central bank governor, reviving rivalry with Tripoli.

Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco: quiet on politics. Libya beat Tunisia 2 to 0 in AFCON qualifying, putting Tunisia's streak of 17 straight appearances at risk.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM-linked reporting describes the Qeshm strike as "self-defense" with precision munitions. It followed an IRGC drone attack on a US radar at Sheikh Isa air base in Bahrain; no official CENTCOM release was found.

Analysts call the tit-for-tat a "status quo" that could slide into full war.

Israel says about 400 Hezbollah fighters have been killed in Lebanon since its ceasefire (Israeli claim).

12. Economy and energy.

Brent closed near 91.68 dollarson 18 November and traded near 92 dollarson 19 November. Tanker attacks keep Hormuz flows near half of pre-war levels, yet a supply surplus is capping prices.

The IEA sees a 1.7m b/d fourth-quarter surplus, and Goldman expects Brent to average 88 dollarsin 2027.

Gaza reconstruction pledges stand at 17 billion dollars, with a 1 billion dollars EU pledge.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump meets Putin one-on-one on Thursday on the sidelines of a Shenzhen summit with Xi. Trump said Iran will be on the agenda; European allies fear concessions to Moscow.

The EU is preparing new Iran sanctions over alleged cyberattacks on French supermarkets. France hopes for adoption on 14 December; Kallas also confirmed new sanctions on Hamas finance and readiness to engage Israel's next government.

Qatar and Pakistan remain the main Iran mediators.
