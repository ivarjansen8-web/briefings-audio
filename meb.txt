Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Friday, 27 November.

Iranian drones kill two Italians in Kuwait as Houthis hit Saudi refinery; Baghdad-Erbil standoff deepens.

Compiled from sources dated 22 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Iranian drones killed two Italian Rheinmetall technicians in Kuwait on 26 November, and Tehran denies responsibility. Kuwait says the men were helping intercept incoming drones; Foreign Minister Araghchi called the incident "suspicious" and "a precooked recipe for war", and it is the deadliest Iran-linked strike on a Gulf state in weeks.

Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated sharply: a projectile killed a Nepali worker at Aramco's Jazan refinery on 26 November. Saudi Arabia also reports ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh and strikes on Red Sea ports, which widens the Yemen war into direct attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure.

Baghdad and Erbil are in their sharpest standoff in years. Iraq closed two Kurdistan border crossings after a court ruling allowing federal forces on all borders, and thousands of Kurds protested; Baghdad denies sending troops north.

Brent settled at 96.57 dollarson 26 November, up about 2%, on Hormuz tanker attacks and the Jazan strike. Oil flows through the strait are reportedly about half of normal.

Israeli coalition talks continue a month after the 27 October election, with neither bloc at 61 seats. Eisenkot's Yashar signed a deal with the left-wing Democrats on 26 November.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israel killed Education Ministry official Khaled Abu Nada in central Gaza, the third senior Hamas figure killed since Tuesday. Medics say nine people died in overnight strikes, and the ceasefire framework is eroding.

Defence Minister Israel Katz threatened an operation displacing about one million Gaza City residents if Hamas does not disarm. Israeli troops reportedly control about two-thirds of Gaza, up from just over half at the ceasefire.

The Board of Peace says Hamas and other factions accepted a disarmament roadmap, but implementation is limited. It reportedly ties any Israeli withdrawal to full disarmament, and the stabilization force is stuck without a status-of-forces agreement (the Israeli rejection is reported by one source, unconfirmed).

More than 700 settlers were involved in violence in the West Bank, according to Israeli media figures cited by WAFA. The Palestinian news agency is a party to the conflict, so treat the framing with care.

Eisenkot's Yashar signed a coalition agreement with the Democrats, while Likud filed a formal appeal over the election results. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats and the opposition bloc 48, with Arab-led parties on 19, so UTJ's refusal to back Netanyahu is pivotal.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

The IDF says it struck dozens of Hezbollah rockets and launchers at a Radwan Force camp on 26 November, with no injuries reported. Israel keeps a self-declared security zone about 10 km deep in south Lebanon, and Hezbollah rejects the arrangement.

Syria announced two seizures of Iranian IRGC weapons bound for Lebanon (20 and 22 November), and CENTCOM praised Damascus. This shows the new Syrian government working against Hezbollah's supply line.

The Lebanese army seized 264 kg of explosives, including about 203 kg of C4, smuggled from Syria on 22 November, and arrested three people. JNS, November (unconfirmed, single source)

President al-Sharaa issued a law lifting the bar on court challenges to government decisions on 25 November. It is a modest rule-of-law step by the transitional authorities.

4. Iran.

US-Iran nuclear talks have stalled over Iran's 60%-enriched uranium stockpile, according to a 21 November report. Washington reportedly wants written guarantees, while Iran rules out shipping the material abroad. Republic World, 21 November (unconfirmed)

Trump said the Iran war ends "whenever I want it", while a US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues. On 12 November he had told Vance that no deal was needed.

Iran says three tankers hit mines in Hormuz despite US escort, which CENTCOM disputes. The claim is Iranian and uncorroborated, and the Treasury widened sanctions the same day.

Araghchi denies Iran carried out the Kuwait strike and offers to cooperate in an investigation.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Iraqi border forces closed the al-Waleed (Syria) and Bashta (Iran) crossings in the Kurdistan Region. This follows a Federal Supreme Court ruling allowing federal forces on all international borders.

Thousands protested in the Kurdistan Region against Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's push to take over Kurdish-held oil fields. It tests the post-2003 federal settlement.

Baghdad denied reports of army movements toward the Kurdish region, and the KRG rejected any return to a "centralised system".

A national dialogue is scheduled for 1 December. News Minimalist, November (unconfirmed)

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Kuwait was hit by Iranian drones that killed two Italians, and the EU's von der Leyen urged more pressure on Tehran. Italy's Tajani said Rome would lodge a complaint with the Iranian embassy.

Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar condemned Iran's attack on Bahrain (22 November) and the strike on Kuwait. Bahrain and Kuwait have taken the brunt of Iranian strikes, which had lulled from April under ceasefire talks.

A Houthi attack on a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District injured 11 people, according to Saudi Civil Defence.

Turkey completed ratification of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan.

Israel's Mossad reportedly has not approved resuming direct Israel-Dubai flights, even though FlyDubai is selling tickets. AP via Yankton, 26 November (unconfirmed)

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Houthis hit Saudi Red Sea ports at Jazan and Jeddah on 25 November, which they described as retaliation for Saudi strikes on Hodeidah. A projectile landed in Jazan port with minor damage, per coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki, and a drone was intercepted near Jeddah.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree threatened Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports and told shipping firms to withdraw vessels.

Saudi Arabia intercepted two ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh on 26 November. The kingdom has faced near-daily Houthi attacks since early last month, and fighting reignited in July after a four-year lull.

The Houthis claim 105 coalition air strikes on Sanaa and 10 governorates on 25 November, and 19 dead in later raids. Both figures are Houthi claims and unverified.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egyptian and Jordanian foreign ministers met in Cairo on 24 November. They urged stability in Gaza and unhindered aid, and condemned attacks on Jordan and Saudi Arabia.

A fuel-tanker fire on Giza's Ring Road killed 31 people and injured 21, per Jordan News (unconfirmed, single source). Jordan sent condolences on 26 November.

Egypt and Jordan initialled an MoU to expand Cairo-Amman flight capacity.

Egypt is also courting both Libyan camps: it reopened its Tripoli embassy on 7 November, and Sisi hosted Dbeibah.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan hosted Libya's Khalifa Haftar in Ankara on 25 November, his first official visit to Turkey, and reportedly agreed a visa waiver. Ankara is hedging between rival Libyan camps ahead of an expected Erdogan trip to Libya in December, his first in 15 years.

Erdogan signed off the Makkah defence pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, completing Turkish ratification. It anchors Turkey in a new Gulf-linked security grouping.

Erdogan phoned Besiktas's president after crowd incidents at the Europa League match in Batumi, Georgia.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Haftar's Ankara visit and Egyptian outreach to Tripoli mark a shift toward Turkish and Egyptian competition for influence in Libya. Turkey hosted talks in October between the two administrations.

A Libyan Army statement says two naval personnel were killed in a Ukrainian strike in Benghazi on 25 November, and Tripoli has asked Ukraine for clarification. France 24 / Horn Review, 25 to 26 November (unconfirmed, single source)

Libya beat Tunisia 2 to 0 in an AFCON qualifier, and the Libyan army chief met a Moroccan counterpart in Rabat.

Algeria plans to expand from 58 to 69 wilayas, and a Maghreb forum is urging Libya, Tunisia and Mauritania to mediate between Morocco and Algeria.

11. Defence and security.

A third US carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, joined the fleet enforcing the blockade of Iranian ports. CENTCOM says all main shipping lanes are clear of mines, which Iran disputes.

Israel's defence minister said Israel could strike Iran independently of the US, and claimed Iran is developing chemical weapons (unconfirmed, single source).

Saudi forces destroyed a Houthi missile launcher in Hodeidah, per the coalition, after the Jazan refinery attack.

Israel continues to strike Hezbollah arms in the Beqaa and south Lebanon.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent settled at 96.57 dollarson 26 November (+2.07%) and traded near 96.19 dollarsearly on 27 November.

Ship traffic in Hormuz is reportedly about 9% of pre-war levels and oil flows about half of normal as of 24 November. This comes from one analysis, so treat it as an estimate.

ANZ says Brent can still push above 100 dollars; OPEC+ is expected to hold output targets through March 2027.

The Jazan refinery halted some operations, and Houthis also reportedly targeted Ras Tanura on 25 November.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump says the US does not "need" an Iran deal, while Vance says there is "no daylight" between them. Diplomacy is mostly frozen apart from back channels.

The EU's von der Leyen called for more pressure on Iran after the Kuwait deaths, but no sanctions decision has been reported.

Russia's Lavrov spoke with Araghchi on 16 November and voiced concern over Lebanon on 23 November.

China's Wang Yi urged dialogue on 2 November and planned a special envoy to Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia that month.

Australia sanctioned two individuals over IRGC-linked antisemitic attacks.
