Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Monday, 16 November.

Iran strikes Bahrain as Trump eyes pause; Houthis hit Riyadh and take Bab el-Mandeb.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 16 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) said it struck a US-linked base in Bahrain on 15 November, and Bahrain says it intercepted the attacks. It was Bahrain's first nationwide alert in months, and Washington denies that any damage was done.

Trump says the US no longer needs a deal with Iran and may hold off new strikes if there are no fresh provocations from Tehran or the Houthis. He says Gulf allies asked him not to escalate, which leaves a stalemate rather than a settlement.

The Saudi-led coalition says it shot down two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and three drones over the Eastern Province on 15 November. Saudi authorities put deaths from Houthi attacks since 23 September at 26, so the Yemen war is now a direct Saudi-Houthi confrontation.

Israel killed Hamas Rafah Brigade commander Samir Mahmoud Abu Younis in southern Gaza overnight on 14 November, according to both Hamas and the IDF. It is the second senior commander killed in about two months of a stagnating ceasefire.

Yemeni government forces withdrew from positions near the Bab el-Mandeb strait, leaving the Houthis in control, according to US News (Reuters-sourced). This apparently ends a six-week counteroffensive and tightens Houthi leverage over a key shipping chokepoint.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said the offensive will continue until Gaza is demilitarized and there is no Hamas. That contradicts the idea of a functioning ceasefire that took effect over a year ago.

Israel struck a southern Gaza hospital complex on 12 November; Hamas authorities say 6 people were killed. The IDF said the target was a Khan Younis Brigade commander, and the claims have not been independently verified.

Israel says more than 91,000 tents have entered Gaza ahead of winter but it refuses heavy rubble-clearing equipment. COGAT says reconstruction can begin only after Hamas disarms, which keeps the humanitarian situation tied to the disarmament dispute.

Israeli authorities advanced 11 West Bank settlement plans covering 895 housing units, including 288 at Kedumim on Kafr Qaddum land, per the Palestinian Wall and Settlement Resistance Commission. The figure comes from a Palestinian body, but it fits a pattern of rapid expansion.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians in a village near Ramallah on 12 November, and further olive-harvest attacks followed on 14 November. OCHA recorded 457 attacks causing casualties or damage in October, the most in any month since 2006.

Coalition talks continue after the 27 October election, in which Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats, short of a majority. Haredi parties are being courted by several sides, so the next government is still undecided.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

The IDF said on 15 November it destroyed a 35-metre Hezbollah tunnel at Majdal Zoun, and jets also hit Al-Qantara and Kfar Tebnit, per Lebanon's National News Agency. Strikes continue despite a June ceasefire.

UNIFIL said on 11 November it is still logging daily Israeli violations in the south. Israel still occupies parts of southern Lebanon, according to background reporting on the 2026 war.

Hezbollah chief Naim Qassem reportedly told Lebanese critics to let Israel attack, while Israel says strikes continue until Hezbollah is disarmed. The disarmament standoff keeps the ceasefire fragile.

An Israeli patrol advanced to the entrance of Jubata al-Khashab in Quneitra on 10 November, and relatives of Syrians held in Israel protested on 13 November. Israeli presence in the south undermines de-escalation, as Syria's UN envoy Ibrahim Olabi told the General Assembly.

SDF commander Mazloum Abdi said integration of SDF formations into Syrian state institutions will be completed by end-2026. Analysts see it as a new test of Damascus-SDF implementation.

Syria and Jordan signed agreements at their Higher Coordination Council in Damascus on 9 November, and the border reopened after a probe into deadly air strikes. The strikes followed the Karak church attack.

4. Iran.

Foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said on 15 November that improved cooperation with the IAEA is unlikely under current circumstances. Iran says inspections cannot resume while it faces US military threats.

A confidential IAEA report dated 13 November, obtained by AP, says the agency cannot rule out diversion of declared nuclear material. The agency has had no access since the June 2025 attacks, which is a verification gap rather than evidence of diversion.

A reported Qatari proposal would cap Iranian enrichment at 3.67 percent, dilute highly enriched uranium and tie IAEA access to reopening Hormuz (unconfirmed). Parliament speaker and lead negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Washington has not formally answered Iran's seven conditions.

Iran's IRGC claimed on 15 November that tankers hit naval mines in Hormuz and that it shot down another US drone, which the US military disputes (unconfirmed). Ghalibaf said Hormuz will never return to its pre-war state.

Al Jazeera reports Iran formally backs the nuclear treaty even as hardliners raise the prospect of a bomb. The split matters for any deal.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Iraqi forces sent reinforcements to Jurf al-Sakhr in Babil on the evening of 15 November amid tension with Kataib Hezbollah. Sources say Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi set a 20 November deadline for the group to hand over security of the area (unconfirmed, Shafaq sourcing).

Al-Zaidi met Kurdistan PM Masrour Barzani in Baghdad on 9 November on oil management, salaries and the KRG's share of the 2027 budget. The cabinet was due to vote on the draft budget, but I could not confirm the outcome.

Iraq's public debt is projected at 174 dollarsbillion in 2026, 71.1 percent of GDP, based on IMF figures. Debt pressure complicates the budget.

A senior PUK official says political ties with the KDP have effectively collapsed, and Talabani dismissed a dialogue offer from the Kurdistan Region President on 12 November. The impasse over forming the next KRG cabinet continues.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Bahrain's air defences intercepted Iranian attacks on 15 November after its first nationwide alert in months. Bahrain hosts the US Fifth Fleet, so it is the Gulf state most directly exposed right now.

Gulf states have demanded full compensation from Iran for thousands of missile and drone attacks since February, and Qatar's mediation is stalled.

The coalition said Houthi missiles and drones targeted Riyadh and the Eastern Province; the Houthis claim hits on King Khalid airport (unconfirmed). Damage is verified only through coalition statements and Houthi claims.

A Houthi strike last week damaged an administrative building belonging to Saudi Aramco in Riyadh's financial district.

UAE, Kuwait and Oman: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Government forces withdrew from the Bab el-Mandeb area toward Aden, and the Houthis took control.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the group downed a Saudi aircraft, its fifth since the escalation (unconfirmed, Houthi claim).

Houthi attacks have grounded flights at Saudi airports and hit energy facilities, and the group has targeted Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea. The UN Secretary-General reported three attacks on commercial vessels between 9 August and 9 September.

Trump has tied any pause on Iran strikes to the Houthis staying quiet. That links the Yemen front directly to US-Iran escalation.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

A court ordered the closure of independent outlet Mada Masr and fined editor Lina Attalah EGP 3 million, with an appeal set for 25 November. The Supreme Judicial Council rejected claims of trial irregularities, which is a notable press-freedom setback.

Egypt's intelligence chief met Libya's Saddam Haftar in Cairo on the political process. Cairo is positioning itself in the US-backed Libya track.

Jordan's State Security Court sentenced three men to death over the 23 October attack on St. George's Church in Karak. The verdict is subject to appeal.

Jordan's navy arrested an Israeli national who tried to swim into Jordanian waters on 14 November.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan met Iranian President Pezeshkian at the COP31 summit in Antalya on 12 November to discuss ending the US-Iran war. Ankara is trying to position itself as a mediator.

Erdogan said Turkey "will never abandon" the Cyprus cause on the 43rd anniversary of the Turkish Cypriot declaration of independence on 15 November.

Erdogan told the TRT World Forum on 6 November that the global system is "bankrupt" over Gaza.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

US adviser Massad Boulos has urged immediate implementation of a Libya unification deal and warned of sanctions on spoilers, according to a Libya Observer headline of uncertain date (unconfirmed). The plan keeps Dbeibah as prime minister and gives Saddam Haftar a reformed Presidential Council.

AFRICOM's commander held talks with Dbeibah in Tripoli on military cooperation (undated, unconfirmed).

Spain backed a EUR 26 million increase in EU aid to Morocco, bringing the Southern Neighbourhood budget to about EUR 1.81 billion, amid a Ceuta security dispute.

Tunisia and Algeria: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM said it shot down two Iranian one-way attack drones aimed at Bahrain on 13 November and denies Iran's claim of hitting a radar site.

CENTCOM says it is still strictly enforcing the naval blockade on Iranian ports.

UKMTO raised the regional maritime threat level to severe, citing Iranian activity near Hormuz (reported via live blogs citing Reuters).

Hamas's armed wing said a senior commander was killed in an Israeli strike, and the IDF says it hit Hezbollah infrastructure in southern Lebanon.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent crude traded near 95 dollars-96 per barrel around 14 to 16 November, after a roughly 16 percent weekly drop, its steepest since the start of the pandemic. Traders cited an Iranian interim proposal to reopen Hormuz.

The IEA sees its first supply surplus since the Iran war began, and OPEC+ produced 41.23 million barrels a day in October, 97 percent of pre-war February levels.

Middle East oil flows through Hormuz have almost recovered to pre-war levels, though mine claims keep risk elevated.

Iraq's budget and debt (see section 5) and Egypt's plan to invest state surpluses in government securities are the other regional fiscal items.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

A US-China-Russia leaders' meeting on nuclear arms control, Iran and trade is reported for 19 November in Shenzhen, excluding Ukraine (unconfirmed, aggregator-sourced). Trump says he expects to meet Xi and Putin at APEC in China.

Putin met Iranian security council chief Mohsen Rezaei in Moscow on 5 November, and China's Middle East envoy Zhai June met Iranian officials in Tehran. Beijing's mediation role is being discussed.

Trump said on 11 November the US has already won the Iran war and wants "total victory", while later signalling no deal is needed. The mixed messaging adds uncertainty.

EU: Quiet: no significant EU action on the region surfaced in the last 72 hours.
