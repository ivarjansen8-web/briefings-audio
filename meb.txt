Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Tuesday, 24 November.

Iran vows to break US Hormuz blockade as Houthi-Saudi fighting escalates and Gaza ceasefire frays.

Compiled from sources dated 21 to 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Iran said on 23 November it will use "every means" to counter the US naval blockade of its ports, a day after Trump threatened to sink Iranian tankers that try to break it. The Foreign Ministry called the blockade military aggression and invoked UN Charter Article 51, and talk of a tanker convoy is so far unconfirmed by any Iranian official.

US-Iran nuclear talks have stalled over Iran's 60% enriched uranium stockpile, and Trump has reportedly threatened renewed strikes after the new year if talks fail. Araghchi calls keeping the material in Iran a red line, which leaves no obvious compromise.

An Israeli strike killed UNRWA worker Adel al-Helo at a water well in Jabalia on 23 November, in a day when strikes killed at least three people in Gaza. Israel had not responded at the time of reporting; the killing adds to evidence that the October 2025 ceasefire is hollow.

Houthi-Saudi fighting is escalating, with Saudi forces intercepting a drone and ballistic missile and Houthi media reporting a strike on Sanaa. Riyadh says Houthi attacks have killed 26 people in the kingdom since September.

A Russian ship was hit by a suspected drone at Benghazi's naval base on 22 November, and Ukraine's SBU claimed it sank the corvette Merkury (unconfirmed). Libya's eastern authorities reported injuries and no deaths; the gap between the accounts is large, and it draws the Libya conflict into the Russia-Ukraine war.

2. Israel and Palestine.

An Israeli airstrike killed UNRWA employee Adel al-Helo in Jabalia on 23 November, and at least three people died in Gaza strikes that day. UNRWA says its staff were doing routine work on the well; the Israeli military said it was reviewing a request for comment.

Israeli forces continue demolishing buildings beyond the "Yellow Line" in Gaza. Arab News says Israeli troops now hold around two-thirds of the territory, up from just over half at the ceasefire, which shows how far reality has drifted from the October 2025 deal.

Hamas accepted a phased disarmament roadmap in July, but Netanyahu rejected it and demands full disarmament before any withdrawal. The sequencing dispute keeps the Board of Peace plan frozen; Kushner warned Israel faces a choice between security and "global pariah status".

Settlers burned 12 vehicles in Ramallah and al-Bireh on 22 November, per WAFA (Palestinian Authority agency) as cited by Al Jazeera. A 17 November settler attack killed a 72-year-old woman and wounded 10, and ACLED says 2026 is on course to be the worst year for settler violence it has recorded.

Israeli authorities advanced 11 settlement plans with 895 housing units, WAFA reported. This is a Palestinian-side count and adds to de facto annexation pressure.

Post-election coalition talks remain unresolved after the 27 October vote gave neither bloc 61 seats, and Eisenkot and Bennett reportedly split over giving Likud a deadline in unity talks (unconfirmed). Results and coalition details were hard to verify in today's search.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israeli strikes and artillery hit southern Lebanon on 22 to 23 November as Lebanon marked a muted Independence Day. President Aoun urged both sides to take steps towards peace; few expect a quick end to the conflict.

Israel said on 19 November it struck a Hezbollah missile-production site at Janta in the Bekaa, with no casualties reported. This is an Israeli military claim, unverified, and shows strikes continuing despite the 20 June ceasefire.

The Lebanese army seized 264 kg of explosives smuggled from Syria near Qaa on 22 November. Syrian forces separately seized weapons in Damascus countryside destined for Lebanon, a sign of rare border cooperation.

Syria's Sharaa spoke with UAE President Mohamed bin Zayed on 23 November about economic cooperation. Gulf economic backing is central to Damascus's reconstruction plans, while SDF integration, Druze south and ISIS cells remain open questions.

4. Iran.

Iran's Foreign Ministry vowed "every means" against the US blockade, and said Hormuz could reopen within hours or two days if Washington lifts the blockade and sanctions. Sources differ on the timeframe, but Tehran ties reopening to US commitments under the Islamabad memorandum.

Iran announced a court judgment ordering the US to pay 290 dollarsbillion in damages, per AFP via Dawn. It is a symbolic legal step with no enforcement route.

The IRGC claimed supertankers struck mines in Hormuz, and Iran's navy said three tankers hit mines (Iranian claims, unconfirmed). Trump accused Iran of inventing ship attacks to lift oil prices.

Retirees protested in Tehran, Isfahan and Shush on 22 November and truckers struck for a second day on 23 November, per NCRI, an opposition group (treat framing with caution). State-affiliated Jahan-e Sanat acknowledged that a 22.6% freight-rate rise falls short of costs.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

KDP and PUK held talks in Erbil on 21 November and voiced cautious optimism, yet remain split over the legitimacy of the Kurdistan Parliament and calls to separate Sulaymaniyah administratively. The deadlock has left the KDP-led caretaker cabinet in place.

The PUK withdrew from the Kurdistan Regional Government on 5 November, and Bafel Talabani threatened a parallel administration. The Federal Supreme Court is weighing the caretaker government's legal status.

Egypt's Sisi held talks with Iraq's prime minister, and the two countries signed tax, health and cooperation agreements. It deepens Cairo-Baghdad ties as Gulf security worsens.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Etihad cancelled all flights between Abu Dhabi and Riyadh and Dammam through 30 November. The airline cited continuing regional disruption from Houthi attacks.

The Houthis claimed a strike on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery; Riyadh has not confirmed it (unconfirmed). Hitting Red Sea energy sites would raise the stakes for Saudi export routes.

Two ballistic missiles targeted Riyadh on 15 November, and the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain and Syria condemned the attacks. Gulf solidarity is visible, but Qatar and the UAE were not targeted in the period.

Qatar says its US-Iran mediation continues. Doha remains a key channel while talks stall.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Houthi media said a Saudi attack on Hodeidah killed 17, and a strike on a Sanaa fuel station wounded at least six. These are Houthi claims; the coalition claims hundreds of strikes and 122 Houthi fighters neutralised.

The Houthis warned shipping firms to evacuate Saudi Red Sea ports and airlines to avoid Saudi airspace. This follows their maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia declared on 20 July.

One tracker's latest logged ship attack dates from 4 October 2026. Few confirmed commercial attacks in recent weeks, though sources conflict.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Sisi and Iraq's prime minister discussed bilateral ties and the Gaza ceasefire on 23 November. Cairo keeps pressing for ceasefire implementation.

Libya's eastern command denied reports that Egyptian military experts were withdrawn from Benghazi. The denial matters given the drone incident in the port.

Jordan's parliament was due to hear the 2027 budget address on 23 November, and Amman condemned drone attacks on Khartoum. Routine, but shows fiscal pressure from the regional war.

Israeli President Herzog said King Abdullah's concerns deserve Israel's attention. A conciliatory signal towards Amman.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan urged a swift end to the investment-fund probe after the 23 November cabinet meeting. Ankara is working to liquidate affected funds and repay investors, a domestic economic risk.

Erdogan will visit Libya for a Turkiye-Africa summit, announced by Fidan. The trip, with meetings in Tripoli, underlines Turkey's stake in Libya's unification.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Libya demanded clarification from Zelenskyy over remarks on the Benghazi strike, and the GNU warned against turning Libya into a foreign battleground. Both rival governments are reacting to the incident.

The UN condemned attacks on vessels at Benghazi and called for an independent investigation, as Libya loses about 365,000 barrels a day to a Sharara pipeline valve closure. An outlaw group closed the valve, per the National Oil Corporation.

An armed group obstructed a planned House of Representatives session in Zintan (unconfirmed, newsletter summary). Institutional rivalry continues.

Tunisia, Algeria and Morocco: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours. Stimson's 18 November outlook noted Algerian criticism over the Western Sahara UN resolution fight.

11. Defence and security.

Suspected drones hit a Russian vessel at Benghazi, and Ukraine's SBU claimed seven naval drones sank the Merkury (unconfirmed). Libya's eastern government reported injuries only.

Syria announced on 8 November that it would deploy 600 special forces soldiers to Saudi Arabia, and a car bomb killed two in a Damascus suburb on 15 November. Damascus is aligning with Riyadh as threats persist.

Washington is reportedly considering training Syrian soldiers in demining. An early sign of deeper US-Syria military engagement.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent closed at about 89.37 dollarson 23 November, its lowest since August, as Libya's output problems eased and markets shrugged off Hormuz risk. Sources vary between 88.97 dollarsand 90.63 dollars.

The EIA forecasts Brent near 100 dollarsin November-December, well above the market. Ship-to-ship transfers off Oman keep some flows moving.

Saudi Arabia reportedly needs 94 dollars-96 oil for its wartime budget, and OPEC+ meets on 29 November. The gap with spot prices adds pressure.

The Iranian rial has collapsed to about 2.2 million per dollar, per Iran-focused reporting (figure unverified). Strikes and inflation feed the unrest.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump has called the Islamabad memorandum void and the talks "nonsense", per Euronews, while New Arab reports US officials prefer prolonged economic pressure to renewed war. The signals are mixed, so policy is hard to read.

Xi, Putin and Trump issued a declaration on strategic stability in Shenzhen on 19 November, omitting Iran and Hormuz. It signals great-power coordination that has not reached the Gulf crisis.

G7 foreign ministers meeting in Paris on 9 to 10 November called on Iran to stop attacks on civilian vessels in Hormuz and backed regional mediators. It is the Western reference position on the crisis.

Iran's foreign minister said Russia and China oppose any change in Iran's nuclear doctrine (Iranian claim, 22 November). Tehran is signalling backing from both.

By 30 November: Saudi-Houthi escalation. Etihad's Riyadh and Dammam cancellations run to 30 November; watch for confirmation of the Rabigh claim.

29 November: OPEC+ meeting. Brent is below Saudi budget needs.

Coming days: Erdogan's Libya visit and Benghazi fallout. Watch Russian and Ukrainian statements and the UN investigation call.

Coming days: Israeli coalition talks. Eisenkot, Bennett and Likud unity talks, and any Gaza ground escalation.

Coming weeks: US-Iran talks. Trump's reported post-New-Year strike threat sets the outer deadline.
