Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Monday, 9 November.

Khor Mor strike blacks out Kurdistan, Houthis hit Riyadh, Eisenkot gets Israel mandate.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 9 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

A drone strike on Iraq's Khor Mor gas field on the night of 8 November halted production and cut about 2.8 GW of power capacity in the Kurdistan Region. Al Jazeera says more than 70 percent of the region lost power, and Reuters-based reports call it a suspected Iranian drone strike (unconfirmed; no claim of responsibility), which would widen the Iran war's spillover into Iraq.

Houthi missiles damaged a Saudi Aramco building in Riyadh on 8 November, according to Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry. The Houthis claimed strikes on Aramco storage tanks and King Khalid airport, so the Yemen war is now a direct Houthi-Saudi exchange.

President Herzog gave opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot the first mandate to form Israel's next government, a setback for Netanyahu. Reported seat counts are 53 for Netanyahu's bloc and 48 for Eisenkot's, with Arab parties holding 19, so neither side has a majority.

CENTCOM launched "Task Force Iron Gate" on 7 November to choke Iran's land and Caspian supply routes, extending the Hormuz blockade. The IRGC dismissed it as "merely wordplay", but Trump says it is already in effect.

Trump meets Qatar's prime minister on 9 November on a ceasefire and the restart of nuclear talks with Iran. Qatar says the talks are alive, and Iran reportedly passed a nuclear proposal via Doha on Thursday.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Herzog tasked Eisenkot (Yashar) with forming a government after the late-October election. Likud is the largest party with 27 seats against Yashar's 21, and Netanyahu is calling for a unity government while ruling out Arab-list partners, which makes the 19 Arab-party seats decisive.

Trump is reportedly pressing Netanyahu to advance the Gaza plan whoever leads the government. The report cites unnamed officials, so a transition may not slow Washington's Gaza push (unconfirmed).

Floods killed five people, four of them children, in Gaza and left tens of thousands without shelter. Gaza's government media office put damage at 3 million dollars; the UK is also rallying about 50 states to urge Israel to ease winter aid restrictions, per Israel's UN mission.

Settlers beat three Palestinians with clubs near Salfit, according to WAFA via Middle East Eye. OCHA counts 377 settler attacks tied to the olive harvest between 1 September and 2 November, more than in any previous season.

Eleven people were arrested in a mass brawl in Lod after a far-right "sovereignty march" in an Arab neighbourhood. It shows communal tension surfacing in the post-election period.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel is reportedly preparing for a new confrontation with Hezbollah, and Defence Minister Katz threatened to hit Beirut's southern suburbs if Hezbollah and Iran attack. Prime Minister Salam warned Hezbollah against "another reckless adventure".

The Israeli army detonated a large explosion in Qantara on 7 November amid shelling and search operations in the south. It said it found more than 340 Hezbollah weapons and items near Beaufort and the Litani.

Syrian Internal Security Forces entered the Roj camp on 7 November, previously run by the now-dissolved SDF, ahead of foreign repatriations. Somali and Sudanese women and children were moved out first, a sign of Damascus taking over the northeast's detention sites.

President al-Sharaa met Mohammed bin Salman in Riyadh on 4 November and signed agreements including a 90 million dollars loan for the Aleppo power plant. It deepens Gulf reconstruction backing for Damascus.

4. Iran.

The IRGC rejected the new US pressure plan as "wordplay", while Trump says fresh attacks are "highly unlikely" if Tehran strikes a nuclear deal and reopens Hormuz. The mixed signals leave the blockade standoff unresolved.

A senior Iranian official called Qatar's draft plan unacceptable, yet Iran reportedly sent a nuclear proposal through Qatar on Thursday. The proposal is reportedly conditional on sanctions relief (unconfirmed).

Debate over a nuclear bomb is spreading on official Iranian platforms, though the Supreme National Security Council secretary says no doctrine change has been decided. Hardline MPs are chanting "nuclear warhead is our indisputable right".

Washington is offering up to 20 million dollars for information on Iran's nuclear programme, while Israel's Katz says the mission is "not complete". Both signal that renewed strikes remain on the table.

Iran reported two tankers hitting mines and catching fire in and near Hormuz. This is Iran's own account and is unverified.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The Khor Mor attack cut gas to power plants serving Erbil, Sulaimaniyah, Duhok, Bazian and Chamchamal. Preliminary reports show no casualties, and the region is using Dokan and Darbandikhan hydro stations for hospitals and water.

Prime Minister Ali Falih al-Zaidi ordered a joint investigation with Kurdish authorities. Kurdish officials have long blamed Iran-backed armed groups for strikes on this field.

The PUK has reportedly quit the Kurdistan Regional Government over a parliament dispute with the KDP. Shafaq reports it as a scoop, and it threatens the region's governing arrangement (unconfirmed).

A US State Department spokesperson told Shafaq that Washington backs "a strong and resilient" Kurdistan Region. It urged quick strengthening of institutions.

Syria and Iraq agreed on water management measures. The detail is thin, but it matters given Tigris-Euphrates scarcity.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Saudi Arabia says its Aramco building in Riyadh sustained minor damage in an "attempted" attack. Saudi forces also intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Najran on 6 November.

Qatar condemned the Houthi attacks and said Saudi security is "an integral part of Qatar's security". Doha is at once a Saudi ally and the main US-Iran mediator.

Arab states have warned Iran of individual and collective retaliation over attacks, per WSJ via Iran International. The report is single-source (unconfirmed), and Gulf patience appears to be thinning.

UKMTO is investigating a bulk carrier incident in the Gulf of Oman. CENTCOM separately said it disabled a cargo vessel there.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Houthi-run Health Ministry says 33 people, including 13 children, died in coalition strikes on al-Shariyah near Sanaa. This is a Houthi figure and not independently verified.

Houthi officials say a coalition strike on a Taiz market killed at least 20 people. This is also unverified, and the war is escalating on both sides.

The Houthis announced a blockade of Saudi-linked shipping on 20 July and have since claimed hits on two Saudi tankers. A US MARAD advisory runs to 22 March 2027 and flags higher risk for ships tied to Israel, the US, UK or Saudi Arabia.

One outlet reports a deadly Houthi attack on Yemen's presidential palace. I could not confirm the details (unconfirmed).

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egypt reopened its embassy in Tripoli on 7 November for the first time since 2014, and Foreign Minister Abdelatty discussed energy with Libya's oil minister. It signals Cairo's growing role in the Libyan file.

A court ordered Mada Masr closed and fined editor Lina Attalah EGP 3m; a retrial hearing is reported for 11 November. One source gives 25 November, so the date is uncertain.

Egypt has also detained the whole staff of the fact-checking platform Matsadaash. Together with Mada Masr, this marks a tightening on independent media.

Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and the Arab League condemned an armed attack in southern Egypt, and Egypt condemned an attack that killed a Jordanian security officer. Both headlines are undated, so details are unclear (unconfirmed).

9. Turkey.

Erdogan told the TRT World Forum on 6 November that the international system is "bankrupt", citing Gaza and West Bank settler violence. It fits Ankara's pitch as a leading critic of Israel.

Erdogan spoke to Trump by phone on 7 November about easing US-Iran tensions. Turkey positions itself as a mediator alongside Qatar.

Erdogan said he hopes the US midterms, in which Democrats took both chambers, will help regional peace. The shift may change congressional pressure on Gulf and Israel policy.

Turkey's intelligence chief hosted a Libyan delegation as US-led power-sharing efforts gather pace. Ankara remains a key Libya player.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Fawzi Abu Khazam took over as Libya's central bank governor from Naji Issa on 8 November. The handover ends a leadership dispute at a key institution.

US-led efforts to broker power-sharing between Libya's rival camps are gathering pace. Egypt's embassy reopening and the Turkish meeting both fit this track.

The WHO identified 111 malnutrition cases among 941 children screened in Qatrun, southern Libya. It points to humanitarian gaps in the south.

Algeria attributed a 205 kg cannabis seizure to Morocco on 8 November, and the dirham fell 3.7% against the dollar between September and October. Both Algerian claims and the currency figures come from single-outlet listings; Algiers-Rabat ties remain frozen.

Tunisia: Quiet, nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM says a US aircraft disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman that repeatedly tried to breach the blockade. It shows the blockade is enforced kinetically.

Task Force Iron Gate is a reported 20-person interagency team based in Tampa; CENTCOM has not said how it will enforce restrictions. The land-route mission is vague (unconfirmed detail).

The US military is reportedly ready to act at short notice if Trump orders renewed strikes, though he told 60 Minutes he is not looking to escalate.

No International Stabilization Force has deployed in Gaza a year into the ceasefire; six states have pledged troops under CENTCOM planning. Israeli strikes continue almost daily, per critics.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded near 97.67 dollarson 9 November, down about 64 cents, per Reuters. It slipped after Trump called fresh attacks on Iran "highly unlikely" while talks progress.

Brent closed at about 98.32 dollarson 6 November after a weekly fall of more than 16 percent, per secondary reports. Reports conflict on exact levels.

OPEC+ left its December output target unchanged. Shipping disruption, not quotas, is driving prices.

Khor Mor's outage removes a major source of Kurdistan's electricity and gas. Restoration timing is unknown.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump meets Qatar's PM on 9 November, with VP Vance also meeting him, to seek a new ceasefire and restart nuclear talks. Doha is the live channel between Washington and Tehran.

Trump expects meetings with Xi and Putin next week, reportedly at APEC. Gulf News reported it; the schedule is not confirmed by the other sources (unconfirmed).

Democrats won both chambers in the US midterms, per Hurriyet's account of Erdogan's remarks. Expect more oversight of the Iran war and Gulf arms.

The EU remains split: ministers failed in September to agree trade sanctions on Israel. I found no new EU decision in the last 72 hours.
