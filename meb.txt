Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Qatar-Türkiye sign defence pact as Hormuz mine claims and Houthi missiles on Riyadh test Iran talks.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Qatar and Türkiye signed a mutual defence pact in Istanbul on 13 November, committing both to a unified stance against any armed attack on either. It follows August's Türkiye-Pakistan-Saudi pact and an October multilateral memorandum, and signals Gulf hedging beyond the US security umbrella; Ankara says it is not aimed at any state and is open to others, including Egypt.

The IRGC Navy said two more tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz on 13 November, the latest in a string of similar claims since 6 November. The claims are unverified and CENTCOM has rejected an earlier one, but they keep shipping insurers and transit coordination under Tehran's "traffic control" in play.

Trump told a Veterans Day audience on 11 November he wants "total victory" over Iran, while Iran International reports Tehran rejecting concessions and threatening a wider war. Mediated proposals via Qatar are alive but far apart.

The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and three drones over the Eastern Province, announced Sunday. It is part of a Houthi escalation against the kingdom since the Yemen truce collapsed in July.

Brent settled near 95.84 dollarson 13 November, a second weekly fall. Markets are pricing hopes of talks against continued Hormuz incidents.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Two Palestinians were reportedly killed in a drone strike in Jabalia, northern Gaza; the IDF said it targeted two militants. Strikes continue more than a year into the October 2025 ceasefire.

The UAE and 17 other states condemned an Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza that reportedly killed six, including a surgeon, and wounded 20. The IDF said the target was a Hamas commander; the casualty figures are as reported by local sources.

The EU urged Israel to allow unimpeded aid and reopen all Gaza crossings as winter floods hit displaced families. Dozens of states made similar demands.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians in Atara, north of Ramallah, on 12 November, according to Yesh Din and WAFA. OCHA recorded 457 settler attacks causing casualties or damage in October, the most in any month since 2006.

Settlers took over eight Palestinian homes in Ein a-Duyuk near Jericho in early November, displacing dozens. Settlers claim legal purchase; Palestinian officials say documents were forged.

Israel's 27 October election left no clear winner, with exit polls showing a near tie between Netanyahu's bloc and Eisenkot's. I found no reporting on the state of coalition talks; Herzog spoke on 14 November of the "next government".

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Lebanese authorities seized a large cache of weapons in a Beirut southern-suburb warehouse, and Hezbollah acknowledged it was theirs. Arrests followed, a rare visible state-Hezbollah test; five members were also detained near Batroun on 11 November.

Israel occupies about 620 sq km of southern Lebanon and conducts near-daily strikes, per L'Orient Today. Hezbollah has claimed no attack since the mid-June Iran-US memorandum.

An Israeli paper reports Israel preparing for a possible new confrontation with Hezbollah if a large Israeli-US operation proceeds (unconfirmed). Single-source and from early November.

Dozens protested in Aleppo on 13 November demanding accountability, and criticised al-Sharaa for meeting a person accused of war crimes. It shows transitional-justice pressure on Damascus.

The Sijil Center documented 395 Israeli violations in Syria in October, the highest monthly total of 2026, and the UN says 2.4 million Syrians need winter aid.

4. Iran.

Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran will not ship enriched material abroad, ruling out a Russia-based arrangement. He also said no decision had been made to change nuclear doctrine.

A Qatari-mediated package reportedly would reopen Hormuz first, then resume talks on the 60% stockpile; Tehran wants the US naval blockade lifted, frozen funds released and oil sales allowed. Iran International is an opposition-leaning outlet and the details are as it reports them.

The Assembly of Experts called for a review of defence doctrine, which could open a path to reversing the religious ban on nuclear weapons.

Erdoğan met President Pezeshkian in Antalya on 12 November and urged a return to the negotiating table "without further delay".

Trump reportedly said 18 US service members have died in the conflict and criticised the June memorandum. Single-aggregator report (unconfirmed).

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

A drone hit the Khor Mor gas field on 8 November, suspending production and, per Al Jazeera, leaving over 70% of the Kurdistan Region without power. No group has claimed it; Kataib Hezbollah is a suspect in reporting.

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi set up a joint inquiry with Kurdish authorities and reportedly lets militias keep weapons until June 2027. US envoy Tom Barrack backed him.

The PUK withdrew from the KRG cabinet, calling the reactivated parliament illegitimate, and analysts warn of two rival administrations. The parliament had elected a KDP-bloc speaker (52 votes) after a two-year deadlock.

The KRG says Baghdad sent none of the allocated funds against IQD 65 trillion charged for sovereign expenditure.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Houthi missiles and drones targeted Riyadh and Saudi Eastern Province; Qatar, Bahrain, the UAE and Syria condemned earlier attacks on Riyadh's financial district and an Aramco site. The Houthis also claimed strikes on King Khalid airport and a pipeline station that Riyadh has not confirmed.

The US Embassy in Riyadh issued a security alert on 13 November, noting Gulf airports in Qatar and the UAE remain operational. Emirates, flydubai and Air Arabia have disrupted some Saudi routes.

Al-Sharaa visited Riyadh on 4 November and signed agreements on industry, agriculture and environment.

Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours beyond the statements above.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Houthis launched ballistic missiles at Aden on 13 to 14 November, per ISW via a secondary aggregator (unconfirmed). They also began a combined offensive in Lahij on 11 November aiming to cut the Mokha-Aden road, Critical Threats assesses.

Government forces claim new advances in "Operation Dawn of Yemen" as Houthi border attacks on Saudi Arabia resume. The Houthis seized much of Yemen's Red Sea coast in September.

Red Sea shipping: I found no reports of new commercial-ship attacks in the last 72 hours; the Hormuz theatre dominates.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Lewis Hamilton visited the Egyptian side of Rafah on 11 November to see the Egyptian Red Crescent's Gaza aid operation. The crossing reopened in February under EUBAM supervision.

Egypt reopened its embassy in Tripoli in early November after twelve years.

Jordan: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

9. Turkey.

Türkiye and Qatar signed the defence pact in Istanbul on 13 November; Erdoğan said they would work to prevent a resurgence of war in the Gulf.

Erdoğan met Pezeshkian at the COP31 gathering in Antalya on 12 November and signed 5.1 dollarsbillion in development-bank agreements.

Erdoğan called the international system "bankrupt" on 6 November and said he hoped the US midterms (Democrats now hold both chambers) would help regional peace.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Tripoli's Criminal Court sentenced seven former security personnel to death, per the Libya Observer homepage on 13 November.

Aircraft linked to an Emirati firm reportedly arrived in Benghazi on 13 November, citing Libya Observer (unconfirmed).

Morocco is claiming diplomatic gains after two days of UN Sahara debate, and Spain backed a 26 eurosmillion rise in EU southern-neighbourhood aid.

Tunisia, Algeria: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM has previously rejected IRGC mine claims, while the Joint Maritime Information Center's 12 November advisory logged several tanker attacks in the strait and Gulf. The UN says 25 seafarers have been killed in or around Hormuz.

Iran has warned it would hit US bases if "Pickaxe Mountain" were attacked.

Arab states warned Iran of individual and collective retaliation over attacks, per the WSJ as relayed by Iran International (unconfirmed).

The Board of Peace's July Hamas disarmament roadmap remains stalled: the ISF is not yet deployed and Netanyahu rejected it on 9 August.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent dipped below 90 dollarson 10 November for the first time since August, then settled near 95.84 dollarson 13 November. Reports differ slightly on the close.

OPEC+ output rose about 2 million bpd in October to roughly 97% of pre-war levels, and the IEA sees a fourth-quarter surplus.

The Khor Mor outage cuts gas to Kurdistan power plants; Dana Gas said production remains suspended.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

The US midterms left Democrats controlling both chambers of Congress, per Erdoğan's remarks as reported by Hürriyet Daily News. It may constrain war powers on Iran.

The EU calls for unimpeded Gaza aid and says a sustainable Iran settlement requires negotiation while condemning Iranian disruption of Hormuz navigation.

Russia and China back a return to diplomacy based on the Islamabad memorandum; I found no new joint statement in the last 72 hours.

US envoy Tom Barrack met Iraq's prime minister in Baghdad and condemned the Khor Mor attack.
