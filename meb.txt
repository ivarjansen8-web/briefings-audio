Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

Saudi-Houthi clash escalates at Hodeidah as Netanyahu loses Knesset speakership and Iran strike talk grows.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 21 November 2026 (mostly 17 to 20 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Saudi-led coalition struck Yemen's Hodeidah port on 20 November and the Houthis threatened Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports in reply. The coalition called it a "special operation" against weapons smuggling and told civilians to leave the port area; the move opens a new phase of the Saudi-Houthi war.

Israel's opposition bloc won the Knesset speakership 66 to 52 on 17 November, electing Matan Kahana of Eisenkot's Yashar party. It weakens caretaker Prime Minister Netanyahu's control of parliament and lets the bloc move to repeal parts of the judicial overhaul.

Reports say Netanyahu is pressing Trump for a joint 4 to 7 day air campaign on Iran, while Avigdor Liberman said on 20 November any strike now would be politically motivated. Trump reportedly says he is "not rushing" and will wait until January; Israel's military establishment sees no urgent need, per Channel 13. Times of Israel, 20 November (partly unconfirmed)

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council on 17 November that the Gaza ceasefire faces imminent collapse without action. It blamed both Israel and Hamas for missed commitments on disarmament, withdrawal and reconstruction.

Trump, Xi and Putin held a first trilateral summit on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen on 19 November and issued a "Declaration toward Peace". A US official said the Iran war was on the agenda, and Europe was notably absent.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Gaza's Health Ministry counted 1,650 Palestinians killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, as of 16 November. Strikes continue, including one on Nasser Medical Complex on 11 to 12 November that killed at least six, among them a surgeon.

A storm flooded about 70,000 tents in Gaza within 12 hours, and Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov went to Israel to seek approval for tents and faster reconstruction. Shelter, not just fire, now drives the humanitarian risk.

The Board of Peace says 17 dollarsbillion has been pledged for Gaza but only 280 dollarsmillion deployed, and that Israel has not let the new technocratic administration start work. It also accuses Hamas of taxing goods entering Gaza; Hamas agreed to the disarmament plan on 30 July but progress is minimal.

An unarmed Palestinian, Hamza Masoud Salim Abdo, 42, was shot dead on 20 November by the civilian security coordinator of Beit El settlement, per WAFA as cited by Times of Israel. It fits a pattern of settler-linked violence in the West Bank. Times of Israel, 20 November (single-source, unconfirmed)

UN experts warned of mounting violence against rights defenders in the West Bank, and a WAFA photographer said soldiers beat and held him for six hours near Jericho. The account is from WAFA, a Palestinian Authority agency.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israeli artillery kept shelling villages in the occupied south of Lebanon and struck a target in the Bekaa on 17 November. Fighting dates from 2 March 2026, and Israeli media describe preparations for wider war involving Hezbollah and Iran.

A UN Security Council text on Lebanon is increasingly built around a tripartite framework agreement. Disputes remain over the successor to UNIFIL and over how disarmament is monitored.

Five Hezbollah members arrested in northern Lebanon were charged with forming an armed faction, and President Aoun told Hezbollah to hand over its weapons. US envoy Mike Waltz said the group "must disarm voluntarily". Terrorism Information Center, 2 to 9 November (Israeli think tank) (headline-level, unconfirmed)

Syria's President al-Sharaa named Ahmad Rawad Ramadan economy minister on 18 November. Homs also began clearing rubble at 75 sites over 90 days, a sign of reconstruction.

Israeli patrols continue incursions in Quneitra, which a Syrian official says undermines de-escalation.

4. Iran.

Foreign Minister Araghchi said on 21 November that Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz the day after the US meets its commitments, and that no US reply has come to its proposal. He also said Iran's seven conditions must be met and that enriched uranium stays in the country.

Trump said on 12 November "we don't need a deal with Iran" and pulled Vice President Vance from the talks effort. Vance had written to Mohsen Rezaei proposing direct talks; Rezaei denied any meeting was planned.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed two more tankers hit mines in Hormuz on Thursday 19 November. No maritime authority has confirmed this, so treat it with care. FinanceFeeds, 20 November (citing Reuters) (unconfirmed)

A Qatari compromise reportedly would cap enrichment at 3.67% and dilute stockpiles domestically, with Hormuz reopening after IAEA inspectors return. Iran International reports hardliners openly debating a bomb.

Iran's foreign ministry called US chemical-weapons allegations "fraudulent" on 19 November.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The Kurdistan Region is in its deepest rift in years between its two ruling parties, reviving fears of separate Erbil and Sulaymaniyah administrations.

The KRG rejected Baghdad's draft 2027 budget and demands 19.74 dollarsbillion.

Masrour Barzani met Iraqi PM al-Zaidi in Baghdad on oilfield management and the budget share. Salary arrears are a recurring pressure point.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

The Houthis fired ballistic missiles at Riyadh's financial district and an Aramco facility, with minor damage to an Aramco building. The UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and the Muslim World League condemned the attack.

Reuters reported Saudi Arabia is preparing for the Yemen war to last well into 2027, up to six more months.

Qatar continues to mediate between Washington and Tehran, and UAE and Qatari foreign ministers spoke on 17 November.

Araghchi visited Oman around 19 to 20 November and criticised "foreign interference" in the region. Breitbart, 19 November (partisan outlet) (single-source)

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The coalition said it hit Houthi missile and drone capabilities in Sanaa and Hodeidah and pledged further strikes. An earlier 17 November strike on the port killed at least 14 to 15 people, according to the Houthis and other reports.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree warned airlines that Saudi airspace is unsafe and named the Shuqaiq power station and Jazan port as targets. The Houthis claim hits on both; this is unverified. MarineLink, 20 November (unconfirmed)

The UN warned that Yemen's Red Sea ports must stay open, as Hodeidah is the main entry for food into the north. Yemen's information minister said the port was still fully working.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Mada Masr's editor was fined EGP 3 million and the outlet ordered closed; an appeal is set for 25 November. A separate recusal hearing is due 26 November.

Egypt's Supreme Judicial Council rejected claims of trial irregularities in the case.

Jordan: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan announced the Bayraktar Kizilelma unmanned fighter entered service, with Baykar delivering the first three aircraft.

An Ankara court convicted Istanbul mayor Ekrem Imamoglu of voter fraud on 18 November with a two-year-one-month sentence and a possible political ban. It could open Istanbul to Erdogan's party.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

The US envoy urged implementation of a Libya unification deal and warned of sanctions against spoilers; Libya-Russia military cooperation is also in the headlines. Only headlines were visible. Libya Observer, 20 November (unconfirmed)

Air Algerie restarted flights to Tripoli after 12 years, signalling warmer ties with western Libya.

Tunisia: a Washington Institute commentary warns of escalating protests and repression. Morocco: Quiet on hard news.

11. Defence and security.

US missile strikes reportedly hit the outskirts of Qeshm Island in Iran, and CBS reports the US and Israel are awaiting Trump's go-ahead on a possible offensive. Times of Israel, 20 November (unconfirmed)

Trump said Iran may be working on chemical or biological weapons, which Tehran denies.

Saudi-led forces intercepted Houthi drones aimed at Riyadh and the south, plus a ballistic missile.

Israeli forces exchanged fire with a gunman at a checkpoint near Hebron. CNN Middle East page, November 2026 (unconfirmed)

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded around 92 dollars-93 on 20 November and WTI near 83, dollarsrebounding on Houthi attacks and tanker incidents. Oversupply keeps prices muted despite Hormuz risk; the IEA says supply moved above demand in October.

Sources conflict on Hormuz: one tracker lists it closed (day 265), another cites Reuters on shipments back near pre-war levels.

Turkey signed 5.1 dollarsbillion of financing deals with four development banks at COP31 events.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

The Shenzhen summit produced a "Declaration toward Peace" covering UN reform and the ICC. Commentators note a Middle East angle: a US-Russia-China understanding could leave Israel more exposed on Iran.

European capitals are sidelined; Zelensky voiced concern about European security being discussed without Ukraine. US-Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi began a third round on 10 November.

Trump said the US would keep its naval blockade and pressure on Iran's economy.

What to watch.

22 to 23 November: whether the coalition strikes Jeddah-threat-linked targets or the Houthis hit Saudi ports, and whether Hodeidah stays open.

25 November: Mada Masr appeal against closure and fine; recusal ruling 26 November.

Coming days: a UN Security Council Lebanon text on the framework deal and a UNIFIL successor.

Through January: Trump's reported timeline on any Iran strike; watch Araghchi's Hormuz offer and Qatari mediation.

Ongoing: Eisenkot bloc moves on judicial-overhaul repeal and talks with UTJ; Board of Peace follow-up on Gaza policing and tents.
