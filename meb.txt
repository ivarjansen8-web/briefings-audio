Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Monday, 23 November.

US reportedly delays Iran strike as Hormuz blockade standoff and Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalate.

Compiled from sources dated 19 to 23 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The US has reportedly put a strike on Iran on hold for at least a week, according to Ynet as relayed by Iran International on 23 November, even as CENTCOM stays at maximum readiness and Israel presses for action. The pause leaves the Qatar- and Pakistan-channelled diplomacy as the only live track (unconfirmed, single-source).

Iran is preparing 14 tankers to test the US naval blockade, with at least 31 laden sanctioned tankers already outside the strait, Gulf News reported. Washington has reportedly warned that ships taking Iranian cargo via ship-to-ship transfer risk military action, a claim relayed second-hand from Reuters.

The Houthis claim a hit on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery on 22 November, plus a strike on a radar site at Wadi ad-Dawasir. Saudi authorities did not confirm damage, and the coalition says it intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at the western Red Sea coast, so the Houthi-Saudi front is now a direct war.

Israel's ground push beyond Gaza's "Yellow Line" near Nuseirat and Bureij is the first confirmed since the October 2025 truce. The IDF says it targets a tunnel; Hamas calls it a flagrant violation.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israeli tanks crossed the Yellow Line near Nuseirat and Bureij around 18 November, forcing thousands to flee. The IDF says the operation, with the Shin Bet, aims to dismantle a tunnel and that the line itself has not moved; Palestinians say it keeps shifting inward.

Settlers attacked several villages near Ramallah and al-Bireh, burning 12 vehicles and spraying racist slogans, citing WAFA. Foreign Minister Sa'ar condemned the latest rampage while complaining European media ignore stepped-up enforcement.

A Palestinian shepherd was reportedly shot dead by a settler east of Bethlehem, and another man was killed by Israeli fire east of Deir al-Balah on 22 November, per WAFA (Palestinian Authority agency; unconfirmed by Israel).

Israeli forces use explosive-laden quadcopters outside the Yellow Line, Palestinians told Tehran Times (state-aligned), which suggests the truce is eroding in practice.

The IDF says it struck three Palestinian Islamic Jihad operatives in northern Gaza, rejecting claims they were aid workers.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel keeps striking south Lebanon despite the US-brokered ceasefire, with two strikes on Kfar Tebnit and artillery on Tyre district on the night of 20 to 21 November, per the National News Agency. This strains the US-backed framework.

Israel is widening its "buffer" with fire along a 1 to 2 km strip beyond areas it holds, mostly the Litani valleys, security sources told Asharq Al-Awsat.

Israel's defence minister has acknowledged destroying over 20,000 homes in its self-declared security zone, NPR reports, as villagers return to ruins.

Hezbollah said on 21 November that Lebanon cannot be independent while Israel occupies its land, while five alleged members arrested in the north face military prosecution, which Hezbollah denies. Disarmament pressure from US envoy Mike Waltz is the backdrop.

Syria is upgrading the al-Bukamal crossing with Iraq, announced 22 November, alongside a 12.8% 2026 growth forecast and an unconfirmed abduction of a Suwayda activist blamed on the National Guard.

4. Iran.

Iran again refuses IAEA access to its sites, and the agency says it has no information on its declared material. Trump claims "there's indication" of chemical or biological weapons work (unconfirmed).

The Assembly of Experts is urging an NPT exit, and Iran International says a nuclear-bomb debate is gaining ground on official platforms. Foreign Minister Araghchi said on 21 November that doctrine is unchanged and that China and Russia oppose any change.

Iran says an air-defence missile damaged a US F-16 on 20 November; CENTCOM flatly denies it. Treat Iranian battlefield claims with caution.

Araghchi named Qatar and Pakistan as the only mediation channels, and an Iranian technical team visited Doha this week.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The PUK has withdrawn from the Kurdistan Regional Government cabinet after parliament reopened without it, and the KDP's Vala Fareed Ibrahim was elected speaker. This opens a serious rift in Erbil-Sulaymaniyah power-sharing.

A suspected drone hit the Khor Mor gas field on 8 November. Electricity and cooking-fuel supply suffered.

Masrour Barzani and Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi reportedly agreed a single Kurdistan allocation in the draft 2027 budget, while Erbil requests 19.74 billion dollars; PJAK says an Iranian drone targeted Penjwen sites.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Iran-linked drones hit Bahrain and Kuwait again on 17 to 18 November. Bahrain says its air defences intercepted several strikes, and the IRGC claims it hit US radar and Ali Al Salem base. The US answered with strikes on Qeshm.

The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh on 21 November after strikes on Sanaa and Hodeidah.

Etihad extended cancellations of Abu Dhabi-Riyadh and Dammam flights through 30 November, and the UAE and Oman are searching for a missing Jordanian sailor after a Hormuz tanker attack.

Witkoff and Kushner met Qatari officials in Berlin on 19 November. Qatar trades concrete proposals while Oman mostly carries messages.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Houthis claim strikes on the Rabigh refinery and Wadi ad-Dawasir radar, unverified, and Reuters notes Riyadh has not confirmed refinery attacks.

Renewed fighting since July has let the Houthis take territory from the recognised government, per the same reporting, and L'Orient Today headlined that Riyadh is preparing a six-month campaign.

An Egyptian crew foiled three suspected pirate attacks in the Gulf of Aden on 17 November.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egypt and Jordan initialled an aviation cooperation MoU on 21 November to widen air links.

Jordan sentenced three men to death over the Karak attack, per an Al-Ahram Weekly listing (date not confirmed).

Otherwise quiet: no major political developments found in the last 72 hours.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan tied shorter trials to judicial reform on 21 November at an Istanbul courthouse opening.

Erdogan says Turkey is "entering a new era" after hosting COP31 and the July NATO summit, and called the global system "bankrupt" over Gaza.

An African-Turkish Summit is being prepared in Tripoli, which signals Ankara's reach into Libya.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Algeria says its border guards killed two alleged Moroccan smugglers in Naama on 20 November, per APS via Hespress; unverified and likely to inflame bilateral tension.

Morocco beat Algeria 2 to 1 in the African U-12 team tennis final in Cairo. A Crisis Group warning on MINURSO withdrawal and Morocco-Algeria conflict risk was also flagged (date unconfirmed).

Libya's House of Representatives will summon the central bank governor over the economic crisis (18 November), and US envoy Massad Boulos is active on university ties.

Tunisia: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

CENTCOM reportedly finalised post-midterm strike plans and favours a joint operation with Israel, while Washington weighs delay.

Iran launched drones at a US site in the Gulf in mid-November, its first such attack in two months, which ended a lull.

Tankers were hit in and near Hormuz on 21 November, and Iran is tightening its grip on shipping.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded near 92.3 dollars-92.7 on 23 November, down about 4% on the week, with WTI near 83.3 dollars. Hormuz disruption and the Houthi claim capped losses.

LSEG says normal Hormuz flows are not possible under current conditions.

OPEC+ ministers meet on 29 November to set 2027 output policy.

Syria's Finance Ministry forecast 12.8% growth in 2026, but fuel subsidies fall short of heating needs.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump is oscillating: he says Iran will not accept a deal "acceptable to me", says he will not decide next steps until the new year (Axios), yet also promises a "permanent" deal.

The US, China and Russia issued a joint strategic-stability statement after a 19 November Shenzhen summit that omits Iran and Hormuz.

G7 foreign ministers in Paris (9 to 10 November) backed diplomacy and said Iran must never obtain a nuclear weapon.

Democrats took both chambers of Congress in the midterms, per Hurriyet Daily News, which may constrain war powers.

What to watch.

By about 30 November: whether the reported US strike delay holds, and any Israeli pressure for a joint operation.

Coming days: whether the 14 Iranian tankers try to run the blockade.

29 November: OPEC+ meeting on 2027 output.

Through 30 November: Gulf airline cancellations on Saudi routes, a gauge of the Houthi threat.

Coming days: Doha and Pakistan-channel contacts and any Witkoff-Kushner follow-up.

Next week: whether Israel widens operations beyond the Yellow Line in Gaza.
