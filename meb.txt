Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Trump keeps Iran blockade as Hormuz tankers hit; Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia continue.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 14 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump says the US does not need a deal with Iran and will keep the naval blockade in place. Back-channel talks look stalled, and Washington and Tehran each demand concessions before talks restart.

Iran's military said four oil tankers were hit by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday after they ignored warnings. The Revolutionary Guards separately said a gas tanker hit a mine, so shipping is still at risk despite the US blockade of Iranian ports.

The IRGC claimed it destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain; the US denied it and said it shot down two Iranian drones headed for the island. This is a contested claim on both sides.

Houthi missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia continue. Riyadh, Jazan and the East-West pipeline are among the claimed targets (Houthi claims; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed all of them).

Brent settled near 96 dollarsa barrel on Friday and is set for a second weekly decline. Prices are well above the roughly 72 dollarsof late February, before the war.

2. Israel and Palestine.

The IDF struck a top Hamas commander at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis on 12 November; six people were reported killed. The strike shows Israel is still hitting targets during the "ceasefire".

Dozens of states and organisations demand that Israel let more winter supplies into Gaza, and the WHO warns that cold, flooding and crowded shelters will worsen disease.

Israel has reportedly authorised about 250 foreign troops for the US-backed stabilisation force, under a CENTCOM-named commander, Maj. Gen. Jasper Jeffers. Deployment has not yet begun.

The Board of Peace said the "time for assurances without implementation has passed"; a Jerusalem Post analysis says the IDF controls about 70% of Gaza and Hamas has not disarmed.

In the West Bank, troops raided Jaba' and al-Mughayyir and shot a young man, and settlers kept a family's home under siege for a 40th day. WAFA, as relayed by Middle East Eye, reports the incidents; a 14-year-old was killed in Burqa on 10 November. The UN counts at least 1,143 Palestinians killed in the West Bank since October 2023.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

The Lebanese army said on 13 November it seized a large arms and ammunition depot in Beirut's southern suburbs and made one arrest. It is a concrete step in the state's disarmament push against Hezbollah.

A Lebanese military court charged 13 people on 30 October over a half-ton explosives shipment to Hezbollah.

Israeli Defence Minister Katz reportedly threatened to strike Beirut's southern suburbs if Hezbollah and Iran attack Israel (unconfirmed; L'Orient-Le Jour headline, paywalled).

SDF leader Mazloum Abdi says integration with Damascus will be completed by the end of 2026. An analyst calls the date a fresh test of both sides' commitments.

Relatives of Syrians held in Israeli prisons protested at UNDOF headquarters in Quneitra, and protests over Israeli violations in the south continue.

The French frigate Surcouf made the first visit of a French warship to Syria, which Paris frames as the start of naval cooperation.

4. Iran.

Foreign Minister Araghchi says there are no plans for direct talks with the US and that contacts continue through mediators. Tehran wants the blockade lifted, frozen funds released and oil sales allowed first.

Iran's top security official said Tehran will not hand over "even a shred" of enriched material, and Iran has ruled out the return of IAEA inspectors. The IAEA says it cannot conclude there was no diversion of declared material.

Iran's Assembly of Experts has urged the leadership to consider leaving the NPT, according to Tasnim and Press TV as cited by CBS.

Araghchi's "2-day proposal" (US steps on day one, Hormuz reopens on day two) still stands, he said on 8 November, arguing US voters rejected the war in the midterms. Tehran Times is state-aligned.

Treasury Secretary Bessent says he will not be cowed by Iranian death threats as Washington runs a sanctions campaign on Iranian banks.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

Parliament formed a temporary committee on 11 November to review intelligence on the 8 November drone attack on the Khor Mor gas field, which halted production.

Kurdistan has restored about 1,500 MW of generation after the shutdown, but supply is still six to eight hours a day (single source, undated post).

PUK leader Bafel Talabani rejected a dialogue offer from KDP President Masoud Barzani on 12 November. The KDP and opposition are ready to reconvene parliament, while the PUK demands a prior deal on key posts.

Baghdad's government denied a nationwide airspace closure; the Air Defense Command's live-fire exercise covers a limited area. Ali al-Zaidi has been prime minister since 14 May.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

The US Mission to Saudi Arabia issued a countrywide alert on 13 November, requiring special authorisation for government employees to use any Saudi airport after missile and drone attacks on airports.

The Arab Parliament launched a plan to isolate the Houthis and designate them terrorists, and the Saudi foreign minister discussed the attacks with a Chinese envoy.

Trump said in an interview released Friday that Saudi Arabia is "disintegrating". The remark is likely to offend Riyadh at a tense moment.

The GCC approved an advanced headquarters for its Unified Military Command in Kuwait and signed a general agreement to advance the GCC railway.

Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement on Friday (Reuters wire, via Al-Monitor).

Flight disruption continues: Etihad extended cancellations to Riyadh and Dammam, and Kuwait Airways' Riyadh flights remain cancelled. A Fujairah road accident killed 14.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

The Houthis fired cruise missiles and a drone at Riyadh on 11 to 12 November, which Saudi forces say they intercepted. CFR also lists strikes on Aden and a claimed attack on a Saudi command centre.

Yemeni government forces claim new advances in an operation begun on 5 October, as Houthi cross-border attacks resume (government claim).

The US maritime advisory, effective through March 2027, warns of elevated risk to ships linked to Israel, the US, the UK or Saudi Arabia in the southern Red Sea.

Coalition forces said they destroyed drones launched at Saudi Arabia on 13 November (coalition statement).

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest item is Egypt's 5 November condemnation of a terrorist attack in Amman that killed a Jordanian security officer.

Qatar, the UK and Jordan called at a UN briefing for unhindered aid to Gaza.

9. Turkey.

Erdoğan met Iranian President Pezeshkian at the COP31 summit in Antalya on 12 November to discuss ending the US-Iran war. Ankara is positioning itself as a mediator.

Turkey signed 10 agreements worth 5.1 billion dollars with four development banks. Erdoğan said so at the COP31 business forum.

Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence agreement, and Turkey's parliament last week ratified the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement with Saudi Arabia. Together they deepen Ankara's Gulf security role (Reuters wire via Al-Monitor; Arab News).

Erdoğan met Libya's Dbeibah on the COP31 sidelines.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Libya's Central Bank reconstituted its Monetary Policy Committee, which warned that monetary tools alone cannot close the gap between official and parallel exchange rates (single source, a roundup).

Dbeibah stated his stance on Sudan's territorial unity; a Libya Observer opinion piece asks what happens to the US-brokered "Boulos deal" after the midterms.

Casablanca police arrested a 15-year-old suspected of a false terror threat against schools.

Morocco's government called a Casablanca blackout "exceptional". Algeria and Tunisia: Quiet, nothing significant found.

11. Defence and security.

A US-facilitated record 135 Hormuz transits occurred in the 96 hours to 11 November, averaging 33.8 vessels a day, per the UANI tracker (advocacy group). The blockade continues to constrain Iranian oil exports.

The US has resumed its blockade since 14 July after a June memorandum broke down.

The US reportedly sold 5,250 interceptors to Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar and the UAE for replenishment (unconfirmed; Liveuamap).

The Lebanese army's depot seizure and Israel's ongoing Gaza strikes are covered above.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded near 96 dollarsand WTI near 88.50 dollars; Brent is down 2.4% this week after a 16% drop the week before.

The IEA says the oil market will be in surplus this quarter for the first time since the war began, as Middle East exports recover.

Trump told Vice President Vance to focus on domestic issues, not Iran talks, which cooled hopes of a deal and supported prices.

The Khor Mor shutdown cut Kurdistan's power supply, and Iraq weighs new ministries after a dinar devaluation (undated).

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Trump says he expects to meet Xi and Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit in China next week, and has floated trilateral talks on the Iran war (unconfirmed; no schedule confirmed).

China and Oman issued a 20-point statement urging a comprehensive ceasefire, after Wang Yi met Iran's Ali Bagheri Kani on 2 November.

Iran's Mohsen Rezaei met Putin on 5 November and said Iran gave the US seven conditions. Russia has offered to mediate.

The EU calls for de-escalation, while France, Germany and the UK jointly condemned Iran's retaliation.

Dozens of states demand more Gaza winter aid, and Qatar, the UK and Jordan add their voices at the UN.
