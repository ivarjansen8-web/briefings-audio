Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Friday, 13 November.

Hormuz tanker attacks lift Brent to 95.82 dollarsas Iran rejects uranium export; Israeli coalition talks stall.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 13 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. Search coverage for 13 November itself was thin; the latest reliable items are from 12 November.

1. Top stories.

Brent crude settled at 95.82 dollarson 12 November, up 3.3%, after Houthi claims of strikes on Saudi energy sites and Iranian claims of four tankers hit in Hormuz. Energy infrastructure is now a primary target set in the Iran war, which keeps pressure on any mediation effort.

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran will not move "a single atom" of enriched uranium out of the country and that no negotiations with Washington are under way. This cuts against Qatar's claims of progress and sets up the core sticking point of any deal.

Trump said after meeting Qatar's prime minister that there is "positive momentum" with Iran and that renewed US strikes are "quite unlikely". Rubio, however, said the indirect talks had yielded little so far.

Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats in the 27 October election and coalition talks continue, with Eisenkot's Yashar the largest party on 22. Whether Netanyahu can reach 61 decides who runs Israel's Gaza, Lebanon and Iran policy.

UNIFIL confirmed its mandate ends on 31 December, and the G7 found no agreed replacement. A security vacuum in south Lebanon is looming while Hezbollah-Israel positions remain deadlocked.

2. Israel and Palestine.

An Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis targeted the Hamas Khan Younis Brigade commander; local health officials put the toll at six. Strikes inside medical facilities continue to draw scrutiny despite the nominal ceasefire.

Israeli fire reportedly killed seven people in Gaza, including a 13-year-old boy; the IDF said it hit terror targets. The ceasefire remains violent and one-sided in casualty terms.

The UK, Qatar and Jordan lead dozens of states demanding Israel allow more winter supplies into Gaza; Israel reportedly resists dual-use items (unconfirmed detail). Winter exposure is a growing humanitarian risk.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians in Atara, near Ramallah, on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA as cited by Haaretz. Settler violence is at what the UN calls an unprecedented level.

Israeli forces killed a 14-year-old boy in Burqa on 10 November, per Al Jazeera.

The International Stabilization Force had still not deployed in Gaza as of October; Israel controls just over half the Strip and Hamas has rejected sequenced disarmament.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Lebanese state media reported an Israeli air strike near Qantara and shelling around Majdal Zoun on 10 November. Israel and Hezbollah have been at war since 2 March and Israel occupies parts of the south.

Israel conditions withdrawal on Hezbollah's nationwide disarmament; Hezbollah conditions any weapons talks on an end to Israeli violations. The UN Secretary-General calls these "competing sequencing demands".

G7 ministers in Paris found no mechanism to replace UNIFIL; Rubio floated a civilian monitoring mission and Italy reportedly does not back the French proposal.

Syria's UN envoy Ibrahim Olabi told the Fourth Committee on 11 November that Israeli violations undermine de-escalation. Enab Baladi's monitors counted at least 89 Israeli violations in southern and central Syria on 1 to 7 November.

The French frigate Surcouf visited Latakia on 11 November, the first French warship visit to Syria. It signals growing France-Syria military cooperation.

The Dabousiya crossing with Lebanon reopened, the fourth to reopen.

4. Iran.

Rezaei told Al Mayadeen that Iran will not remove "a single atom" of enriched material and warned of consequences if the US strikes again.

Iran says it stays committed to the NPT, but hardliners are openly discussing quitting the treaty and building a bomb. The debate shows how far domestic politics constrain negotiators.

Erdogan met President Pezeshkian at the COP31 summit in Antalya on 12 November to discuss ending the US-Iran war. Turkey is positioning itself as a second mediator beside Qatar.

Iran International reports Trump vowing "total victory" while Iran rejects concessions (opposition-leaning outlet, single source).

Foreign Minister Araghchi called the Doha talks "ongoing and progressing" while saying Tehran is ready for "both defense and diplomacy".

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

A prime-ministerial aide said Iraq will not finish disarming Iran-backed militias this year; PM Ali al-Zaidi's deadline is 30 June 2027. US forces left Iraq at end-September, and Washington is pressing for faster action.

An Iraqi outlet claims al-Zaidi discussed a leaked US war plan using Iraq as a staging ground (unconfirmed, single-source and contested).

The PUK and New Generation filed a Federal Supreme Court suit to annul the 4 November Kurdistan Parliament session, and the PUK has quit the KRG cabinet. The KDP-PUK split deepens.

Al-Zaidi and Masrour Barzani discussed oilfield management and Kurdistan's 2027 budget share.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh and the Eastern Province were intercepted, and the Houthis claimed hits on an East-West pipeline pumping station and a Yanbu facility. The claims are Houthi-sourced and unverified.

An ADNOC Logistics vessel was damaged in UAE waters on 10 November. Details were provisional.

Etihad cancelled flights to Dammam and Riyadh until 19 November.

Qatar says it is "bridging the gap" between Washington and Tehran and sees "progress".

Egypt confirmed a military presence in Saudi Arabia.

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Yemeni government forces claim new advances under "Operation Dawn of Yemen" (launched 5 October) as Houthi border attacks resume. These are government claims.

The Houthis keep firing missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia, including oil facilities. The conflict has become a Saudi-Houthi war again.

A US MARAD advisory warns of Houthi risk to shipping in the southern Red Sea until 22 March 2027, and insurance premiums have risen to 0.7 to 1.0% of hull value (Africa Center estimate).

8. Egypt and Jordan.

Egypt condemned a terrorist attack that killed a Jordanian security officer (date and details unconfirmed).

A New Cairo appeals court set a 25 November hearing in the Mada Masr closure case after editor Lina Attalah's conviction. Press freedom is under pressure.

Syria and Jordan signed a cloud-seeding MoU for the Yarmouk basin on 9 November.

9. Turkey.

Erdogan met Pezeshkian in Antalya on the COP31 sidelines on 12 November.

Erdogan held his first call with Trump on 7 November, after hoping the US midterms would aid regional peace.

Erdogan called the global system "bankrupt" over Gaza and the West Bank at the TRT World Forum on 6 November.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

Libya's Dbeibah is in talks with the UN envoy on the 4+4 process and the election path; eastern parliament named Fawzi Abu Khazam central bank governor.

Air Algérie resumed Tripoli flights after 12 years, read as warmer Algeria-western Libya ties.

Morocco's parliament approved the El Mansouri government's programme 204 votes; the PM puts a two-state solution at the centre of foreign policy. Tunisia's October inflation was 5.6%.

11. Defence and security.

Reuters reports 37 ships were attacked in October, the highest monthly total of the war, and expects no easing in November.

UKMTO reported a tanker hit 19 nm northeast of Lima, Oman, on 11 November.

The IRGC is blamed by open-source analysts for the 8 November strike on the ADNOC-linked Navig8 Messi (attribution unconfirmed).

Iran's military claimed four tankers were hit by explosions in Hormuz on 12 November; Reuters could not verify.

12. Economy and energy.

Brent settled at 95.82 dollarsand WTI at 88.74 dollarson 12 November.

October OPEC+ output rose, led by Saudi Arabia, which is nearing pre-war levels. Higher supply capped gains.

Ras Al Khaimah reopened beaches after an oil spill from a damaged tanker.

War risk premiums on Red Sea transits have jumped roughly sevenfold.

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

G7 foreign ministers in Paris (9 to 10 November) said Iran must never have a nuclear weapon, urged it to stop attacks in Hormuz and backed regional mediators. They also condemned Chinese and North Korean support for Russia.

Iran's security chief delivered a Khamenei message to Putin in early November as Moscow pushes a gas pipeline deal.

Putin called outside interference in the Middle East "unacceptable"; China urges an end to hostilities and respect for Iranian sovereignty.

Trump said the US "did not need a deal" with Iran on 12 November, hours after calling the war militarily "effectively over".
