Here is your Daily Middle East Briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

Third US carrier arrives as Iran hits Bahrain, Houthis advance in Yemen.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 17 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

A third US carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, reached the Middle East on 16 November, joining the George H.W. Bush and George Washington enforcing the blockade of Iranian shipping. Trump alternates between threatening new strikes and saying the war is "effectively over", so the build-up is leverage as much as preparation.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say they hit a US radar at the Al-Riffa base in Bahrain with six drones on 15 November; the US says it intercepted two drones and suffered no damage. It is the second claimed attack on Bahrain in three days, and Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and Egypt condemned it.

Israel struck deep inside Lebanon on 16 November, hitting what it called a Hezbollah arms shipment near the Syrian border at Janta. Haaretz calls it the first such strike after a period of restraint at US request, which tests the stalled Israel-Lebanon framework.

Houthi forces are advancing in south-west Yemen, and government forces withdrew from the Red Sea coastal plain and western Lahij on 15 to 16 November. The Houthis are also firing at Saudi Arabia, which widens the Yemen war into a Gulf security problem.

A Reuters/Ipsos poll released 16 November put Trump's approval at 32%, with 31% of Americans backing the Iran war, as reported by Iran International's live blog (unconfirmed, secondary relay). Fuel prices are cited as a driver, which constrains Washington's appetite for escalation.

2. Israel and Palestine.

Israel killed Hamas commander Samir Abu Younis in Khan Younis on 15 November, and both the IDF and Hamas confirmed it. CNN notes it came two months after Israel killed his predecessor, while the Gaza ceasefire stagnates.

CNN reports more than 1,600 people killed in near-daily Israeli strikes since the ceasefire was signed a year ago, and the international stabilisation force has still not deployed. The Board of Peace said on 14 November it had discussed a civilian Gaza police force with 17 countries.

Hamas authorities say a 12 November Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in southern Gaza killed six people; the UAE condemned it. Winter storms have also flooded tens of thousands of tents, and the EU demanded "immediate, unimpeded" aid.

Israel advanced 11 West Bank settlement plans covering 895 housing units, according to the Palestinian Wall and Settlement Resistance Commission via WAFA. OCHA counted 457 attacks causing casualties or damage in October, the most in any month since 2006.

Settlers seized eight Palestinian homes in Jericho on 4 November, and residents fear a wider takeover. The Palestinian Authority has opened an investigation and made arrests.

Coalition-building continues after the 27 October election, in which Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats and neither side reached 61. United Torah Judaism is the pivotal holdout, and Likud has weighed not recognising the results.

3. Lebanon and Syria.

Israel's 16 November strike hit the Janta area; Lebanese state media reported no casualties, and the IDF said the shipment likely came from Iran. Hezbollah acknowledged the explosives were its own.

The IDF said on 15 November it demolished a Hezbollah tunnel in southern Lebanon, where Israeli forces hold a self-declared zone about 10 km deep. Hezbollah refuses to disarm, so the US-sponsored framework calling for gradual Israeli withdrawal is stalled.

Middle East Monitor says Israel abducted two Lebanese brothers on 16 November (unconfirmed, single outlet, partisan source). Such incidents feed the argument that the ceasefire is one-sided.

Damascus and the SDF say integration is due by year-end, with forces from Afrin and Aleppo's Sheikh Maqsoud in military training. Enab Baladi asks what remains outstanding, which is the main test of Syria's territorial unity.

Syria and Jordan are moving towards a higher coordination council after a civilian airstrike crisis, and a Lebanese delegation discussed refugee returns in Damascus. Both show Damascus normalising regional ties.

4. Iran.

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said on 11 November that no talks are under way and that Hormuz stays shut until seven demands are met. Foreign Minister Araghchi has said the strait could reopen within two days if they are, and that indirect talks run via mediators including Qatar.

Iran's UN ambassador Gholamhossein Darzi urged the US on 14 November to end its aggression, lift the maritime blockade and give guarantees (Tehran Times, state-aligned). Tehran presents sanctions relief and guarantees as preconditions for any nuclear talks.

Iran says it will not concede on its nuclear programme even if the US accepts its Hormuz proposal, per Iran International (opposition-leaning). A June US-Iran memorandum committed both sides to negotiate a "final deal", but the sequencing is disputed.

The IRGC said at least three ships hit mines in Hormuz on 14 November, which the US military disputes. CENTCOM says it has redirected 150 merchant vessels and disabled five since June.

Araghchi arrived in Muscat on 16 November after an ECO session in Islamabad (unconfirmed, single source). Oman remains a channel for mediation.

5. Iraq and the Kurds.

The PUK withdrew from the Kurdistan Regional Government in early November, after a parliamentary session in Erbil held without it. Analysts warn the region could revert to two rival administrations.

Baghdad postponed the cabinet vote on the 2027 budget; it offers Kurdistan 12.67% and demands 450,000 barrels a day of oil exports, while Erbil wants 14%. Kurdistan24 reports the KRG says it cannot meet the export figure.

A suspected drone attack hit the Khor Mor gas field on 8 November, cutting electricity generation. It is a recurrent threat to Kurdistan's power supply.

Prime Minister Masrour Barzani met a parliamentary bloc delegation on 16 November. The KRG did not say whether the PUK rift was on the agenda.

6. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)

Saudi air defences intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh on 15 November, and two drones near Riyadh and Mecca on 16 November, per the coalition. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed hits on King Khalid airport, which is unconfirmed.

Bahrain's foreign ministry blamed Iran for the drone attacks, and the GCC, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait and Oman condemned them. Oman, usually a mediator, called for restraint.

UAE president Mohamed bin Zayed's security adviser Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed was received by Qatar's emir in Doha. Gulf News says they discussed regional developments, which signals Gulf coordination on mediation.

Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan spoke to his Qatari and Egyptian counterparts on 16 November about de-escalation.

GCC defence ministers approved a joint command centre in Kuwait, and no attack on Kuwait, the UAE or Qatar was reported on 15 to 16 November (unconfirmed, aggregator).

7. Yemen and the Red Sea.

Al Jazeera reports the Yemeni army claims 661 attacks on Houthi positions, killing or wounding 173 fighters, while the Houthis push towards Aden. The Houthis reportedly fired ballistic missiles at Buraiqeh on Aden's western coast.

Critical Threats says the Houthis opened a combined-arms offensive in Lahij on 11 November to cut government forces off on the Red Sea coast. A government-allied group pulled out near Bab el-Mandeb, helping the Houthis consolidate the strait.

No new ship attacks were reported on 16 November, though the Houthis retain the ability to hit vessels in the Gulf of Aden.

Riyadh Air delayed its Jakarta launch to 23 November after Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport. The strikes now cost Saudi Arabia commercially as well as militarily.

8. Egypt and Jordan.

A Cairo court postponed the retrial of Mada Masr editor Lina Attalah to 18 November, on charges of false news and running an unlicensed site. She was fined EGP 3 million on 27 October and the site ordered closed, and the appeal is set for 25 November.

Egypt's intelligence chief met Libya's Saddam Haftar in Cairo on 13 November about the Libyan political process.

Egypt and Jordan joined Saudi Arabia and Qatar in condemning Iran's attacks on Bahrain.

Jordan's State Security Court filed terrorism charges against an Amman attack suspect, and a verdict in the Karak church attack case was expected (unconfirmed, dates inconsistent).

9. Turkey.

Erdogan told an AKP student gathering in Ankara on 16 November that Turkey is entering a "new era" and regaining a prominent international place. It continues his argument that Turkey is a central actor.

Erdogan pledged on 15 November that Turkey will "never abandon" the Cyprus cause, on the 43rd anniversary of the Turkish Cypriot declaration.

At the TRT World Forum on 6 November he called the international system "bankrupt", citing Gaza and the West Bank.

10. North Africa (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco)

US UN ambassador Mike Waltz is visiting Lebanon and Morocco from 15 to 17 November, with Morocco's autonomy plan for Western Sahara and the Abraham Accords on the agenda. No readout had appeared.

Morocco World News says Algerian President Tebboune renewed threats against Morocco in a speech (partisan Moroccan outlet).

A Moroccan lawyers' club opened a confidential hotline for Tindouf camp residents who want to return (Hespress, Moroccan outlet).

In Libya, a Tripoli court sentenced seven former security personnel to death on 13 November for killing two women. The 4+4 framework signed on 30 August remains the main political track.

Tunisia and Algeria: Quiet, nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

11. Defence and security.

Three US carrier groups are now in theatre, and CENTCOM says it has redirected 150 vessels under the blockade.

Iran International says US intelligence flagged a possible Iranian dirty-bomb plot in Washington (unconfirmed, single source). Vice President Vance said there would be no "forever war".

Bahrain issued a threat alert and the US Embassy in Manama warned of flight disruption and possible attacks.

The Saudi coalition has stepped up air operations against Houthi positions, per Reuters as relayed by a secondary outlet (unconfirmed).

12. Economy and energy.

Brent traded around 95 dollarson 16 November, down 0.7% per CNBC, as recovering Middle East exports bolstered surplus expectations. Sources differ on the exact print, and the IEA expects a Q4 surplus.

OPEC+ meets on 29 November to reassess output. Goldman expects Brent to average 88 dollarsin 2027, against EIA's 104 dollarsfor November.

Saudi stocks fell 0.7% and Qatar's 1.8% around 15 November.

Houthi attacks on Saudi airports, ports and energy infrastructure have intensified, including claimed drone strikes on East-West Pipeline pumping stations (Houthi claim, unverified).

13. Diplomacy and outside powers (US, EU, Russia, China)

Washington reportedly plans tighter financial sanctions and trade limits on Iran to secure a phased Hormuz deal that would gradually ease the blockade.

Saudi Foreign Minister Faisal bin Farhan pressed EU ministers in Brussels for naval escorts and a broader Aspides mandate before the 16 November Foreign Affairs Council (single source, Saudi-linked site). The outcome was not found.

Chinese envoy Zhai June was scheduled to visit Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia in November. Beijing backs Iranian sovereignty but says it does not endorse attacks on Gulf states.

Putin met Iran's Rezaei in Moscow on 5 November, and Lavrov spoke with Araghchi. Moscow positions itself as a broker.
