Houthis close on Aden as Hormuz mine war, a third US carrier and Baghdad's militia showdown test Trump's restraint.

1. Houthis close on Aden as Saudi-backed forces abandon the Bab al-Mandeb coast.

What happened. Government-aligned forces in Yemen have withdrawn from Dhubab and the coast near the Bab al-Mandeb to reinforce Aden, the government's de facto capital. Al Jazeera reports the pull-back. AFP via France 24 cites military sources placing Houthi forces about 60 km from Aden on Sunday, after they seized two districts in Lahj and positioned themselves at the al-Kharaz camp in al-Madariba. Yemeni military officials quoted by the Washington Times say Houthi troops reached as far as Qawah, a port town about 16 miles west of Aden. Accounts differ: Al-Monitor, citing Reuters, places the most forward elements around Khor Omairah, about 100 km west of Aden.

The Saudi-backed Southern Armed Forces described the move as a "redeployment" from Dhubab airport and surrounding areas. One officer quoted there welcomed it as a way to also relieve the Houthi siege of Taiz. The Houthi-run Saba agency says government forces were pushed out after their rear supply lines were cut. Government forces are digging tunnels and building sandbag defences around Aden, according to the Japan Times/AFP.

On Monday a Saudi royal decree replaced the commander of the coalition's Joint Forces: Lt. Gen. Fahd bin Hamad Al-Salman replaces Gen. Fayyad bin Hamed Al-Ruwaili. In parallel, the coalition said it intercepted two Houthi drones aimed at Makkah and Riyadh.

Background. This is the culmination of a year in which the anti-Houthi camp lost cohesion and momentum. In January, Saudi-backed forces moved on Aden after the flight of STC leader Aidarous al-Zubaidi, and Riyadh took direct charge of the southern military architecture. The informal Saudi–Houthi truce unravelled in July when the Houthis declared a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia. In mid-September the Houthis took Mocha and islands in the strait. PLC chief Rashad al-Alimi's October counter-offensive, announced on 4 October, has visibly failed. Reuters already had the Houthis within 80 km of Aden on 2 November, their closest since 2015. Houthi drone campaigns against Saudi airports and the East-West pipeline have meanwhile emptied Riyadh's main airport. Cirium data cited by Modern Diplomacy show 4,071 of 4,736 scheduled flights at King Khalid International cancelled between 8 and 25 October, and Air India has extended its Riyadh/Dammam suspension to 30 November.

Analysis. The southward withdrawal concedes the Bab al-Mandeb shoreline to the Houthis, the Red Sea counterpart to Iran's grip on Hormuz. For the first time since the war began, the Iran-aligned "axis" has positional leverage over both of the Gulf's maritime exits at once. That changes the arithmetic of the US–Iran war. Any US escalation against Iran now risks a simultaneous Red Sea shutdown that would also hit Egypt's Suez revenues and the alternative routes around Hormuz that Gulf exports now depend on. For Riyadh, the command change reads as an admission that the October offensive failed. The Washington Institute sees the reshuffle as a possible policy shift, either towards deeper direct involvement or towards a negotiated retrenchment. The likely losers are the PLC's credibility, the southern secessionist project and the UAE's residual network in the south. The Houthis gain leverage both in any regional settlement and against Saudi Arabia directly. Egypt pledged in October to take "any measure" to defend Saudi Arabia against Houthi attacks, and how far Cairo goes is now a live question.

What to watch. Whether the Houthis try to take Aden or settle for encircling it. Whether Riyadh escalates its air campaign or quietly reopens channels via Oman. Any Egyptian deployment. Fresh Houthi strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure, which moved Brent 3.3% on 12 November.

2. Hormuz: more mine claims, a third US carrier and the strike-or-squeeze dilemma.

What happened. The IRGC Navy says two more oil tankers hit naval mines while using a southern route Tehran has not authorised, and that six tankers caught fire in such incidents on Sunday and Monday. State-aligned Tehran Times carried the IRGC version. US Central Command has repeatedly rejected such reports and says the main lanes are clear of mines. Independent verification of the casualties is still lacking. The IMO has recorded 136 security incidents involving vessels in Hormuz and the wider region between 1 March and 11 November. A US official told Al Jazeera the USS Theodore Roosevelt has arrived, joining two other carriers in CENTCOM.

Other developments over the same days.

On 14 November the US said it shot down two IRGC drones heading for Bahrain. Tehran claimed it hit a US early-warning radar there in retaliation for an attack on a telecoms tower in Hormozgan. Qatar was among the states condemning the IRGC's claimed targeting of radar positions at Bahrain's Riffa air base.

Vice President JD Vance told Fox News the war "won't be a forever war" and will not last even two years, while insisting he and Trump are "totally aligned".

CNN, citing seven US officials, reported new intelligence that Iran is developing plans to target senior administration officials with a "dirty bomb" or chemical device in Washington. Officials described this as option-building rather than an imminent plot.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran faces "one of the fastest economic collapses" in history, projecting a 25% contraction as the pressure campaign widens beyond the naval blockade.

Background. The US and Israel launched the war on 28 February. A ceasefire in April left a US naval blockade of Iranian ports (formally announced on 13 April) facing an Iranian closure of Hormuz to all but approved vessels. Since September, Tehran's diplomacy has centred on Araghchi's "seven-day roadmap", conveyed through Qatar, under which Hormuz would reopen within a week in exchange for a region-wide ceasefire. Before the 3 November midterms, Gulf officials said Washington had told them to prepare for a short, intense round of strikes as early as 4 November. No large-scale resumption has been confirmed since 6 November, and Trump's signals of restraint drove Brent to its steepest weekly fall since 2020.

Analysis. Tehran's "unauthorised route" framing matters. The IRGC is not trying to stop all traffic, which would invite a US response and alienate China and India. It is asserting a licensing regime over the strait: who passes, on which corridor and at what fee. Iran reportedly lets Indian and Chinese ships through against transit fees and Qatari ships for free. Each "mine strike" on a bypass route is a message to insurers and flag states that only Iran's corridor is safe. Washington's answer is to squeeze, not strike: a blockade plus financial warfare, with carriers as deterrent. Vance's "no forever war" line is aimed at a domestic audience after the midterms. The CNN dirty-bomb leak cuts the other way, building a threat case that could justify renewed strikes. Gulf states are the most exposed. Bahrain has absorbed renewed IRGC drone fire, flights across the Gulf remain disrupted, and Qatar's economy contracted 11.8% year on year in Q3. Markets are, for now, betting on restraint and on Gulf exports rerouted around the strait.

What to watch. Any CENTCOM confirmation or rebuttal of specific vessel names. Lloyd's/JWC war-risk pricing. Whether Pakistan's and Qatar's mediation produces a US response to the roadmap. Whether Trump raises Iran with Xi and Putin in Shenzhen on 19 November.

3. Baghdad versus Kataib Hezbollah at Jurf al-Sakhar, with Tehran mediating.

What happened. On Monday, Iraqi authorities ordered the army to enter Jurf al-Sakhar (Jurf al-Nasr) in Babil, Kataib Hezbollah's (KH) closed stronghold since 2014, and reportedly set a Friday deadline for the group to vacate. KH said on Sunday it would not give up the area or its weapons and denied the oil-smuggling allegations that triggered the crisis. Al Arabiya, cited by Iran International, reported that an Iranian delegation arrived in Baghdad to defuse the standoff. A KH security official warned PM Ali al-Zaidi that he would bear "full responsibility" for any strike on the group's positions, while investigators backed by Counter-Terrorism Service, army and Federal Police units entered parts of the area.

Background. The confrontation began with an Al-Hurra investigation on 9 November alleging oil smuggling through Jurf al-Sakhar. A government committee bringing together the interior, justice and agriculture ministries and the PMF Commission then began fieldwork there. Al-Zaidi, a Coordination Framework nominee, won a confidence vote on 14 May with only 14 of 23 ministers approved. The US-led coalition completed its exit in September, and KH announced a suspension of military activity afterwards. Disarmament deadlines for the factions have repeatedly slipped.

Analysis. Jurf al-Sakhar is a symbolic test as much as a military one: a strip of Iraqi territory where the state has had no presence for over a decade. Al-Zaidi has picked a fight framed around corruption and oil rather than "resistance", which is the frame that gives him the widest political cover, including from Shia rivals of KH. Tehran's immediate dispatch of mediators shows its priorities. With its economy under blockade, Iran needs Iraq as a financial lung and cannot afford an intra-Shia war that could pull Iraq into the US orbit. Its intervention also exposes the limits of Iraqi sovereignty. As the Jerusalem Post's analysis argues, the outcome could reshape Iranian influence in Iraq. The most likely result is a face-saving arrangement in which KH withdraws from parts of the area under a PMF fig leaf, which Shafaq reports senior figures are already discussing.

What to watch. The Friday deadline. Whether other factions (Nujaba, Kataib Sayyid al-Shuhada) close ranks with KH. Any drone incidents near energy sites. Sirens sounded at the Khor Mor gas field after a drone overflight, the same field whose production was halted by a drone attack on 9 November.

4. Israel: Knesset speaker showdown as Eisenkot tries to turn a parliamentary majority into power.

What happened. The Knesset votes today at 16:00 on replacing Speaker Amir Ohana (Likud) with Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party. Ohana scheduled the vote after 61 MKs signed a request. The Eisenkot-led bloc says it will use control of the Knesset to begin repealing laws that weakened the attorney-general and then reverse changes to the Judicial Selection Committee. Yashar and Likud negotiators met in Herzliya on Sunday for "unity" talks with little visible progress. Separately, the Times of Israel's live coverage on 16 November reported that an ex-PMO employee who told police about election-day vote-buying denied any conspiracy.

Background. In the 27 October election the pro-Netanyahu bloc won 53 seats against 48 for the Jewish anti-Netanyahu parties, with Arab-led parties holding the balance. President Herzog gave Eisenkot the mandate after 67 MKs recommended him, but those recommendations do not add up to a coalition. Liberman rules out dependence on Arab parties, UTJ is uncommitted, and Haredi conscription remains the core obstacle. Netanyahu and his allies have pushed claims that the election was "stolen" by Arab parties.

Analysis. Taking the speakership would give the anti-Netanyahu majority agenda control and the ability to legislate against the judicial overhaul even while Netanyahu stays caretaker PM. That is a "cohabitation" scenario with few precedents in Israel. As Manara Magazine puts it, there is a majority against Netanyahu but no coalition to replace him. The foreign-policy implication is continuity by default. A caretaker Netanyahu still directs the Iran, Lebanon and Gaza files, and Eisenkot (a former chief of staff) has not signalled a softer line on Iran or Hezbollah. The Arab parties' role as kingmakers, and the "stolen election" narrative aimed at them, is the most dangerous domestic fault line. It feeds directly into how Palestinian citizens of Israel are policed and represented.

What to watch. Today's vote count, in particular whether any Likud partners abstain. Whether Ra'am formally backs an Eisenkot government from outside. UTJ's decision. Any court challenges to the speaker vote.

5. Gaza's second post-"ceasefire" winter: deaths in the rubble and in the tents.

What happened. A mass funeral was held on Sunday for Palestinians whose remains had lain under rubble for years. Palestinian officials say 70 skeletal remains were found beneath homes in northern Gaza, at least 10 of them children. Gaza's Health Ministry says Israel has killed 1,650 Palestinians since the October 2025 "ceasefire" (same source). WAFA reported a man shot dead at displacement tents in Jabalia and three people, including a child, wounded by gunfire inside a school south of Khan Younis. Israel said it killed Hamas commander Samir Abu Younis; Al Jazeera's weekly round-up dates the drone strike in al-Mawasi to 14 November. The Palestinian Centre for Human Rights reports drones dropping explosive-laden boxes on remaining houses in al-Zaytoun. A nine-month-old, Kinan Ahmed Raqiq, died of hypothermia. UNICEF says flooding had affected more than 106,000 people by 9 November and that about 950,000 displaced people live in flood-prone areas.

Diplomatic pressure is mounting, at least in words. The foreign ministers of the UK, Australia, Canada and Italy jointly urged Israel to allow "immediate, rapid and unimpeded" humanitarian access. Germany's Johann Wadephul called for significantly more aid before winter. EU Commissioner Hadja Lahbib said dual-use humanitarian supplies are let in only once a week. Jordan evacuated 12 children and 11 relatives in the 38th group of its medical corridor.

Background. The Trump-chaired Board of Peace announced a 15-point Hamas disarmament roadmap on 30 July, under which governance passes to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza and an International Stabilization Force (ISF) supports it. As of October, Hamas had agreed to hand weapons to the NCAG but rejected "disarmament" while Israel continues strikes and has not withdrawn, and no ISF had deployed. Troop pledges have been minimal, with commitments from Kosovo and Albania.

Analysis. Gaza has become a frozen conflict in which the "ceasefire" mostly governs the tempo of killing rather than ending it. Israel's political limbo means no government in Jerusalem has the mandate to move to phase two, and the Iran war has drained Washington's bandwidth. European statements are notable for who signed them: the Anglo-Italian-Commonwealth group rather than the full EU, which still cannot agree on leverage. They remain humanitarian appeals, not conditionality. The 8,000 people estimated to still be under the rubble are a reminder that the war's death toll is still rising retrospectively.

What to watch. Any EU Foreign Affairs Council language on aid conditionality. Israeli crossing schedules. Further hypothermia deaths as winter storms arrive. Whether a new Israeli government constellation changes the Board of Peace timetable.

6. West Bank: deadly night near Jericho in a settler-annexation hotspot.

What happened. A 60-year-old Palestinian woman was killed and about 10 people wounded by gunfire in Duyuk al-Tahta, west of Jericho, overnight Monday to Tuesday, according to Palestinian official TV and hospital officials. Palestinian accounts blame settlers. The IDF says the violence followed a car-ramming attack on Israelis near Jericho, after which troops opened fire to "remove the threat", and did not say whether the woman was among those shot. The Palestinian foreign ministry called it an escalation of settler attacks (TRT World, Turkish state broadcaster).

Background. On 4 November around 30 armed settlers linked to the Nachala movement took over eight Palestinian homes in the same neighbourhood, displacing at least 70 people. Palestinian officials say the purchase documents were forged.

Analysis. Jericho has historically been one of the West Bank's calmer, PA-administered enclaves. Settler "purchases" inside Area A/B fringes, backed by force, mark the spread of annexation-by-facts beyond the usual hotspots of Hebron, Masafer Yatta and the Jordan Valley. The dispute over whether the woman was shot by settlers or soldiers illustrates the growing operational overlap between the two, a pattern Western governments have tried to address through settler sanctions without effect on the ground.

Israeli troops raided Kfar Chouba, a Sunni village, seizing two brothers. Drone strikes were reported near Mayfadoun and shelling near Zawtar al-Sharqiya.

Implementation of the US-brokered security arrangement remains stalled. Israeli troops stay in parts of the south, and Naim Qassem demands an end to strikes and a full withdrawal before any weapons discussion.

Context: More than 4,300 people have been killed in Lebanon since Hezbollah joined the Iran war on 2 March. Aoun's strategy of a security deal plus negotiated disarmament is hostage to Israel's continued occupation.

Syria.

International.

EU foreign ministers discussed Syria in Brussels, with support for advancing an EU–Syria Association Agreement. UN officials briefed the Security Council that progress is "real but fragile".

Saudi–Syrian defence cooperation and a Saudi loan package (over 400 million dollars in development loans, per the CFR tracker) deepen Riyadh's patronage of Damascus.

Local.

SDF integration is due by year-end. Enab Baladi details what remains, mainly civilian governance, after Mazloum Abdi declared military and administrative integration complete in August.

Al-Sharaa has replaced ministers and governors amid fuel-price protests and economic hardship.

Context: Damascus wants an Israel security deal conditional on withdrawal from the buffer zone. Meanwhile Israeli strikes on Hezbollah's Syrian supply lines show Syria is still a corridor in other people's wars.

Jordan.

International.

Amman's medical corridor from Gaza continues (38th group). Jordan was among the states hit by Iranian strikes earlier this year, according to GCC statements.

Local.

An ex-convict, Muhannad Al Hmouri, faces terrorism charges over the 5 November stabbing of a police corporal outside the Sri Lankan embassy. The PM vowed an "iron fist", and raids in Karak, Irbid and Amman suburbs followed.

Context: The Soufan Center asks whether Jordan is "back in the crosshairs" of jihadists. The Karak church massacre and the Amman stabbing suggest IS recidivism exploiting regional chaos.

Iraq (incl. Kurdistan Region)

International.

An Iranian delegation is in Baghdad to defuse the Jurf al-Sakhar standoff. Washington is watching a test of al-Zaidi's autonomy after the US troop exit.

Local.

The army has been ordered into Jurf al-Sakhar with a reported Friday deadline. Kataib Hezbollah refuses to leave.

Kurdistan: sirens sounded at Khor Mor after a drone overflight. The 9 November attack had cut the Region's power capacity by 70 to 80%. PM Masrour Barzani is meeting parliamentary blocs as the PUK boycott of the KRG continues.

Context: Iraq is trying to convert the US withdrawal into state consolidation over the militias, but its energy system, especially in the KRI, remains hostage to drone sabotage and Kurdish factionalism.

Iran.

International.

The IRGC claims new tanker mine strikes on "unauthorised" routes. A third US carrier has arrived. CNN reports US intelligence about Iranian "dirty bomb" contingency plans.

Araghchi is working the Pakistan and Qatar channels and says neighbours back Iran's Hormuz plan. Tehran is also mediating in Baghdad.

Local.

The rial trades at about 2.66 to 2.69 million to the dollar on the open market versus 1.92 million officially. Air defences were activated in Isfahan with no stated cause.

Executions continue apace: at least 691 this year per IHR, with more death sentences for January protesters. Note: NCRI material on fuel unrest is from an opposition source.

Context: Under Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, the Islamic Republic is betting it can outlast a blockade by monetising control over Hormuz while repressing a population hit by the currency's collapse.

Turkey.

International.

The Turkey–Qatar mutual defence pact extends Ankara's new web of Gulf-South Asian security agreements after the August Saudi–Pakistan pact. Ankara says it is open to Egypt joining.

After hosting COP31 in Antalya, Erdoğan held bilateral meetings with nine leaders, including Dbeibah and Pakistan's PM (TRT World, state broadcaster).

Local.

The central bank raised its 2026 inflation forecast to 29.5% citing the Iran war. BDDK chief Kavcıoğlu offered his resignation.

The İBB (İmamoğlu) mega-trial continues. A related trial resumes on 19 November and the next main hearing is reportedly on 30 November.

Context: Turkey is positioning itself as a security provider to Sunni Gulf states hedging against US unreliability, while domestic repression of the CHP continues.

Saudi Arabia.

International.

A royal decree replaced Joint Forces commander Gen. Fayyad Al-Ruwaili with Lt. Gen. Fahd bin Hamad Al-Salman as the Houthis advance on Aden.

The coalition intercepted Houthi drones aimed at Makkah and Riyadh. Houthis claim strikes on King Khalid airport and East-West pipeline pumping stations on 13 November (claims compiled by House of Saud, a partisan site, and unconfirmed by Riyadh).

Local.

Investment licences more than doubled in Q3 and the Tadawul closed flat at 10,264. Carriers including Etihad (to 19 November) and Air India (Riyadh/Dammam to 30 November) keep services suspended.

Context: Riyadh is caught between the Iran war's economic fallout and a collapsing Yemen policy. Vision 2030's hub ambitions (Riyadh Air, KKIA) are directly under Houthi fire.

United Arab Emirates.

International.

Abu Dhabi hosted a third round of US–Ukraine–Russia talks, keeping the UAE's mediator brand alive. Talks with Israel on resuming flydubai flights continue.

Local.

HRW documents the arbitrary detention of at least five Shia foreign residents since February. Abu Dhabi's environment chief says the emirate was "ready" to protect nature from Iran-war threats.

Context: The UAE is doubling down on its role as a diplomatic switchboard while securitising its Shia minority. Its Yemen footprint, by contrast, is visibly receding as the Houthis advance.

Qatar.

International.

Qatar signed the mutual defence agreement with Turkey (QNA, state agency). It condemned the IRGC's claimed drone strike on Bahrain's Riffa air base and remains a key US–Iran channel for Tehran's roadmap.

Local.

Q3 real GDP contracted 11.8% year on year, improving from −15.7% in Q2. The IMF sees −15.6% for the year.

Context: Qatar is paying the war's heaviest economic price among the Gulf states, and is hedging with Ankara because the US security umbrella has not kept its LNG flowing.

Kuwait.

International.

No major external moves in the last 48 hours. Kuwait remains among the states struck by Iranian missiles during the war and covered by GCC condemnations.

Local.

Juvenile cases fell 58% in nine months. KIPCO reshuffled executive management, and Qatar is guest of honour at the 49th Book Fair. The emir travelled to the US for medical checks earlier this month.

Context: Kuwait is quiet domestically, but the emir's health and the war's fiscal strain are long-run risks.

Bahrain.

International.

The US says it downed two IRGC drones heading for Bahrain. Iran claims it hit a US early-warning radar, and the US embassy issued a security alert. Bahrain requested the 13 November UNSC meeting on maritime security.

Local.

A court reportedly sentenced 64 people to 15 years to life over an alleged IRGC-linked group (TVG brief; unconfirmed). Iran International reports Iranian students protested outside Bahrain's Tehran embassy over Shia clerics.

Context: Bahrain is the Gulf state where the Iran war most directly becomes a domestic sectarian security question, as the host of the US Fifth Fleet with a Shia-majority population.

Oman.

International.

Oman remains a quiet channel. Araghchi's diplomacy has routed through Muscat before. An Oman–India naval exercise and the visit of Uzbekistan's president were on the agenda.

Local.

The Shura Council opened its new session on 15 November, and Oman Chamber elections on 23 November feature 110 candidates. Muscat hosted the Global Plan Forum on tolerance.

Context: Oman keeps its neutral brand, but Lufthansa's absence from Muscat and EASA airspace bulletins show the war's spillover costs.

Yemen.

International.

Saudi command change, coalition interceptions of Houthi drones aimed at Saudi cities, and international concern over Bab al-Mandeb shipping.

Local.

Houthis are reportedly within about 60 km of Aden, with government forces fortifying the city and redeploying from the west coast, partly to relieve Taiz. The Houthi-run Saba agency presents the withdrawal as a rout.

Context: For the first time since 2015 the Houthis threaten the government's last urban stronghold, and their control of the Bab al-Mandeb coast hands Iran's axis a second maritime chokepoint.

External powers and regional organisations.

United States: Three carriers in theatre, a blockade, and Bessent's economic war against Iran; Vance's "no forever war" message. AFRICOM is active in Libya and Washington is engaged in the Sahara.

EU/UK/France/Germany: EU FAC on Syria and Gaza; the UK in the four-FM Gaza statement; Germany's Wadephul pressing on aid. France's FM had earlier warned that renewed US–Iran escalation "would create nothing but losers".

Russia/China: Both will be at the Shenzhen table with Trump on 19 November. They call for an end to military operations against Iran, and they vetoed a Gulf-backed Hormuz resolution in April.

India: Protecting its seafarers by banning Indian crews on Hormuz routes, with the navy quietly escorting Indian ships.

GCC/Arab League/OIC: The GCC's "attack on one is an attack on all" language from June has not produced collective action. Instead, members are building bilateral pacts with Turkey and Pakistan. No new collective GCC, Arab League or OIC statements surfaced in the window.
