Here is your Middle East briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

First, Iran. The US and Iran war has moved into a decision window. Trump says a choice between renewed strikes, continued economic pressure and a deal is coming soon. US Central Command is reported to have finished planning for a short, intense campaign against nuclear, missile and drone sites and Iran's energy sector, and US officials point to the new moon on the 9th, which favours air operations. On Friday Trump said US forces destroyed a newly built anti-aircraft site on Qeshm Island, calling it a minor strike. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claim two more tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, a claim no one has independently confirmed.

Second, the Qatar channel. Qatar denies that talks have collapsed, and Trump spoke with the emir. Tehran has sent a nuclear proposal through Doha, but only to be discussed once its preconditions are met: sanctions lifted, frozen funds released, fewer US forces in the region, and, in other reporting, reparations and an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon. The outline under discussion trades a reopened Hormuz, within two days, for sanctions relief, with nuclear talks to follow.

Third, Israel. President Herzog is due to hand Gadi Eisenkot the mandate this evening after parties with 67 members of parliament recommended him. But he has no obvious majority. He has ruled out relying on Arab parties, and the ultra-Orthodox want draft exemptions he opposes. Likud is contesting the result, helped by a Shin Bet probe into alleged election interference, though the election committee found no evidence of fraud. A fresh election early next year remains possible.

Fourth, the Saudi and Houthi war is getting deadlier. Saudi Arabia says it has intercepted at least 15 missiles and drones this month, including launches at Riyadh today. The Houthis claim six attacks on the 7th, on oil sites, Riyadh's main airport and a National Guard camp, which Riyadh has not confirmed. Houthi health officials say Saudi-led strikes near Sanaa killed 33 people, mostly women and children.

Fifth, a regional realignment around Riyadh and Ankara. Saudi Arabia signed a defence agreement and more than 403 million dollars in loans with Damascus, and Syria is weighing joining the Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani defence pact known as the Mecca pact. Egypt reopened its embassy in Tripoli after 12 years, a sign it is hedging on Haftar in Libya.

What to watch. This evening, Eisenkot's formal mandate and the ultra-Orthodox response. On the 9th and 10th, the new moon window for possible US strikes on Iran, or a deal announcement, and any US written reply through Doha. On the 10th, the Iraqi cabinet takes up the 2027 budget, and the US and China trade truce expires, though it is expected to be extended. Also watch for any Saudi admission of damage to Aramco or Riyadh airport, and whether the Palestinian elections on the 28th are postponed.
