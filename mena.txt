Here is your Middle East briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Qatar buys two weeks for Iran diplomacy as Hormuz attacks, Houthi salvos on Riyadh and a Gaza hospital strike test every truce.

Five things mattered most today. First, Qatar told G7 ministers in Paris it has bought "at least two weeks" before any US strike on Iran, but the gaps on sequencing (the blockade, enrichment, frozen funds) are still wide, and Tehran publicly denies it is negotiating at all. That window is now the region's main timetable. Second, it is unclear whether a deal-or-strike decision belongs to Washington or Jerusalem: Israeli media report a "shouting match from hell" between Trump and Netanyahu over restarting the war, while Netanyahu is a caretaker whose bloc is eight seats short and whose challenger, Gadi Eisenkot, broke off talks with Likud on Wednesday. Third, the Hormuz shipping war has not paused for diplomacy. There was another reported mine strike, a damaged ADNOC vessel inside UAE waters and a "possible attack" on a Saudi-flagged tanker, and Brent climbed back above 92 dollars. Fourth, the Saudi–Houthi war is escalating in both directions. Riyadh intercepted missiles over its capital for a second straight day, the G7 explicitly backed Saudi self-defence, and the Syria–Saudi military axis (training, a possible "Mecca Pact" accession) is redrawing Levant–Gulf alignments. Fifth, a dawn strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, which killed a surgeon among at least six dead, landed on the same day the Israeli PMO said it "backs" Trump's Gaza plan. It is a sharp illustration of how far implementation of phase two remains from the rhetoric.

1. Qatar buys a "two-week" window on Iran, but the sequencing gap is the whole problem.

What happened. Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, met Trump and Vice President Vance at the White House on Monday. On Tuesday he made an unscheduled stop at the G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris, where, according to diplomatic sources, he told ministers Doha had secured "at least two weeks" in which the US would hold off striking Iran. He was also blunt that the parties remain far apart. He cited "disagreement over the sequencing of steps," including lifting the US naval blockade, the nuclear file and the unfreezing of Iranian funds. A side session in Paris brought together Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the E4 (France, Germany, Italy, UK) on de-escalation and maritime navigation. Qatar's foreign ministry had earlier rejected reports that the PM had declared the talks failed, calling them "false and misleading". It has also urged both sides to accept a "good" deal rather than chase a "perfect" one.

The package Doha carried has been reported, but neither government has confirmed it. Iran would reopen Hormuz and stop attacks on Gulf tankers. The US would lift its naval and economic blockade and release roughly 8 dollarsbillion in Iranian funds held in Qatar. Critical Threats, citing reporting on the Qatari compromise, describes a nuclear component: Iran would cap enrichment at 3.67% and dilute its highly enriched uranium domestically, and would "open" the strait after IAEA inspectors are admitted. Trump said on Truth Social that progress had been made and that Iran is "militarily, effectively already defeated." He also left further strikes on the table. Iran International reports Trump predicting on Wednesday that the war would "end soon" while seeking "total victory".

Tehran's public line points the other way. Al Jazeera reports Iran denying it is in talks with the US and insisting that Hormuz stays shut until its demands are met. Mohsen Rezaei, now Iran's most prominent security voice, called talk of negotiations "a pure lie." He vowed that "not a single particle" of enriched material would leave the country, and warned that US bases from the Gulf to Europe would not be safe in a new war. Middle East Monitor, meanwhile, quotes Iranian officials saying a "clear response" has been sent and the ball is in Washington's court. Iran's "seven conditions" have never been published officially. Composite lists include.

lifting the blockade.

guaranteed passage through Hormuz.

removal of unilateral sanctions.

exemption of oil exports.

release of frozen assets.

retention of nuclear material inside Iran.

a formal end to the war "on all fronts", explicitly including Lebanon.

Background.

The war began with US–Israeli strikes on 28 February. Ali Khamenei was killed on the first day, and the Assembly of Experts elevated Mojtaba Khamenei in early March. An April ceasefire collapsed on 8 July after Iranian attacks on commercial shipping. Since then the US has run a naval blockade that Rubio frames as targeting only Iranian shipping. On 7 November CENTCOM extended it overland with "Task Force Iron Gate," a roughly 20-person cell in Tampa meant to choke Iran's land borders and Caspian routes. Tehran dismissed it as "word play". The US midterms, which Tehran read as a rebuke to the war, are the other backdrop. Mojtaba Khamenei warned on 4 November that anyone expecting a change in US hostility is "seriously mistaken".

Analysis.

The dispute is not over the content of a deal, which on paper (blockade for strait, funds for inspections) is a recognisable trade. It is over sequencing and verification under conditions of zero trust. Each side's leverage is the instrument the other wants removed first: the blockade for Washington, the strait for Tehran. That makes a simultaneous, staged exchange the only plausible design, which is exactly what Qatar is trying to choreograph. The winners from a deal would be Doha, which turns mediation into strategic insurance and unlocks its own LNG exports, Gulf exporters and Asian importers. Israel's caretaker government is the most obvious loser, since a deal that leaves enriched material inside Iran is precisely what Netanyahu has fought. That is the context for the reported Trump–Netanyahu clash (Story 2). For Iran, the regime's maximalism is partly domestic: with the rial at record lows and executions of protesters accelerating, conceding "the strait first" would look like capitulation. The Rezaei–Araghchi division of labour (hard-line denial of talks; conditional openness) also reads as deliberate signalling rather than incoherence.

What to watch.

How long the "two-week" window lasts. Counting from the 9 to 10 November meetings, it points to late November. Also watch whether the IRGC actually stages its promised unveiling of an underground "missile production city". Finally, watch whether Washington drops its rhetoric about Pickaxe Mountain, the deeply buried site near Natanz that was untouched in both rounds of strikes.

2. Trump and Netanyahu reportedly clash over restarting the Iran war, as Israel's coalition puzzle hardens.

What happened. Ynet, citing sources, reported a weekend phone call in which Netanyahu pushed to resume strikes on Iran while Trump wanted to give diplomacy another chance. One source called it "a shouting match from hell." Netanyahu's office dismissed the report as "fake news" and "complete nonsense," and a senior Israeli official said it was "very much not a difficult conversation". Separately, Channel 12 and Axios reported that in a post-election call Trump told Netanyahu to stop obstructing the Gaza plan and to advance it regardless of who forms the next government.

At home, the 26th Knesset was sworn in on Tuesday; 46 of its 120 members are new. Netanyahu used his address to call for a "unity government" while accusing the Arab parties Ra'am and the Joint List of seeking "the destruction of the Jewish state". Eisenkot, who holds President Herzog's mandate on the back of 67 recommendations, called the speech "delusional, divisive, full of hate and shameful." His Yashar party cancelled the Wednesday meeting with Likud negotiators. Eisenkot's bloc nonetheless agreed nominations for key Knesset posts, with the next speaker expected to come from Yashar. United Torah Judaism is negotiating separately with Eisenkot and Lieberman and demands a draft-exemption law before any government is formed.

Background.

In the 27 October election, Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats and Eisenkot's 48, with Arab parties at a record 19. Likud has 27 seats, Yashar 21, Bennett's Together 12, the Joint List 11, the Democrats 8, Ra'am 8 and Yisrael Beytenu 7, on 77.9% turnout. Eisenkot's deadline is 6 December, extendable to 20 December. Netanyahu stays caretaker prime minister until a government is sworn in.

Analysis.

The arithmetic is unforgiving. Without either the Arab parties (in some form of outside support) or the Haredim, Eisenkot cannot reach 61. Each option carries heavy costs: the first is politically toxic on the Israeli right, and the second reopens the conscription question that helped bring down the last coalition. Eisenkot's floated "national emergency government" is a way of postponing that choice, and Netanyahu's "unity" call is an attempt to split Eisenkot's bloc. The Iran dispute with Trump matters because a caretaker prime minister with an incentive to keep Israel on a war footing faces a US president with an incentive to bank a deal. If the reported clash is accurate, Washington is no longer simply deferring to Israeli threat perceptions on Iran. That would be the most significant shift in the US–Israel relationship since February.

What to watch.

Whether Yashar–Likud contacts resume. Whether Eisenkot moves to an outside-support formula with Ra'am. And Israeli leaks timed to the Qatar window.

3. The Hormuz shipping war intensifies in UAE waters, mines and a Saudi-flagged tanker.

What happened. On Tuesday, ADNOC Logistics and Services said one of its vessels was damaged in an attack inside UAE territorial waters in the Gulf. It named neither the ship nor an attacker. Separately, UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unidentified projectile about 19 nautical miles northeast of Lima, Oman, at 13:38 UTC the same day. On Wednesday Iran's navy said a tanker had caught fire after striking a mine in the eastern part of the strait, where Iran targets vessels transiting "without authorisation." The US military says the strait has been cleared of mines. India's shipping minister said the Saudi-flagged tanker NCC Fajr, with three Indian crew, was involved in a "possible attack" while transiting, and that all crew were safe.

Markets reacted immediately. Brent had slipped below 90 dollarson Tuesday for the first time since August, touching 89.16 dollarsbefore settling at 91.49 dollars. On Wednesday it rebounded to about 92.50 dollars, with WTI near 86.70 dollars. Earlier in the week the Qatar proposal had knocked Brent down 3.6% to 94, dollarsextending a 16% slide the previous week.

Background.

By one tracker's count the strait has been effectively closed for 256 days. The IRGC has claimed a series of mine strikes this month, four tankers on 4 November and two on 6 November, which CENTCOM has repeatedly rejected as inaccurate. Some reports count 37 attacks on ships in October and ten so far in November.

Analysis.

These attacks are coercive signalling timed to the negotiating window. They raise the cost of the status quo for Gulf producers and Asian buyers without crossing the threshold of a mass-casualty event that would justify US strikes. An attack inside UAE territorial waters is a notable escalation in geography. It tells Abu Dhabi that its waters are not a safe harbour, and it adds weight to the UAE's bypass investments. ADNOC is accelerating a second crude pipeline to Fujairah, which Argus expects as early as Q2 2027, and ADNOC Gas is studying an LNG terminal outside the strait. The markets are pricing two scenarios at once: a deal, at roughly 90, dollarsand an unconstrained tail risk. The 89 dollarstrough shows how much a deal is already priced in, and therefore how sharply prices could gap up if the Qatar window collapses.

What to watch.

Formal attribution of the ADNOC incident by Abu Dhabi. Any CENTCOM statement on the NCC Fajr. War-risk insurance quotes for Gulf loadings.

4. Saudi–Houthi war escalates: Riyadh under fire, the G7 takes sides, and Damascus edges into a Gulf military role.

What happened. On Wednesday the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three Houthi cruise missiles aimed at the Riyadh area, two drones heading toward Mecca and the south, and two missiles fired westward. It was the second consecutive day of interceptions over the capital. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree denied targeting Mecca and claimed a drone strike on an oil pumping station at Yanbu, the Red Sea terminus that lets Saudi crude bypass Hormuz. Riyadh has not confirmed that strike. Riyadh residents received phone shelter warnings for the third straight day on Tuesday. On Sunday a Houthi strike damaged an Aramco building in the King Abdullah Financial District.

The G7 joint statement from Paris (9 to 10 November, published Wednesday) condemned Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia, affirmed Saudi Arabia's right to self-defence, demanded the release of 70 detained UN staff and called on "all actors, including Iran" to stop arming the group. The US Embassy in Yemen dismissed Houthi "blockade" claims as "just slogans." It cited UN data showing 272 commercial ships unloaded at Hodeidah, Salif and Ras Isa between January and September.

On the ground, the government's month-old "Dawn of Yemen" offensive continues. Yemen's government and the Houthis traded heavy-loss claims on Wednesday, and the army claimed a surprise attack at al-Mudaribah. Yemen Monitor reports government claims of recaptured "strategic positions" and 127 Houthi fighters killed or wounded in a day.

Syria's role.

AP reported Syria preparing to send roughly 600 special forces personnel to Saudi Arabia, and Yemeni officials told AFP that up to 3,000 could deploy. Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani insists there is no deployment to Yemen and that the 4 November Saudi–Syrian defence agreement covers training and rebuilding Syria's army. Two Syrian officials say a decision to join the Saudi–Turkish–Pakistani "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement" has been taken but not announced.

Background.

Fighting resumed over the summer. The UN counts more than 1,600 killed and 300,000 displaced. A Houthi strike on King Khalid International Airport on 10 October killed 13 people, the deadliest attack on the kingdom in over a decade.

Analysis.

The Houthis are acting as Iran's most autonomous and most deniable lever. Hitting Yanbu threatens the one Saudi export route that does not run through Hormuz, which ties the Yemen front directly to the Iran negotiation. For Riyadh, the war is now a fiscal problem as well as a security one. The 9-month deficit (SR244bn) has already overshot the full-year target, and Q3 GDP contracted 3.8%. That helps explain the search for partners. A Syrian contribution, even if limited to trainers, would be a striking reversal. A Damascus that was Iran's land bridge two years ago is becoming a junior partner in a Sunni defence architecture anchored by Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad. The Soufan Center warns that Houthi advances could eventually threaten the government's Aden base. That cuts against the government's offensive narrative and is worth tracking.

What to watch.

Any confirmation of Syrian personnel in the kingdom. Whether Washington folds the Houthis into the Qatar package; Iran's "all fronts" condition implies it might. Damage assessments at Yanbu.

5. Gaza: a strike on Nasser Hospital, flooding, and a plan everyone "backs" but no one implements.

What happened. An early-morning Israeli strike hit the surgery department, on the second floor of the Al-Yasin building at Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis. It killed at least six people, including surgeon Dr Rezik Abushira, and wounded about 20, according to Gaza Civil Defence. The IDF and Shin Bet said the strike targeted "a senior terrorist and additional Hamas terrorists" hiding in the hospital. Outlets differ on whether the strike happened late on 11 November or early on 12 November. Al Jazeera's tally put deaths across Gaza at 13 for the day and quoted hospital staff saying this was the seventh time Nasser had been hit. The same day, the Israeli PMO said it backs Trump's plan and called Hamas's refusal to disarm "the principal obstacle" to reconstruction.

Humanitarian picture.

The first winter storm flooded about 70,000 tents, according to CARE. A sand-dune collapse in Deir al-Balah killed four children and a woman on 8 November. The Board of Peace, Trump's own body, said on 9 November that Israel's steps were "insufficient" and demanded immediate delivery of shelter and drainage materials. Israel replied that 8,200 trucks carrying 91,000 tents had entered, and that "Hamas doesn't get to escape responsibility every time it rains". Gaza's Health Ministry counts 1,622 Palestinians killed since the October 2025 ceasefire.

Background.

Crisis Group's latest report finds that phase two has not started. There has been no disarmament, no further Israeli withdrawal, no International Stabilisation Force and no deployment of the technocratic NCAG. The Board of Peace's 2.45 billion dollars initial reconstruction plan is conditioned on progress toward disarmament.

Analysis.

The Board of Peace publicly criticising Israel is new and matters. It suggests Washington's patience with obstruction, which the Axios reporting attributes to Netanyahu's domestic politics, is thinning just as an Eisenkot-led government becomes possible. But neither the US nor the Board has a mechanism that compels either Israel or Hamas. Hospital strikes justified by Hamas presence will continue to erode the international legitimacy of the plan's security track.

What to watch.

Whether Blair and the Board press a winter "humanitarian surge" benchmark. Whether the ISF troop-contributor conversation revives once Israel has a government.

Context: The war's centre of gravity has shifted to Kordofan, and drones are its signature. Diplomacy has been eclipsed by the Gulf crisis.

Mauritania.

International.

Murphy Oil signed two offshore PSCs (5 November), and Mauritania holds the Arab Group presidency in New York for November.

Prefects in Chami and Tmeimichatt warned residents away from the Sahara border after MINURSO's exit.

Local.

Parliament approved the second Ould Djay cabinet 145 to 21. The "bloggers abroad" interrogations concluded without announced charges.

Context: Nouakchott is quietly pivotal. It is a neutral neighbour exposed to a de-policed Sahara and a new frontier for Western energy capital.

Israel.

International.

A reported Trump–Netanyahu clash over restarting the Iran war, which the PMO denies, and US pressure to implement the Gaza plan.

Israel rejected Erdoğan's "ecocide" charge as a "campaign of lies".

The Board of Peace publicly called Israel's winter aid effort "insufficient".

Local.

The Knesset was sworn in. Eisenkot froze talks with Likud, and UTJ is demanding a draft law first.

Arrests followed the Lod "sovereignty march" brawl, and Mordechai David was summoned for questioning.

A Jerusalem Post op-ed urges the new Knesset to tackle urgent national needs before a coalition deal.

Context: Israel is between governments in the middle of a war. External pressure is rising just as the domestic capacity to make decisions is at its weakest.

Palestine (Gaza and West Bank)

International.

The Trump–Netanyahu Gaza-plan pressure, the PMO's statement that it "backs" the plan, and the Board of Peace's public rebuke over flood response.

The AU, Arab League and OIC called for withdrawal and ICJ accountability.

Local.

The Nasser Hospital strike killed at least six, including a surgeon, with 13 dead across Gaza per Al Jazeera's count.

In the West Bank, a 14-year-old was killed in Burqa and settlers rampaged in Beita.

Abbas's October decree postponed legislative elections, which had been set for 28 November, to 11 September 2027, eroding PA credibility further.

Context: Phase two of the ceasefire has stalled at every step. Violence is "low-intensity" only by comparison with 2023 to 25, and Palestinian politics is frozen from Ramallah to Gaza.

Lebanon.

International.

The eighth round of Israel–Lebanon talks was suspended over withdrawal and verification. Israel conditions withdrawal on nationwide Hezbollah disarmament.

UNIFIL's mandate ends on 31 December, with most troops out by mid-2027. France and Italy are developing post-UNIFIL support options, and Gebran Bassil has floated a plan of his own.

Iran's "all fronts" condition explicitly ties Lebanon to any US–Iran deal.

Local.

The ISF arrested five Hezbollah members in the north, and Salam declared the "era of impunity" over.

The Dabousieh–Aboudiyeh crossing between Akkar and Homs reopened on 9 November.

Israeli drone strikes continue in the south, including a fatal one in Qabrikha.

Context: The Lebanese state is testing its authority against Hezbollah more openly than at any point since 2005, but under Israeli occupation of the south and without a UN buffer from January.

Syria.

International.

The defence cooperation deal with Riyadh and 403 million dollars in Saudi loans, alongside reported preparations to send trainers or special forces to Saudi Arabia and a possible Mecca Pact accession. Damascus denies any Yemen deployment.

Analysis of what Mecca Pact ambitions mean for regional security.

Local.

ANHA reports calm restored in Wadi Barada after clashes the previous day, rising transport costs in Hasakah after the end of diesel subsidies, and new limits on Kurdish-language schooling.

Enab Baladi documents ministry pricing in which subsidised diesel at 115 SYP/L sits alongside a 175 SYP standard tier.

Context: Al-Sharaa is converting Syria's neutrality in the Iran war into Gulf patronage. At home, subsidy reform and the Kurdish question are the stress points.

Jordan.

International.

Ties with Damascus are described as "at an all-time high" despite Jordan's lethal strikes in Daraa, and the joint investigation committee continues.

Local.

The King promoted and decorated Captain Ananzeh with the Order of Valour (10 November).

The security narrative remains anchored in the Karak church attack and the Nassib border reopening.

Context: It was a quiet 48 hours. Amman's priority is a hard-edged counter-IS policy toward southern Syria without rupturing its new partnership with Damascus.

Iraq (incl. Kurdistan Region)

International.

Zaidi is acting as a US–Iran message carrier, after his Tehran trip to meet Qalibaf and the reshuffling of border crossings.

Al-Mada reported a leaked US plan with Iraq as a transit route into Khuzestan; this is unconfirmed.

Local.

The oil-smuggling probe in Jurf al-Nasr challenges Kataib Hezbollah. Fuad Hussein says several factions accept weapons controls, and Kataib Sayyid al-Shuhada denies it.

Kurdistan: the 2027 budget is stalled over oil quotas, a Khor Mor attack probe is under way, and the KRG cabinet urged the PUK to return to sessions.

Context: With the US-led coalition gone, Baghdad's leverage over the militias is being tested by money (smuggling, budgets) rather than by force.

Iran.

International.

Tehran publicly denies talks while sending a "clear response." Rezaei threatens US bases up to Europe, and the G7 commits to sustained pressure.

Iranian officials raise COP31 and wartime "ecocide" in climate forums.

Local.

Executions of protest detainees continue, with three Karaj protesters executed and announced on 8 November.

The rial is at record lows and bus drivers in Yazd have been striking since 31 October, the latter reported by the NCRI, a partisan exile group.

The IRGC is promoting underground weapons production.

Context: A wartime regime under Mojtaba Khamenei is betting that coercion at sea and at home outlasts Washington's patience.

Turkey.

International.

As COP31 host, Erdoğan used the platform to call Gaza an "ecocide" and to demand climate finance from developed states. Israel was excluded.

Turkey is a party to the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, which Syria may join. Egypt is contesting the 2019 Turkey–Libya maritime MoU.

Local.

The 108th İmamoğlu hearing was held, the CHP congress case was adjourned to 18 November, and rights groups are pressing the ECtHR.

Disinflation has stalled near 30% because of imported energy costs, and Governor Karahan presents the inflation report today.

Context: Ankara is using the war to raise its diplomatic profile while lawfare against the main opposition continues at home.

Saudi Arabia.

International.

The G7 backs Saudi self-defence against the Houthis. The Saudi foreign minister joined the Paris Qatar–E4 session.

Riyadh is building a Syrian security partnership through the defence deal and loans.

Local.

A second day of missile interceptions over Riyadh, debris in the Makkah region, and Houthi claims on Yanbu.

The fiscal deficit overshoots and GDP is contracting.

Context: For the first time since Vision 2030 began, war is directly reshaping Saudi fiscal and security priorities.

United Arab Emirates.

International.

The US nominated Jeff Colyer as ambassador. The UAE now supplies an estimated 15 to 20% of India's energy imports.

The National's podcast frames the ship attack, Israel's new Knesset and the ambassador nomination together as the week's Gulf story.

Local.

An ADNOC L&S vessel was damaged in territorial waters.

DP World's overland rerouting of more than a million containers shows the logistics adaptation under way.

Context: Abu Dhabi is hardening its economy against a long Hormuz disruption rather than betting on a quick deal.

Qatar.

International.

PM Al Thani's White House–Paris shuttle secured a reported two-week reprieve.

The LNG force majeure runs through year-end, and Orlen's termination is the first customer exit.

Local.

The Amir reconstituted QatarEnergy's board by Amiri Decision No. 42 of 2026 (5 November), which looks like personnel preparation for a post-war restart.

Guterres praised the late Father Amir's mediation legacy.

Context: Doha's mediation is also economic self-rescue: its main export depends on the strait reopening.

Kuwait.

International.

The US Embassy alert of 7 November warns of possible Houthi attacks and airspace closures, and the Level 3 advisory stands.

Local.

The Amir travelled to the US for routine medical checks (6 November). The army held the "Desert Flash 1" drill, Kuwait unveiled its first locally made light ammunition, and authorities ordered cooperatives to secure food stocks and prepare 95 shelters.

About 6 billion dollars in debt repayments falls due next year as borrowing rises.

Context: There was little new in 48 hours. Kuwait is in quiet civil-defence mode and borrowing more as oil revenue comes under pressure.

Bahrain.

International.

No major foreign-policy development in the window. Bahrain's earlier role as drafter of the UN Hormuz resolution remains its main profile in the crisis.

Local.

The government is reviewing a proposed 50% rise in foreign-worker fees and has rejected extending personal loans to ten years over debt concerns. The Bahrain Chamber has named 147 committee members.

Context: Little happened. Fiscal consolidation through expatriate fees is the domestic story to watch ahead of December's GCC summit, to which the King has invited the Qatari Amir.

Oman.

International.

During Sultan Haitham's Beijing state visit (2 November), Xi said the Gulf "should not become an arena for great-power rivalry," and both sides called for safe navigation through Hormuz.

Local.

Sohar and Duqm are emerging as Hormuz-bypass hubs. Sohar handled 52m tonnes in H1, up 52%, though ports were hit by Iranian drones earlier in the war.

The 10 November UKMTO incident occurred off Lima, in Omani waters.

Context: Oman's neutrality has given way to Qatar's mediation lead, but its geography makes it the strait's economic winner.

Yemen.

International.

The G7 condemned Houthi strikes and called on Iran to stop arming the group. The US Embassy says the "blockade" claims are slogans.

Local.

The "Dawn of Yemen" offensive continues on the Lahj, al-Jawf and Marib fronts, with the Nation Shield Forces active in Lahj. Taiz is under siege.

The Soufan Center warns the PLC's Aden base could be threatened if Houthi counter-advances succeed.

Context: Yemen has gone from a frozen conflict to a hot, two-front war: the Houthis strike Saudi cities while government forces try to break into Houthi territory.
