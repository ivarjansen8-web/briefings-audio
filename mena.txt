Here is your Middle East briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

Bahrain–Qeshm tit-for-tat strains the Iran truce on the eve of Shenzhen as Houthis close on Aden.

Day 264 of the US–Israel war on Iran brought the sharpest Gulf exchange in weeks. The IRGC sent drones at a US radar on Bahrain's Sheikh Isa air base, the third Iranian drone attack on Bahrain in five days. US forces answered by striking missile launch sites on Qeshm Island, and Bahrain and Kuwait spent Wednesday morning intercepting further waves. This comes as Qatar and Oman try to keep a frozen ceasefire from collapsing and Trump prepares to raise Iran at Thursday's first Trump–Xi–Putin summit in Shenzhen. Two second-order shocks may matter more over time. In Yemen, the Saudi-backed front has broken: Houthi forces are reported about 60 km from Aden after government troops left the Bab al-Mandab coast, and Riyadh has replaced its joint-forces commander. In Iraq, Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi is in a standoff with Kataib Hezbollah over Jurf al-Sakhar, with a Friday 20:00 deadline, and has postponed a trip to Beirut over it. In Gaza, the Board of Peace told the UN Security Council the ceasefire could collapse without immediate action. In Israel, the anti-Netanyahu bloc took the Knesset speakership with Arab-party votes, a first institutional sign that power is shifting. Iraq restarting the Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline after 12 years is the clearest sign yet that the Hormuz crisis is redrawing the region's energy map.

Top stories.

1. Bahrain–Qeshm–Kuwait: the Gulf front of the Iran war reignites.

What happened. On Tuesday 17 November the IRGC said it had carried out a "precision operation" with drones against the US-operated radar at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa air base. It called the strike a response to "repeated violations of Iranian airspace by US military drones". US Central Command said the drones were intercepted without damage to US equipment or personnel. Washington then struck what it described as Iranian missile launch sites on Qeshm Island, the Hormuz island that anchors Iran's anti-ship and drone architecture. On Wednesday morning Bahrain sounded two danger alerts in quick succession and intercepted further attacks. The Bahrain Defence Force accused Tehran of a "systematic hostile approach" and of "criminal attacks". Kuwait's army said its air defences were engaging "hostile" drones. Kuwait did not name the origin, but Iran said it had hit a US base in Kuwait. Earlier reports put the 17 November Kuwaiti interceptions over Ali Al Salem air base. Risk consultancy Solace Global logs Iranian drone attacks on US assets in Bahrain on 13, 15 and 17 November. The 13 November strike was framed by Tehran as retaliation for a US hit on a telecoms tower in southern Iran.

The regional reaction came quickly and followed bloc lines. The GCC secretary-general condemned the attacks and urged Security Council action. Saudi Arabia, Jordan's Ayman Safadi, Egypt and the UAE all issued statements of solidarity with Manama.

Background. The war began on 28 February 2026 with US–Israeli strikes that killed Ali Khamenei in the opening hours; Mojtaba Khamenei succeeded him. An indefinite US ceasefire was declared on 21 April. Since then the conflict has cycled through blockade, a June memorandum and a renewed blockade. The GCC says its members have been targeted by more than 7,000 missiles and drones since 28 February. On 9 November Trump said renewed major combat was "quite unlikely" and that the war was "effectively over". He also declared a "land blockade" in effect. A third carrier, USS Theodore Roosevelt, entered CENTCOM's area on 15 to 16 November and joined the George H.W. Bush and George Washington.

Analysis. Bahrain is a deliberate choice of target. It hosts the Fifth Fleet, it is the most exposed Gulf state, and its Sunni monarchy has a long history of friction with Tehran. Hitting a US radar there lets Iran claim it is striking American, not Arab, targets. It also raises the cost for every Gulf host of US forces. The pattern is calibrated: drones rather than ballistic missiles, sensor sites rather than personnel, and claims framed as retaliation. This looks like an effort to restore deterrence and coercive leverage before the Shenzhen summit and the Qatari track, not a bid to restart full war. Washington's response is also calibrated. Strikes on Qeshm launch sites, described as self-defence, signal a willingness to hit Iranian territory without returning to the strategic bombing of March–April. Gulf states lose either way. Iran's strikes undercut the "neutral host" posture that Qatar, Oman and to some degree the UAE have cultivated, while the US carrier build-up revives fears that a better-prepared Iran would retaliate harder if Washington escalated. Kuwait's refusal to name Iran is telling. It reflects the instinct of smaller Gulf states to avoid locking themselves into a public casus belli.

What to watch. Whether CENTCOM's targeting moves beyond Qeshm. Whether Bahrain or the GCC brings a draft resolution building on UNSC Resolution 2817 (2026), which already condemned Iranian attacks on GCC states and Jordan. And whether Iran resumes attacks on Kuwait's Ali Al Salem or on Saudi soil.

2. Diplomacy under fire: Doha, Muscat, the "dirty bomb" story and Shenzhen.

What happened. On 17 November Qatar's foreign ministry spokesman Majed al-Ansari announced a meeting with Iranian officials "in the coming hours" to discuss proposals already on the table. He warned that the escalation in the Gulf poses a "grave danger" to the chances of a US–Iran deal and urged both sides to "avoid any escalation". The same day, FM Abbas Araghchi met Omani FM Badr al-Busaidi in Muscat on maritime security and "efforts to revive diplomatic channels". UKMTO reported that a tanker was attacked twice off Oman overnight, with an engine-room fire and crew unaccounted for. Reporting from a Saudi-aligned outlet says the Muscat meeting ended without agreement on Hormuz.

In Washington, CNN cited seven US officials saying intelligence indicates Iran is planning a dirty-bomb or chemical attack in Washington. Speaking in the Philippines, Trump said Iran would "pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before". Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said such an attack "would cause an all-out war". The White House added that there were "no credible threats to the homeland" at this time. Tehran called the reports a "fabricated media narrative" meant to build a case for "further aggression".

Background. Iran says Hormuz will reopen only after Washington meets seven conditions, which have never been published as one official list. Versions cited by Iranian officials include lifting the naval blockade and sanctions, releasing frozen funds, ending hostilities on all fronts including Lebanon, a formal end to the war, a reduced US military presence and recognition of an Iran–Oman shipping regime in the strait. Trump met Qatar's prime minister on 9 November. One mediation idea gives Iran access to interest on roughly 6 dollarsbillion frozen in Qatari banks in exchange for confidential IAEA inspections. Meanwhile the IAEA reported on 13 November that it still cannot verify Iran's damaged sites and called the information gap "a matter of proliferation concern".

Analysis. Three things are converging. First, US politics has shifted. After a sweeping Democratic midterm win, Trump played down another round of strikes, and Defense Secretary Hegseth survived an impeachment attempt. That strengthens Tehran's case that time is on its side. Second, the dirty-bomb leak serves the opposite function. Whatever the intelligence is worth, it builds a domestic justification for escalation if Washington chooses it, and it pressures Beijing and Moscow before Shenzhen. Third, the Trump–Xi–Putin summit on Thursday 19 November in Shenzhen, on the APEC sidelines, will have the Iran war on its agenda. A White House official says Ukraine will not be raised, and nuclear arms control is the other headline item. Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen beforehand. One analysis floats China and Russia as co-guarantors of a Hormuz reopening built on the June memorandum. That is speculative but structurally plausible: China takes roughly 90% of Iran's oil exports, and Moscow has offered to take custody of Iran's enriched uranium. For Gulf mediators, this would shift influence from Doha and Muscat toward great-power guarantors, which is a mixed blessing.

What to watch. Any readout from Doha's meeting with Iran. Whether Shenzhen produces language on Hormuz or the "seven conditions". Iranian behaviour in the 48 hours around the summit: a lull would signal Tehran is banking on Beijing, while continued strikes would signal it doubts the channel.

3. Yemen: the anti-Houthi front cracks and Riyadh changes commanders.

What happened. Houthi forces have advanced through districts of Lahij to the al-Kharaz camp area at al-Madaribah, about 60 km from Aden, the interim seat of the internationally recognised government. The territorial claims could not be independently verified. Middle East Eye puts the front about 70 km from Aden's northern entrance. Government forces pulled back from the Red Sea coast and Bab al-Mandab to defend Aden, and the Houthis say they have taken the evacuated areas. According to Yemen Monitor, the withdrawal followed a Houthi flanking operation at al-Mudharaba on 10 November that threatened supply lines. On 16 November Saudi Arabia replaced joint-forces commander Lt Gen Fahad bin Hamad Al Salman with Chief of General Staff Lt Gen Fayyad Al Ruwaili, who holds both posts. Two Western diplomats tied the change to the battlefield setback.

The air war has escalated alongside. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed 81 to 88 Saudi air and missile strikes in 24 hours on Sanaa, Saada, Taiz, Lahij, al-Jawf and Marib, and Houthi-run Saba reported 17 killed and 39 wounded. These are unverified one-sided figures. A Khaleej Times headline attributed strikes on Houthi facilities to the coalition. The Houthis say they hit pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West (Petroline) pipeline with drones. The coalition says it downed drones heading for Riyadh and Makkah. On 10 November Riyadh said the pipeline had been targeted but operations were unaffected. Yemen's transport ministry ordered airports and land crossings to activate emergency plans. Separately, the ICRC facilitated the release of 600 government detainees on 16 November.

Background. Fighting between the government and the Houthis has intensified since July, particularly in Taiz, which overlooks Bab al-Mandab. The anti-Houthi camp has never had a unified command. Analysts again point to that fragmentation.

Analysis. This is the most important structural change in Yemen since the 2022 truce. If the Houthis hold the western coast down to Bab al-Mandab, an Iran-aligned actor controls both maritime chokepoints flanking Arabia, Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab, at a time when Saudi and Emirati crude is being rerouted to Yanbu and the Red Sea precisely to avoid Hormuz. Attacks on the East-West pipeline therefore target Riyadh's main hedge against the Iran war. Replacing the commander signals that Riyadh recognises the scale of the setback. Combining the joint-forces post with the chief of staff role points to centralisation rather than disengagement. For the US and Europe, Red Sea security is back on the table just as Suez traffic was recovering (see Egypt). Watch the UAE-aligned southern forces. Their willingness to defend Aden and their relationship with Riyadh will decide whether the city holds.

What to watch. Fighting at al-Madaribah and the Aden approaches. Any Houthi move on Mocha or the Bab al-Mandab islands. Saudi strikes on Hodeidah and Sanaa industrial sites. Shipping advisories for the southern Red Sea.

4. Iraq: al-Zaidi's Jurf al-Sakhar gamble, and a pipeline that bypasses Hormuz.

What happened. PM Ali al-Zaidi has postponed his Beirut visit indefinitely because of the standoff with Kataib Hezbollah (KH) over Jurf al-Sakhar, its fortified stronghold south of Baghdad. A security source gives the withdrawal deadline as 20:00 on Friday 20 November. Two counter-terrorism brigades and a rapid response force have been deployed around the area. If no deal is reached, the plan is a limited entry rather than a full assault. A KH commander told AFP the group will not leave. KH wants any handover delayed to 30 June 2027, the deadline for bringing all weapons under state control. The Shia Coordination Framework failed to agree a plan at a late-Monday crisis meeting. Kurdish and US-funded outlets report that Zaidi and Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani have agreed that KH will withdraw "soon" and hand over to a joint PMF–state force. The terms are unconfirmed (Kurdistan24; Alhurra, US-government funded). The confrontation began with an oil-smuggling probe: on 15 November Zaidi sacked nine security and administrative officials.

The same day, Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair announced that the federal Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline was pumping crude again after a 12-year shutdown caused by ISIS attacks. It starts at 120,000 b/d and aims for 500,000 b/d, and Khudair says it allows crude to be sold without discounts.

Background. Zaidi's cabinet was only completed in mid-October, five months after he took office. Hormuz disruption has sharply cut Iraq's southern oil revenue, and Iraq has absorbed spillover attacks such as the 8 to 9 November drone strike on Khor Mor. That strike cut about two-thirds of the Kurdistan Region's power, and Iraqi authorities later blamed Iran-backed militias.

Analysis. Reuters calls the planned crackdown Zaidi's "riskiest move yet". Iraqi governments have avoided entering Jurf al-Sakhar since 2014. The site is a test of whether a weakened Iran will trade militia autonomy for regime stability in Baghdad. The reported Qaani channel suggests Tehran prefers a managed retreat to an open fight that would split the Coordination Framework. Washington, Ankara and the Gulf all benefit if the state reasserts control over smuggling corridors. The pipeline fits the same logic. Northern exports through Turkey reduce Baghdad's exposure to Hormuz and to Iran-aligned actors in the south, and they increase Turkey's leverage over Iraqi energy.

What to watch. The Friday deadline. Whether KH puts on further armed displays. Coordination Framework cohesion. Any US or Israeli action against KH that could hijack the process.

5. Gaza: the Board of Peace warns the Security Council the ceasefire may collapse.

What happened. On 17 November the Board of Peace, the body created under UNSC Resolution 2803 to oversee the US-brokered ceasefire, told the Security Council that the deal could collapse without full implementation. It warned that civilians face "another winter in tents and rubble". The report blames both Israeli strikes and Hamas military activity. It says Israel has still not allowed the board's technocratic administration to take up its work in Gaza, and that the lack of approvals has delayed the International Stabilization Force, which is the precondition for Israeli withdrawal, Hamas decommissioning and reconstruction. The underlying document is S/2026/418. On the same day, Gaza civil defence and hospitals said Israeli forces killed six people, including a six-year-old boy. The IDF said it struck "several terrorists" and was "not aware" of a child being killed. A UN spokesperson said an Israeli strike hit very close to a UN-led fuel convoy in Rafah. Haaretz data cited by Democracy Now show declining food deliveries and shortages of medical supplies, tents and repair materials (Democracy Now, progressive outlet).

Background. Hamas announced it had dissolved its Gaza government in July in favour of the Cairo-based technocratic committee chaired by Ali Shaath. On 31 July Trump announced a disarmament agreement. The sequencing dispute was never resolved: Israel demands disarmament before withdrawal from the "Yellow Line", while Hamas demands withdrawal first. Chatham House noted Israeli control had grown from about 53% to nearly 70% of the Strip.

Analysis. The report is unusually direct in naming Israeli obstruction. It comes from a body chaired by Trump's own initiative, which gives it more weight in Washington than UN-agency criticism. It lands during an Israeli interregnum. Netanyahu heads a caretaker government while Eisenkot negotiates, and any successor coalition will inherit the decision on approving the ISF and the technocratic committee. The Arab League, OIC and African Union jointly called for the second phase to be implemented on 5 November. Egypt and Qatar have mediated the process from the start. Without the ISF the plan cannot advance, and a Gaza with no reconstruction and slow, permanent Israeli entrenchment becomes the default.

What to watch. Security Council follow-up. Statements from troop-contributing candidates for the ISF. Whether Eisenkot's coalition guidelines address Gaza administration.

6. Israel: the anti-Netanyahu bloc takes the Knesset with Arab votes.

What happened. On 17 November Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party was elected Knesset speaker 66 to 52, defeating Likud's Ofir Katz and replacing Netanyahu ally Amir Ohana. His majority depended on the Arab parties. Katz said he would not want to be elected "with the votes of terror supporters". Smotrich had offered support only if no Arab MK became deputy speaker. Eisenkot replied that "it is customary to distinguish between the Knesset and the coalition".

Background. In the 27 October election, Eisenkot's bloc won 67 seats including 19 from Arab parties, against Netanyahu's 53. President Herzog gave Eisenkot the mandate on 8 November with six weeks to form a government. Eisenkot has ruled out formally including Arab parties and has opened unity talks with Likud. The election was shadowed by election-eve arrests of Ra'am activists for alleged vote-buying. The attorney general is examining whether intelligence passed through the Prime Minister's Office was used to trigger the arrests.

Analysis. The speakership controls the legislative agenda, so the anti-Netanyahu camp can now shape legislation even while Netanyahu remains caretaker. Several outlets note it could pass key bills with Arab support before any coalition is sworn in. The episode also normalises Arab parties as parliamentary kingmakers, which is a long-term shift in Israeli politics, even as Eisenkot keeps them out of the cabinet. The pressure for a Likud–Yashar unity deal is now greater, because Netanyahu's leverage is declining. The foreign-policy stakes are concrete: approval of the Gaza ISF, Lebanon withdrawal terms, the US-mediated Syria track, and coordination with Washington on Iran.

What to watch. The deputy speaker allocations. Likud–Yashar negotiating rounds. Any legislative move to set an election-integrity inquiry.

Kahana was elected speaker 66 to 52 with Arab votes, and Likud called it illegitimate.

Likud–Yashar unity talks continue under negotiators Ofir Katz and Chili Tropper.

The attorney general is examining whether PMO-sourced intelligence drove election-day arrests of Ra'am activists.

Context: Israel is between governments. The Netanyahu era is ending through parliament before it ends in the cabinet, and every major foreign-policy file is on hold until Eisenkot's coalition question is settled.

Palestine (Gaza and West Bank)

International.

The Board of Peace report (S/2026/418) warns of collapse. The ISF has not deployed, and Israel has not let the Cairo-based technocratic committee operate.

The Arab League, OIC and AU earlier demanded implementation of phase two.

Local.

Gaza: six people were killed on 17 November, including a six-year-old, according to local health officials. An Israeli strike landed near a UN fuel convoy in Rafah.

West Bank: settler raids were reported in Deir Dibwan (Ramallah) and al-Lubban al-Sharqiya (Nablus). A woman was killed near Jericho and six were hurt in a vehicle attack in Tuqu' (Palinfo, Hamas-affiliated; SANA).

The PA's decision to postpone elections to September 2027 still stands.

Context: A year after Resolution 2803, Gaza is stuck between ceasefire and reconstruction, and the West Bank's harvest season again exposes settler impunity.

Lebanon.

International.

Waltz pressed for "voluntary" Hezbollah disarmament. Salam wants a post-UNIFIL international presence under a UNSC mandate with Resolution 1701 as the reference.

France is reportedly planning joint patrols and training bases. The EU is planning a separate advisory mission.

Iraqi PM al-Zaidi's visit was postponed because of the Jurf al-Sakhar crisis.

Local.

President Aoun told Hezbollah to hand over its weapons, saying they "serve regional projects" rather than protect Lebanon, and argued that talks with Israel are needed to rebuild the south.

The IDF says it hit a weapons convoy from Syria at Janta and arrested two members of the Hezbollah-guided Resistance Brigades. Israel says it will not withdraw until Hezbollah disarms.

Procedural objections were dismissed in the Dahr al-Baidar case.

Context: Lebanon's leadership is using the clearest anti-Hezbollah language in decades. The state still lacks the capacity, and the international cover, to enforce it before UNIFIL's mandate ends on 31 December.

Syria.

International.

The EU agreed to relaunch the Association Agreement revision, and Commissioner Šuica visited Damascus.

The Israeli strike on a Hezbollah-bound shipment near the border shows Syria remains a smuggling corridor despite Damascus's hostility to Hezbollah.

Al-Sharaa signed a defence cooperation agreement and more than 400 dollarsmillion in development loans with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman on 5 November.

Local.

According to SANA (state media), Israeli raids and shelling continued in western Daraa, and Damascus called for full Israeli withdrawal from the south.

State media also report al-Sharaa ordering tax exemptions for war-damaged businesses.

Context: Damascus is deepening its integration with Europe and the Gulf while Israel's southern "security zone" hardens. The US-mediated security track, last active in August in Amman, has gone quiet.

Jordan.

International.

FM Safadi declared solidarity with Bahrain after Iranian attacks.

Jordan, itself named in UNSC Resolution 2817 as a target of Iranian attacks, continues medical evacuations from Gaza.

Local.

The first municipal sukuk raised 564 dollarsmillion.

The national team beat Australia 2 to 0, a boost in a World Cup year.

Context: Jordan is balancing alignment with the Gulf against Iran with its exposure to Gaza and the West Bank, and is turning to new domestic financing tools.

Iraq (incl. Kurdistan Region)

International.

The federal Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline restarted after 12 years. Turkey gains as a transit hub and Iraq reduces its dependence on Hormuz.

A reported deal between al-Zaidi and Qaani on KH's exit shows Tehran's continued hold over Baghdad's security file.

Local.

The Friday 20:00 deadline for KH to leave Jurf al-Sakhar stands. KH refuses publicly, and the Coordination Framework is divided. A PM adviser warned KH that Baghdad won't back down.

KRG President Nechirvan Barzani met Germany's development minister in Erbil. Kurdistan has restored 1,500 MW after the Khor Mor attack, and Erbil–Baghdad revenue disputes are holding up the 2027 budget.

Context: Iraq is using the Iran war to claw back sovereignty over oil and armed groups, and Jurf al-Sakhar is the first real test.

Iran.

International.

Iran exchanged fire with the US over Bahrain and Qeshm. Araghchi was in Muscat and Qatar's channel stays open. Tehran rejects the US "dirty bomb" claims as fabricated.

The IAEA says it cannot account for about 440 kg of highly enriched uranium and calls the gap a proliferation concern. Iran says there will be no inspections until the war ends.

Tehran is looking to Beijing and Moscow for leverage ahead of Shenzhen.

Local.

Petrol rationing has tightened and is causing public anger. A deputy oil minister denies any new decision.

The IMF expects at least a 7.6% contraction in 2026. The rial is around 2.72 million per dollar.

Executions linked to the January protests continue.

Context: Under Mojtaba Khamenei, the regime is betting that coercion at sea and in the Gulf can outlast the blockade before economic collapse forces concessions. Pezeshkian said on 9 November that the leader wants an end to "neither war nor peace".

Turkey.

International.

Restarting Kirkuk–Ceyhan makes Ankara a key route for Iraqi oil around Hormuz.

Ankara hosted both Libyan camps and is active in Beirut, as Bilal Erdoğan's visit shows.

The August Mecca pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan is the backdrop to Turkey's Gulf security role.

Local.

Trustees were appointed to Sözcü TV and its executives detained.

Nine were detained in an operation against a new Kurdish alliance. Öcalan was moved to a villa on İmralı, but DEM says the peace process is stalling.

A capital-markets bill and fund liquidations follow a financial scandal (Daily Sabah, pro-government).

Context: Ankara is gaining from the region's realignment while tightening control at home. Repression of the media and opposition sits uneasily with the Kurdish peace track.

Saudi Arabia.

International.

Riyadh condemned the Iranian attacks on Bahrain.

The Yemen front has collapsed toward Aden, and the joint-forces commander was replaced by Lt Gen Al Ruwaili.

Local.

Houthi drones targeted East-West pipeline pumping stations and the Riyadh and Makkah regions. The coalition reports interceptions.

Etihad flight cancellations to Riyadh and Dammam continue.

Context: Saudi Arabia is absorbing costs on two fronts, Iran and the Houthis. Its Red Sea export bypass is now the target.

United Arab Emirates.

International.

The UAE condemned renewed Iranian aggression against Bahrain, and its foreign minister spoke with Qatar's on regional developments.

Anwar Gargash has said Abu Dhabi seeks a "political off-ramp".

Local.

Etihad extended its cancellations to Saudi airports. ADGM continues to attract European law firms.

Context: Abu Dhabi is trying to remain the region's safe haven while hedging between Washington and de-escalation with Tehran.

Qatar.

International.

Doha announced a fresh meeting with Iran, warned escalation is a "grave danger" to a deal, and continues as the main US–Iran mediator.

Qatar is hosting the ITU Plenipotentiary through 27 November.

Local.

No major domestic developments were reported in the window. The latest notable items are the reconstitution of the QatarEnergy board and the ratification of a border-authorities agreement with Saudi Arabia.

Context: Qatar's role as mediator is stretched to its limit as fighting around Bahrain moves closer to Doha.

Kuwait.

International.

Kuwait intercepted "hostile" drones on 17 and 18 November without naming Iran. Tehran claims it hit a US base in Kuwait.

Local.

The Kuwait Times front page leads on AI and tech investment for diversification. Citizens can now register as ride-hailing drivers.

Context: Kuwait hosts US forces at Ali Al Salem and Arifjan. It is again a target, and its studied ambiguity about attribution is a strategy for avoiding entanglement.

Bahrain.

International.

Bahrain was hit by the third Iranian drone attack in five days, on the Sheikh Isa radar. The BDF accuses Iran of a "systematic hostile approach". The GCC, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Egypt and the UAE expressed solidarity.

Local.

Authorities issued two danger alerts and told citizens to stay calm. Earlier in the war, MPs approved urgent economic and food-security measures in response to Iranian attacks.

Context: Hosting the Fifth Fleet makes Bahrain Iran's preferred pressure point. Its security now depends almost entirely on US and GCC air defence.

Oman.

International.

Araghchi held maritime-security talks with Badr al-Busaidi in Muscat. Iran's conditions reportedly include recognition of an Iran–Oman shipping regime in Hormuz.

A tanker was attacked twice off Oman (UKMTO). Ties with Uzbekistan were upgraded.

Local.

The Shura Council opens a session today. Tougher penalties for vehicle stunts were announced.

Context: Oman's role as Hormuz co-littoral state gives it unusual weight in any reopening formula, but also makes it a target.

Yemen.

International.

The Houthi advance threatens Bab al-Mandab and Red Sea shipping just as Suez traffic was recovering.

Local.

Houthis are reported about 60 km from Aden, and government forces are digging in. Houthi media claim 81 to 88 Saudi strikes in 24 hours and 17 dead.

The Transport Ministry has activated emergency plans at airports and border crossings. Iran's Tehran Times (state-aligned) celebrates that "Sanaa foils Saudi military plans".

Context: This is the biggest shift in Yemen's balance since 2015. Aden's fate will decide whether a two-state reality survives.

External powers and regional organisations.

US: A third carrier is in theatre, US forces struck Qeshm, and the dirty-bomb intelligence was leaked. Waltz's Rabat–Beirut tour combines Sahara recognition with pressure on Hezbollah.

China and Russia: The Shenzhen trilateral is set for Thursday with Iran on the agenda. China is Iran's dominant oil buyer and Russia has offered to take Iran's enriched uranium.

EU/France: The EU is moving on a Syria association deal and new Iran sanctions. France is pursuing a Lebanon force.

India: Repeated attacks on Indian-crewed tankers. New Delhi has barred its seafarers from Hormuz transits since July.

GCC/Arab League/OIC: The GCC condemned the Iranian attacks on Bahrain. The last joint Arab League–OIC–AU statement, of 5 November, focused on phase two in Gaza.
