Here is your Middle East briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

Gulf war reignites as Israel crosses Gaza's Yellow Line and Houthis close on Aden.

Four fronts moved at once in the past 24 to 48 hours, and they are connected. After about two months of relative quiet, the US–Iran war has heated up again in the Gulf: the US struck an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island, Iran answered with drones and missiles at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem base and at Bahrain, and tankers are being hit again near Hormuz. In Gaza, Israeli tanks crossed the "Yellow Line" into Hamas-held central Gaza for the first time since the October 2025 truce, a day after the Board of Peace told the UN Security Council the ceasefire could collapse. In Yemen, the Saudi-led coalition says it hit 60 Houthi targets while Houthi forces sit roughly 60 to 100 km from Aden and now hold the coast at Bab al-Mandeb. That puts both of the region's maritime chokepoints under pressure at the same time. Two domestic stories also matter for the long run. In Iraq, the al-Zaidi government has set a Friday deadline for Kataib Hezbollah to leave Jurf al-Sakhar, the biggest test yet of Baghdad's plan to disarm the militias. In Turkey, a court gave Ekrem İmamoğlu a new prison term and political ban, which further tilts the field in Erdoğan's favour. Watch for the outcome of Qatar's and Oman's mediation, the IAEA Board in Vienna, and whether Israel's incoming Eisenkot-led bloc pulls policy on Gaza and Iran in a different direction from the caretaker Netanyahu cabinet.

Top stories.

1. The Gulf war heats up again: Qeshm, Ali Al Salem, Bahrain and Hormuz.

What happened. The US says it struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday 17 November, in response to an Iranian attack earlier that day on a US radar installation in Bahrain. On Wednesday the Iranian army said it had targeted US drone and missile installations at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base, after Kuwait reported that its air defences were countering hostile drones. Bahrain said it repelled another Iranian attack early Wednesday. Bahrain, home to the US Fifth Fleet, has now faced four Iranian attacks in six days. The Washington Times reports these are the first direct strikes on Kuwait in more than two months. Iran's official IRNA, quoting a regional official, said the Qeshm strike caused no serious damage. The US military said no US personnel were hurt in any attempted Iranian attack this week. At sea, UKMTO received a delayed report of a tanker hit in the engine room by an unidentified projectile north of Khasab, in Oman's Musandam. A fire on board was put out. Reports disagree on whether one crew member is missing. Washington has reissued security warnings to Americans in the Gulf.

Background. The war began with US–Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026. An April ceasefire and a June US–Iran memorandum of understanding did not stop fighting around Hormuz. An interim arrangement broke down in July, and the US has since enforced a naval blockade of Iranian ports. Tehran, for its part, insists that ships coordinate their passage with its armed forces. The current round started around 13 November, when the IRGC claimed a strike on a US early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for an attack on an Iranian telecom tower in Sirik, Hormozgan. CENTCOM said it shot down two drones and denied that any radar was destroyed. The 17 November attack on Bahrain came right after a Bahraini court sentenced 64 people, mostly Shia clerics, to life for "colluding with Iran". Iranian signalling and Bahraini domestic security are clearly feeding each other here.

Analysis. This is a calibrated tit-for-tat, not a return to the high-intensity phase of March. Iran chooses targets that are tied to US forces (a radar, an air base) but located on Arab soil. That keeps up costs and fear in the GCC without hitting US personnel directly, which would invite a major US escalation. The move is coercive: Tehran wants Gulf capitals to lobby Washington to ease the blockade, and it wants to show that US basing remains a liability for its hosts. The GCC response so far has been condemnation, not distance from Washington. The GCC Secretary-General Jasem Albudaiwi called on the UN Security Council to act, and the UAE condemned the "renewed hostile attack" on Bahrain. The "dirty bomb" story raises the political temperature in Washington. CNN, citing seven officials, reported intelligence that Iran is exploring a radiological or chemical attack on senior officials in Washington. Trump, speaking in Manila, said the US takes it "very seriously". Officials stressed that no attack was imminent, a senior official told Fox the intelligence was "not new", and Tehran called the report "baseless". Expect this story to be used to justify more strikes, much as the WMD framing has been used before. The winners in the short run are the hardliners on both sides. The losers are Gulf economies already under fiscal strain (see Economy) and the Qatari and Omani mediation tracks.

What to watch. Whether the US answers the Kuwait strike. Whether Iran extends its targeting to Saudi or Emirati facilities. A reported Israeli Channel 12 item, which we have not independently verified, says Israel is weighing action against Iran "in the coming days". Also watch the IAEA Board of Governors in Vienna this week, where the agency again says it cannot verify the location of Iran's roughly 440.9 kg of 60%-enriched uranium.

2. Gaza: Israeli armour crosses the Yellow Line for the first time since the truce.

What happened. On Wednesday 18 November, Israeli tanks and engineering vehicles pushed west of the Yellow Line into Hamas-held central Gaza near Nuseirat and Bureij. Witnesses told Reuters that some tanks reached Salahudeen Road, the Strip's main north–south highway. It is the first ground operation across the ceasefire boundary since the US-brokered truce took hold in October 2025. The IDF says it is going after a tunnel and a "threat to troops", and it has warned civilians to stay away. This follows its claim days earlier that it destroyed a roughly 300-metre tunnel reaching its side of the line. Medics said an airstrike near the raid killed one person. Two more people were killed by Israeli fire in Deir al-Balah and Gaza City, and dozens of displaced families fled. Defence Minister Israel Katz said troops would stay in Gaza until Hamas is dismantled and the territory demilitarised. Hamas called the incursion a "flagrant" violation.

Background. The day before, the Board of Peace gave the UN Security Council another bleak progress report. It said Israel has not allowed the interim technocratic civil administration to operate inside Gaza. That has held up the international stabilisation force (ISF), Israeli troop withdrawals and Hamas disarmament. The report warned that "a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely". The sequencing dispute dates back to the July Cairo framework. Under it, heavy-weapons decommissioning begins only after the Palestinian committee arrives and the ISF deploys. Hamas will not disarm before Israel withdraws, and Israel will not withdraw before "genuine disarmament". Israel still holds roughly two-thirds of the Strip. Gaza health authorities count at least 1,600 Palestinians killed since the truce began, and the IDF reports five soldiers killed.

Analysis. The incursion appears tactically limited, but its political meaning matters more. It turns the Yellow Line from a ceasefire boundary into a line Israel can cross whenever it sees a security need, much as it has treated the "five points" and pilot zones in south Lebanon. It also comes during a domestic interregnum. Netanyahu heads a caretaker government, the opposition now controls the Knesset presidium, and Gadi Eisenkot holds the mandate to form a government (see Israel below). One reading is that the outgoing security cabinet is creating facts on the ground before a new coalition could narrow its room for manoeuvre. Another is that the IDF's operational logic now drives policy whoever governs, since Eisenkot himself is a former chief of staff. For Washington, the incursion undercuts the Board of Peace's credibility at the very moment it is asking the Security Council for patience. For Cairo and Doha, the mediators, it raises the cost of pressing Hamas on disarmament. Qatar has restated that it is pressing Israel to stop violations (SANA, Syrian state media). Turkey's Erdoğan used the day to call Gaza "the world's largest concentration camp" (Middle East Monitor, partisan outlet).

What to watch. Whether the operation pulls back after the tunnel is destroyed or becomes a permanent forward position. Whether Hamas responds militarily. Winter shelter needs: aid agencies say about 26,000 households were flooded in the season's first storm and that 121,000 tents are still needed.

3. Yemen: two-front war as the Houthis close in on Aden and strike at Riyadh.

What happened. On Wednesday the Saudi-led coalition said it had destroyed 60 Houthi military targets in Sanaa, Hodeidah, Amran and Saada. Spokesman Turki al-Maliki said the strikes began Tuesday and hit tunnels used as missile workshops and storage. The coalition says it intercepted one or more Houthi cruise missiles aimed at the Riyadh region. The Houthis say they fired a "large number of cruise missiles" at King Khalid International Airport and a Riyadh financial district. Early Thursday another missile aimed at Riyadh was intercepted, and debris caused damage that is still being assessed. Civil defence alerts were also issued in Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait. The Houthi-run health ministry says 17 people were killed in coalition strikes. That figure is unverified. In Taiz, local reports say six civilians were wounded by Houthi shelling and sniper fire.

Background. This is no longer the frozen conflict of 2022 to 25. Government-aligned forces withdrew from Dhubab, near Bab al-Mandeb, on 15 November to defend Aden, giving up ground they had retaken only in September and October. AFP put the Houthis within about 60 km of Aden after they took the al-Maqatirah and al-Madaribah districts. The National has them at Khor Omeira, roughly 100 km west of Aden, and says an assault is not imminent. In late October Egypt's parliament authorised Sisi to provide military support to defend Saudi territory, expressly excluding an attack on Yemen. The Houthis called that a "declaration of war".

Analysis. The strategic logic is about chokepoints. With Hormuz effectively closed or contested since the spring, Bab al-Mandeb and the Red Sea are the Gulf's export lifeline, including Saudi crude moved west through the East–West pipeline to Yanbu. Houthi control of the Dhubab coast gives the Axis leverage over both straits at once. France 24's analysis argues the Houthis are more likely to besiege Aden than storm it. A siege would let them squeeze the internationally recognised government's capital, revenues and the Southern Transitional Council's base without the cost of urban combat. For Riyadh, the 2022 truce and the roadmap talks with the Houthis have failed. The kingdom is back in an air war it tried to leave, now with Egyptian backing and its own cities under fire. That also strains Vision 2030's investment story (see Economy).

What to watch. Fighting at Tur al-Bahah and on the Lahj approaches. Whether Egyptian forces actually deploy. Any Houthi attack on Red Sea shipping, which would turn a ground war into a global trade shock.

4. Iraq: Baghdad versus Kataib Hezbollah at Jurf al-Sakhar.

What happened. Iraq's armed forces command approved a plan to send army, federal police and Popular Mobilisation Forces units into Jurf al-Sakhar, Kataib Hezbollah's (KH) fortified enclave in Babil province. KH has reportedly been ordered to withdraw by 8 pm on Friday 20 November. The stated trigger is a government committee finding of oil smuggling in the area, involving dozens of tanker trucks and unauthorised facilities. KH denies this. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi postponed a planned Wednesday visit to Lebanon because of the standoff. Parliament's Security and Defence Committee backed the deployment while urging that bloodshed be avoided. Iran International reports that IRGC Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani met al-Zaidi until dawn on Tuesday and reached a tentative deal for KH to withdraw "soon". KH's local leader said the group would not leave "even over our bodies, our blood", but also insisted fighting would be avoided (Iran International, Persian-language opposition-leaning outlet).

Background. Jurf al-Sakhar was emptied of its Sunni residents after it was retaken from ISIS in 2014 and has been a no-go zone for the state ever since. It is believed to host KH weapons stores and detention facilities. The government has pushed back its deadline for bringing militia weapons under state control from 30 September 2026 to June 2027. Al-Zaidi, prime minister since May, has a new interior and a new defence minister, both appointed in October.

Analysis. The Soufan Center describes Baghdad as "realigning" and enforcing its writ against Iran-aligned groups. Choosing oil smuggling rather than "disarmament" as the legal basis is smart. It frames the operation as anti-corruption, which is harder for other Coordination Framework factions to oppose. Qaani's dawn diplomacy shows that Tehran, under military pressure and short of money, prefers a managed retreat to an intra-Shia firefight that could hand Washington a pretext. The Middle East Forum's critique is that a prime minister who has to negotiate access to his own territory is not yet sovereign (MEF, hawkish US think tank). The likeliest outcome is a face-saving partial handover: neither full disarmament nor an assault (EJ Magnier, commentator close to the "Axis" narrative).

What to watch. The Friday 20:00 deadline, whether KH units actually leave or simply rebadge as PMF, and whether Asa'ib Ahl al-Haq exploits the moment.

5. Turkey: İmamoğlu convicted again, banned from politics.

What happened. On 18 November an Ankara court convicted jailed Istanbul mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu and eight others of vote manipulation at the CHP's 2023 congress. Each received two years and one month in prison and a political ban. Three defendants, including İzmir mayor Cemil Tugay, were acquitted. The ban takes effect only once higher courts uphold the verdict, and the defendants have two weeks to appeal. Reuters notes this adds to earlier bans from 2022 and 2025 that are still under appeal. Most of the defendants have since joined Özgür Özel's New Party, which Özel founded after a court annulled his 2023 CHP leadership and reinstated Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu (Stockholm Center for Freedom, Gülen-linked outlet).

Analysis. Taken together, the judicial strategy has decapitated and split Turkey's main opposition. The CHP brand has been returned to the old guard, the reformers have been pushed into a new party, and the strongest presidential candidate is being disqualified. If the ban is finalised, the Istanbul council could elect a new mayor, giving the AKP the metropolis it lost in 2019. On the same day, Erdoğan told parliament that 136 of 500 suspects have been arrested in the stock-market and fund scandal that began in mid-September. That probe has reportedly erased more than 100 billion dollars in market value and is under scrutiny ahead of MSCI's November index review. Abroad, Ankara is enjoying unusual leverage: it has a new mutual defence pact with Qatar, it ratified the Mecca pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, and it is mediating in Libya. That makes Western criticism of the İmamoğlu case even less likely to bite.

Lower oil revenue and lower Aramco payments are the main causes. Houthi strikes on Riyadh add a risk premium to giga-project financing.

The rial hit about 2.27 million to the dollar in September, inflation was about 66% in July, and real GDP fell 10.1% year-on-year in spring.

Syria: President al-Sharaa received a World Bank Group delegation led by Managing Director of Operations Anna Bjerde, with Finance Minister Mohammed Yisr Barnieh present (SANA, state agency). This is another step in Syria's reintegration into the international financial system.

Egypt: PM Madbouly witnessed a NEVERA–Hassan Allam partnership for a 36 million m² mixed-use development in western Greater Cairo (Egyptian Gazette, state-owned). Gulf-linked real estate capital keeps underpinning Egypt's growth story despite pressure on the currency.

Turkey: The fund and market-manipulation probe has 136 arrests and the MSCI review is coming.

UAE as investor: The Abu Dhabi Fund for Development signed its first cooperation agreement with Mozambique, worth about AED 1.84bn (500 million dollars). Emirati development finance into the Indian Ocean and East Africa continues despite the war.

Lebanon.

International.

Waltz's visit carried a "two armies" message and opened the debate over what replaces UNIFIL after 31 December.

New US Treasury sanctions target Hezbollah finance. Bessent said "Iran's attempts to infringe on Lebanon's sovereignty through terror proxies like Hizballah will not be tolerated". Iraq's PM postponed his Beirut visit.

Local.

A judicial source told AFP the judiciary has charged five Hezbollah members with recruiting and arming others, as state pressure on the party increases.

Israeli demolitions continue in Zawtar al-Sharqiyah and Haddatha. Hezbollah has not publicly responded to the latest operations.

Context: The state is criminalising Hezbollah's armed activity step by step, while Israel's continued occupation gives the party its main argument for keeping its weapons.

Syria.

International.

A World Bank Group delegation (Anna Bjerde) met al-Sharaa in Damascus (SANA, state agency).

Egypt and other Arab states condemned Israeli incursions into Quneitra and Daraa. Turkey told the UNSC that SDF integration "falls short".

Local.

Decrees 202 and 203 appointed an economic adviser and a new economy and foreign-trade minister who also heads the investment authority (SANA; Hawar News, Kurdish-aligned).

Debate over the stalled Kurdish integration process has intensified. Presidential adviser Mazloum Abdi accused unnamed parties of trying to derail it.

Context: Damascus is consolidating economic governance to attract reconstruction finance, while Israel's southern buffer zone and the unfinished Kurdish integration still cap its sovereignty.

Jordan.

International.

The Jordan–EU Investment Conference opens at the Dead Sea on 19 November. On 13 November the Crown Prince spoke with US Vice President Vance.

Australia's travel advisory notes recent strikes "including towards targets in Jordan".

Local.

A Black Hawk crash in training injured five soldiers.

On 16 November the King marked 25 years of the King Abdullah II Fund for Development. The National Water Conveyance project is moving ahead.

Context: Little happened in the window. Amman is using EU and Gulf finance for water security while staying exposed to spillover from the Iran war.

Iraq (incl. Kurdistan Region)

International.

Qaani's overnight talks with al-Zaidi show Tehran managing its proxy's retreat. The Soufan Center sees Baghdad as realigning.

German officials in Erbil said Berlin will keep and strengthen cooperation with Baghdad and the KRG despite having withdrawn its troops.

Local.

The Jurf al-Sakhar deadline is Friday 20:00. Parliament's defence committee supports the deployment.

Kurdistan Region: the PUK boycotts the KRG cabinet. PM Masrour Barzani sees "positive" budget signals from Baghdad. Heating-oil prices are deepening winter hardship.

Context: With coalition forces gone, Baghdad is testing whether it can bring the "muqawama" factions under state control without US cover. Erbil is distracted by KDP–PUK paralysis.

Iran.

International.

Iran has struck Kuwait and Bahrain again after the US hit Qeshm. Araghchi was in Muscat, and Qatar is hosting talks with Iranian officials.

Tehran called the "dirty bomb" report baseless. The IAEA Board meets this week, with Iran refusing monitoring talks "under military threat".

Local.

Protests in Abdanan after 13 workers died.

Gasoline rationing and severe air pollution in Tehran.

Context: The Islamic Republic is fighting a low-intensity war of attrition abroad while its war economy erodes legitimacy at home. The periphery (Ilam, Kurdistan, Baluchistan) is where that erosion shows first.

Turkey.

International.

The new mutual defence pact with Qatar comes after parliament ratified the Mecca pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan on 6 November (279 to 119).

At the UNSC, Ankara criticised the SDF integration. Erdoğan made harsh statements on Gaza.

Local.

İmamoğlu was sentenced to two years and one month and banned from politics.

The market-manipulation probe has 136 arrests.

Context: Ankara is turning wartime regional demand for its security role into immunity from criticism of its domestic authoritarian consolidation.

Saudi Arabia.

International.

The coalition reports 60 strikes on the Houthis and missile interceptions over Riyadh. Egypt's defensive support mandate stands in the background.

Local.

Debris from a missile intercepted over Riyadh early Thursday caused damage. Alerts were issued in Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait.

The Q3 deficit was SAR 84.3bn and the year-to-date deficit is well above budget.

Context: The kingdom's hoped-for exit from Yemen has reversed. It is now fighting the Houthis while its fiscal position worsens.

United Arab Emirates.

International.

The UAE condemned the "renewed hostile Iranian attack" on Bahrain and expressed full solidarity. ADNOC shipping remains exposed in Hormuz.

ADFD signed a 500 million dollars cooperation agreement with Mozambique.

Local.

Not much domestic news in the window. Earlier this month TA'ZIZ closed AED 5.1bn of financing for Ruwais projects, so industrial diversification continues despite the war.

Context: Abu Dhabi keeps its investment diplomacy going while its energy exports depend on a contested strait.

Qatar.

International.

Qatar met Iranian officials on 17 November over US–Iran proposals and urged de-escalation.

The Turkey mutual defence pact. Ongoing Gaza mediation.

Local.

Nothing significant in the window beyond the official framing of the defence pact as part of strengthening national security.

Context: Doha is hedging, mediating with Tehran while adding a Turkish security guarantee alongside its US one.

Kuwait.

International.

Iran targeted US installations at Ali Al Salem air base. Kuwait's foreign ministry strongly condemned the drone attacks. Egypt expressed support for Kuwaiti sovereignty.

Local.

Authorities reported the projectiles intercepted with no damage or casualties. The 2026 nationality decree barring naturalised citizens from voting remains a domestic flashpoint while parliament stays suspended.

Context: The first direct strikes on Kuwait in about two months end its period of relative calm in the war.

Bahrain.

International.

Bahrain has faced four Iranian attacks in six days targeting US Fifth Fleet-linked sites. The GCC, UAE and others condemned them.

Local.

Shelter alerts have resumed for the first time since September. Sixty-four people, mostly Shia clerics, were sentenced to life for "colluding with Iran".

Context: As the smallest and most exposed host of US forces, Manama is securitising its sectarian politics more heavily under fire.

Oman.

International.

Araghchi met FM Albusaidi and Sultan Haitham in Muscat. A tanker was hit off Khasab in Omani waters.

Local.

Little domestic news in the window. The October state visit to the UK, where King Charles praised Omani peace efforts, still frames Muscat's role.

Context: Oman shares Hormuz with Iran and remains the indispensable but strained channel.

Yemen.

International.

Saudi-led strikes and Houthi missiles at Riyadh. Analysts warn that Houthi gains at Bab al-Mandeb give them leverage over global shipping.

Local.

Houthis are roughly 60 to 100 km from Aden. Government forces have redeployed to al-Suqyah. Taiz is under siege pressure. The Houthi Saba agency portrays the withdrawal as a rout (partisan outlet).

Context: The 2022 truce has fully collapsed into a Houthi offensive aimed at the south's capital.
