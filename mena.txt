Here is your Middle East briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Five things mattered most.

First, Iran. The US–Iran war has entered a decision window. Trump says a choice between renewed strikes, continued economic pressure or a deal is coming soon. CENTCOM has reportedly finished planning a short, intense campaign against nuclear, missile and energy targets, and US officials point to the new moon on 9 November, which favours air operations. Meanwhile the US keeps hitting Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, and has disabled an eleventh ship trying to break the blockade. The Revolutionary Guards claim two more tankers hit mines, which has not been confirmed. In Tehran, Mojtaba Khamenei warned that hopes of a softer US line after the midterms are mistaken.

Second, Qatar's mediation is strained but alive. Doha denies the talks have collapsed, and Trump spoke with the emir. Iran has sent a nuclear proposal through Qatar, but insists on its seven conditions first: lifting the blockade, sanctions relief, frozen funds, reparations, and an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon. The deal taking shape would trade a reopening of Hormuz for sanctions relief, with nuclear talks later. The core dispute is sequencing: Washington wants the nuclear file first, Tehran wants relief first.

Third, Israel. President Herzog hands Gadi Eisenkot the mandate tonight, after parties representing 67 members recommended him. But he has no obvious majority. He has ruled out relying on the Arab parties, and the ultra-Orthodox parties want draft exemptions he opposes. Netanyahu is calling for a unity government and warning against an "Arab-Jewish coalition". Likud is challenging the result, helped by a Shin Bet probe into alleged foreign funding. A new election in early 2027 remains possible.

Fourth, the Saudi–Houthi war is getting deadlier. Saudi Arabia says it has intercepted at least 15 missiles and drones this month. The Houthis claim six attacks on Saturday alone, including on Riyadh's airport and oil sites. Houthi health officials say Saudi-led strikes near Sanaa killed 33 people.

Fifth, the region is realigning around Riyadh and Ankara. Saudi Arabia signed a defence pact and 403 million dollars in loans with Syria. Damascus is considering joining the Saudi–Turkish–Pakistani defence pact. And Egypt reopened its embassy in Tripoli after 12 years, a sign Cairo is hedging on Haftar.

What to watch in the next 48 hours: whether US strikes come around the new moon or Trump announces a deal framework instead; a US written reply through Doha; Eisenkot's first coalition talks and whether Likud goes to the Supreme Court; and further Houthi attacks on Saudi territory.
