JNIM urban push and supply blockades dominate as AES-ECOWAS talks inch on.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 20 November 2026 (the search index returned almost nothing from the last 24 to 72 hours, so most sections are widened well beyond 72 hours). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Crisis Group (20 November) warns JNIM has escalated attacks on big cities and blocked supply routes while calling for the overthrow of the military regimes in Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger. The briefing frames the urban heartlands, not the periphery, as the next phase of the war.

AES ministers from Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger met in Ouagadougou on 11 to 12 November to review the confederation's second-year roadmap. They condemned terrorism and regretted Guinea's expulsions of African nationals.

ECOWAS and the AES agreed in October to set up joint working groups on a trade and security deal, after a first ministerial meeting in Dakar on 17 October (per Daily Trust, as summarised by search results). It signals a pragmatic thaw despite the 2025 exit.

Jihadists attacked civilians in central Mali on 8 November; RFI sources confirmed jihadists were responsible, with reports dated 14 to 16 November. Violence against civilians continues alongside the military campaign.

2. Mali.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel on 2 November (unconfirmed, a JNIM claim). It shows the group still targets the Russian-backed convoys directly.

A Sahel chronology updated 18 November lists JNIM claims in Mopti region, including seizure of a militia headquarters near Djenne (unconfirmed, single compilation). It points to continued pressure on pro-government militias in the centre.

A double attack hit two central Mali villages, with sources confirming jihadist responsibility for the 8 November attack. Civilian toll figures were not available to me.

Background: at least 50 soldiers and five Africa Corps paramilitaries were killed at an army base in September, according to AFP security and local sources; Reuters sources put army deaths above 100 that month. The army's losses explain the urban-threat framing above.

3. Burkina Faso.

JNIM claimed it took a Burkinabe military outpost at Mantou near Fada N'Gourma (unconfirmed, JNIM claim relayed in a chronology updated 18 November). Fada N'Gourma is a regional hub in the east, so any loss nearby matters.

ACLED's weekly update for 7 to 13 November describes JNIM strikes on the military, attacks on population centres and transit routes, and IEDs along the RN24. The pattern is road interdiction.

Burkina listed its first diaspora bonds on the regional BRVM on 3 September, raising 151.5 billion FCFA against a 125 billion target. This is older, but it shows the junta tapping domestic and diaspora finance.

4. Niger.

ACLED's 7 to 13 November update says Islamic State Sahel carried out attacks in the Dioundiou and Tera departments, and a JNIM attack in Kirtachi displaced thousands. The west and south-west remain the most violent front.

More than 30 civilians were reportedly killed in western Niger, per a report from 16 November (unconfirmed, single compilation). Attribution and exact date were not clear in the source.

Algeria's president reportedly confirmed his country sent fighter jets to Niger during an August coup attempt (older, relayed by a newsletter). If accurate, it complicates the usual Niger-Algeria hostility narrative.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

AES ministers met in Ouagadougou on 11 to 12 November and welcomed roadmap progress; no Guinean reply to their statement on expulsions had been found by 17 November. Guinea is the new friction point.

ECOWAS and the AES set up joint working groups in October; ECOWAS named former Guinean prime minister Lansana Kouyate chief negotiator in March. No signed deal was found.

AES territorial administration officials approved an additional protocol on cross-border cooperation on 15 to 16 September in Ouagadougou. Institutional integration continues.

SWP Berlin (September) notes ECOWAS states have gradually dropped their policy of isolating the AES since 2025. Useful framing for the thaw.
