Here is your Sahel briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

JNIM-Islamic State clashes reshape Burkina Faso as Nigeria claims cross-border pursuit pact with Niger and Chad.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 18 November 2026 (widened to October-November where noted). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Coverage note: This edition is thinner than usual. Direct page fetches (Reuters, RFI, France 24, Lefaso.net, allAfrica) failed in the routine's sandbox, so it relies on search-result summaries only. Nothing was read in full, and details need checking against the originals. Sections with no dated reporting are marked as such rather than filled from memory.

1. Top stories.

Nigeria says Cameroon, Niger and Chad agreed to cross-border pursuit of armed groups up to five kilometres deep, announced 16 November in Abuja by Defence Minister Christopher Musa (unconfirmed, single-source summary). Implementation details are unpublished and a Cameroonian officer told RFI that reciprocity is an open question.

ACLED's 17 November update says JNIM is hitting Burkina Faso's army harder, striking population centres and planting IEDs along the RN24 while competing with Islamic State Sahel (ISSP) for territory. The jihadist-on-jihadist rivalry is reshaping where the state loses ground.

Al Jazeera reports fighting between JNIM and ISSP around Ganadaouri (Oudalan, northern Burkina Faso) escalated from 9 October, and Islamic State's Al-Naba claimed more than 400 JNIM fighters killed. This is an IS claim and unverified.

An AFP analysis of ACLED data, carried by Macau Business, put JNIM-attributed deaths in 2026 at 2,100 as of 7 November, an annual record. Attribution rests on ACLED's coding.

2. Mali.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on 2 November on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna. The claim is JNIM's own and casualties were not independently confirmed.

ACLED's 7 to 13 November update says JNIM and the Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) increasingly attack in the northwest, while JNIM targets Dozo militias in the centre and civilians in the south and west. The pattern points to a campaign extending beyond the north.

Wikipedia's summary of the 2026 offensives says joint FLA-JNIM attacks began on 25 April and that the FLA held Kidal until October 2026. This is a secondary source and the October change of control should be verified.

JNIM has also claimed attacks on Senegal-Mali fuel convoys, saying they breached its blockade orders. Date unclear.

3. Burkina Faso.

Ibrahim Traoré's recorded public agenda this month was ceremonial, including closing the Tour du Faso cycling race on 8 November. No presidential security statement turned up in this window. Présidence du Faso (state source)

ACLED reports IEDs along the RN24 and JNIM pressure on towns and transit routes. See Top stories.

Al Jazeera reports the JNIM-ISSP fighting in Oudalan and the state's retreat.

4. Niger.

ACLED's 7 to 13 November update says ISSP attacked in the Dioundiou and Téra areas and a JNIM attack in Kirtachi commune displaced thousands. Displacement numbers were not given.

Niger lost 1 to 0 to Morocco in AFCON 2027 qualifying on Tuesday (17 November). Peripheral to security, but notable as a junta-era sporting result. allAfrica, 18 November (unconfirmed)

A Reuters-sourced report of a Niamey base mutiny put down in late August 2026 with Africa Corps help surfaced in search, but its date and claims are unverified. Sahel summary via search (unconfirmed)

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

Quiet: no dated AES or ECOWAS news found in the last 72 hours. Background: the three AES juntas have ruled out returning to ECOWAS, and Traoré was named AES head at the December 2025 Bamako summit.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

The JNIM-ISSP rivalry is now a central dynamic: ACLED sees them competing for territory, and Al Jazeera reports escalating clashes in Oudalan. It matters because it can divert each group's resources while raising civilian risk.

Nigeria's reported pursuit accord with its neighbours suggests Abuja sees cross-border sanctuary as a core problem. No ISWAP-specific incident was found for this window. allAfrica, 18 November (unconfirmed)

Crisis Group's analysis of JNIM's expansion beyond the Sahel is the main reference for coastal spillover (Benin, Togo). No new coastal incident was found in the last 72 hours.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers.

Africa Corps personnel were reportedly in the vehicle JNIM claimed to have hit near Sévaré on 2 November. This indicates Russian forces still operate in central Mali.

An analysis (Rio Times, undated) says Russia's deployment has so far failed to lift the fuel blockade. Treat as commentary.

Quiet on France, US, Turkey, UAE and China: no dated reporting found.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

Thousands were displaced by the JNIM attack in Kirtachi, Niger, according to ACLED's weekly summary.

UNICEF has warned that one million children under five in Mali face malnutrition risk amid polio and measles outbreaks. The date is unclear (months old).

Quiet on migration routes: nothing dated found.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Mali's JNIM fuel blockade, in place since September 2025, continues to strain supply: a Malian government claim cited by African Arguments says over 900 tankers reached Bamako under escort on 14 September. Figure from the government, not independently verified.

Reuters, as cited by OilPrice, reported the junta cancelled more than 90 foreign-held exploration permits in late October for breaching the 2023 mining code.

Kodal Minerals says its Bougouni lithium mine still operates, though fuel allocations are reportedly under half of normal.
