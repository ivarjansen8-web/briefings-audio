Here is your Sahel briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

Niger accuses Chad of French-backed drone strike; Africa Corps tied to Mali drone war; Chad purges commander.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 16 November 2026 (older context flagged). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. Coverage note: this run's search tool surfaced little fresh reporting, and page fetches failed on several hosts (impact.sn, timbuktu-institute.org, adf-magazine.com), so most items rest on search-result summaries, not full articles.

1. Top stories.

Niger accuses Chad of hosting a deadly drone strike in the Agadez region, with alleged French support (unconfirmed). Niamey says the strike hit the Emi Lulu area near the Algerian and Libyan borders, summoned Chad's ambassador and plans to take the matter to the UN Security Council and the AU Peace and Security Council; N'Djamena denies it ordered, conducted or supported any air operation. A Niger-Chad rupture would add a new fault line between the Sahel juntas and a Western-aligned neighbour.

Rights groups say Russia's Africa Corps runs Mali's drone war. An Africa Defense Forum report of 10 November says footage and wreckage in northern Mali show Africa Corps directing the junta's drone strikes, and The Sentry attributes some strikes to Russian commanders. It matters because it ties Moscow directly to civilian-harm allegations.

Chad's president suspended a regional military commander and ordered civilians disarmed. Mahamat Idriss Deby Itno suspended Gen. Hamoda Mahamat Bechir, head of defence zone no. 8, at the end of a six-province security tour. It signals unease over armed civilians and command discipline in the east and centre.

2. Mali.

Russian forces and the Malian army hold Kidal after the Tuareg-led FLA retreated in early October. Wikipedia's running account of the 2026 offensives says Kidal had been under FLA control until then; the junta and Africa Corps call the April attacks a thwarted coup attempt. This is a secondary source, so treat details with care.

Human Rights Watch says the army and Africa Corps committed new abuses. HRW (22 October) reports a 16 September drone strike on Guelediie village, Mopti region, that killed eight civilians and wounded at least six, plus hundreds of civilians detained at military bases; neither defence ministry replied.

More than 100 Malian soldiers were reportedly killed in September attacks, according to unnamed sources cited by Reuters. The figure is not independently verified and predates this window.

JNIM's fuel blockade of Bamako was reimposed on 28 April and, per a July report, had not eased. I found no reporting on its status in November, so current conditions are unknown.

3. Burkina Faso.

Burkina has seen no large-scale jihadist attack since late June, but AFP says the calm is deceptive. Analysts and data show attacks are less deadly but just as frequent, so the lower toll may reflect tactics and information control as much as improvement.

Junta politics: no news this week. Background: the junta broke diplomatic ties with France effective 26 June and extended Traore's rule to 2029; IPS (August) describes the end of party politics.

4. Niger.

Niger says Chad and France enabled a drone strike near Emi Lulu (unconfirmed). See Top stories; the claim comes from Niger's government, which has a record of blaming neighbours and France, and Chad denies it.

Algeria has reportedly decided to deploy military units to Niger (unconfirmed). The report comes from a single search summary; if true, it marks a security tie between two states that were at odds earlier in the decade.

A report of at least 37 civilians killed by armed men in western Niger on 16 November (unconfirmed). I could not verify it: my follow-up search only found a similar toll from an August 2021 attack, so this may be a misdated item. Do not cite until confirmed.

5. Chad.

Deby suspended Gen. Hamoda Mahamat Bechir and ordered civilian disarmament after touring six provinces; he also urged unity in Batha.

A prison break at Mongo, Guera province, on Sunday 15 November left two dead and three seriously hurt, with 30 inmates still at large per a provisional toll. It adds to pressure on Chad's internal security.

N'Djamena rejects Niger's drone-strike accusation and says Niger harbours Chadian armed groups; spokesman Gassim Cherif Mahamat said the government never ordered, conducted or supported such an operation.

6. Mauritania.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. (My searches returned no relevant Mauritanian reporting; local outlets such as Alwiam and Cridem were not reached.)

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

ECOWAS and the AES held their first ministerial meeting of a cooperation dialogue in Dakar on 17 October and agreed to negotiate a deal protecting trade and security ties. ECOWAS chief negotiator Lansana Kouyate (appointed in March) told RFI the aim is not to ask the AES to return. This is a month old, but it is the latest formal step I found.

The AES launched a unified military force in December 2025. Its performance against JNIM, and whether Niger's quarrel with Chad strains the bloc's unity, are open questions.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

HumAngle analyses JNIM's cross-border attacks as a threat to the Sahel's security architecture. The piece is undated in my results; it frames JNIM expansion toward coastal states as the main strategic risk.

Mali's April offensive showed JNIM and the FLA can coordinate nationwide. Reuters, via The Star, reported a joint claim on 25 to 26 April, including the attack on Bamako's airport and the killing of the defence minister.

ISWAP and Islamic State Sahel: no relevant reports found in the last 72 hours.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers (France, US, Turkey, UAE, China)

Africa Corps leads the junta's drone campaign, per ADF (10 November).

The CFR tracker (updated 5 November) says the April offensive forced Russian forces to withdraw temporarily from northern positions. Moscow's staying power in Mali remains the key variable.

France is accused by Niger of backing the Chad-based strike (unconfirmed). Paris has not been heard in the material I found.

US, Turkey, UAE, China: no relevant reports found.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

Nearly 4 million people are displaced across Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger, including over 818,000 refugees, UNHCR said on 9 October.

OCHA estimates 24.3 million people in the Sahel need aid in 2026; only 21% of funds were mobilised by mid-May. UNHCR's Sahel response was 27% funded as of September against 329.6 dollarsmillion needed.
