Here is your Sahel briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

Bamako blockade grinds on as JNIM-ISSP rivalry splits Burkina front and AES ministers prepare summit.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 16 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Sourcing note: Reporting dated inside the last 24 hours was not retrievable today. Several outlets could not be fetched from this environment and search results were often undated, so most sections draw on the last one to two weeks and some on older context. Dates below are those the search results gave; treat them as approximate.

1. Top stories.

JNIM's blockade of Bamako is still biting in mid-November. Analysts describe fuel queues and school closures in the capital, with the group reportedly extending its campaign to fuel trucks entering from Niger on 1 November (unconfirmed). It shows the junta's control over supply lines is contested well beyond the north.

JNIM and Islamic State Sahel Province are fighting each other in Burkina Faso. Al Jazeera reports the rivalry could give Ouagadougou breathing room if JNIM diverts fighters. It matters because jihadist infighting may reduce pressure on states while worsening civilian risk.

AES ministers met in Burkina Faso on 11 to 12 November to prepare the next heads-of-state session. The communique reviews the confederation's second-year roadmap. It signals the bloc is institutionalising while talks with ECOWAS proceed.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on a Malian army and Russian Africa Corps vehicle between Sévaré and Konna on 2 November. The claim is unverified and Bamako has not released casualty figures.

2. Mali.

JNIM declared a full blockade of Bamako on 28 April and the pressure persists. Malian forces escort convoys on the Bamako-Kayes, Bamako-Ségou and Bamako-Sikasso routes. Escorts keep goods moving but expose the army to ambush.

JNIM claims it hit an army and Africa Corps vehicle near Sévaré-Konna on 2 November (JNIM claim, unconfirmed). Similar IED claims in Mopti region recur.

AFP analysis of ACLED data says JNIM attacks had killed 2,100 people this year by 7 November, an annual record (unconfirmed, date inferred). It frames the scale of the insurgency across the region.

Reporting on a 16 November commentary says the blockade has stranded containers on routes to Bamako and cost Senegal port revenue (opinion). It shows the blockade's cross-border costs.

3. Burkina Faso.

AFP reported on 6 November that the army has not faced a large-scale jihadist attack since late June. Attacks are less deadly but remain frequent. State agency AIB (junta-controlled) said a JNIM leader was killed late October.

JNIM and IS Sahel are fighting for control, per Al Jazeera on 13 November. It suggests the state is losing ground to rival armed groups rather than winning it back.

The national assembly reportedly passed a law restricting privatisation of strategic state firms, 70 votes to none with one abstention (unconfirmed, Substack roundup). It fits the resource-nationalist line of the junta.

Burkina Faso hosted the AES Council of Ministers on 11 to 12 November.

4. Niger.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Most recent context: a mutiny in Niamey on about 29 August was put down with Africa Corps help.

Tiani signed a decree on 17 September stripping five exiled Nigeriens of nationality, per Human Rights Watch. It shows repression continuing after the mutiny.

Niger and Algeria reportedly signed a military cooperation memorandum on 21 October, and Algerian lawmakers approved troops for Madama (unconfirmed, Substack). If true it marks a thaw after earlier tensions.

Niger accused Chad of a drone strike in the Salvador Pass killing two civilians (CFR tracker, undated, unconfirmed).

5. Chad.

President Mahamat Déby began a security tour on about 7 November, starting in Ennedi Ouest, and sanctioned military and administrative officials. APA says he suspended General Hamoda Mahamat Béchir and ordered civilian disarmament (date not given). It points to unrest in the northeast.

Chad ended military cooperation with France earlier (undated France 24 note). Background for French basing.

Déby was named laureate of an African peace prize for 2026 (afrik.com, undated).

6. Mauritania.

President Ghazouani called for solidarity with Mali on about 10 November. APA's weekday for that date looks wrong. Nouakchott is signalling concern about spillover.

Prime Minister Ould Djay faced the National Assembly on 9 to 10 November; 21 deputies voted against the government. The cabinet resigned on 9 October after power-utility fires.

The EU fisheries agreement expires in November and is up for renegotiation (search summary).

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

The AES Council of Ministers met on 11 to 12 November to review the second-year roadmap before the next heads-of-state session (date not announced).

ECOWAS and the AES agreed in October to set up joint working groups to protect trade and security ties.

ECOWAS named Lansana Kouyaté chief negotiator with the AES in March 2026, and the AES leaders have ruled out rejoining.

Niger-Chad and Niger-Algeria frictions and moves are noted above (unconfirmed).

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover.

JNIM-ISSP infighting is the main dynamic, per Al Jazeera (13 November) and Jamestown.

Coordinated JNIM attacks hit Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger in the first week of November.

Coastal spillover: no November 2026 incident in Benin, Togo or Nigeria turned up; the latest found was 15 soldiers killed in northern Benin in March. Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours found.

ACLED-based research says attacks in the Niger-Benin-Nigeria border zone are soaring (February).

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers.

Quiet this week; latest: a Jeune Afrique, Bellingcat and The Sentry investigation (16 September) of about 60 drone strikes found Turkish-financed Malian drones and Russian-linked ones in parallel.

Africa Corps has reduced ground combat and relies on drones, reportedly paid about 10 million dollars a month (unconfirmed).

About 15 Ukrainian drone specialists are reportedly aiding Tuareg separatists in Mali (unconfirmed).

No US, UAE or China developments found.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

UNHCR said in October that almost 500,000 Burkinabe refugees sought protection abroad in 2026, up from over 300,000 at end-2025.

OCHA (April) counted 2.8 million internally displaced people in Burkina Faso, Mali and western Niger and only 28% of funding secured.

Bamako schools closed over the fuel crisis (reported late October to mid-November, unconfirmed date).

Migration routes: nothing found for the period.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Fuel and supply disruption continues around Bamako.

A ministry plan reportedly shows Mali's gold output staying below 60 tonnes through 2029 (July headline only).

Gold miners said in April they would stay in Mali despite attacks; Barrick regained control of Loulo-Gounkoto in December 2025.

Burkina's privatisation law (see section 3, unconfirmed).
