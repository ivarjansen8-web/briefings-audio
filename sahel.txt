Here is your Sahel briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

JNIM widens Mali fuel blockade to Niger route as IS–al-Qaeda war escalates.

Compiled from sources dated 1 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Coverage note: this run could only use search-result summaries. Fetching of Studio Tamani, Afropages and ModernGhana failed because those hosts are outside the session's allowed network domains. Most items below are therefore single-source. Please verify them against the originals before relying on them.

1. Top stories.

JNIM says it extended its fuel blockade of Mali to tankers entering from Niger, in a statement dated 1 November (unconfirmed). Until now the blockade targeted routes from Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Guinea, so this would cut a further supply line to Bamako.

JNIM claims an IED attack on a vehicle carrying Malian soldiers and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna on 2 November (unconfirmed, JNIM's own claim). It shows the group still hits Russian-accompanied convoys in central Mali.

Islamic State Sahel Province (ISSP) claims it killed more than 400 JNIM fighters in a battle near the Mali-Niger border (unconfirmed, ISSP figure, unverified). If even partly true it marks a sharp escalation in the IS-versus-al-Qaeda war that is spreading toward western Niger and north-west Nigeria.

Mali's Assimi Goïta visited Burkina Faso and Niger on 5 November to deepen AES cooperation, per a Studio Tamani-based summary (unconfirmed). The trip would signal AES unity while Mali is under heavy pressure.

2. Mali.

JNIM's fuel blockade is the dominant story. Open-source counts cited in reporting put destroyed tankers above 300 since the blockade began, with long queues and rationing in Bamako (figures from analysts, not government).

The IED claim near Sévaré-Konna on 2 November is unconfirmed. Neither the army nor Africa Corps comment appeared in what I could access.

Goïta's regional tour (5 November) pairs with a plan for the 11th Mali-Burkina joint commission (unconfirmed).

3. Burkina Faso.

AFP reports no large-scale jihadist attack since late June, with violence down from a June peak. ACLED data cited by AFP show 612 deaths in June, 195 in July, 231 in August and 29 in September, while the number of attacks stays constant and most are claimed by JNIM.

Burkinabè special forces reportedly killed jihadist leader Djabiri Mangan, "Le Borgne" (unconfirmed, likely junta-sourced). A headline-list item dated around 4 November; treat as an official claim.

4. Niger.

Goïta's visit to Niamey on 5 November is the main item (unconfirmed). Security cooperation and AES integration were reportedly discussed.

Maliweb reports Algeria stepping up diplomacy toward Niger and Chad (unconfirmed, headline only). Algiers is courting both after its rift with Bamako.

5. Chad.

President Mahamat Idriss Déby Itno is on a security tour of the provinces, and in Ennedi Ouest reportedly sanctioned officials and ordered civilian disarmament (unconfirmed, Tchad Infos headline of 8 November). Disarmament drives often follow communal violence.

6. Mauritania.

Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours. This may reflect search gaps (Cridem, Alwiam and Senego could not be reached) rather than a quiet week.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

Goïta's 5 November tour of Ouagadougou and Niamey is the main AES event (unconfirmed).

Background: the AES's joint AES-Russia statement in July pledged continued Russian support. No new ECOWAS-AES development was found this week.

8. Jihadist groups and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

The ISSP claim of 400+ JNIM dead is unverified. The same source says IS-JNIM fighting is spreading toward western Niger and north-west Nigeria, easing movement of fighters and know-how toward ISWAP.

ACLED flags JNIM's growing drone use against villages and ISSP retaliation against the Dawsahak community.

No dated ISWAP or coastal-state incident was found for this window.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers.

Africa Corps personnel were reportedly in the convoy hit near Sévaré on 2 November (unconfirmed, JNIM claim).

Background: reporting says Africa Corps left Kidal under a safe-passage deal and fell back to Kati/Bamako earlier in 2026.

No new news on France, the US, Turkey, UAE or China was found in the last 72 hours.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours. Fuel shortages in Bamako (see sections 1 to 2) are the main humanitarian pressure point, but no fresh figures were found.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

The blockade extension to Niger-origin tankers would hit Mali's fuel supply and import economy (unconfirmed).

No dated gold, lithium or uranium news was found for this window.
