Here is your Sahel briefing for Friday, 20 November.

Sahel: JNIM-Islamic State border war in Burkina, Erdogan courts Niger, Nigeria strikes ISWAP chiefs.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 19 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Method note: In this run most news sites could not be opened, so the items below rest on search-result summaries rather than full articles. Several sections had little dated reporting from the last 24 hours, so I widened the window to roughly 2 to 3 weeks and say so where it applies. Treat figures as attributed claims, not verified facts.

1. Top stories.

Burkina Faso's two main jihadist rivals, JNIM and Islamic State Sahel, are fighting increasingly deadly battles along the northern border with Mali, Al Jazeera reported on 13 November. The report says the state is losing ground, which makes the rivalry a driver of violence, not just a side effect.

Human Rights Watch says a group linked to al-Qaeda killed at least 27 civilians in two attacks in Burkina Faso's Yatenga province in September. The report is dated 4 November and attributes the attacks to JNIM (HRW's account).

Turkey's president reportedly phoned Niger's leader Abdourahamane Tchiani to discuss bilateral ties and Sahel security (item dated 19 November). It shows Ankara keeping a channel open to Niamey months after the August mutiny there. (unconfirmed: single Turkish outlet)

Nigeria's army says airstrikes on 16 to 17 November killed two senior ISWAP commanders around Monguno and Arinna in Borno State. This is the army's own claim and has not been independently checked.

2. Mali.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on 2 November on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna. It is JNIM's own claim, reported by a Ghanaian outlet. It shows Russian personnel are still exposed on central Mali's main road. (unconfirmed)

JNIM has also targeted Senegal-Mali fuel convoys, saying vehicles broke its blockade orders. The blockade it announced in September 2025 on Bamako and other cities remains its main economic pressure tool.

A September attack on an army base killed at least 50 Malian soldiers and five Africa Corps paramilitaries, according to AFP's security and local sources; a separate report cites sources saying more than 100 soldiers died in attacks that month. The two figures differ and neither is official.

No dated reporting on Bamako politics or the junta turned up for the past 72 hours.

3. Burkina Faso.

Mondafrique reported on 7 November that JNIM fighters attacked a gold-panning site at Soubo, killing 24 and wounding at least 20. Artisanal gold sites are both a civilian target and a funding source for armed groups. (unconfirmed: single outlet)

HRW documented at least 27 civilian deaths in Yatenga in September. See Top stories.

The JNIM versus Islamic State Sahel fighting near the Mali border is the main military development.

4. Niger.

Tchiani reportedly spoke with Erdogan by phone, with Sahel security on the agenda.

Background: soldiers mutinied at a Niamey base on 29 August, and the government said it contained the revolt. Al Jazeera noted Tchiani's whereabouts were unconfirmed at the time. This is older context, not new.

Nothing else dated in the last 72 hours was found.

5. Chad.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest Chad-specific reporting I found concerned the Lake Chad basin; see section 8. Background: Timbuktu Institute analysis on Chad's room to maneuver between Niamey and Moscow.

6. Mauritania.

Mauritania hosts nearly 300,000 refugees, mostly Malians, and arrivals continue, according to UNHCR. That strains the south-east border region.

MSF said on 9 October that refugees in Hodh el Chargui live in informal sites near the border and that wounded civilians cross regularly for care. The date is outside the window; it is the most recent item found.

Diplomatic background: in March, Mali accused Mauritania of letting two Malian soldiers escape from the M'Berra camp, and Nouakchott rejected the claim. Tension eased after talks.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

ECOWAS military chiefs met in Freetown and stressed coordination with the AES while planning a standby force of an initial 2,000 troops by the end of 2026. The force is meant to counter jihadists without forcing a confrontation with the juntas.

The AES launched a joint force of about 5,000 troops in December 2025, with command in Niamey. Its record against JNIM is untested in this run's sources.

Interior officials from the three states validated an additional protocol on cross-border cooperation in Ouagadougou on 15 to 16 September. The date is older than the window.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover.

Boko Haram and ISWAP both tax and surveil civilians across the Lake Chad Basin and raid Cameroon's Far North, according to Africa Defense Forum (17 November). ADF is a US military-affiliated outlet.

The Global Centre for the Responsibility to Protect (16 November) says ISWAP is stepping up attacks on Nigerian security forces around Lake Chad.

Nigerian army airstrikes on 16 to 17 November reportedly killed two ISWAP commanders. Army claim.

JNIM versus Islamic State Sahel clashes continue on the Burkina-Mali border.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers.

Africa Corps personnel were reportedly in the vehicle JNIM claims to have hit near Sévaré on 2 November.

Russia's ambassador said Africa Corps helped Niger's presidential guard suppress the August mutiny; three guardsmen died. Russian official claim, from late August, as relayed in a search summary; I could not open the original. (unconfirmed)

Turkey is courting Niger at presidential level (see section 4).

No dated November items found on France, the US, the UAE or China.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

Mauritania's refugee load of nearly 300,000 is the clearest current humanitarian signal.

OCHA's April dashboard counted 5.8 million internally displaced people in the Sahel and 2.6 million refugees in neighbouring countries. Those are April figures, not new.

No new migration-route reporting turned up.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Barrick signed a new collective agreement with unions at the Loulo-Gounkoto gold mine in Mali, reported 28 September. It reduces strike risk after the earlier tax dispute.

Niger said it will return seized uranium concentrate to Orano, per Northern Miner; the article's date was not shown. (unconfirmed timing)

Mali may raise up to 500 billion CFA francs for energy, water and transport using mining-reform revenue, per Mining Weekly.
