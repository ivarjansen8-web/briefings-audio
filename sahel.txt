Here is your Sahel briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Sahel: JNIM hits Mali convoy, Chad loses soldiers in gold zone, ECOWAS-AES thaw.

Compiled from sources dated 1 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Coverage note: Search turned up almost nothing dated in the last 24 to 72 hours, and several local outlets (ModernGhana, APA, ADF Magazine) could not be opened from this session, so those items rest on search-result summaries only. Most sections therefore use a window of up to three weeks, stated in each bullet.

1. Top stories.

JNIM claimed an IED strike on 2 November on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna. The claim is JNIM's own and follows similar attacks on convoys near Timbuktu and Boni-Douentza, so it shows the group still contesting central Mali's main road axis (unconfirmed).

ECOWAS and the AES held their first formal ministerial session since the 2025 split, in Dakar on 17 October, and agreed to build a cooperation framework with joint working groups. The talks are about normalising relations, not about the AES rejoining.

Rival jihadist groups are fighting each other and spreading the contest. ADF Magazine, citing the dispute between al-Qaeda- and Islamic State-linked groups, says fighting that began in Liptako-Gourma has reached Niger's Tillabéri and Dosso regions and Nigeria's Kebbi state since April.

At least 23 Chadian soldiers were reported killed in fighting with a local armed group in a gold-rich area. Sahara Reporters gave the figure on 11 November and the government had not confirmed it (unconfirmed).

2. Mali.

Fuel is reaching Bamako again: APA reported on 21 November that 2,261 tanker trucks had arrived since early November. That included a 665-tanker convoy on 7 November and 840 more vehicles in mid-November, which suggests convoys are getting through after JNIM's earlier blockade of fuel imports.

Mali said on 18 November that it had dismantled its main Bamako site for people expelled from Guinea, with 467 still waiting in a youth hall. The report counted 13 deaths and 59 medical evacuations as of 16 November, which points to a strained response.

JNIM's 2 November IED claim targeted a joint Malian-Russian vehicle near Sévaré (unconfirmed). No independent confirmation or casualty figure was found.

Background: ACLED asked in July whether the Malian military is losing to JNIM, after the coordinated JNIM and Azawad Liberation Front offensive of 25 April that took Kidal. The offensive framed Mali's current security position.

3. Burkina Faso.

Analysts describe a lull in large-scale attacks since late June, but attack frequency has not fallen. Attacks are said to be less deadly and concentrated in the north, mostly claimed by JNIM against the army and its civilian volunteer force.

JNIM has reportedly launched strikes on the military and planted IEDs along the RN24, according to an ACLED update cited in search results (unconfirmed, date unclear). I could not open the underlying page.

Burkina Faso's junta does not publish casualty tallies, so all battlefield figures come from jihadist claims, analysts or ACLED, not the authorities.

4. Niger.

ACLED's weekly update for 7 to 13 November describes Islamic State Sahel attacks in the Dioundiou and Téra departments and a JNIM attack in Kirtachi that displaced thousands. The pattern shows both rival groups active in Niger's west at once.

Niger's junta is tightening control of extractives. It reassigned uranium licences held by Orano and GoviEx to domestic firms and signed a deal in September with Madaouela Mining Company to relaunch a northern uranium project.

Earlier this year, Reuters via Yahoo reported over 100 soldiers killed in two attacks in Tahoua and Dosso on 24 and 26 May. Niger's government has not published an official toll, so treat the number as a media figure.

5. Chad.

At least 23 Chadian soldiers were killed and 10 injured in fighting with a local armed group in a gold-rich region, per Sahara Reporters on 11 November (unconfirmed). The government had not confirmed the figures or explained the fighting.

French military aircraft are stopping over in Chad during an exercise in Côte d'Ivoire and Gabon from 8 to 30 November. This follows a defence declaration signed in Paris on 13 October, a quiet return of French military contact after Chad ended its defence pact in 2024.

Chad's president sanctioned military and administrative officials in Lennedi-Ouest, per Alwihda Info on 7 November. I saw only the headline, so the reasons are unknown.

6. Mauritania.

Opposition leader Biram Dah Abeid announced his resignation as a deputy on 9 November, during the plenary confidence vote on the prime minister's general policy statement. Search results gave no further detail on the reason.

France and Mauritania signed a EUR 41 million financing agreement on 11 November for drinking water in Nouakchott and Nouadhibou, a sign of continuing French development ties (unconfirmed, single search summary).

The EU fishing agreement was not renewed when it expired on 14 November, but Mauritanian authorities say the wider partnership continues.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

ECOWAS and the AES agreed in Dakar on 17 October to build a framework covering diplomatic relations, economic ties and development, with joint working groups. It was the first formal ministerial session since Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger left ECOWAS in January 2025.

ECOWAS chief negotiator Lansana Kouyaté said the aim is not to ask the AES to return, and the AES side framed the talks as formalising new relations. That lowers the stakes of the talks.

Russia's foreign minister said Moscow is ready to support normalisation between ECOWAS and the AES, per a meeting with Ghana's counterpart. It shows Moscow positioning itself as a broker.

Analysts note ECOWAS's planned counter-terrorism force will depend on AES cooperation. Without it, the force faces the same limits as past regional missions.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

Fighting between JNIM and Islamic State Sahel has spread from Liptako-Gourma into Tillabéri, Dosso and Kebbi state since April, per ADF Magazine. ADF is a US military-linked outlet, so read its framing with that in mind.

Nigerian troops reportedly killed senior ISWAP commanders in Kukawa, Borno, on or around 1 November, including a named Shura council member (unconfirmed, army-sourced).

In Kebbi, troops said they repelled a Lakurawa attack on a forward base at Maiyama in October and recovered 17 motorcycles and weapons. Lakurawa's links to ISWAP are disputed.

Crisis Group has published a briefing on handling JNIM's threat to the Sahel's urban heartlands. I could not date it from search results.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers (France, US, Turkey, UAE, China)

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest material is older. A War on the Rocks analysis from September says Russia has built air bridges from Syria and Libya and port arrangements in Togo to resupply its Mali deployment.

ADF Magazine argued in March that Africa Corps' hands-off approach in Mali has proven costly. The Sentry's April report alleges abuses by Africa Corps in northern Mali, which are advocacy-group allegations, not verified findings.

France is re-engaging Chad militarily at low profile (see Chad). No new US, Turkish, Emirati or Chinese items turned up.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

UNHCR's 9 October briefing counted nearly 4 million uprooted people across Burkina Faso, Mali and Niger, including over 818,000 refugees, with its Sahel response 27 percent funded against 329.6 dollarsmillion needed.

OCHA estimates 24.3 million people need assistance in 2026, with a 3.7 dollarsbillion appeal only 21 percent funded by mid-May. The shortfall limits what aid can reach blockaded or displaced communities.

Almost 500,000 Burkinabè refugees and asylum-seekers had fled to neighbouring countries in 2026, up from over 300,000 at end-2025.

No November migration-route reporting was found.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Mali's fuel supply is recovering, with 2,261 tankers reaching Bamako since early November.

Niger moved uranium licences from Orano and GoviEx to domestic firms and cancelled gold concessions for Comini, Afrior and Ecomine. It also denied a licence extension to UK firm Savannah on Agadem oil blocks.

The World Bank projects Mauritania's 2026 growth at 4.6 percent, driven by gas and gold, while inflation has accelerated.

Chad's gold-region clash (see Chad) points to the security risks around artisanal and industrial gold zones.

What to watch.
