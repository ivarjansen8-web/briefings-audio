Dozens of civilians killed in Niger's Téra ambush as JNIM presses Mali's army and Sahel juntas show unity.

1. Top stories.

Gunmen killed at least 37 civilians in an ambush near Téra, western Niger, on 12 November (unconfirmed). AFP local sources gave 37 dead and a former local elected official later said 38. The attackers are unidentified. The area borders Mali and Burkina Faso, where JNIM and Islamic State Sahel both operate. Africaradio, 13 November; KOACI, 13 November (reports "at least 30")

JNIM claimed an IED attack on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna on 2 November (unconfirmed). This is a JNIM claim, and I found no independent confirmation of casualties. It shows JNIM still reaches central Mali's main military axis.

Fighting between rival armed groups is reported along the Burkina-Mali border (unconfirmed). An article dated 13 November says two groups have fought increasingly deadly battles there. It also says the Traoré government is struggling to retake territory. The summary names no casualty figures.

2. Mali.

JNIM says it hit a Malian army and Africa Corps vehicle near Sévaré-Konna on 2 November. The claim is unverified, and casualty numbers are not in what I could read.

JNIM's fuel blockade of Bamako, reimposed on 28 April 2026 according to later analyses, is still described as ongoing. Open-source counts cite more than 300 tankers destroyed since it began. Some analysts note that escorted convoys by the army and Africa Corps still get through, so the "siege" is partial.

I found no dated 12 to 13 November reporting from Mali. The items here are the most recent I could verify.

3. Burkina Faso.

Reports of clashes between rival armed groups on the northern border with Mali (unconfirmed). The source is a single aggregator-style article dated 13 November with no figures, so treat it with caution.

Captain Traoré sent a delegation to Niamey on 12 November to offer condolences to Gen. Tiani on the death of Tiani's father. The visit is read as a display of AES solidarity. It comes from a single outlet, so I count it as unconfirmed.

A World Bank 2026 to 2031 partnership framework targets electricity for about 1.9 million people (early October). This is older context, not news from the window.

4. Niger.

At least 37 civilians were killed in an ambush near Téra on 12 November, per AFP local sources (unconfirmed). KOACI's headline gives "at least 30", so the toll varies by outlet. The attackers have not been named.

A coup attempt against Tiani in 2026 is documented, reportedly by junior officers. I could not confirm dates or the aftermath from the snippet. Algeria's president later confirmed Algeria sent fighter jets to Niger during an August coup attempt, per an October newsletter.

Niger is reviewing uranium cooperation with Russia's Rosatom, and a contested stockpile sits at Somaïr (early October). Context for the economy section.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

The AES leaders showed public unity on 12 November, when Traoré sent a delegation to Niamey for Tiani's bereavement (unconfirmed). It is a symbolic item.

In September the three juntas opened their own parliament in Niamey, deepening the split with ECOWAS. The AES also launched a unified joint force in December 2025. Background, not a development in the window.

No ECOWAS-AES negotiation news turned up for 12 to 13 November.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

JNIM attacks have killed about 2,100 people this year, an annual record, per an AFP report citing ACLED data (reported 7 November). The page I saw mixes in unrelated content, so verify the date and figure against the original AFP and ACLED.

JNIM and Islamic State Sahel have been fighting each other as well as the juntas. The Jamestown analysis describes a "fratricidal war" alongside JNIM's expanding offensives. The Burkina-Mali border clashes reported on 13 November may fit this pattern, but nothing in my sources says so.

A UN Secretary-General report covering November 2025 to June 2026 says JNIM launched attacks in northern Benin, and Togo's Savanes region has seen repeated displacement from armed attacks and IEDs. These are older data and not new events.

I found no dated ISWAP or Nigeria incident for the last 72 hours.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers (France, US, Turkey, UAE, China)

Africa Corps personnel were reportedly in the vehicle targeted on 2 November near Sévaré (JNIM claim, unconfirmed). Africa Corps and the Malian army are also credited with escorting fuel convoys.

Niger is reviewing its uranium cooperation with Rosatom (early October), and Algeria's jets reportedly supported Niamey during the August coup attempt. Both show competing outside influence around Niamey.

Quiet: I found no dated reporting on France, the US, Turkey, the UAE or China in the last 72 hours.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

OCHA's 2026 Sahel overview, published 3 June, estimates 24.3 million people need aid, and funding is at a ten-year low. At end-2025 the region had 5.8 million forcibly displaced people.

More than 66,000 refugees from Nigeria have reached Niger and Benin, with a UNHCR appeal covering November 2026 to January 2027 (unconfirmed; headline and summary only).

The Téra ambush adds to civilian risk on the Niger-Mali-Burkina tri-border market routes. My inference, not a sourced claim.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Niger is reported to be handing roughly 95,000 tonnes of uranium concentrate back to Orano, and Mali is moving to extend Barrick's Loulo-Gounkoto licence by ten years. The article is undated in my results, and sources conflict on Barrick's tax figures (430 million dollars settled versus 500 million dollars disputed). Check the original before relying on it.

The Bamako fuel blockade keeps pressure on Mali's economy, with more than 300 tankers destroyed according to open-source counts.

The EU-Mauritania fisheries protocol expires in mid-November.
