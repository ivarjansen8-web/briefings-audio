Here is your Sahel briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

JNIM and Islamic State fight each other on Burkina's Mali border as AES-ECOWAS talks resume.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 18 November 2026 (widened well beyond 72 hours: search surfaced almost nothing dated 19 to 21 November, and several outlets, including Al Jazeera and Rio Times, could not be opened directly, so those items rest on search summaries). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Burkina Faso's two main jihadist groups, JNIM and Islamic State Sahel, are fighting each other in increasingly deadly battles along the northern border with Mali. This matters because rival-group violence signals the state has lost ground there, not that it is gaining.

JNIM is launching what ACLED calls unprecedented strikes on Burkina Faso's military, hitting population centres and transit routes and planting IEDs on the RN24. ACLED's weekly update shows the pressure is on logistics, not only on remote posts.

Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger resumed dialogue with ECOWAS in October and agreed to work toward a "framework agreement" on cooperation. It would be the first formal channel since the AES states left the bloc.

Nigerian soldiers are being killed in repeated attacks on bases in Borno, and attacks resumed in southern Borno on 17 November. Local reporting is the main source and claims on both sides are unverified.

2. Mali.

JNIM and the Tuareg-led FLA are increasingly carrying out attacks in northwest Mali, and JNIM is also targeting Dozo militias in the centre. This pairing of an Islamist and a separatist force is the most dangerous development for Bamako.

Background: JNIM announced a full blockade of Bamako on 28 April 2026 after the fuel blockade had eased earlier in the year. The latest dated figure I found (Rio Times, 15 June, citing more than 300 tankers burned) is five months old, so the current state of fuel supply is not confirmed in this briefing.

Fighting in August and July: at least ten soldiers were killed in an attack on a base in central Mali (AFP, 9 August), and JNIM and the FLA reportedly killed at least 50 soldiers in a convoy attack on 18 July. Both are older and shown only for trend.

3. Burkina Faso.

JNIM and Islamic State Sahel are clashing along the Mali border, according to Al Jazeera's 13 November report. It argues the state is losing ground there, and that is also the pattern in ACLED's data.

JNIM is hitting the army, towns and the RN24 road with IEDs, per ACLED. ACLED's wording is "unprecedented", which is its characterisation, not an independently verified count.

The Global Centre for the Responsibility to Protect says the UN human rights office will permanently close its Burkina Faso office by 30 November, after its activities were suspended in February. It also lists more than twenty arrest warrants against journalists, activists and former officials.

The junta dissolved political parties on 29 January 2026. That is background for why no civilian channel remains for contesting security policy.

4. Niger.

Islamic State Sahel (ISSP) attacked in the Dioundiou and Tera departments in the week of 7 to 13 November, and a JNIM attack in Kirtachi commune displaced thousands, per ACLED. The numbers of dead are not given in the summary I could access.

Niger signed a memorandum of understanding with eastern Libya's commander Khalifa Haftar to expand bilateral cooperation. It points to Niamey hedging toward Libya's east for security and trade routes.

The US has signalled it will pursue pragmatic relations with Niger's junta, according to CFR. (unconfirmed: single-source summary)

5. Chad.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Latest items: former prime minister Succès Masra left Chad for medical treatment around 22 October, two months after a presidential pardon freed him and eight other opposition figures.

President Mahamat Déby toured several provinces on security matters in early November, according to an APA bulletin dated 7 November. (unconfirmed: bulletin only seen as a search snippet.)

6. Mauritania.

Opposition leader Biram Dah Abeid announced his resignation as a member of parliament on 9 November, during the confidence vote on the prime minister's general policy statement. The move reshapes the opposition's role ahead of local contests. (unconfirmed: single source)

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

AES foreign ministers met in Ouagadougou on 11 to 12 November to review their joint confederation roadmap ahead of a leaders' session. Rio Times is the only source I found, so the outcome is not confirmed. (unconfirmed)

The three juntas resumed dialogue with ECOWAS in October. They want a framework on trade and movement after their January 2025 exit.

Niger's state news agency reported the AES confederal parliament held its inaugural session in Niamey from 24 to 29 August; independent confirmation was not available. It is junta-controlled media, so treat it with caution. (unconfirmed)

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

Suspected Boko Haram fighters renewed attacks on military positions in southern Borno, with one incident on Yamtake Road in Gwoza on 17 November at about 10:00, according to the Borno South Youth Alliance. No casualty figures were given and the source is an advocacy group. (unconfirmed)

Nigerian troops reported killing two ISWAP fighters after an attack on a position in Adamawa on 9 November. The army's account is the only one available.

In October, about 15 soldiers were reportedly killed in Borno (Daily Trust said 10 soldiers and four civilians), and the army said it killed 48 ISWAP fighters in a five-hour battle in Yobe on 30 October. Casualty claims conflict and come from the army or press reports.

Per ACLED as summarised in press, Q2 2026 was Nigeria's most violent quarter since its records began in 1997.

I found no dated reporting on coastal West Africa (Benin, Togo) for this window.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers (France, US, Turkey, UAE, China)

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Wagner's Sahel operations were absorbed into the Russian Defence Ministry's Africa Corps, and analysts say the juntas lean on Russia as security worsens.

CFR says Washington signalled pragmatic ties with Niger's junta. (unconfirmed)

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

About 4.5 million people in Burkina Faso need humanitarian aid in 2026, according to UN planning figures cited by Al Jazeera. Al Jazeera cautions that this should not be read as a newly verified count.

The JNIM attack in Niger's Kirtachi commune displaced thousands of residents, per ACLED.

The closure of the UN human rights office in Burkina Faso by 30 November narrows outside monitoring of abuses.

No dated reporting on migration routes found for this window.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The only recent material is background on Mali's fuel blockade (see section 2); I found no dated November reporting on mining, gold or uranium.
