1. Top stories.

Burkina Faso says it has received 70,734 nationals leaving Guinea, including more than 14,000 children, as of 18 November (unconfirmed, single-source summary). It points to a sizeable return flow with regional diplomatic and humanitarian weight.

Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger ministers met in Ouagadougou on 11 to 12 November to review progress on the AES confederation roadmap. It shows the bloc is still building institutions despite the security crisis.

A Crisis Group analysis, listed as about four days old, reports (without confirmation from either side) that Mali's junta freed militants in exchange for JNIM easing its Bamako blockade. Fuel convoys reportedly still need military escorts.

JNIM and Islamic State Sahel Province (ISSP) have fought a turf war since April 2026 that has reached Niger's Tillaberi and Dosso regions and Nigeria's Kebbi state, per Africa Defense Forum (a US military-funded outlet). It raises spillover risk toward the coast.

2. Mali.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on 2 November on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sevare and Konna (JNIM claim, unconfirmed). It fits a pattern of roadside attacks in the centre.

Crisis Group says JNIM declared a "total blockade" of Bamako after its late-April offensive on Bamako, Kati, Mopti, Gao and Kidal, coordinated with Tuareg FLA rebels. The status of the blockade today is disputed across sources.

Earlier in autumn, AFP reported at least 50 soldiers and five Africa Corps personnel killed in an attack on a central army base (mid-September). This is background, not new.

3. Burkina Faso.

ACLED weekly coverage describes JNIM attacks on major towns and transit routes, IEDs along the RN24, and competition with ISSP for territory. Figures are not given in the summary I could access.

The UN human rights office is reported to be closing its Burkina Faso office permanently by 30 November, after its activities were suspended in February. Monitoring of abuses will be weaker.

Returns from Guinea have reached Bobo-Dioulasso (see Top stories; unconfirmed).

4. Niger.

ACLED reports ISSP attacks in the Dioundiou and Tera departments and a JNIM attack that displaced thousands in Kirtachi commune. Dates were not visible in the summary.

Niger's National Labour Council opened its first ordinary session in Niamey on 18 November (unconfirmed).

Rio Times reported in September that Africa Corps helped Niger's presidential guard end a mutiny at a Niamey base in late August, per Russia's ambassador (single-source, unconfirmed).
