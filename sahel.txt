Here is your Sahel briefing for Monday, 9 November.

Goïta tours Niamey and Ouagadougou after Moscow summit; JNIM claims IED on Africa Corps convoy.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 6 November 2026 (widened well beyond 72 hours because search returned little fresh reporting). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. This edition is thin: treat absences as gaps in retrieval, not as evidence that nothing happened.

1. Top stories.

Mali's junta leader Assimi Goïta visited Niamey and Ouagadougou on 5 November, with no official communiqué. The trip, reported from sources close to the meetings, follows his late-October attendance at the Russia-Africa summit in Moscow and signals AES coordination on Russia and Algeria policy (unconfirmed, single-source relay).

JNIM claimed an IED attack on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel on the morning of 2 November. The claim is by the group itself; casualties were not independently verified (unconfirmed).

Niger's Tiani reportedly told Goïta that Niamey's rapprochement with Algeria is not directed against the AES. It matters because Algeria-Mali relations are strained and the AES is trying to hold a common line (unconfirmed).

2. Mali.

JNIM says it hit a joint Malian army and Africa Corps vehicle with an IED on 2 November. This continues the pattern of attacks on Russian-backed convoys; the claim is JNIM's own (unconfirmed).

Background, not new: JNIM and Tuareg FLA launched coordinated offensives from 25 April, and Goïta took the defence portfolio on 4 May. The fuel blockade of Bamako was reported as reimposed in July; I found no current-status report for November.
