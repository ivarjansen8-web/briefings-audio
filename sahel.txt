1. Top stories.

Niger and Chad are trading accusations over a deadly drone strike, and Algeria is mediating. Algeria's foreign minister reportedly carried letters from President Tebboune to the Chadian and Nigerien leaders on 3 to 4 November; it is unclear whether Tiani and Déby will speak directly. A Niger-Chad rift weakens the Sahel's two main non-AES security partners' ability to coordinate on Lake Chad and the Libyan border. The claim is attributed to Le Monde and relayed by Morocco World News; the same report says Algerian soldiers may come only for one-off missions, not a permanent presence. If true, it marks Algiers' return to Niger after the 2023 to 25 chill. Morocco World News, November (unconfirmed).

2. Mali.

JNIM's fuel blockade of Bamako, reimposed on 28 April 2026 after an early-2026 easing, is described by analysts as continuing. Open-source counts cited by one outlet put destroyed fuel tankers above 300, with queues, rationing and school closures. The capital is operating under siege-like conditions. The Opscon, 5 July; Wikipedia, Mali fuel blockade. I found no November-dated update on the blockade, so its current state is unverified.

JNIM and the Tuareg FLA launched coordinated attacks on 25 April, including around Bamako, Kati and Sévaré, and later took most of Anefis (July situation report). Analysts called it the most coordinated offensive since 2012. Allafrica, April; Stratfor, July. This is background; nothing comparable was found for the last 72 hours.

Bamako publicly rejected reports of a US role in the anti-JNIM fight in late July. The government called the claims false. Africanews, 24 July. Background only.
