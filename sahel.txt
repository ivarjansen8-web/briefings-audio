Here is your Sahel briefing for Friday, 27 November.

Mali's JNIM issues Tonka blockade ultimatum; 37 civilians killed in Niger's Téra area; AES-ECOWAS talks stall.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 26 November 2026 (most sections widened beyond 24 hours; see notes). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. Note: most outlet pages could not be fetched in this session (network allow-list), so items rest on search-result summaries, not full-article reads.

1. Top stories.

JNIM gave residents of Tonka (Timbuktu region, Mali) until 27 November to leave before it blockades the town. The ultimatum follows the arrival of Malian army and Russian Africa Corps troops, and extends JNIM's blockade strategy, used on fuel convoys since September 2025, to the north.

At least 37 civilians were reported killed by "armed men" in western Niger around 12 November. The attack was near Téra in Tillabéri, a recurring hotspot; no perpetrator was named in the summary I could see.

Burkina Faso's JNIM and Islamic State Sahel are reported fighting each other, per Al Jazeera. Clashes intensified around Ganadaouri (Oudalan) on 9 October; this may ease pressure on the state, which Al Jazeera says is losing ground.

AES-ECOWAS talks continue without a deal. One outlet reports no final agreement or new visa/border measures as of 25 November, and the Sahel side says the talks are not about returning to ECOWAS.

2. Mali.

JNIM's Tonka ultimatum expires today, 27 November. It targets a Timbuktu-region town newly garrisoned by the army and Africa Corps; whether a blockade follows is the key near-term indicator.

The army and JNIM traded competing strike claims in central Mali around 18 November (Léré, Kadiana, Boulkessi). Neither side's claims are independently verified, and one report says four central villages emptied (unconfirmed).

Malian and Russian forces reportedly hold Kidal, Anefis and Aguelhok but not Tessalit or Tinzaouaten; the FLA has not laid down arms. An FLA claim of strikes on Anefis on 5 November is unverified.

JNIM claimed Russian airstrikes hit civilians in Tamardant (Kidal) and an Africa Corps strike hit Ti-n-Essako on 23 November (unconfirmed). These are militant-side or single-source claims.

Analysts say JNIM in central Mali has shifted toward administration (taxes, dispute mediation). This suggests entrenchment rather than only raiding.

3. Burkina Faso.

Quiet on fresh battlefield reporting: I found no dated attack report for the last 72 hours, so this section widens to November. The junta restricts media, so independent confirmation is thin.

Human Rights Watch (4 November) documented a JNIM attack on 1 September on a gold-mining site at Soubo, killing 24 people. JNIM acknowledged it but says the victims were VDP auxiliaries, not civilians.

ACLED counted 125 JNIM attacks in Burkina Faso from January to August 2026, with 188 civilians killed (per the search summary of HRW/ACLED data).

Burkina Faso reportedly restored the death penalty (date not confirmed in my results). It fits a pattern of junta tightening.

4. Niger.

At least 37 civilians killed near Téra, Tillabéri, per AFP (about 12 to 13 November). Attackers are described only as "armed men".

Dawn reports an attack on a military detachment in Téra killing 16 soldiers and wounding 23, per Niger's state news agency (date unconfirmed, possibly November; could be a July event). Treat as (unconfirmed).

Tiani and Goïta pledged at an AES meeting in early November to intensify counter-terrorism. This is junta-aligned messaging.

Background: a soldiers' mutiny at Niamey's airbase on 29 August was followed by purges and a new army chief of staff, Gen. Mamane Sani Kiaou (11 September). Regime cohesion remains a question.

5. Chad.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. My searches returned no recent Chad reporting.

6. Mauritania.

Mauritania's terrorism prosecutor has brought proceedings against 24 people for conspiracy against state security, incitement to hatred, disclosure of military information, false news and money laundering (date not confirmed). It signals tight domestic security control.

Otherwise quiet: nothing else significant found in the last 72 hours.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

AES and ECOWAS opened formal talks in October (Dakar), with no framework deal reported as of 25 November. Sources differ on dates (17 versus 20 October).

AES ministers met in Ouagadougou on 11 to 12 November to review the year-two roadmap. They condemned terrorism and regretted Guinea's expulsions of African nationals; the roadmap content was not published per one source.

The AES launched a joint channel, Tafouk TV, to push a sovereignty-focused narrative. Treat its output as junta-controlled media.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

Boko Haram/ISWAP pressure on Nigerian bases persists in the northeast. A local youth group reported attacks on military formations in Gwoza LGA on 17 November (unconfirmed; no army confirmation found).

Nigerian troops reportedly killed an ISWAP Shura Council member in Borno on or about 1 November (single-source).

Context: about 15 soldiers were reportedly killed at Mairari, Borno, in mid-October, and the army claimed 48 fighters killed in Yobe on 30 October. The latter is a military claim.

Coastal West Africa: no dated reporting found in this run.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers (France, US, Turkey, UAE, China)

Africa Corps is operating alongside the Malian army in Kidal region and Tonka. Claims of its strikes on civilians come from JNIM and are unverified.

Earlier in the year, Africa Corps disputed JNIM's claim to have taken Hombori base during the 25 April offensive, in which Defence Minister Sadio Camara was reportedly killed.

Nothing found on France, US, Turkey, UAE or China in the last 72 hours.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

Villages in central Mali have reportedly emptied after clashes around Léré and Boulkessi (unconfirmed).

An explainer cites roughly 4 million people uprooted across the AES states. Not a fresh figure.

Quiet on migration routes: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

JNIM's fuel-convoy blockade strategy, in place since September 2025, remains a drag on Mali's supply routes. The Tonka ultimatum suggests the tactic is spreading.

Burkina Faso opened its first gold refinery, Raffinor, in Ouagadougou on 28 September. The presidency framed it as economic independence.

Mauritania reportedly ordered a European fishing fleet out of its waters (date unconfirmed).

What to watch.

Coming days: whether JNIM or the FLA targets Tessalit and Tinzaouaten, which are outside government control.
