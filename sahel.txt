Here is your Sahel briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

Niger reports 13 soldiers killed in Tillabéri; Burkina's JNIM-ISSP war; Barrick-Mali talks resume.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Method note: direct page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked for most outlets in this run, so every item below rests on search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not on a full read. Very little indexed reporting was dated within the last 24 to 72 hours, so most sections reach back up to three weeks and say so. Treat all figures as provisional and check the linked pages.

1. Top stories.

Thirteen Nigerien soldiers were reportedly killed in an attack in Tillabéri, according to an allAfrica press review dated 27 November (unconfirmed). I could not find a second source or the underlying report, and a targeted search surfaced only earlier 13-fatality incidents, so the date and toll need checking; Tillabéri remains Niger's deadliest theatre.

The AES states validated three joint health-cooperation mechanisms on 27 November, per the same press review (unconfirmed). It is a small but concrete step in building parallel institutions to ECOWAS.

Barrick's new head of non-North American mining reportedly met Mali's prime minister in Bamako on 25 November (unconfirmed). The meeting is described as the first since the two-year gold dispute and reportedly included a site visit to Loulo-Gounkoto.

Burkina Faso has seen no large-scale jihadist attack since late June, AFP reported on 6 November, but analysts call the lull deceptive. Attack frequency stays high while lethality has dropped, and JNIM's rivalry with Islamic State Sahel may be absorbing fighters.

Mauritanian opposition leader Biram Dah Abeid resigned his parliamentary seat on 9 November. He announced it during the prime minister's policy-statement debate, a sharp signal of political tension in a state otherwise seen as the Sahel's stable exception.

2. Mali.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on 2 November on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna. This is a JNIM claim; no casualty figure was given in the summary I saw, and the claim is not independently verified.

Mali's council of ministers met on 27 November and announced a national exports day for 7 December in Bamako. The communiqué comes from junta-controlled channels and shows the government projecting normality despite the war.

The wider context is the April 2026 JNIM and FLA offensive, which the US National Counterterrorism Center says took Kidal and hit sites near Bamako. Mauritania's president called on 10 November for solidarity with Mali, which he described as a war reshaping the region (see section 6).

No new battlefield reporting from the last 72 hours was retrievable. Mali is therefore effectively Quiet in the sources available to me, which is an artefact of my access and not proof of calm.

3. Burkina Faso.

No large-scale jihadist attack since late June, per AFP (6 November), and state media reported a JNIM "terrorist leader" killed in late October. The kill claim comes from Burkinabè state media and is unverified.

JNIM and Islamic State Sahel are fighting each other in Burkina, with the state losing ground, Al Jazeera reported on 13 November. The rivalry could buy Ouagadougou breathing room if JNIM diverts forces to defend its positions.

Human Rights Watch said on 4 November that an al-Qaeda-linked group killed at least 27 civilians in two September attacks in Yatenga province. This is an HRW finding about earlier events and underlines continued civilian targeting.

HRW's April report accuses all parties of war crimes, and the Traoré government rejects that the country is in an armed conflict. It matters for any future accountability debate.

4. Niger.

Thirteen soldiers were reportedly killed in Tillabéri, per an allAfrica review dated 27 November (unconfirmed). Niger's defence ministry usually issues tolls days later, so the figure may change.

The most recent confirmed large attack I could find was on 17 July near Doungourou on the Bankilaré-Téra axis, where the defence ministry reported 16 soldiers killed and 23 wounded. This is a government figure and shows the pattern in Tillabéri.

Niamey hosted the first AES ministerial conference on education on 10 to 11 November. It adds to the confederation's institution-building.

No other Niger developments within 72 hours could be retrieved.

5. Chad.

President Mahamat Idriss Déby began a security tour of provinces on 7 November and suspended General Hamoda Mahamat Béchir, commander of defence zone no. 8, at Torboul in Ennedi Ouest. The decision was reported by Chadian outlets close to the presidency and signals unease over security in the north-east.

Déby inspected infrastructure projects in Abéché on 11 to 12 November. The stop is near the Sudan border, where refugee inflows strain the east.

A partial legislative election is reportedly set for 13 December in Assoungha department (unconfirmed). It is a minor vote with little weight on the national balance.

Alwihda reports a "triumph" for Chad at an extraordinary ECCAS summit, and the 27 November press review says chairmanship passes to Chad (unconfirmed, sources conflict on the date and the post). Alwihda is Chadian and sympathetic to the government.

6. Mauritania.

Biram Dah Abeid resigned as MP on 9 November during the prime minister's confidence debate. He leads the best-known anti-slavery and opposition movement, so the move reshapes the opposition.

Prime Minister El Moctar Ould Djay was reappointed after resigning with his government, and a 29-minister cabinet was formed (dates unclear in my sources). One report ties the resignation to a Somelec utility fire (unconfirmed, single source).

President Ghazouani called for solidarity with Mali on 10 November, describing the war as upsetting the whole region's balance. The year of the source is unclear.

Debate over a possible third term for Ghazouani has complicated national dialogue, per a June dispatch. It frames the domestic politics behind the opposition's moves.

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

AES members validated three shared health mechanisms on 27 November (unconfirmed, single source). It continues the drive toward joint institutions.

The AES held its first education ministers' conference in Niamey on 10 to 11 November. Together with the health step, it points to a sector-by-sector build-out.

Earlier this year the AES launched a unified military force of about 5,000 troops and named Burkina's Ibrahim Traoré head of the alliance at the December 2025 Bamako summit. Older context, but still the baseline for the ECOWAS split.

ECOWAS is deploying a regional standby force, per Al Jazeera in March. It remains a counterweight that the AES rejects.

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover to coastal West Africa and Nigeria.

JNIM and Islamic State Sahel are in open conflict, with Al Jazeera (13 November) arguing the state is losing ground as they fight each other. For analysts it is the key structural dynamic in the central Sahel.

Suspected Boko Haram fighters renewed attacks on military sites in southern Borno, the latest on 17 November along Yamtake Road in Gwoza, according to an advocacy group's account. No casualty figure or army confirmation was found (unconfirmed).

Nigerian troops said they killed 48 ISWAP fighters in a five-hour battle in Yobe on 30 October. This is a military claim, unverified.

ISWAP reportedly killed about 15 soldiers in a mid-October base attack in Borno, after which the army chief visited troops. It shows ISWAP's continued base-overrun capability.

No coastal West Africa (Benin, Togo) incident from the last 72 hours was retrievable.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers (France, US, Turkey, UAE, China)

Africa Corps personnel were the target of the 2 November JNIM IED claim near Sévaré-Konna. It shows Russian forces sharing the exposure of Malian convoys.

French military aircraft are to make a technical stopover in Chad linked to an exercise running 8 to 30 November in Côte d'Ivoire and Gabon (single source, unconfirmed). Chad ended its defence accord with France in 2024, so even a brief stop is politically notable.

About 2,000 Russian personnel were reported across the three AES states as of March. An older estimate.

Quiet: nothing found on the US, Turkey, UAE or China in the last 72 hours.

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

The regional food-security body CILSS warned of fodder and water deficits affecting over 60% of the Sahelian zone (per the 27 November allAfrica review, unconfirmed). It points to a hard pastoral dry season.

OCHA estimated about 24.3 million people in the Sahel needing urgent aid in 2026. This is a UN figure from early in the year; aid funding is reportedly at a ten-year low.

UNHCR called on 9 October for urgent investment as displacement deepens across the Central Sahel. Only the headline was visible to me.

Nothing on migration routes within 72 hours was retrievable.

11. Economy, mining and energy.

Barrick and Mali's government appear to be restarting dialogue after the 2025 settlement, with a reported 25 November meeting in Bamako (unconfirmed). Mali's gold revenue depends on the complex's recovery.

Barrick signed a collective agreement with unions at Loulo-Gounkoto in late September, easing a strike threat. It removes one operational risk.

The IMF warned on 12 November of financing and debt risks in the CEMAC zone (Chad's monetary area). It is context for Chad's budget pressures.

A Franco-Mauritanian business forum was reported for 26 November in Nouakchott, year unconfirmed.
