Sahel: JNIM IED near Konna, HRW on Burkina civilian killings, Chad weighs MNJTF exit.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 16 November 2026 (widened well beyond 24 hours because few current reports were retrievable). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Coverage note: Search returned little dated reporting for the last 24 to 72 hours. Many outlets (RFI, Africanews, HRW, Buttondown) could not be fetched from this session, so several items below rest on search snippets only. Sections with nothing verifiable say so rather than filling space.

1. Top stories.

JNIM claimed an IED attack on 2 November on a vehicle carrying Malian army and Russian Africa Corps personnel between Sévaré and Konna (unconfirmed). It fits the group's pattern of targeting the Mopti axis and the junta's Russian partners.

Human Rights Watch said on 4 November that JNIM (GSIM) killed at least 27 civilians in two September attacks in Yatenga province, Burkina Faso. HRW also cites army and VDP militia abuses against Fulani civilians, so civilians are squeezed from both sides.

Chad's president began a security tour of several provinces on 7 November after a recent attack on the army in Lac province, per Tchadinfos. The article says he launched Operation Haskanite and considered withdrawing Chad from the Multinational Joint Task Force, which would weaken the Lake Chad front.

2. Mali.

JNIM claimed the 2 November IED attack near Konna (unconfirmed, claim by the group itself). No casualty figure was found.

JNIM's fuel blockade, first announced in September 2025 and reimposed in April 2026 according to one analysis, remains the central strain on Bamako (unconfirmed for current status). I could not find a dated mid-November report, so current fuel conditions are unknown.

Quiet: no other verifiable Mali developments in the last 72 hours.

3. Burkina Faso.

HRW reports GSIM killed at least 27 civilians in two attacks in Yatenga in September 2026. The report adds that 24 civilians died in a 1 September attack on the Soubo gold-mining site and cites ACLED counting 125 JNIM attacks in Burkina Faso from January to August 2026 (figures attributed to HRW/ACLED).

A Sidwaya listing dated 3 November mentions the army "eliminating" a terrorist leader named Saïdou Diallo (unconfirmed; snippet only; Sidwaya is state media). Treat as an official claim without independent verification.

4. Niger.

Quiet: nothing significant verified in the last 72 hours.

Background: Niger's dispute with Orano over the nationalised Somaïr uranium continued into 2026. On 5 May Niger denounced Orano's legal proceedings as near "legal harassment," per AFP via Boursorama; it is a useful baseline for any new ruling.

5. Chad.

Chad's president started a tour of several provinces on 7 November, per a presidency statement relayed by Tchadinfos. It follows an attack on the army in Lac province.

The third Lake Chad Basin development forum opened in N'Djamena on 5 November, against the backdrop of that attack. The same coverage says Operation Haskanite was launched and a withdrawal from the MNJTF was considered (unconfirmed, single source; I could not confirm which of two Tchadinfos links carries which detail).

6. Mauritania.

UNHCR counted 190,815 registered refugees and asylum seekers in Mauritania on 30 September 2026, mostly Malians in Hodh Chargui (as reported by the Rio Times, 28 October). Nouakchott bears the spillover of the Malian war.

Mauritania is lobbying at the Francophonie summit (14 to 16 November, Phnom Penh) for its candidate Coumba Bâ (unconfirmed, single source).

7. Alliance of Sahel States (AES), ECOWAS and regional diplomacy.

Quiet: no verifiable AES or ECOWAS development dated in the last 72 hours.

Background: the AES joint force and a confederal parliament are being built. Reports say the parliament met in Niamey in late August 2026 (state-media sourced, unconfirmed).

8. Jihadist groups (JNIM, Islamic State Sahel, ISWAP) and spillover.

JNIM remains the main driver of violence across Mali and Burkina Faso, per the November items above. ACLED-based reporting also finds JNIM and IS Sahel expanding in the Benin-Niger-Nigeria borderlands.

Background: 15 soldiers were killed in northern Benin in March 2026, and jihadists killed an army general in Borno in April. I found no November events for the coastal states or Nigeria.

9. Russia's Africa Corps and other outside powers.

The 2 November claimed IED attack involved Africa Corps personnel (unconfirmed).

10. Humanitarian situation and migration routes.

Mauritanian pushbacks tied to an EU deal sharply cut arrivals in Europe, per Al Jazeera (April). Migrants are hiding as deportations to Mali and Senegal continue.
