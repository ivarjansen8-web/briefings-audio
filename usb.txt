Here is your US briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

Trump heads to Xi-Putin summit as Iran talks stall; Miller takes over as chief of staff.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 17 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump is flying to Asia for a first-ever Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral on 19 November on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen. Nuclear arms reduction and the Iran war are on the agenda, but officials expect no agreement and Beijing has called joining disarmament talks "neither fair, reasonable nor realistic".

Stephen Miller became White House chief of staff on 9 November, replacing Susie Wiles after the GOP midterm losses. The Washington Post reports the move will likely push the agenda further right, and a GOP lawmaker called it a "mistake".

US-Iran diplomacy is stalled while a third carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, arrived in the region. Iran has passed seven conditions to the US through Qatar, including sanctions relief and an end to the naval blockade, and awaits an answer.

A nationwide FAA ground stop on 13 November cancelled more than 12,000 flights. Transportation Secretary Duffy blamed a contractor router swap during a copper-to-fiber upgrade and said there was no sign of a cyberattack; Trump blamed the Biden administration.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November after admitting an affair with a woman US intelligence officials deemed a Chinese spy. Trump said he "would not mind" Huang returning, while Treasury Secretary Bessent wants an outside CEO and the board is not considering a return; Nvidia reports earnings today after the close.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump defended his White House renovations on 16 November amid the FAA chaos, and clashed with NBC's Yamiche Alcindor. Fox and the Washington Times covered it as a combative exchange, and critics say it shows misplaced priorities.

Wiles stays on for political operations after leaving as chief of staff on 6 November. Trump announced the switch days after Republican losses and reportedly was furious at the results.

The White House has warned congressional Democrats that investigating the administration may be a crime (unconfirmed, single aggregated report). It previews the oversight fight once Democrats take the House in January.

Trump blamed Fox News for the midterm losses, and Vice President Vance said anger over the Iran war was the "real reason". The split in explanations shows how the right is arguing over the result.

Trump told advisers he may hold off further Iran strikes unless Tehran or the Houthis provoke the US. Reuters, via Gulf News, says he also posted on 12 November that the US does not need a deal.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The House killed Rep. Thomas Massie's Hegseth impeachment resolution on 10 November, 211 to 204. The vote referred it to Judiciary; Massie was the only Republican voting no, 11 Democrats missed the vote, and Jeffries called it "just the beginning".

The lame-duck session faces a 11 December funding deadline, and a stopgap looks likelier than a full-year package. Some Democrats want to use it to block Iran war funds, while conservatives want a third reconciliation bill for the Pentagon, which Thune says lacks the 50 votes.

Senate Democrats are fighting over Schumer's leadership, with Chris Van Hollen saying he can block a fast-track to cement Schumer. A Punchbowl tracker showed 14 senators for Schumer, five for Van Hollen and 36 uncommitted; Israel policy is part of the dispute.

House Democrats meet Wednesday 18 November to nominate Hakeem Jeffries for speaker. Punchbowl expects no rank-and-file defections.

The NDAA must pass before year-end and a debt-limit increase is being weighed. Defense fights are heating up in the lame duck.

The Senate's last recorded roll call was 12 November, a 49 to 26 cloture vote on Anna St. John for the Fifth Circuit. No votes were recorded as of 16 November.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court on 13 November agreed to hear the administration's suspension of asylum at the southern border. It took two of three questions (legality of the ban and class-wide relief); the restrictions stay in force, and a ruling is expected by end of June.

On 16 November the Court refused the administration's appeal over DEI-linked research grants to University of California researchers. No justice dissented, leaving in place a ruling that the cuts violated the First Amendment.

The Court also turned away a Second Amendment challenge to "sensitive places" gun bans. The denial comes on the same order list.

A Massachusetts federal judge struck down DHS's rule capping foreign students' stay at four years on 16 November. Judge Dennis Saylor called it "arbitrary and capricious".

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval averages about 36% with roughly 61% disapproving, and Democrats lead the generic ballot by about 8.6 points. Aggregator pages conflict on seat counts, so treat this as pre-certification data.

Alaska's Senate race is still uncertified, with Mary Peltola at 49.98% to Dan Sullivan's 45.93% in first-choice votes. Sullivan's campaign says it will not concede until certification, and ranked-choice tabulation may decide it.

Democrats flipped five governorships in the midterms. NBC's live blog framed the night as voters rejecting Trump's GOP.

Republicans are split on the Iran war's role in their losses, with a Reuters/Ipsos poll finding 71% want Congress to legislate an end to it. That includes 51% of Republicans.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Iran's seven conditions for a deal include lifting sanctions, ending the US naval blockade, unfreezing assets and a ceasefire on all fronts including Lebanon and Gaza. NPR says Trump's strategy is effectively to keep things as they are, and Iran bars UN inspectors from damaged sites until the war ends.

Six tankers reportedly struck mines and burned in the Strait of Hormuz (Iran's claim, via Drop Site; unconfirmed). Trump accuses Iran of inventing ship strikes to push up oil prices; ISW says Iran keeps attacking shipping but shows no sign of re-mining the southern route.

Houthi forces are advancing in Yemen toward Jabal Kharaz, about 100 km from Aden, and Saudi air defences reportedly intercepted two Houthi drones near Riyadh and Mecca on 16 November. The Houthis had not claimed them.

Bahrain says it intercepted Iranian threats after the IRGC claimed a strike on a radar at a Bahraini air base, retaliating for a US strike on an Iranian telecom tower. The tit-for-tat shows the ceasefire is fragile.

The US-China trade truce was reportedly extended on 9 November to 10 January 2027. It frames the Shenzhen meeting.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

The Pentagon is preparing its own prosecution of Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the Joint Staff's top planner, fired last month over alleged leaks. Axios cites an internal report saying he admitted leaking classified Ukraine and Iran material (unconfirmed); he is reported to deny it, and no charges exist.

The Freedom of the Press Foundation and The Intercept sued the Pentagon on 16 November over records on an alleged journalist "blacklist". It adds to disputes over Hegseth's press-access rules.

CREW, Public Citizen and POGO sued on 2 November over Hegseth's Project Meridian advisory committee. They allege Federal Advisory Committee Act violations; the claims are unproven.

Reports say Hegseth ignored warnings before six US troops died, then pushed out officers who spoke up (unconfirmed, from partisan outlets). Spokesman Sean Parnell called the claim "disgusting".

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Israeli intelligence reportedly warned the CIA of an Iranian plot to hit Trump and senior officials with explosive drones (unconfirmed). Israel Hayom says officials were moved to bases and Trump scaled back appearances; Tehran calls it disinformation and the White House and CIA have not confirmed.

US officials estimate Iran retains 1,500 to 2,500 missiles, a low-confidence figure per the Wall Street Journal. Just Security relayed it on 16 November.

Nvidia is investigating, via Wachtell Lipton, whether national security or trade secrets were compromised in the Huang affair. The US intelligence assessment of the woman involved is reported, not independently verified.

Open-source (unverified): ISW says Hezbollah is trying to rebuild its drone arsenal, though it cannot verify Israeli stockpile estimates. ISW also sees none of the public threats that preceded the February strikes on Iran.

Open-source (Bellingcat, 5 November): public mapping platforms reveal sensitive details of the US bases Iran targeted. An earlier Bellingcat piece noted 24-hour delays to EU satellite imagery over Gaza and parts of the West Bank.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Vanguard raised its year-end 2026 core inflation forecast to 3.2% and expects a Fed rate hike in early 2027. It cites tariffs, energy and AI-related demand; the Fed held in October.

The Fed's September minutes said inflation progress was insufficient and flagged further tariff increases as an upside risk. Commentators dispute how much tariffs drive prices.

The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February, and the temporary Section 122 duty's current status is unclear (sources conflict). Section 232 and 301 China tariffs remain.

Brent was about 103 dollarson 4 November, and no fresher price was found. Hormuz disruption keeps energy a political liability.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE made about 483,200 deportations and 491,000 arrests in fiscal 2026, a record. The nonprofit TRAC says the count includes voluntary departures, and it falls short of the 1 million goal.

Trump reportedly acknowledged days earlier that the crackdown may have been "too tough". It marks a rhetorical shift after the midterm backlash.

The Supreme Court's asylum case and the student-stay ruling are the main legal fronts. See section 4.

The FAA outage raised homeland-security questions, but the FAA administrator said there was "zero evidence of anything nefarious".

11. States and governors.

D.C. officials want the five newly elected Democratic governors to pull National Guard troops from Washington. About 3,100 troops are deployed, 628 of them from the flipped states; Georgia's Keisha Lance Bottoms criticised the mission, while Iowa's Rob Sand has not said what he will do.

Alaska's Senate count (section 5) is the main state-run election process still open.

Quiet: no other significant state-level story surfaced in the last 72 hours.

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump is publicly feuding with Fox News over its polling and guest bookings. A Fox poll found 60% call the Iran military action the wrong decision, though it was fielded in September.

Journalism groups are suing the Pentagon over press restrictions. See section 7.

Trump's approval sits near record lows, with Reuters/Ipsos finding about 31% satisfied in October.
