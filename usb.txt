Here is your US briefing for Monday, 16 November.

Trump–Xi–Putin summit set for 19 November as Iran talks stall and Democrats' midterm sweep reshapes Washington.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 16 November 2026 (mostly 12 to 16 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

China, Russia and the US will hold their first-ever three-way leaders' meeting in Shenzhen on 19 November, on the sidelines of APEC. Beijing's foreign ministry and the Kremlin gave the date and the White House confirmed the summit without specifying a time; Iran and nuclear arms limits are reportedly on the agenda.

Democrats won both chambers on 3 November; reporting now puts the Senate at 56 to 44 after the last two races were called. Projections for the House range from roughly 250 to 268 Democratic seats, so the final margin is not settled.

Trump's approval hit record lows in two polls, 32% in Economist/YouGov (9 to 11 November) and 32% in Reuters/Ipsos (6 to 9 November). Averages sit near 37 to 38% approve, so the post-midterm slump is tied to prices and the Iran war.

Iran diplomacy is stalled and the Strait of Hormuz stays shut. Trump says "we don't need a deal," Rubio calls Iran's terms "outrageous," and Iran says Hormuz stays closed until its demands are met.

The Fed looks set to hike in December after a hot October CPI (+0.4%, core +0.3%). A quarter-point move at the 8 to 9 December meeting is largely priced in.

2. White House and the administration.

Stephen Miller replaced Susie Wiles as chief of staff, announced 6 November and effective 9 November. Axios and CNN tie the move to Trump's anger over the midterm losses, and Wiles moves to the Trump political operation.

Trump has questioned the midterm results and revived a voting-machine conspiracy claim. CNBC's headline feed also reports him saying a restart of the Iran war is "highly unlikely"; Hassett denied a report that he is leaving. CNBC, 15 November (unconfirmed, headline-feed summary)

Trump said the immigration crackdown may have been "too tough." The remark came days before DHS touted record deportations, which suggests an attempt to manage the political damage while keeping the policy.

Sen. Richard Blumenthal is clashing with Trump and a US attorney over the White House ballroom. He sent a letter demanding records be preserved in a probe of him; the ballroom dispute is also before the Supreme Court.

Cabinet officials including Rubio, Hegseth and Bessent have reportedly moved onto military installations over security concerns. Fox frames it around threats to officials. Fox News, November (unconfirmed)

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The House voted 211 to 204 on 10 November to refer Rep. Massie's Hegseth impeachment articles to the Judiciary Committee, effectively killing them. Massie was the only Republican to side with Democrats; Jeffries said "it's just the beginning."

Massie says he will force a House vote on an Iran war powers resolution (H.Con.Res. 126) before his term ends in January. Reporting points to mid-December, and the House has already passed similar measures three times without ending US involvement.

Funding runs out 11 December and the FY2027 NDAA is stuck. The Senate version failed a 60-vote hurdle, and Sen. Van Hollen led a 10 November letter urging Thune and Schumer to strip Israel cooperation provisions (Sections 219, 618 and 1217).

Incoming House Intelligence chair Jim Himes plans "aggressive" oversight of CIA covert action once Democrats take over in January.

Floor roundup for 15 November: the House passed a veterans' disability COLA bill (H.R. 8552) and the Senate advanced the "Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act" and confirmed a district judge. The source is one local roll-call digest. Santa Fe New Mexican, 15 November (unconfirmed)

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court agreed to hear the administration's appeal over its suspension of asylum at the southern border. It will review a DC Circuit ruling that struck the policy down as overreach.

The Court let the administration keep building the border wall (9 November) and declined to step into a California ballot dispute (2 November). Both are interim-docket moves, not merits rulings.

The administration has asked the Court to end the White House ballroom dispute (10 November).

A federal judge let The Atlantic seek FBI records and froze most discovery in Kash Patel's defamation suit.

SCOTUSblog is tracking major Trump cases that may return to the Court. The November argument session wrapped up this week.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Democrats flipped seven GOP Senate seats on election night (KS, IA, TX, OH, ME, NC, SC), with two more called later. Jeffries is in line to be Speaker; five governorships and up to 12 legislative chambers also flipped per CNBC's early tally.

Approval is 32% in Economist/YouGov (net −31) and Reuters/Ipsos, with Silver Bulletin at 37/60. Rasmussen's likely-voter number is about 40%, so pollster and population choice matter.

Massie lost his primary and leaves in January, but is using his last weeks to force Iran and Hegseth votes.

A newsletter reports Alaska was the last undecided Senate race and ten House races remain uncalled. Treat the tallies as provisional. The Sunday Update, 15 November (unconfirmed)

6. Foreign policy (Middle East focus)

Iran says Hormuz stays shut until its seven conditions are met, including sanctions relief, an end to the US blockade and war compensation. It reported three more tankers hit mines overnight, and it ties any deal to ceasefires in Lebanon and Gaza.

The administration looks split: Rubio backs the blockade while Vance wants a deal. HNGN reports Vance weighing direct talks with Iran's security chief, possibly in Doha. Al Jazeera, 11 November; HNGN, 15 November (unconfirmed)

The NYT reportedly says the US and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure. Rubio says there is "no need" to restart combat now. New Arab, November; CBS News, November (unconfirmed)

Israel continues strikes in Gaza, killing at least nine to ten people including at Nasser hospital, and says it will not leave its security zones in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria. Saudi Arabia also intercepted Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh.

Rubio says US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi were productive, and a 60-day suspension of Graham Act sanctions is meant to give them room. He also doubts a quick deal.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth survived impeachment and held his first Pentagon press briefing in six months. Trump backs him publicly but has reportedly asked allies about his standing.

Hegseth's 1.5 dollarstrillion budget request is in doubt now that Democrats control both chambers. Fortune says they will seek significant changes to defense spending bills.

The Pentagon put the Iran war's cost at 47.1 dollarsbillion as of 16 October, with about 29 dollarsbillion for munitions. It is seeking 67 dollarsbillion in supplemental funds, and 23 US service members have died per the latest figures cited.

Hegseth's office called a report that he ignored warnings before an Iranian drone attack that killed six US troops "disgusting." CBS reports survivors dispute his account of the strike. Raw Story, November (unconfirmed)

MS NOW reports Hegseth bypassed merit-based senior promotion processes, and CBS reports he fired the Pentagon's top general Brett Sylvia over alleged leaks. The Pentagon defends the changes as "reviving the warrior ethos." MS NOW, November; CBS News, November (unconfirmed)

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Foreign Policy reports Director Ratcliffe told the president airstrikes would not spark regime change, contradicting some CIA analysts. The article says analysts are now pushing back on the White House's Iran narrative.

A senior US Army soldier was arrested in the UK on suspicion of assisting Russian intelligence (10 November). Patel called the case important.

The FBI says it disrupted roughly 400 counterintelligence operations in 18 months, while the Washington Times questions whether arrest counts measure success.

Himes's incoming Democratic oversight of the CIA is the main institutional risk for the agency in 2027.

Open-source (unverified): ISW's 15 November assessment finds no confirmed Russian advance north of Sumy, limited Russian attacks near Borova and infiltration missions near Kharkiv and Hulyaipole. Bellingcat's most recent Iran work in these results is its April radar damage-mapping tool; I found no new November output.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

October CPI rose 0.4% (core 0.3%, third straight), and markets price a December hike at about 88% or higher. Waller would back a quarter-point rise and floated a half-point if core stays hot; Logan reportedly wants two more hikes.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh stresses price stability but has given no timing hint. The Fed hiked in September and held on 28 October; the 10-year yield is reported near 5.14%. NBC News, November; Pomegra, November (unconfirmed)

The White House is keeping tariffs and claims they are reshoring manufacturing, crediting them for October's 165,000 jobs. Independent analysts have not confirmed that link, and an official statement is partisan framing.

Breitbart argues the Fed's own model shows tariffs can lift investment; mainstream coverage stresses cost pressures. Fed officials attribute part of the inflation overshoot to tariffs, though Waller doubts services inflation is mostly tariff-driven.

This week's data include the Empire State survey and remarks from Warsh and NY Fed's Williams on 16 November.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE made over 491,000 arrests and about 483,200 removals in FY2026, both records. It also claims nearly 1.2 million deportations since January 2025.

TRAC disputes the framing, saying FOIA case records through 24 August show removals fell after January 2025. DHS also changed its wording from "illegal immigrants" to "migrants" in claiming over 3 million departures.

Rep. Bennie Thompson, incoming Homeland Security chair, says Democrats will investigate enforcement tactics.

The Supreme Court will review the asylum suspension at the southern border.

11. States and governors.

Gov. Kathy Hochul has not committed to a New York redistricting amendment and says she is "very much against midterm redistricting." She will consult Jeffries and legislative leaders.

Newsweek says four states may redraw congressional maps after the midterms, with Maryland seen as most likely. Shapiro in Pennsylvania prefers redistricting reform to an arms race.

Gov. Brian Kemp rejected a special session to limit future governors' powers or redistrict in Georgia.

Democrats flipped five governorships on 3 November.

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump's approval is at or near record lows, with a net −31 in Economist/YouGov.

Mediaite says the White House claimed Trump was getting "constant updates" on a rare FAA ground stop while he stayed publicly silent.

Coverage splits sharply on Iran and tariffs: Al Jazeera and The Intercept stress the war's costs and the NDAA's Israel ties, while Breitbart and the White House stress tariff gains.

Fox News frames the Hegseth impeachment as a failed Democratic push, while CBS and NPR report the 211 to 204 vote.
