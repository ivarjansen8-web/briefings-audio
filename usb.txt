Here is your US briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Trump curbs contractor cooperation with Congress as US-Israel weigh new Iran strikes and cyber outages mount.

Compiled from sources dated 17 to 22 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump signed an executive order telling federal contractors to check with the White House before answering congressional inquiries and to withhold communications the White House deems privileged. It lands days after Democrats won both chambers and is aimed at the investigations Reps. Raskin and Garcia are preparing; both called it unlawful.

The US and Israel are reportedly weighing a new assault on Iran, but Trump says the decision may wait until after 1 January. CBS cites unnamed sources, and Trump told Reuters and Fox he is in no rush and wants a "permanent" deal, which signals the war could run into 2027.

A week of cyber-related outages hit government and industry, including the NSA's classified network, a US air traffic ground stop and JPMorgan Chase. Officials report no evidence of an attack so far; the cyber director, Sean Cairncross, is leaving the administration.

The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center database. Trump called it "not good" on Fox; details come from an AP source and the FBI declined comment (unconfirmed).

Trump returned from a Shenzhen summit with Xi and Putin with few announced specifics. Russia launched a massive attack on Ukraine hours after the meeting, and critics note the promised quick Ukraine deal is long overdue.

2. White House and the administration.

Stephen Miller has been chief of staff since 9 November, replacing Susie Wiles, who moved to the political operation. The shake-up followed the midterm losses and puts the architect of the immigration crackdown atop the West Wing.

At a lengthy 20 November news conference Trump defended his relationships with Putin and Xi and introduced Miller as "the most powerful man in the world." He also said he would push out a senior cyber official and accused Iran of bad-faith negotiating.

Trump said US companies building AI data centers abroad would face a "tremendous tax," and urged Nvidia's board to bring Jensen Huang back. Outlets differ on whether this is a ban or a tax threat, and no details were given; Nvidia's board has signalled it is moving on.

Trump is pressing ahead with White House renovations despite the midterm loss. It is a recurring flashpoint for incoming House Democrats.

White House Counsel Will Scharf reportedly told agencies to get his office's approval before appearing before Congress. Together with the contractor order, this signals a hard line on oversight.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

Democrats take the House in January and are lining up Raskin (Judiciary) and Garcia (Oversight) to lead investigations, focused on contracts tied to Trump-linked companies, notably at the Pentagon. The contractor order is the administration's first pre-emptive move.

The House voted 211 to 204 on 10 November to refer Rep. Massie's Hegseth impeachment articles to committee, effectively shelving them. Massie was the only Republican to break ranks; the articles cite the Iran war and the boat strikes under the War Powers Resolution.

FBI Director Patel skipped a House Homeland Security hearing on worldwide threats, sending his deputy, Christopher Raia. The chairman called it a departure from committee precedent (partisan blog source, unconfirmed).

Floor business for the Thanksgiving week is thin: Quiet on major votes in the sources found. The lame-duck Congress faces year-end funding questions.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

A second federal judge, Leonie Brinkema, indefinitely blocked construction of Trump's 250-foot memorial arch in DC; the DOJ said it will appeal immediately. Judge Chutkan separately extended her restraining order to 3 December.

The government withdrew its emergency application asking the Supreme Court to block a ruling on protected status for Venezuelans (19 November). It also brought a climate-grant dispute to the court's emergency docket.

The justices' 16 November order list denied review in dozens of cases, including two DEI disputes and a challenge to Maryland's "sensitive places" gun law.

SCOTUSblog is mapping major Trump cases likely to return to the court. Expect tariff, immigration and removal-power fights to dominate the winter.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval sits at or near career lows after the midterms: averages about 36 to 39%, with Reuters/Ipsos at 32% and Economist/YouGov at a record-low 32%.

Republican loyalty is softening: strong approval among GOP voters fell to 40% from 48% before the election; Verasight finds 71% of 2024 Trump voters approve. Americans are split on impeachment in the same poll.

The 2028 race is stirring: Democrats await Ocasio-Cortez and Harris, while Trump says Vance and Rubio are "doing fantastically." One poll has Vance at 33% to Rubio's 24%.

Some DNC members are calling for Chairman Ken Martin to be replaced, a sign of internal friction despite the win.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

US and Israeli planning for a third major round of strikes on Iran is reportedly under way, per CBS (unconfirmed, unnamed sources). In Israel, Avigdor Liberman said there is no security justification for striking now and that a decision would be politically motivated.

CNN reported US intelligence that Iran is developing options for a dirty bomb or chemical attack in Washington targeting senior officials; Tehran calls it baseless. Trump said on 20 November it was a "rumor" the US would run down, and Reuters has not verified the report.

Hormuz shipping was reportedly about 65% below pre-war levels in mid-November, and Trump blamed Iran for high fuel prices.

A Board of Peace adviser is pressing Israel to authorize Gaza's International Stabilization Force, a new police force and the committee meant to replace Hamas, without requiring withdrawals.

At Shenzhen Trump held a trilateral with Xi and Putin and a separate sit-down with Putin, their first since Alaska. Putin reportedly said he wants the war ended, yet Russia struck Ukraine hours later.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

The Pentagon reportedly issued a classified memo restricting some US military cooperation with Canada; it says NORAD, NATO and defense-intelligence ties are untouched. The Washington Post broke it on Friday and the scope remains unclear.

Maj. Stephen Ramsbottom told 60 Minutes that leaders ignored warnings that the Shuaiba port base in Kuwait was not fortified against aerial attack, and he filed a whistleblower complaint with Congress. The Pentagon disputes his claims.

A New Republic report accuses Hegseth of ignoring key Iran strike intelligence (single outlet, unconfirmed).

Hegseth earlier fired the Joint Chiefs' top planner, Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, over alleged mishandling of classified information on Iran and Ukraine.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

The NSA's classified internal network went down on 17 November; CBS sources tied it to urgent PHP patching, and the agency called it scheduled maintenance. Service was reported restored with no evidence of a cyberattack.

The FBI is probing an apparent hack of the NCIC criminal justice database (AP source).

US intelligence reportedly indicates Iranian contingency planning for a dirty-bomb or chemical attack in DC, framed as options and not an imminent plot.

Open-source (unverified): no ISW or Bellingcat November output surfaced in searches. Earlier material includes Bellingcat's Sentinel-1 damage proxy map for Iran, built after commercial imagery over Iran was restricted.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Fed Governor Chris Waller said inflation is spreading into core services and the Fed may need to act strongly, with talk of reversing 2025 cuts. KBC expects a 25bp hike in December and estimates October inflation near 3.95%.

New York Fed research cited by Motley Fool puts tariffs' boost to goods inflation near 2.9 points by February 2026.

Consumer sentiment rose to 51.5 in November from 48.0, still low. The holiday week brings second-estimate Q3 GDP and PCE inflation.

Trump's data-center tax threat adds tariff-style uncertainty for tech.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS reports a record 483,200 ICE deportations and over 491,000 arrests in fiscal 2026, still short of the one-million goal. Backlash is growing after the midterms.

A leaked report says ICE recruits completed weeks of training in about two hours (single source, unconfirmed).

Miller's elevation to chief of staff points to continued enforcement pressure.

The government dropped its Supreme Court bid over Venezuelan protected status.

11. States and governors.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Searches returned only older Guard-deployment and redistricting litigation.

12. Media and public opinion.

Poll trackers show Americans split on impeachment and favouring bills to cut costs and corruption.

Oversight fights over the contractor order are set to dominate coverage; MS NOW frames it as thwarting Democratic investigations while the White House calls the probes harassment.

Cyber outages and the Nvidia comments drew heavy coverage.
