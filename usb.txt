Here is your US briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

GOP revolt over Nvidia's Huang, Iran strikes threat for 2027, Air Force cloud breach.

Compiled from sources dated 25 to 28 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Republicans are openly breaking with Trump over the return of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO. Senators Cotton, Cornyn, Cramer and Curtis have criticised the reinstatement, and Trump told CNBC "I made the deal" and floated a pardon for Huang as an option. It matters because it is a rare bipartisan push for an inquiry into administration ties to Nvidia and Trump's own holdings.

Trump says strikes on Iran could resume after the New Year if talks fail. He said he is not rushing a decision, while Iran has stepped up shipping attacks near Hormuz and the US Navy keeps its blockade of Iranian ports. The window is limited by war funding, which Trump says runs through January.

Hackers reportedly breached the Air Force's Cloud One platform, Reuters reported on 27 November. Three sources say intruders reached the control plane of a system that hosts pay data and supports air-defence radar command and control; Cyber Command has been called in and the Air Force declined comment. The scope and perpetrator are unknown (unconfirmed, single-source).

The administration proposed a new tariff wave on China, the EU, Brazil and others over subsidised factories. Duties are unlikely to bite before February or March, with an industry hearing set for 25 January.

A 11 December funding deadline looms for a lame-duck Congress. A stopgap runs out that day, and some Democrats want to attach Iran-war conditions.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump met NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte at Mar-a-Lago on 25 November. Their joint statement urged Russia not to "expand the war" but announced no ceasefire, deadline or peace plan. Newsweek reports Trump's own version of the text differs from NATO's by two words (not verified which).

Trump said he is fine with Huang returning to Nvidia and that "the board made the correct and proper decision." The Pentagon denies a Washington Post report that Hegseth discussed using a Cold War-era law to pressure Nvidia's board; a defence official told Newsweek the talks never happened. Treat the Post report as contested.

The White House is reportedly weighing moving the press corps out of the West Wing. This follows a ruling that its ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico likely violated the First Amendment; the administration is appealing.

Approval remains weak. Aggregates put Trump around 36 to 38% approve, with Economist/YouGov at 32% and Echelon at 26% in mid-November. Independents drive the slide.

Kilmar Abrego Garcia landed in Spain as a "free man" after his long deportation fight. NBC reports the case has ended for now.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

No significant floor votes were reported on 27 November. Congress is in the Thanksgiving recess; the lame-duck session resumes next week.

Funding runs out on 11 December, and a lapse would begin on Saturday 12 December. Thune says the public does not want another shutdown, while some Democrats want to use the deadline to press on the Iran war; the farm bill and defence authorisation are also queued.

Bipartisan concern over Nvidia is growing in the Senate. Cotton called the Megaspeed affair "just the tip of the iceberg" and Republicans have joined Democrats in seeking an investigation.

Democrats are projected to take both chambers in January. ABC's projection pages show roughly 259 House seats for Democrats and a 56 to 44 Senate; the figures are projections, so check final certified counts. The outgoing Republican Congress holds power until 3 January. ABC News Senate map (unconfirmed)

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court agreed on 13 November to decide whether Trump can suspend asylum at the southern border. Two lower courts rejected the rationale but the policy stays largely in force; SCOTUSblog points to argument in spring, while another outlet says December.

The Court will also hear an Eighth Amendment challenge to Alaska's seizure of a pilot's plane carrying beer. The Alaska Supreme Court upheld the forfeiture.

Arguments on AR-15 bans in Illinois and Connecticut are set for Wednesday, with a decision pending on whether to dismiss the Cook County case as moot. iHeart, 27 November (unconfirmed)

Judge Timothy Kelly's injunction keeps CNN, MS NOW and Politico reporters in the White House. He found the ban likely retaliatory and viewpoint-based, and the government is appealing.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

After the 3 November midterms, Democrats hold the advantage in Washington's next Congress. Trump's approval fell further in the weeks after; net approval is about -22.

Republican approval of Trump has slipped. CNN's October poll had 74% of Republicans approving, down from 88% in February 2025, while only about 21% of independents approve.

Appetite for mid-decade redistricting is fading in both parties after the blue wave. MS NOW argues Trump's map push could not withstand the swing; this is a left-leaning outlet's framing.

Republican senators are now publicly diverging from Trump on Nvidia, an early sign of lame-duck independence.

6. Foreign policy (Middle East focus)

Trump said strikes on Iran may resume after the New Year if no deal is reached. A June framework to end hostilities stalled over Hormuz navigation; Iran says it will not discuss reopening until its conditions are met.

Israeli media report Mossad chief Roman Gofman is pushing for an independent Israeli strike on Iran if Washington declines a joint one. This is a Yedioth/Ynet claim relayed by Xinhua (unconfirmed).

Israel's security cabinet was due to meet Sunday on Trump's stalled Gaza plan, including an international stabilisation force. Kushner reportedly warned Netanyahu that Israel risks pariah status, and Board of Peace adviser Josh Gruenbaum said the board could hand Gaza back to the UN.

Defence Minister Israel Katz threatens a fresh Gaza offensive if Hamas does not disarm. It cuts against the Board of Peace push.

Trump and Rutte urged Russia not to widen the Ukraine war. It came after Trump's Shenzhen talks with Putin and Xi produced no ceasefire, and hours before Russian missiles hit Kyiv.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

The Pentagon is barring lawmakers from Gulf bases. Hegseth's 3 March memo requires his personal approval for congressional delegations, and Sen. Chris Murphy was denied access to Al Udeid, calling it a "cover-up." Former officials call it a lack of transparency; the Pentagon says the rule applies uniformly.

The Air Force Cloud One breach has revived Signalgate scrutiny. An inspector general earlier found Hegseth violated policy in his Signal use; the platform's prime contractor changed from SAIC to Leidos in 2025.

A reported classified directive would restrict military cooperation with Canada. The Pentagon says NORAD, NATO and intelligence cooperation are untouched, and Ottawa sees no change. CBC (unconfirmed)

The Navy keeps its blockade of Iranian ports, and US forces recently struck Qeshm Island after an Iranian attack on a base in Kuwait.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Reuters-sourced reporting says Cyber Command is assessing the Air Force Cloud One intrusion. No attribution has been made public and the Air Force will not discuss network security (unconfirmed, single-source).

Israeli press reports on Mossad's push for independent action against Iran are the main intelligence-adjacent item (unconfirmed, second-hand).

CIA, FBI, NSA, ODNI, DIA: Quiet. Nothing significant found in the last 72 hours; the latest searchable hearings were from March.

Open-source (ISW, unverified): Russia is moving to criminalise sharing unapproved information about Ukrainian strikes. ISW also assesses Moscow may prepare a false flag in Moldova, says North Korea paused military aid while its missile crews remain, and notes Ukrainian strikes on an Iskander plant on 26 to 27 November.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

The Fed is priced for a December rate hike. Futures put a 25bp move near 95%+ at the 8 to 9 December FOMC; Governor Waller floated a half-point move. The target range is 3.75 to 4.00%.

October CPI rose 0.4%, with inflation near 4% annually. Prices have matched or outpaced wage growth for three months.

Third-quarter growth was 4.3% annualised and jobless claims were 189,000. Axios says the economy looks overheated, with core PCE up 0.3% in October.

The proposed tariff hikes on China, the EU and Brazil await industry feedback and a 25 January hearing.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE set records in fiscal 2026, with 491,000+ arrests and 483,000+ deportations. Nearly 1.2 million have been deported since January 2025, still short of Trump's one-million-a-year goal; CBS frames it as a political "reckoning."

Port of Seattle says Sea-Tac had a day's notice of an ICE arrest of a father and daughter on a jet bridge. That reverses its earlier account, and the family's lawyer disputes a final removal order exists (unconfirmed, single outlet).

ICE confirmed it will not use ships for deportations. Removals continue by air and land.

The Supreme Court will review the asylum suspension. The policy stays largely in effect meanwhile.

11. States and governors.

New York's redistricting amendment must pass again next term and then go to voters. Gov. Hochul said she wants to look at it "long term."

Missouri's Gov. Kehoe is not focused on redistricting for the 2027 session, a sign of cooling in Republican states.

Other states: Quiet. Nothing else significant found in the last 72 hours.

12. Media and public opinion.

A federal judge extended the block on the White House ban of CNN, MS NOW and Politico. The Justice Department calls access a privilege and is appealing.

CBS reports Trump is weighing moving the press corps out of the White House. It is a single report so far (unconfirmed).

Public opinion is sour. Trump's approval sits in the mid-to-high 30s, and Economist/YouGov found 63% disapproval.

Conservative outlets such as Breitbart ran the Cloud One breach story while the left-leaning Daily Beast framed it as a Hegseth humiliation, showing the split in framing.
