Here is your US briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Democrats take Congress as Miller becomes chief of staff; Iran standoff frozen.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Democrats won both chambers on 3 November, flipping the Senate by roughly seven seats (exact count still reported as 54 to 44 or 55 to 44). Schumer vowed a "legislative razor wire" against Trump, and the new Congress sits on 3 January 2027, which frames every lame-duck fight.

Trump named Stephen Miller chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles. Jeffries called Miller a "dangerous xenophobe and reckless extremist", and Democrats say it kills hopes of bipartisan immigration talks.

Trump has begun questioning the legitimacy of the midterms after saying on election night he would accept them. He pointed to voting machines without citing a specific discrepancy, and fact-checkers say there is no evidence of vote switching.

The US-Iran standoff is frozen: Rubio says "no need" for major combat operations because economic pressure is working, while Tehran keeps Hormuz closed. The war began on 28 February and has killed 23 US service members, per Pentagon figures cited by CBS.

The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether Trump can suspend asylum at the southern border. It is a major immigration test with arguments expected in spring 2027.

2. White House and the administration.

Miller's elevation reportedly moves control of Cabinet communications away from Wiles' operation. A Fox News report describes an internal memo giving agencies more autonomy, which shows how Miller intends to run the building.

Trump held a surprise briefing on 9 November with UFC chief Dana White, whom he had floated online as press secretary. Trump said he was "sort of kidding" and White said he has no political aspirations, but no press secretary change has been reported.

Trump reportedly told the DOJ to investigate Democrats over "threatening letters" to GOP members, and the White House warned Democrats that probing the administration may be a crime. This signals a combative stance toward incoming House oversight (unconfirmed beyond a news digest).

Trump said restarting the Iran war is "highly unlikely" but warned he could strike. The hedge keeps leverage while avoiding a new commitment.

An armed man was arrested near the White House for allegedly threatening Trump (unconfirmed). Details were thin in the one summary I found.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The House voted 211 to 204 on 10 November to send Massie's Hegseth impeachment resolution to committee, killing it for this session. Massie was the only Republican to back impeachment, and Jeffries said "It's just the beginning", which points to a Democratic push once they control the House.

The government runs out of money on 11 December, with a short continuing resolution into February or March the likely fix. Senate Democrats warned that a Pentagon-funding reconciliation push would raise shutdown risk; it would be the fourth shutdown of Trump's term.

Speaker Johnson will not seek the Speaker post again, and Republicans picked new leaders on 10 November. Whoever leads will negotiate funding with Democrats during the lame duck.

Twelve Senate Democrats led by Van Hollen want Israel provisions struck from the FY2027 NDAA. The sections create a US-Israel defense technology initiative and about 750 dollarsmillion for joint projects, and the bill needs 60 Senate votes, so Democrats have leverage.

Republicans are using the lame duck to push judges and weigh a debt-limit increase. Thune has discouraged a partisan reconciliation package.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court on 13 November took up the asylum-suspension case (Mullin v. RAICES), reviewing a D.C. Circuit ruling that struck the policy down. Conservative outlets such as the Daily Caller frame it as a chance to vindicate the border closure.

On 9 November the Court let Trump resume border-wall construction in Big Bend, Texas; Justice Jackson noted she would deny. It also denied review in over 200 cases that day.

The Court declined the administration's request to intervene in a California ballot dispute. It is a rare loss on election-related emergency applications.

A divided Ninth Circuit (6 to 5) on 3 November lifted limits on DOJ demands to election officials over disputed ballots. A lower court had found the demands intimidating, so this matters for post-election disputes.

Importers asked the Court of International Trade on 10 November to order tariff refunds for all roughly 330,000 IEEPA payers. The administration argues only those who sued are owed refunds, in a pool of about 166 dollarsbillion.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Reuters/Ipsos (6 to 9 November) has Trump approval at 32% and disapproval at 65%, 50% strongly. It is one point above his series low, and the poll averages sit near 38% approve.

Democrats flipped Senate seats in Texas (Talarico over Paxton), Maine, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas, North Carolina and South Carolina. The Texas and South Carolina results were the biggest upsets.

Democrats also flipped five governorships (Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, Texas). That shifts control of Guard deployments and state election administration for 2028.

Trump's election-integrity posts are drawing mockery and alarm as states certify. Election officials report routine voting, and Democrats call the claims groundless.

House Republicans are in a leadership reshuffle after Johnson's decision. The contest decides who leads the minority from January.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Rubio said on 10 November, after a G7 meeting in Paris, that "there is no need for major combat operations" because the economic campaign against Iran is "devastating." He called Iran's conditions "absurd" but left open that this "could change", and said force remains possible if Iran crosses unspecified red lines.

Iran's security chief called reports of Qatar-mediated talks "a pure lie" and says Hormuz stays closed until seven conditions are met. Iran's demands include lifting sanctions, ending the blockade and a ceasefire including Lebanon and Gaza.

Netanyahu is reportedly pressing Trump to resume strikes, and Defense Minister Katz says the Iran mission is not complete. Israel also says it will keep security zones in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria.

Israeli strikes killed at least nine Palestinians in Gaza, including six at Nasser hospital, despite the nominal ceasefire. The toll complicates Washington's regional diplomacy.

Zelensky says US negotiators floated Ukraine leaving Donbas in exchange for NATO membership, which Kyiv rejects. Russia reportedly refused an energy-target ceasefire at the Abu Dhabi round.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth gave his first Pentagon press briefing since May, just before the impeachment vote, casting Massie as a rogue. Trump called the vote an "exoneration."

Hegseth awarded a Joint Service Commendation Medal to an Arlington sentinel who shouted at his group after loud laughter. The episode became a "historically strange week" story.

Watchdogs allege conflict of interest in "Project Meridian", a future-of-warfare study led by Gingrich, Musk and Palmer Luckey. The source is the Daily Beast, a left-leaning outlet, and a lawsuit is cited (unconfirmed).

CENTCOM has reportedly finished preparations to return to major combat without an order to do so. That keeps the strike option credible.

Hegseth plans to expand the National Guard presence in Washington as local officials seek to remove it. About 628 troops come from states with incoming Democratic governors.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Quiet: no major intelligence-agency leadership change or hearing found in the last 72 hours. The latest coverage of Gabbard, Ratcliffe and Patel is from the spring worldwide-threats hearings, where Gabbard called Iran's regime "intact but largely degraded."

OSINT (open-source, unverified): UANI's 13 November update, using Copernicus imagery, shows vessels in Hormuz's southern corridor and the aftermath of a strike on the tanker DOMINICA PROSPERITY. UANI is an advocacy group.

OSINT: Windward counted nine transits of Hormuz on 13 November, all without AIS, and the IMO counts 131 incidents and 25 seafarer deaths since the war began. Windward is a commercial vendor.

OSINT: Bellingcat (5 November) showed US bases targeted by Iran are visible in unblurred Bing imagery even where Google blurs them. It is a reminder of commercial-map exposure.

OSINT: ISW's 14 November assessment says Russian forces made no confirmed advances around Kostyantynivka, while Ukraine advanced south of Novopavlivka.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

The Fed raised rates in September for the first time in three years and held at 3.75%-4.00% in October. Some officials floated faster hikes.

Inflation is running hot, with reported PCE readings of 3.3 to 3.6% (sources conflict). A NY Fed study says tariffs' contribution to inflation has fallen to near zero, with energy and AI demand now pushing prices.

September payrolls rose by only 29,000, with unemployment at 4.2%. Slow hiring plus rising prices limits the Fed's room.

Roughly 166 dollarsbillion in IEEPA tariff refunds is contested. The Supreme Court struck down those tariffs in February, and Section 301 China tariffs remain.

Vanguard now expects one Fed hike in early 2027 and core inflation of 3.2% at year-end.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE made over 491,000 arrests and about 483,000 removals in fiscal 2026, both records. TRAC disputes this and says removals fell after Trump took office, because of counting differences.

Reuters reports ICE detaining migrants at courthouses in New York, Phoenix, Los Angeles and Seattle, sometimes after judges dismissed their cases. Dismissal can open the door to expedited removal.

The Supreme Court's asylum case puts border policy on the 2027 docket.

With Miller as chief of staff, Democrats say immigration deals are off. Rep. Robert Garcia called working with him "not realistic."

11. States and governors.

D.C. Councilmember Brooke Pinto is urging governors-elect in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas to withdraw 628 Guard troops from Washington. Withdrawal could occur by January.

Election officials in states are certifying results while DOJ has issued more than 100 subpoenas to election officials nationwide, per one report. A federal judge earlier found DOJ letters to Democratic-led states intimidating.

Quiet on other state-level developments in the sources I could reach.

12. Media and public opinion.

Coverage of Trump's election-legitimacy claims splits along familiar lines. The Hill, NBC and Newsweek stress the lack of evidence, while PJ Media and the Washington Examiner emphasise Democratic overreaction to Miller.

Reuters/Ipsos finds inflation and cost of living drive the souring of opinion.

The Dana White press-secretary saga dominated entertainment-political media, with TMZ and Mediaite covering it.
