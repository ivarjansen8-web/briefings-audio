Here is your US briefing for Tuesday, 10 November.

Massie forces Hegseth impeachment vote as Miller takes over West Wing and Iran talks stall.

Compiled from sources dated 7 to 10 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Rep. Thomas Massie filed eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and a House vote could come as early as this week. The articles centre on the Iran war and War Powers Resolution violations; with Republicans at 218 to 214 until January, a few defections could carry it, and Rep. Don Bacon is reportedly leaning toward backing it.

Stephen Miller started as White House chief of staff on 9 November, replacing Susie Wiles. The shuffle after the midterm losses signals Trump is not pivoting to conciliation with the incoming Democratic majority.

Lame-duck Congress returned 9 November with funding expiring 11 December. Reporting expects a short-term patch that pushes a full-year deal to the Democratic Congress seated 3 January.

US-Russia-Ukraine talks open in Abu Dhabi (10 to 11 November) as Trump says he sees a "strong inclination" to end the war. Kyiv rejects ceding territory, while Moscow still demands all of Donbas.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump said his deportation policy may have been "too tough" in a post-midterm 60 Minutes interview aired 8 November. He said he would "love to do something on immigration" with Democrats, but naming Miller undercuts that signal.

Wiles moves to the political operation after submitting her resignation as chief of staff on 5 November. She was the longest-serving chief of staff of this term and had questioned Miller's hardline immigration tactics.

Trump vowed to stand with Hegseth if impeached and said further military action in Iran "won't be necessary" for now. He made the remarks at an impromptu White House briefing on 9 November.

Trump defended Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang as an "honorable guy" after the New York Times reported Rubio oversaw a classified memo warning officials about Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence-linked executive. Huang admitted infidelity; the NYT says Trump was briefed in October 2025 (unconfirmed beyond the NYT report); China denies any plot.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

Democrats won both chambers on 3 November: about 254 House seats and at least 54 Senate seats counting independents, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled. It is the largest House majority since 2008 and takes effect 3 January.

The Senate voted to advance the permitting-reform vehicle H.R. 2347 on 9 November and plans to confirm dozens of Trump nominees before the handover. Thune is using the lame duck for a legislative sprint; final vote tallies were not in the sources I found.

The December 11 funding cliff is joined by the NDAA, a January debt-limit deadline, the farm bill and a tax package. Thune says a Republicans-only bill is possible but "we don't have" 50 votes right now.

Leadership shifts: Thune is running unopposed for Senate minority leader, and Speaker Johnson will not run for House minority leader, per Punchbowl. Jeffries is positioned to become speaker.

Republican Rep. Brian Mast and colleagues asked Huang to answer questions by next week. This is an early sign GOP-led oversight of the Nvidia-China story could start before Democrats take over.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court on 9 November let the administration resume border-wall construction in Big Bend, Texas. It denied summary reversal but granted a stay of the lower-court ruling.

The Court declined the administration's request to intervene in a California ballot dispute. A rare loss for the emergency docket on election matters.

The Court added a Goldman Sachs arbitration dispute to its 2026 to 27 argument docket on 6 November. Business-law docket building continues.

No court has ruled on the legality of the Iran war. Lawfare-linked analysis says courts are very unlikely to step in, citing political-question and standing hurdles, so Congress is the live arena.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Reuters/Ipsos (completed 9 November) put Trump's approval at 32%, near his career low, with 21% approving on cost of living. Economist/YouGov had 36% (released 7 November).

Pre-election generic ballots had Democrats up roughly 8 to 9 points, and the result matched. Democrats flipped seven Senate seats and five governorships (Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, Texas).

Trump questioned the election results as Alaska and Mississippi counts continued. Watch whether this hardens into formal challenges.

Democrats condemned the Miller pick as the opposite of a bipartisan signal, and at least one GOP lawmaker called it a "mistake". It sets the tone for the divided-government fight.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Rubio called Iran's proposal to end the war "outrageous and ridiculous" and said a breakthrough is unlikely. Tehran wants talks to restart within two days instead of seven; Rubio then flew to a G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris.

The US naval blockade of Iranian ports passed 100 days, with CENTCOM claiming over 25 dollarsbillion in denied crude exports. A new Task Force Iron Gate aims to cut Iranian arms movement over land borders and the Caspian (single-source via Times of Israel/INSS summaries, unconfirmed).

Trump told Netanyahu on 5 November to take steps to implement the Gaza peace plan, per Axios. Trump also says there is a "good chance" of an Iran deal and no new offensive for now.

Rubio said the midterm losses will not change foreign policy, and G7 ministers are pressing him on Iran, Ukraine and the ICC. Allies appear far apart on all three.

Euronews reports Trump plans to meet Putin and Xi in China next week (unconfirmed). I found no second source, so treat it with caution.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth holds his first Pentagon press conference since 5 May at 8 a.m. ET today. It comes under impeachment pressure, and spokesman Sean Parnell says he is "100% committed" to serving out Trump's term.

Fox News reports several House Republicans privately think the impeachment could pass. Fox is a notable outlet for that framing, since the push is led by a Republican.

Military sources told MS NOW that Hegseth's office ran a "deliberate process" to deny certain officers promotions. The Pentagon says he is "reviving the warrior ethos"; the outlet is left-leaning and the claim rests on anonymous sources.

The articles also cite strikes on the Houthis in Yemen and operations around Venezuela. The NDAA must pass before year-end, so Iran war funding fights will meet it.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

The NYT reported that Rubio oversaw a classified memo built on electronic surveillance warning officials about Huang's alleged relationship with a Chinese citizen executive (unconfirmed, single-source). It raises questions about what the White House did with the counterintelligence warning.

I found no new reporting on CIA, FBI, NSA or ODNI leadership changes in the last 72 hours. Most coverage surfaced was from March's worldwide-threats hearings, when Gabbard called Iran's regime "intact but largely degraded".

OSINT (ISW/Critical Threats, 8 November): Iran claims two ships hit mines in Hormuz, which analysts assess is likely an information operation. The same update says the Houthis claim strikes on Saudi sites including Abqaiq and Riyadh's airport, which ISW could not verify.

OSINT (Bellingcat): free Sentinel imagery over Gaza and parts of the West Bank appears delayed by 24 hours. Published 29 October, so slightly outside the window; analysts-only finding, not independently verified.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

October payrolls rose 165,000 against forecasts near 60 to 65,000, and unemployment fell to 4.1%. Strong data raise the odds of a Fed hike.

October CPI is due today, 10 November. Headline may ease on lower gasoline, but core goods prices have been accelerating, with tariffs and the Iran war cited as drivers.

Prediction markets price roughly a 77% chance of a Fed hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting (single market source, unconfirmed). A hike would be a striking turn from cuts.

Cost of living is the weak point: only 21% approve of Trump's handling in Reuters/Ipsos. It is the issue Democrats ran on.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

Trump's "too tough" comment on enforcement and openness to legal status for some long-settled migrants is a notable shift in rhetoric. Miller as chief of staff suggests policy may not follow.

A 25 October 60 Minutes report found 17 of 19 people reached who were deported to Ghana were then sent to other countries against their will. DHS and ICE dispute the framing and posted names and charges of other deportees.

The Supreme Court cleared border-wall work in Big Bend, Texas. The decision is a win for the administration's enforcement infrastructure.

11. States and governors.

D.C. Council member Brooke Pinto is urging new Democratic governors of Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas to withdraw National Guard troops from Washington. Georgia has 514 and Iowa 110 troops there; troops are federally paid but under state control.

Maryland voters approved Question 3 on 3 November with about 60%, tied to Gov. Wes Moore's redistricting effort. Details of the measure were thin in sources.

Democrats flipped five governor's mansions. New governors take office in January and will test federal-state clashes on Guard deployments and immigration.

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump's UFC-boss stunt: Dana White turned down a joking press-secretary offer at a 9 November White House briefing. A sideshow, but it shows Trump's improvised press style.

Conservative outlets such as Hot Air and Townhall attacked 60 Minutes' deportation reporting, while ICE mocked the report's subject on X. It reflects how contested immigration coverage remains.

Public opinion is sour on Trump: averages sit near 37 to 39% approval. RCP had 38.9% and NYT 37%, with Reuters/Ipsos lowest at 32%.
