Here is your US briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Democrats take Congress as Hegseth impeachment is shelved; Qatar-brokered Iran diplomacy and Abu Dhabi Ukraine talks.

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 11 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Democrats won the House, with NBC showing 254 seats to 168 and 13 races uncalled. The roughly 13-point House popular-vote margin is the largest for a midterm since 1974, according to one tally, and Democrats are also reported to hold the Senate at about 54 seats, with Alaska and Mississippi uncalled (counts differ between outlets, so treat totals as preliminary).

The House killed Rep. Thomas Massie's Hegseth impeachment resolution by referring it to committee on Tuesday. Massie, a Republican, was the lone GOP vote against the motion, and Jeffries said "it's just the beginning."

Trump asked on Truth Social whether the midterm "was honest" and how "the polls could be so wrong." He had said on election night that he accepted the results, and no formal challenge has been announced.

Qatar's prime minister met Trump and Vance at the White House and reported "positive responses" from both Washington and Tehran on ending the Iran war. Iran denies a planned meeting in Qatar, so the diplomacy is real but contested.

Russia and Ukraine held roughly four hours of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, their first in nine months. A second day is under way today, with a prisoner swap the main concrete item.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump issued a Veterans Day proclamation and is scheduled to attend the Arlington wreath-laying at 11:00 today, followed by remarks at the White House. The proclamation also mentions a VA restructuring; the schedule comes from a secondary source, so times are unverified.

A senior White House official said the administration will press ahead with tariffs despite the midterm backlash over prices. Former economic adviser Gary Cohn argued consumers are bearing the cost, and a Reuters/Ipsos poll found 62% support repealing the tariffs.

Trump told the Financial Times he has no immediate plan to strike Iran and has received conflicting advice. Vance said after the midterms that the war must end and the US relationship with Israel must change, which exposes a split inside the administration.

The White House nominated Michael LoSapio, Palantir's chief information security officer, to lead CISA. He would replace acting director Nick Andersen if the Senate confirms him.

Trump's approval averages sit near 38%, with about 60% disapproving. The Economist/YouGov poll (4 to 6 November) had 36/62 and Reuters/Ipsos showed 32%.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

House Republicans elected Jim Jordan as their leader for the next Congress, reportedly 106 to 73 over Steve Scalise. Speaker Mike Johnson had already said he would not seek the minority leader job, and the vote count comes from anonymous sources (unconfirmed).

Senate Republicans kept John Thune as leader by acclamation after steep losses. Ron Johnson and Rick Scott pushed to delay the vote and lost 31 to 15, with no rival emerging.

Democrats are weighing whether to use the 11 December funding deadline to force an end to the Iran war. Options include a rider barring war spending on a stopgap, but Jeffries is noncommittal and some Democrats fear a shutdown.

Massie says he will not bring another impeachment resolution and is pivoting to a war powers resolution. A Reuters/Ipsos poll cited by a conservative outlet found 71% support legislation ending the war, including 51% of Republicans (secondary source).

A Senate unanimous-consent deal discharges H.Con.Res.93 from Foreign Relations no later than 20 November. The resolution's subject was not clear in the source, so the vote is worth tracking.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court let the administration resume border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region. It denied summary reversal but granted a stay of the lower-court ruling; Justice Jackson would have denied the request, and the 8 to 1 count rests on a partisan post (unconfirmed).

The justices heard argument in Nielsen v. Watanabe on when federal officials can be held liable for constitutional violations. This is a Bivens-type question on which the Court has been narrowing remedies for years.

The Court denied review in more than 200 cases on its orders list. It is routine but clears the docket for the winter.

Lawfare highlights law-review arguments that warrantless automated license plate reader use is unconstitutional. It is commentary, not a ruling, but it tracks recent federal and state cases.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Democrats flipped five governorships, including Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas, according to reporting on National Guard recall demands. Arizona is also said to be all-Democratic for the first time since 1966, though the sources are secondary.

Democratic leaders are planning to target Medicaid cuts, the Iran war and tariffs in the next Congress. Jeffries expects to seek GOP cooperation while facing the likelihood of a Trump veto.

Some progressives are pushing to oust Schumer, and Chris Van Hollen has been floated as a challenger. One outlet calls an ouster unlikely.

Israel's opposition leader Eisenkot said after the midterms he would renew the bipartisan US-Israel alliance. It reflects how Israeli politics is reading the shift in Washington.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Trump said the Iran war is "effectively" at an end after meeting Qatar's prime minister, with Vance present. Qatar says it cannot say how close a deal is, and the US war began on 28 February, with an April ceasefire and renewed fighting from July.

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei called reports of a planned Vance meeting baseless and denounced "fake news about imaginary talks." A Turkish intelligence claim that Qatar would host them next week (unconfirmed) conflicts with this.

Netanyahu reportedly pressed Trump to strike Iran after the midterms, and one report describes a tense call (unconfirmed). Axios reporting says Trump wants Israel to stay out of the way of diplomacy.

Netanyahu's office said Israel is doing what it can to let winter equipment into Gaza, blaming Hamas, after criticism from the US-backed Board of Peace. Trump reportedly wants him to advance the Gaza peace plan.

Trump sees a "strong inclination" on both sides to end the Russia-Ukraine war, but Moscow still demands all of Donbas. Witkoff and Kushner are leading the US side, and Russia wants a post-war European security framework.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth held his first Pentagon press briefing since May and called the impeachment push a "farce" and "political spectacle." He said he was proud to execute Trump's military orders and would do so again.

Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell claimed "TOTAL VICTORY" after the vote, but a source close to Hegseth expects the fight to continue (unconfirmed). Sen. Ted Cruz was reported to say Hegseth would either resign or be fired.

Senate Democrats lack the votes to remove Hegseth on their own, so any real pressure runs through the war powers and funding fights. The Iran war is the stated basis of the articles.

Brent crude fell 1.2% to 91.49 dollarsafter topping 112 dollarsat the start of November. Lower prices fit the diplomacy narrative but the Iran conflict has driven energy costs.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Quiet: no new leadership changes or hearings involving the CIA, FBI, NSA or ODNI surfaced in the last 72 hours. The most recent item found is an October report that the FBI circulated a classified internal "Patel Report" on past failings.

Open-source, unverified: analysts at MAIAR told BBC Verify that satellite imagery shows new construction at Garmdareh and Shahroud bases in Iran, with at least 12 other sites under repair. It suggests Iran is rebuilding missile infrastructure, but this comes via an aggregator.

Bellingcat reported that public mapping platforms expose sensitive details of US bases in the Middle East, including Camp Buehring and Camp Arifjan. It is open-source analysis relevant to force protection.

No ISW update could be retrieved for 9 to 10 November. Readers should check ISW directly.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

October CPI rose 0.4% against 0.3% expected, after a 0.6% jump in September. An annual rate could not be reported because of a prior lapse in appropriations.

A Reuters poll of 101 economists unanimously expects a quarter-point Fed hike in December, to 4.00 to 4.25%. Governor Waller backs a hike and has floated a half-point move.

The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.99% from 4.88%, and stocks fell, with the Nasdaq down. Hot inflation raised the odds of a larger December move.

Chicago Fed's Goolsbee said services inflation has picked up, complicating the idea that tariffs alone drive prices. The October jobs report was strong with upward revisions.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there will be "zero change" in immigration policy after the midterm losses, with about 3,600 deportations a day continuing. He ruled out amnesty talks with Democrats.

Democratic lawmakers demanded answers over an 8-year-old Haitian boy taken into custody. ICE cited the Trafficking Victims Protection Reauthorization Act because the uncle could not establish guardianship.

Advocates say 11 Iranian nationals were due to be flown by ICE from Arizona to Pakistan, potentially in violation of a court order. DHS declined to comment (unconfirmed, advocacy-group claim).

The Supreme Court's border wall stay lets Big Bend construction continue. See section 4.

11. States and governors.

D.C. Council member Brooke Pinto is urging the newly elected Democratic governors of five states to withdraw National Guard troops from Washington. Georgia (514 troops) and Iowa (110) could make the biggest dent, but it is unclear whether governors-elect can recall troops at will.

Democrats' wins give them power to redraw maps in several new states, but Georgia's Kemp and New Jersey's Sherrill have ruled it out. Maryland voters relaxed the state's anti-gerrymandering law, and Shapiro says it is not on the agenda in Pennsylvania.

Minnesota drew a DHS release over refusing an ICE detainer, part of continuing federal-state friction. It is DHS's own framing.

12. Media and public opinion.

A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 71% want Congress to end the Iran war, including 93% of Democrats and 51% of Republicans. The figure is reported by a conservative outlet, so check the original.

Trump's disapproval is near 60% in averages, with Reuters/Ipsos at 32% approval. Averages differ by about a point by aggregator.

An anonymous group put a gold satirical statue of Trump on the National Mall referencing his Vietnam draft deferment, through Veterans Day. Coverage is from a tabloid-style outlet (unconfirmed).

Coverage of Trump's election-honesty post is split between outlets stressing "no signs of wrongdoing" (Forbes, Time) and those carrying his claims about voting machines. Framing matters for how contested the result becomes.
