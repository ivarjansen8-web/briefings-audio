Here is your US briefing for Tuesday, 24 November.

Iran strikes on hold as blockade holds; Hegseth faces resignation calls; cyber chief fired after outages.

Compiled from sources dated 19 to 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Washington has reportedly decided to hold off on renewed Iran strikes for at least a week, while the US naval blockade of Iranian ports and the Hormuz standoff continue. Netanyahu is pressing for a joint campaign, and Trump told Reuters on 19 November he would revisit strikes "after the beginning of the year" if there is no deal. Ynet, 22 November (unconfirmed: anonymous US officials, Israeli outlet)

Hegseth is under fresh pressure: ex-chief of staff Joe Kasper told Politico he should resign over the Iran war, the firings of senior officers and his handling of Congress. The Pentagon called Kasper a "disgruntled former staffer", and Trump has signalled he is keeping Hegseth.

Trump fired national cyber director Sean Cairncross after a week of outages hitting the FAA, major banks, Microsoft services and the NSA's classified network. Officials say the incidents were unrelated and that there is no evidence of a cyberattack.

Democrats' midterm win is now being called the biggest for either party in 50 years, with a roughly 14-point House vote margin. They flipped at least 47 seats and take both chambers in January, so the lame duck is the administration's last window with a friendly Congress.

Trump's Shenzhen summit with Xi and Putin produced only broad statements on energy, nuclear risk and strategic stability. Taiwan was not addressed, and Trump is reportedly pushing Kyiv toward a deal by early January.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump said he pushed out cyber director Cairncross "because I didn't think that person was good enough". Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies is stepping in on an acting basis, and any permanent pick needs confirmation by a Senate Democrats will control from January.

Trump signed the bipartisan Common Cents Act, directing the government to stop minting the penny. A small symbolic win in a week dominated by outages and foreign policy.

The White House pulled Heidi Overton's FDA nomination after Republican objections. It shows the limits of GOP deference ahead of a harder confirmation environment in 2027.

The White House sent Congress a list of funding "anomalies" it wants in any stopgap bill. This sets up the pre-recess fight over the 11 December deadline.

Trump initially accepted the midterm results but days later questioned them, and most Republican voters now doubt they were fair. Only 52% of Republicans trust that votes were counted accurately, against 66% of Americans overall.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

Massie introduced new articles of impeachment against Hegseth, arguing he waged war in Iran without congressional authorisation. An earlier effort was sent to the Judiciary Committee 211 to 204 on 10 November, which effectively killed it, and Democrats could try again in January.

Lawmakers from both parties criticised Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as CEO. Schumer promised investigations, Warren (incoming Banking chair) called it "reckless", Rep. Bacon said Nvidia should have moved on, and Republican committee leaders are planning a Huang hearing on export controls.

The stopgap funding law expires 11 December, and a further continuing resolution is expected. No fiscal 2027 appropriations bill has become law, so a lapse would begin 12 December.

Recent floor action: the Senate took up the bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 on 19 to 20 November, and the House passed H.R. 9600 365 to 36 on 17 November. Vote records only, so check Congress.gov for final status.

Incoming Democratic chairs are already signalling oversight priorities. Bennie Thompson says Homeland Security Democrats will investigate immigration tactics, while Schumer and Warren are targeting Nvidia and export controls.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court agreed on 13 November to decide whether a president can suspend asylum at the southern border by proclamation. The D.C. Circuit rejected the government's argument in April, and the restrictions stay in force while the case proceeds.

A Washington Post column on 23 November argued the Court bucked Trump on tariffs but that the asylum case will be harder for challengers. This is opinion, but it reflects the shared read of the docket.

Justice Sotomayor addressed Supreme Court term limits in public remarks. SCOTUSblog covered it as part of the continuing debate over court reform.

On 2 November a federal judge accused the Justice Department of "weaponization" and tossed its case against two ex-FBI officials. It is part of a pattern of courts rebuffing politically charged prosecutions.

No new merits opinion dated 23 November turned up; SCOTUSblog's items that day were commentary. Treat the Court as quiet pending the December argument session.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Democrats won the national House vote about 56%–42%, which NPR calls the widest margin for either party in 50 years. They had flipped 47 seats with two races uncalled, and could reach 260 seats.

Pre-election polls understated the Democratic margin. The final generic-ballot average was about D+8, against a result near D+14.

A Reuters/Ipsos poll (13 to 16 November) found 49% of Democrats optimistic and 52% of Republicans discouraged. Republican distrust of the count is the key post-election story.

Republicans held House and Senate leadership elections in mid-November while killing the first Hegseth impeachment vote. Hegseth's fate and Trump's loyalty to him are an early test of internal GOP discipline.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Iran says it will use "all available means" against the US blockade, and says Hormuz could reopen within two days if Washington halts hostilities, lifts the blockade and waives sanctions. Mediators are working on a framework swapping an end to Iranian attacks in Hormuz for lifting the blockade, but talks have stalled since a June deal collapsed.

Israeli reporting says Israel is unlikely to strike Iran without US backing, despite fears Netanyahu might act alone. Opposition figure Liberman called a strike now politically motivated.

The Treasury issued new Iran-related sanctions on dozens of people and entities. Pressure continues on two tracks, sanctions and the blockade, while strikes are deferred. Iran International, 23 November (unconfirmed: relayed via aggregation)

The US-led Board of Peace warned it could hand Gaza back to the UN if Israel keeps blocking efforts to disarm Hamas. Netanyahu insists on full disarmament before any withdrawal, while the Board says that is already its sequencing. Jared Kushner separately warned Israel is trending toward "global-pariah status".

After Shenzhen, Putin said Ukraine and Europe must accept "compromises" and "realities", and Moscow rejects NATO-style guarantees. The FT reports the administration wants a deal by early January, before Democrats take Congress.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth fired the Pentagon's top military planner, Brett Sylvia, in late October, the second J5 chief ousted under him. It came as Trump weighed new Iran strikes.

Hegseth has reportedly not signed off on about 60 Patriot interceptors already allocated to Ukraine. The Pentagon says there is no change in policy. SRN News citing FT/Reuters, 24 November (unconfirmed: secondary relay)

The Pentagon published a "fact sheet" criticising Canadian defence spending after a classified Hegseth memo reportedly restricted military ties. It says NORAD, NATO and intelligence cooperation are untouched, but exercises and training are unclear.

Kasper's resignation call centres on Hegseth's relations with Congress, which turns Democrats' January takeover into a real oversight threat to him.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

NSANet, the NSA's classified internal network, went down on 17 November and was restored after about three days. The DNI said a software patch for critical vulnerabilities caused it, and officials report no evidence of a cyberattack.

Reporting on NSA staff returning after the outage is from a live blog. NBC said the network issue was resolved and staff were coming back. NBC News live blog (unconfirmed: single-source)

Some commentary links the outages to AI or foreign attack, but this is not supported by official statements. A Democratic senator said AI contributed, while Rubio said the incidents were not connected; low-quality sites make bolder claims.

Open-source: IISS satellite-image analysis, reported by Newsweek on 23 November, suggests a transloader at a Russian site that may mean the RS-28 Sarmat is entering service. This is open-source and unverified, and no ISW or Bellingcat item dated 23 November was found.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Markets broadly expect the Fed to raise rates by 25 basis points on 8 to 9 December from the current 3.75%–4.00% range. NY Fed's Williams said inflation is still too high and further hikes are possible.

Williams sees growth near 2.75%, unemployment edging toward 4% from 4.1%, and 2% inflation only in 2028. He said the tariff effect on prices has largely faded but could return with new tariffs.

Chicago Fed's Goolsbee called the job market stable but inflation "way higher" than 2%. One outlet puts inflation near 3.5%, driven by tariffs, Middle East supply disruptions and AI investment. InvestingLive (unconfirmed: figure from one outlet)

Trump vowed to stop US firms building data centres abroad and threatened a "tremendous tax". He also said "we don't need a deal with Iran", and the Shenzhen leaders pledged to boost oil and refined-product output.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE set records in fiscal 2026, with more than 491,000 arrests and 483,000 deportations. DHS did not say how many of those removed had criminal histories, according to AP and CBS.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin condemned Texas officials planning to curb ICE cooperation once Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa takes office. Texas' 287(g) mandate requires sheriffs to seek ICE agreements by 1 December, so jail cooperation may persist.

Houston's mayor barred police from notifying ICE during nonviolent encounters, and the Texas AG has sued over the ordinance.

DHS's weekend arrest roundup listed 15 people with criminal convictions but omitted totals. The source is an analysis site, so read it as a critique of the data. Immigration Analytics, 23 November (unconfirmed)

11. States and governors.

A D.C. Council member asked incoming Democratic governors in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas to withdraw 628 National Guard troops deployed in the capital. The troops are federally paid but under governors' control.

Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa is at the centre of the ICE-cooperation fight before she takes office.

Virginia's Democratic governor, Abigail Spanberger, is reportedly again facing DHS pressure on ICE cooperation. Only a snippet and a February item were found. Immigration Analytics, 23 November (unconfirmed)

The National Governors Association says any change affecting the Guard needs governors' consent. That sets up friction with the Pentagon.

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump's approval was about 40% among registered voters in pre-election polling, with 60% disapproving.

An NBC poll found voters demanding "a check on Trump", with Democrats leading 52%–45% on which party should control Congress.

Coverage of the outages splits by outlet. Fox emphasised the cyber director's lack of technical background, while fringe sites pushed unsupported attack and AI theories.

Trump defended his relationships with Xi and Putin after the trip. Critics from both parties question what Shenzhen delivered.
