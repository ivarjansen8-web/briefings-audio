Here is your US briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Miller takes over as chief of staff; White House threatens Democratic probes; Hegseth impeachment killed as inflation hits 4%.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 12 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump's new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, took over on 9 November after Susie Wiles was pushed out following the 3 November midterm losses. Miller already ran policy and homeland security, so the move concentrates the immigration-first agenda at the centre of the West Wing; ABC sources say Trump privately blamed Wiles for the defeat.

The White House counsel warned incoming House Democrats that their planned investigations "may be a crime." Will Scharf's letter to Rep. Robert Garcia, the incoming Oversight chair, sets up the first major executive-legislative clash of the new Congress; Jeffries called it "outrageous" and Garcia said probes will go ahead.

The House killed Thomas Massie's Hegseth impeachment resolution 211 to 204 on 10 November by referring it to committee. The vote was procedural, with Massie the only Republican opposed and many Democrats absent, so it says little about Hegseth's long-term standing.

October CPI inflation hit about 4% year on year, with markets pricing roughly 90% odds of a December Fed hike. The White House says it will stay the course on tariffs despite voters punishing Republicans over prices.

Trump says the US has "won" the Iran war while Rubio reports no progress in talks. The blockade continues and the mixed signals on renewed strikes dominate the foreign-policy picture (details in section 6).

2. White House and the administration.

Stephen Miller is now chief of staff, and Wiles moves to the Trump political operation. Trump announced it on Truth Social on 6 November; Wiles reportedly resigned the night before, and the shake-up follows broad GOP losses.

Trump blamed Fox News and GOP candidates for the midterm losses. He wrote that the network "bears responsibility", while also claiming he "wasn't on the ballot"; the attack on his most reliable media ally signals a defensive post-election posture.

The White House is not ruling out a deal with Democrats on long-resident undocumented immigrants, angering parts of the base. It is a notable break from the enforcement-only line, though the substance remains vague.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent reportedly moved to a military base after an Iranian threat against his life (unconfirmed). The claim is relayed by Daily Kos citing the New York Times, so treat it as secondhand.

A federal judge let Trump's 10 billion dollars defamation suit against the BBC proceed (unconfirmed, relayed via The Guardian). It keeps the President's litigation against media outlets in play.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The Senate voted 73 to 25 on 9 November to advance the Trump-backed permitting overhaul (Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act). This was cloture only; final passage and House reconciliation remain, and environmental groups such as the Sierra Club are now opposed.

The House passed H.R. 1013, a charities and educational institutions retirement bill, 295 to 128 on 11 November. A low-salience bipartisan vote amid the lame-duck session.

Government funding expires 11 December, and a stopgap is the likely outcome. The debt limit, NDAA and farm bill also compete for lame-duck floor time before Democrats take the House.

Democrats say they will proceed with oversight despite the Scharf letter. Garcia stressed Article I authority; legal experts noted the letter came from the White House counsel rather than DOJ.

GOP leadership elections took place alongside the Hegseth vote on 10 November. Massie, who leaves Congress in January, is pivoting to a war powers resolution on Iran.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court on 9 November let the administration resume border wall construction in Big Bend, Texas. It stayed the lower-court ruling while declining summary reversal; Justice Jackson would have denied the request.

The same Monday the Court heard two arguments and denied review in over 200 cases. Eleven new relists are being watched for a fourth vote.

A petition over the White House ballroom has reached the Court, and the Kennedy family joined suits to block Trump's proposed triumphal arch (unconfirmed for the arch suit). Both test executive power over Washington landmarks.

Just Security's 11 November edition leads with a judge's ruling on detention and a DOJ green-card case tied to Rep. Ilhan Omar's former bodyguard. The latter is single-source (unconfirmed).

Lawfare's Matthew Tokson argues recent cases indicate license-plate-reader cameras are unconstitutional. A commentary signal for Fourth Amendment litigation.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval averages about 38%, with disapproval near 59%, after the midterms. The latest Reuters/Ipsos (6 to 9 November) put him at 32% approve and 65% disapprove among adults.

Democrats won back the House and made gains at state level, including both Wisconsin legislative chambers. That creates a Wisconsin trifecta and opens a possible pre-2028 redraw.

Maryland voters loosened anti-gerrymandering rules, and Gov. Wes Moore wants a new 8 to 0 map. Kemp and Gov. Sherrill have ruled out redistricting; Shapiro says it is not on the agenda.

A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 62% of Americans back repealing Trump's tariffs. Price anxiety is the dominant post-election theme.

Conservative media are split over blame, with Fox under presidential attack and MAGA outlets sparing Trump. The framing differs sharply by outlet.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Rubio told G7 counterparts in Paris on 10 November the US will not restart the broader Iran war but will keep up the economic and naval-blockade pressure. He reported no progress in talks and called Iran's demands "totally unrealistic".

Iran says sanctions, the blockade and the war must end before any negotiations. Parliament speaker Ghalibaf says a deal is possible and "the ball is in the US court"; the IRGC lifted its missile-range limit and threatened US bases.

Reports describe a split inside Trump's team, with Rubio backing the blockade and Vance favouring a deal. Al Jazeera frames it as a split; the administration has not confirmed this (unconfirmed).

Israel's Defense Minister Katz says the Iran mission is "not complete" and Israel will stay in security zones in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria. Gaza coverage is thin, with winter flooding worsening the humanitarian situation.

Elsewhere, trilateral US-Russia-Ukraine talks resumed in Abu Dhabi, and analysts note a paused 14 billion dollars Taiwan arms sale. Chatham House expects a cooler US stance toward Moscow and Beijing after the midterms.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth held his first Pentagon press briefing since May and called the impeachment effort a "political spectacle". Trump has said his job is safe, but Democrats vow renewed scrutiny in January.

Hegseth ordered an overhaul of senior service colleges with 33 directives, cutting civilian faculty. The 9 November memo wants a focus on "victory" and faculty with direct military experience.

A Tomb of the Unknown Soldier sentinel appeared to rebuke Hegseth's group on Veterans Day. The Pentagon says the guard acted "correctly and respectfully".

The Daily Beast reports Hegseth used petty criteria to deny promotions to women and minorities (unconfirmed, partisan outlet). Single-source and unverified.

CENTCOM says it is enforcing an "ironclad" blockade of Iran and reports an attack on a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman. A new task force aims to disrupt Iranian transport routes.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

US intelligence sharing has fractured under Trump, officials say. Reports describe slowed or halted sharing with Congress and agencies, and the CIA no longer co-authoring assessments with the National Intelligence Council.

Jay Clayton appears to be the current DNI, having taken over in August, and has resumed election-threat briefings (unconfirmed from search snippets). Gabbard's departure and ODNI-CIA tension are referenced in the same coverage.

The FBI reportedly paused some sharing with the NCTC for weeks after the NCTC probed the Charlie Kirk shooting. An example of inter-agency friction cited in the same report.

Open-source: Bellingcat (5 November) showed publicly available online maps expose sensitive details of US bases targeted by Iran. Open-source analysis, not independently verified here.

Open-source: Bellingcat found viral "new" Gaza satellite views were Airbus imagery from February 2026. A reminder to verify satellite claims.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

October CPI rose 0.4% on the month and about 4% year on year, up from 3.7%; core rose 0.3%. Both beat expectations even as gasoline prices levelled off.

Fed officials are weighing faster hikes, and markets see about 90% odds of a December increase. A Reuters poll expects a 25bp hike to 4.00 to 4.25%.

The White House vowed to press ahead with tariffs. A New York Fed study estimates about 26% pass-through to consumers, with effects lagging nine to twelve months.

Stocks edged up on a drop in crude prices. Oil remains sensitive to Hormuz headlines.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said "Deportations will continue" as Democrats prepare to take the House. Roughly 240 billion dollars in recent funding lets ICE and CBP operate through Trump's term regardless of oversight.

ICE detention of California nurse Ann Lawani has drawn demands from Rep. Josh Harder for her release. A judge blocked deportation to Nigeria citing likely persecution; DHS and conservative outlets stress a 2013 final removal order.

The Supreme Court cleared Big Bend border wall work. It is a win for DHS on border infrastructure.

DHS touts a 180-plus arrest operation in Alabama and record-low border crossings. These are DHS's own figures.

11. States and governors.

Gov. Wes Moore, re-elected, is pushing a new Maryland map after voters eased the anti-gerrymandering law. An 8 to 0 Democratic map is possible.

Georgia's Brian Kemp will not call a special session on redistricting this year. Kemp and Gov. Mikie Sherrill both ruled out post-election redraws.

Wisconsin Democrats flipped both legislative chambers. A pre-2028 redraw is plausible, but Gov. Evers is no longer the one deciding.

Missouri's 2025 map faces a veto referendum. The timing details need confirming (unconfirmed).

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump's attack on Fox News follows a ratings slump, with October viewership at 1.1 million, down about 27% year on year. CNN notes he still phones Fox anchors on election night.

Trump's 10 billion dollars BBC suit proceeds (unconfirmed, single secondhand source). A test of his media litigation strategy.

Reuters/Ipsos puts Trump approval at 32%, with 62% favouring tariff repeal. The public mood is dominated by prices.

A Lawfare webinar on the US-Iran war was held 11 November. Expert commentary is shifting toward war-powers questions.
