Here is your US briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

Trump-Xi-Putin summit in Shenzhen, US-Iran Gulf flare-up, FDA nominee dropped.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 19 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen on 19 November for the first-ever trilateral summit on the sidelines of APEC, with nuclear proliferation as the stated focus. A senior US official said no agreement is expected, and the Kremlin said no statements would follow; no readout of outcomes had appeared at time of writing.

The US struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island on 17 November, and Iran then hit at Kuwait and Bahrain and an ADNOC vessel in Hormuz (11 injured, one missing). The US called the strike "self-defense" after an Iranian attack on a radar site in Bahrain, which CENTCOM says was intercepted; it shows the Islamabad memorandum is not holding the line.

The White House withdrew Heidi Overton's FDA nomination on 17 November, a day before a scheduled Senate committee vote. Reporting cites eroding GOP support, an "icy" relationship with RFK Jr. and anti-abortion senators demanding action on mifepristone.

Fed minutes released 18 November show nearly all officials expect a quarter-point hike in December, with two floating half a point. Inflation, partly energy-driven by the Iran war, is the pressure point for a president at 32% approval.

2. White House and the administration.

The White House told congressional Democrats it need not answer document demands while they are in the minority, per a letter from counsel Will Scharf. The posture signals a fight over oversight once Democrats take the House in January; RedState frames it as the White House "turning the tables."

The FDA is now led by acting commissioner Kyle Diamantas, with no replacement named. Sen. Bill Cassidy appeared to rule out another nominee before Republicans lose the Senate in January, so the vacancy may persist.

The White House released details of the Manila leg of Trump's Asia trip, claiming more than 17 dollarsbillion in commercial deals and strategic investments. The figures are administration claims and not independently verified.

Trump told Fox News the Islamabad Iran memorandum was "a waste of time" yet defended signing it. He wants a deal that would "supersede" a future "weak president," and said US forces stay until then.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The Senate voted 65 to 31 on 18 November to invoke cloture on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, the permitting-reform package. Final passage is expected before the Thanksgiving recess; nearly half of Senate Democrats and many House progressives oppose it, so the House is less certain.

House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker on 18 November, reportedly 251 to 11. He needs 218 floor votes in January; Democrats plan probes of Trump-family corruption, corporate corruption, DHS misconduct and the Epstein matter, with Raskin and Garcia positioned to chair Judiciary and Oversight.

Rep. Thomas Massie reintroduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Hegseth, three of them on Iran war powers. An earlier forced vote in September failed; the outcome of this round was not confirmed in my sources.

A Senate resolution to disapprove an HHS health-coverage rule failed 51 to 48 on the motion to proceed. The date of that vote is not clearly confirmed (unconfirmed).

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The administration filed an emergency application (26A714) on 18 November asking the Supreme Court to block Judge Edward Chen's order restoring TPS for roughly 300,000 Venezuelans. The temporary hold lapses Friday 20 November; Sauer warns of releasing about 1,800 detainees and halting 332 removal orders, and calls this the government's third trip to the Court on the case.

The Court agreed to decide whether the administration can suspend asylum access at the US-Mexico border. It is a headline-level item only in my sources; argument timing was not found (unconfirmed).

On 16 November the Court turned away DEI-related disputes and a Second Amendment challenge to "sensitive places" gun bans. It acted without noted dissent in the DEI matters, per SCOTUSblog.

The justices' next conference has transgender servicemembers, E. Jean Carroll's jury award and acting US attorneys on the table. No merits opinion was issued on 18 November.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Reuters/Ipsos (13 to 16 November, n=1,521) put Trump's approval at 32%, with 66% disapproving, the fourth straight poll at that level. It follows the midterm loss of the House; polling averages sit higher, around 36 to 38%, depending on the tracker.

House Democrats' closed-door vote re-elected Jeffries leader reportedly 251 to 11, with some members criticising his fight against the administration. Senate Democrats hold their own leadership vote on 1 December per an advocacy page (unconfirmed).

Democrats say they ran on affordability and now must show delivery alongside oversight. NPR notes open questions about how the party balances investigations with legislating.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Trump said the Iran war will end "one way or the other" and demands a lasting deal, while Rubio said there is "no need" to restart full combat operations. The mixed signals leave a blockade-and-tit-for-tat status quo; Israel is pressing for new strikes.

Trump claimed Iran is "looking at gas, looking at bio" after being shut out of nuclear options; Iran's Foreign Ministry called this baseless. Rubio separately said he sees no imminent threat to the US homeland (unconfirmed on timing).

Bahrain, home of the US Fifth Fleet, has faced four Iranian attacks in six days, and the Arab League called it a "continuous escalation pattern." Gulf states had urged Washington not to attack Iran, fearing retaliation.

The IDF reported an operation inside Gaza west of the "Yellow Line" to remove a threat to troops. Details are thin and come from a single report (unconfirmed).

ISW assessed that Putin used APEC's first day to pose Russia as an equal of China and the US, and that Russia launched 141 long-range drones on 17 to 18 November. No territorial change was recorded that day.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth signed a military cooperation deal in Caracas with Venezuela's interim government, ten months after US forces ousted Maduro. The meeting was not announced in advance, which critics flag as a transparency problem.

Colombia, under President Abelardo De La Espriella, rejected Hegseth's military cooperation agreement, upending a four-country tour that also includes Ecuador and Honduras. The Daily Beast reports he would stop only briefly in Colombia; this is a single outlet (unconfirmed).

Top US and Israeli military chiefs met in Tel Aviv as Israel pressed for renewed Iran strikes. It is a signal of continuing coordination despite Trump's reluctance to resume the full war.

MS NOW reported on Hegseth's criteria for senior officer promotions, drawing a Pentagon rebuttal from spokesperson Sean Parnell. The piece is from about 9 November and critical in tone.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

FBI Director Kash Patel skipped the House Homeland Security Committee's worldwide threats hearing on 18 November, sending Deputy Director Christopher Raia. Chairman Andrew Garbarino (R) said the date was given in August and called it a departure from precedent; Democrat Bennie Thompson also objected, and an FBI official said Patel was visiting field offices.

A partisan opinion site claims Patel attended a Mar-a-Lago gala the night before. It is unverified and from an opinion outlet (unconfirmed).

Open-source, unverified: ISW says Russian services reportedly planned a false-flag sabotage on Kaliningrad blamed on Ukraine, likely disrupted by European warnings about Poland. It rests on reported intelligence, not independent confirmation.

Open-source: Bellingcat (5 November) showed how public mapping platforms expose sensitive details of US bases in the Middle East later targeted by Iran. Commercial imagery over Iran remains restricted, so independent damage assessment of the Qeshm strike is not yet available.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

The Fed minutes show broad support for a quarter-point hike on 8 to 9 December; Governor Waller says a half-point may be needed to "shock people." The policy rate is 3.9% after a first hike in three years in September; Chair Warsh resists forward guidance.

Tariff pass-through is debated: the Minneapolis Fed estimates 0.2 to 0.4 points added to core inflation, while a New York Fed study says goods prices would otherwise have fallen. Trump insists tariffs "cause wealth," and CNBC notes voter backlash over prices.

Cleveland Fed nowcasts cited by Motley Fool put November PCE inflation near 4%. Energy costs from the Iran conflict and AI-driven growth are cited as drivers (single-source, unconfirmed).

Trump's Manila deal claims of 17 dollarsbillion and China talks put trade on the Shenzhen agenda; no tariff outcome has been reported yet.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE set records in fiscal 2026, with about 483,200 deportations and 491,000 arrests. Syracuse's TRAC estimates only about 316,000 formal removals through 24 August, arguing DHS counts non-removal departures.

A report estimates about 91 dollarsbillion in unspent ICE funds that could last through the term. It is one report, not independently verified (unconfirmed).

Bennie Thompson, incoming Homeland Security chair, has said the committee will investigate administration enforcement tactics. The Venezuela TPS fight at the Supreme Court is the near-term test of the program's wind-down.

11. States and governors.

D.C. officials are lobbying incoming Democratic governors in Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas to recall Guard troops stationed in the capital. Georgia supplies 514 of them, and governors' ability to pull them on demand is unclear.

The Ninth Circuit on 23 October upheld an injunction against deploying California's National Guard in Los Angeles and returned control to Gov. Newsom. It is the leading appellate precedent in the Guard litigation.

Quiet: no significant redistricting developments surfaced for 16 to 19 November.

12. Media and public opinion.

Coverage frames Trump's 32% approval as a career low, while conservative outlets such as Fox emphasise his Asia diplomacy and Iran resolve. The gap between trackers (32% versus about 38%) matters when citing "the" number.

Right-leaning outlets cast the White House's refusal of Democratic document requests as turning the tables on investigators; Democratic-aligned outlets cast it as stonewalling.

The FDA nominee's collapse was covered by NPR and CNN as a GOP-internal revolt, while PJ Media stressed the Senate was "ready to vote."
