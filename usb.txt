Here is your US briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Democrats' midterm sweep, Iran war flares as Israel readies strike, Supreme Court takes asylum case.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 14 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Democrats won both chambers in the midterms, with House projections ranging from about 250 to 268 seats and more than 40 Republican seats flipped. Divided government starts in January, giving Democrats subpoena power over an administration whose approval is in the low 30s.

Trump's approval fell to 32% in a Reuters/Ipsos poll taken 6 to 9 November, with 65% disapproving. That is one point above his record low of 31% in mid-October, and it shows no post-election recovery.

The Iran war is escalating again: Israel's security cabinet reportedly ordered stepped-up preparations for an attack, and Iran threatened every base involved "from West Asia to Europe". Trump says restarting the war is "highly unlikely" but has also warned he could strike.

The Supreme Court agreed on 13 November to hear the administration's suspension of border asylum. A lower-court ruling striking the policy stays on hold meanwhile.

Treasury Secretary Bessent moved his family to a military base after reported Iranian assassination plots. A DNI memo of 23 October said such plots had reached an "advanced stage".

2. White House and the administration.

Stephen Miller became chief of staff on 9 November, replacing Susie Wiles. Trump announced it on 6 November and denied it was about the midterms, but CNN and Axios report he was furious over the results.

Trump questioned the midterm results on 9 November, blaming voting machines and early-vote "dumps" without naming any specific discrepancy. This follows his election-night pledge to accept the result. Leavitt framed his posts as support for the SAVE Act.

A CNBC headline says Trump offered UFC chief Dana White the press secretary job (unconfirmed). It is a single headline I could not corroborate, and Karoline Leavitt still appears as press secretary elsewhere.

Trump reportedly told Netanyahu he wants him to take steps to implement the Gaza peace plan. The report comes from Axios via Just Security (unconfirmed).

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The House voted 211 to 204 on 10 November to refer Rep. Massie's Hegseth impeachment articles to the Judiciary Committee. The referral effectively kills them in this Congress. Massie was the only Republican to vote with Democrats, and he said he will not bring another resolution before leaving in January.

Senate Republicans kept John Thune as leader after the election losses. Thune said they would work with Democrats but rejected "leftist fantasies".

Sen. Chris Van Hollen is challenging Chuck Schumer for Democratic leader. Schumer says he has the votes, while Warnock and Warner decline to back him publicly.

Funding runs out on 11 December, and a stopgap looks likely. Democrats are unlikely to negotiate full-year bills before they take control, and the NDAA and the debt limit are also on the year-end list.

The House held only a pro forma session on 13 November and returns for votes Tuesday. The Senate meets Monday at 3 p.m. for a cloture vote.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The justices will hear the challenge to the asylum suspension next term. The question is whether the president can bar migrants from seeking asylum at the border under an "invasion" proclamation.

On 9 November the Court let Big Bend border wall construction resume, with Justice Jackson the only noted dissent. The 341-mile section includes the area around Big Bend National Park.

The Court added a securities-fraud standard case involving Robinhood to its 2026 to 27 docket.

Lawfare notes the legal force of the bicameral Iran war-powers concurrent resolution is doubtful after Chadha, and the administration argues hostilities "terminated" in May. That analysis is older background, not a new development.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Post-midterm polling shows two-thirds of Americans disapprove of Trump, and the Poliwave average is 37.9% approve to 59.9% disapprove.

Reuters/Ipsos found 71% support congressional legislation to end the Iran war. The figure is cited by Middle East Monitor, a Gaza-sympathetic outlet, so check it against the original poll.

Early 2028 polling puts Vance and Rubio on top for Republicans, with Ossoff and Buttigieg named by Democrats.

Alaska and Mississippi Senate races were undecided as of 8 November, and about 75 House races were still uncalled in early counts. Final seat totals may shift.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Israel's security cabinet reportedly ordered the military to prepare for an attack on Iran, and Tehran says it would treat every base involved as a target. Al Jazeera cites Reuters and AP.

Trump says he has "already won" the Iran war and wants "total victory", and he expects to discuss ending it at APEC. Reuters reports Iran is not negotiating directly with the US, with Pakistan and Qatar mediating.

A coalition of more than 50 countries led by the UK, Qatar and Jordan demanded immediate aid entry into Gaza.

Netanyahu's office says Israel will move gradually off US military aid, which contradicts Trump's account.

Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow on 12 November with no breakthrough. Putin rejected European peacekeepers and said war would "resume immediately" if they deploy. Kushner is pushing a large prisoner exchange, and Putin and Trump are expected to talk at APEC.

A Trump-Xi meeting is expected at APEC in Shenzhen on 18 to 19 November, after both sides extended their trade truce.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth survived the impeachment push but called it a "spectacle" and a political stunt. Republicans were frustrated by the Iran war but did not see his conduct as impeachable.

Hegseth wants to raise the top special operations civilian to a service-secretary-equivalent rank. A 2 November memo became public on 12 November, and some of the changes, including moving 100 staff and 45 dollarsmillion, need no congressional approval.

Iran reportedly attacked a US-linked early-warning radar in Bahrain, according to an IRGC claim (unconfirmed). Earlier attacks on Gulf bases and Hormuz shipping continue.

Poland says the White House will decide on a permanent US troop presence at the start of December.

Hegseth awarded a medal to the Arlington sentinel who had chastised his party for noise.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

The DNI warned in a 23 October memo to Rubio that Iran had "substantially increased efforts" to target US personnel, with lethal plots at advanced stages. Officials also say Iran has limited ability to carry out large attacks in the US, and Tehran denies the plots.

Jay Clayton leads the intelligence community, sworn in on 3 August after a 51 to 47 vote, following Tulsi Gabbard's resignation.

The Intercept reports Trump tapped a campaign lawyer and pardon adviser for a top ODNI role (unconfirmed). I could only see the headline, not the name or position.

Open-source: CTP-ISW's 12 November update says it has seen no indicators that Iran has re-mined the Strait of Hormuz. It also covers Houthi-Saudi fighting and says it cannot verify a Houthi drone-strike claim. Analyst assessments, not verified.

Open-source (unverified): SouthFront claims two supertankers exploded in Hormuz, citing the IRGC. SouthFront is a pro-Russian outlet and the claim is unconfirmed.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Futures put about 90% odds on a Fed rate hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting, and Dallas Fed's Logan backs two more quarter-point hikes. Inflation is spreading into the broader economy.

A New York Fed study says tariffs added 2.9 percentage points to inflation in some consumer goods by February, easing to about 2% by August. The full effect may not arrive until 2027.

Right-leaning outlets argue the Fed's own model shows tariffs can boost growth, while critics flag flaws in the NY Fed study.

The US-China truce was extended, with China keeping a 10% levy and suspending 24%. Section 122 replacement tariffs followed the Court's February ruling against IEEPA tariffs.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

ICE reported a record 483,200 deportations and over 491,000 arrests in fiscal 2026. That is below Trump's one-million goal, and the Migration Policy Institute puts the broader total near 660,000.

About 59% of voters in NBC exit polling said enforcement went too far, and only about 20% of removals involved a criminal conviction. Trump has conceded the crackdown may have been "too tough", and Democrats are preparing investigations.

Miller, the architect of the crackdown, now runs the White House. That points to continuity despite the electoral rebuke.

NBC reports an armed man was arrested near the White House for allegedly threatening Trump (unconfirmed).

11. States and governors.

NYC Mayor Mamdani is expected to unveil a plan to disband the NYPD's Strategic Response Group as early as this week (Politico, via Just Security).

Quiet: no other significant state or governor developments surfaced in the last 72 hours. Searches returned mostly 2025 material.

12. Media and public opinion.

Coverage splits along party lines over tariffs, Iran and the election. Breitbart and Hot Air stress tariff benefits, while CNN and Axios stress Trump's anger at the results.

Opinion has hardened against the war and the crackdown: 71% back ending the Iran war and 59% say immigration enforcement went too far.

Conservative commentary is turning on Senate leadership, with PJ Media floating "giving Thune to the Democrats".
