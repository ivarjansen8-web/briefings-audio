Here is your US briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

New overcapacity tariffs loom, Nvidia's Huang returns amid bipartisan backlash, CIA/ODNI open-source network breached.

Compiled from sources dated 22 to 25 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The administration is preparing new "overcapacity" tariffs on 16 trading partners, including China, the EU, Mexico and Japan, which could be unveiled as soon as this week. It is the latest attempt to rebuild the tariff wall after the Supreme Court struck down the emergency tariffs earlier this year; reported rates are 15% or less for the EU, Japan and South Korea and higher for China (rates unconfirmed).

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO after White House input, drawing bipartisan backlash. Huang had stepped down a little over a week earlier amid a scandal reportedly involving an alleged Chinese spy; Schumer vowed to use Democrats' "full power" to investigate.

Hackers breached an unclassified open-source intelligence network used by the CIA and ODNI. Officials say no classified systems were hit and the issue is resolved, but the NYT reports other agencies' data, possibly including NGA's, may have been exposed.

Democrats have secured 258 House seats, the largest majority either party has won in three decades. Jeffries is poised to become Speaker and has not ruled out a third impeachment of Trump.

The December. 11 funding deadline is looming, with the White House asking for Golden Dome money in any stopgap while Democrats weigh using the deadline to constrain Iran war spending.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump pardoned two turkeys, "Freedom" and "Feathers," on Tuesday, at the new White House helipad, in the annual pre-Thanksgiving tradition. It is the only routine event in an otherwise tense week.

Trump publicly criticised the judge in the East Potomac golf course case, implying bias because she was a Biden appointee. Attacks on named judges keep feeding the administration's wider friction with the courts.

Trump named Jay Clayton acting national cyber director (unconfirmed), amid reports the previous director is leaving after a series of tech outages. The move comes as the CIA/ODNI breach adds pressure on federal cyber leadership.

The White House sent Congress a list of funding "anomalies" including 5 dollarsbillion for Golden Dome and 2 dollarsbillion for NASA's Artemis, assuming a stopgap that runs to 31 March, per Punchbowl. Air traffic control and other defence items are also reportedly included.

Trump defended his role in Huang's return while bipartisan critics, including Sen. Kevin Cramer (R-N.D.), argued the national-security risk was too great.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

Both chambers are in a holiday recess with only pro forma sessions; votes resume Monday 30 November. The Senate has a confirmation vote scheduled that day for Kyle Reardon, nominee for the District of Alaska.

Rep. Jimmy Gomez (D-Calif.) is resigning, effective 1 December, after a House Ethics report found he engaged in sexual misconduct with a staffer. Jeffries, Clark and Aguilar called the resignation "appropriate"; Gomez lost his re-election bid and disputes the findings.

House Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and China committees announced Huang will testify next week. Rep. Jim Himes (D-Conn.) said Congress still has questions; Rep. Jay Obernolte (R-Calif.) warned the affair could complicate AI-chip export-control talks.

Funding runs out on Friday 11 December, with a lapse starting 12 December. Reports differ on shutdown odds, and Democrats are split over tying the deadline to Iran war limits.

Three House races remained uncalled as of 24 November (MN-1, LA-5, LA-6). The final seat count could still shift marginally.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court deferred Cook County's request to dismiss the AR-15 ban dispute as moot, citing Acheson Hotels v. Laufer, just over a week before argument. The Court also dropped lead plaintiff Cutberto Viramontes, so the case becomes Khaya v. Cook County.

Oral argument in the assault-weapons-ban challenges is set for 2 December. It is the term's biggest Second Amendment test.

The Court recently turned away DEI-related disputes and a challenge to "sensitive places" gun bans. Denials like these leave lower-court rulings in place.

Lower courts keep ruling against DOJ over prosecutor appointments: a fifth Bondi-appointed US attorney was held unlawfully serving, and the DOJ is appealing (dated earlier than the 72-hour window).

Massachusetts is suing ICE over immigration arrests in state courts, per AILA's 24 November clips.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval averages in the high 30s: Poliwave has 37.8% approve and 60.4% disapprove as of 23 November. An American Research Group poll shows 29% (an outlier), while Rasmussen likely-voter polling shows 43%.

Democrats' 258 House seats mark their strongest result since 1992. Jeffries has declined to rule out impeaching Trump a third time, a move that would split Democrats.

Maryland voters approved Question 3 with about 60%, allowing Democrats to redraw the congressional map. NBC reports the mid-decade redistricting fight continues but is likely to slow after the Democratic wave.

Tom Homan said "elections don't change laws" and vowed to exceed 2026 deportation totals in 2027, framing the administration as unmoved by the result.

6. Foreign policy (Middle East focus)

New US Treasury sanctions hit more than two dozen Iranian entities in energy and shadow banking, and the rial fell sharply. Iran's IRGC says four tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz while the US naval blockade continues.

US Ambassador Mike Huckabee said Trump is ready to resume military action against Iran if diplomacy fails. Israel's energy minister said Israel has acted against US wishes before, fuelling reports Israel could strike Iran without Washington.

The Board of Peace said Hamas and factions accepted a disarmament roadmap, but on-the-ground implementation remains limited. Adviser Josh Gruenbaum threatened to "hand the keys" to the UN, and Kushner reportedly warned Israel of "global-pariah status"; Israeli officials counter that Hamas must disarm first.

US-Iran mediated talks remain stuck on Tehran's demand to lift the blockade and release frozen assets before reopening Hormuz (latest detailed reporting dates from September).

US-Russia-Ukraine talks resumed in Abu Dhabi on 10 to 11 November after nine months, with Kushner saying gaps narrowed but Russia showing no compromise on maximalist demands. No newer round was found.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

The Pentagon released a two-page fact sheet accusing Canada of failing as a credible defence partner, citing no plan to meet spending targets. It follows the Carney government's refusal to prioritise defence spending.

Hegseth reportedly issued a classified directive restricting military cooperation with Canada. Sen. Mike Rounds said it only requires approval before joint events, and the Pentagon says NORAD, NATO and intelligence cooperation are unaffected; Ottawa says it was not told.

The White House is seeking 5 dollarsbillion for Golden Dome in the next stopgap, with more defence requests promised.

The US naval blockade of Iran continues amid mine incidents in Hormuz, keeping the Navy committed in the Gulf.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

The CIA and ODNI breach hit an unclassified open-source analysis system, first reported by the NYT on 24 November. ODNI says there were "no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems", the CIA declined comment, and no attribution has been made.

One outlet reports the FBI is investigating, and links the breach to a recent NSA outage and an apparent FBI database hack (unconfirmed). Acting DNI Jay Clayton has said the outages were separate and tied to maintenance, per the same report; I could not corroborate the Clayton details elsewhere.

The Huang affair has pulled intelligence oversight in: the House Intelligence Committee is among those seeking his testimony over the alleged Chinese-spy link (unconfirmed details).

Open-source note: no new ISW, Bellingcat or satellite-imagery findings dated within 72 hours turned up in my searches. Quiet on that front.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

The coming tariffs rest on a probe launched in March into excess capacity abroad, a route used after the Supreme Court dismantled the emergency tariffs. China calls the overcapacity claim a "false proposition"; the EU wants rates to respect the 15% Turnberry ceiling.

Markets expect a quarter-point Fed hike at the 8 to 9 December meeting, from 3.75 to 4.00%. NY Fed's John Williams said inflation is "too high".

A New York Fed study found tariffs added 2.9 points to inflation in 67 goods categories by February, though the Minneapolis Fed argues tariffs now explain only 0.2 to 0.4 points of core inflation.

Nvidia export-control politics are heating up as senators push bipartisan chip-export bills.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE logged 483,200 deportations and over 491,000 arrests in fiscal 2026, both records, beating FY25's 442,640 deportations.

ProPublica counts 97,050 immigration habeas cases since January 2025 (as of 17 November), reflecting an overloaded courts system.

Headlines on 24 November include ICE reviving decades-old cases against green-card holders and families separated at the Canadian border. These are headline-level items from a clips service.

A federal ruling found immigration authorities repeatedly violated an injunction on warrantless arrests in Oregon. AOL/news summary (date unconfirmed)

11. States and governors.

Massachusetts is suing ICE over arrests in state courthouses. The suit is part of a wider push by Democratic states against federal enforcement tactics.

Missouri's map remains in litigation after a 4 to 3 state Supreme Court ruling that redistricting is not barred outside census years, and a Republican challenge to Virginia's amendment continues.

Governors of New York and New Jersey said they want to explore redistricting to counter Republican efforts, though procedural limits apply.

Maryland's Question 3 passed at roughly 60%.

12. Media and public opinion.

Affordability is weighing on Trump's ratings, with 39% saying their finances are worse than a year ago. The tariff push risks deepening that perception.

Polling averages put net approval around -22.6, with Rasmussen the friendliest outlet and ARG the harshest. Aggregators differ, so treat the exact figures with caution.

Framing differs on Nvidia: Axios and The Hill stress bipartisan alarm, while the administration casts Huang's return as protecting a US champion.

Israeli and Gulf outlets frame the Gaza stall differently: Times of Israel and JNS stress Hamas and Israeli security conditions, while Tehran Times calls the Board of Peace a tool of Israel.
