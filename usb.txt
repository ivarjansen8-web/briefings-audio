Here is your US briefing for Friday, 20 November.

Trump-Xi-Putin summit yields little, tech outages rattle US, Iran strike decision looms.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 20 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump holds a White House news conference at noon today after his Shenzhen meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin. He says he will announce trade agreements with Thailand, the Philippines and others; Democrats such as Schumer call the trip a "historical embarrassment".

The trilateral summit ended with a "peace declaration" that omits Ukraine and Iran. The four agreed points were UN reform, refined-fuel supply, a warning to the ICC and similar bodies, and lower nuclear risk, so concrete results are thin.

A wave of tech outages hit air travel, banking and government systems this week, and officials say there is no sign of a coordinated attack. The FAA grounded flights for hours and Microsoft services were down for nearly two days; Rubio said he had "zero" information on a malign actor. JPMorgan and Mastercard cited third-party provider compromises, so linkage is not ruled out.

Trump is weighing renewed strikes on Iran but says he is "not rushing" and may wait until after 1 January. Israel is pressing for action while Gulf leaders urge economic pressure instead.

The Senate rejected an Iran war powers resolution 48 to 51 on Thursday. It is the latest failed attempt to force the president to end the war or seek authorisation.

2. White House and the administration.

National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving and Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies is named his successor. Axios says Stephen Miller pushed the change; one source close to the White House says Cairncross was fired, others call it amicable.

The administration wants Davies confirmed before Democrats take the Senate in January. After the midterm losses, she would need a confirmation vote in a lame-duck window that is closing.

Trump has told Axios and Israel's Channel 12 he is "not rushing into any decision" on Iran, and has tasked the Pentagon and intelligence agencies with assessing Iranian responses to CENTCOM strike options. Planning is advanced even if no decision is made.

Trump downplayed an Nvidia spying controversy and warned that heavy AI regulation would stifle development (unconfirmed, newsletter digest). It signals the administration's light-touch AI stance as it overhauls its AI team.

Departures are mounting after the midterm defeat. A running list tracks dismissals and resignations in the second administration.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The Senate voted 48 to 51 to reject H.Con.Res.93, the House-passed Iran war powers resolution. One senator did not vote; the resolution would have directed an end to hostilities.

The Senate resumed post-cloture consideration of H.R. 2347, the permitting bill, on Thursday. The bill's title is labelled inconsistently across Senate records.

A 11 December funding deadline looms and analysts expect another short-term extension. Some Senate Democrats want to tie any stopgap to ending Iran war funding while Republicans float reconciliation for defence money.

The NDAA could miss its year-end deadline for the first time in decades. Chip export controls offer a possible bipartisan path.

FBI Director Kash Patel skipped the House Homeland Security worldwide threats hearing on 18 November after attending a Mar-a-Lago gala with a 50,000 dollarsdonor package. Deputy Director Christopher Raia testified instead and criticism was bipartisan; the FBI says ethics officials approved the event.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The administration filed a third emergency application at the Supreme Court on 18 November to end Venezuelan TPS. Solicitor General Sauer is challenging a ruling by Judge Edward Chen, and the Ninth Circuit then extended its stay on protections.

One immigration site says the government withdrew that application on 19 November (unconfirmed). I found no wire or court confirmation.

The December argument session begins Monday 30 November. SCOTUSblog also covers how the Court handles congressional investigations.

A federal judge in Colorado briefly held the government in contempt over ICE re-arresting a freed migrant, then lifted it once the practice stopped. DHS defends re-arrest after judicial release.

Alien Enemies Act litigation continues, with a Fifth Circuit injunction from 2 September and a reported Supreme Court order allowing resumed use (unconfirmed, conflicting dates). Check the docket before relying on current status.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Republicans lost both chambers in the November midterms and Democrats take the Senate with 55 seats in January. Democrats also flipped six governorships.

Trump's approval is 32% in the latest Reuters/Ipsos poll (13 to 16 November, n=1,521). Averages run from 34.5% to 38% depending on the aggregator.

Emerson's 2028 poll puts Marco Rubio at 36% and JD Vance at 20% for the GOP; Jon Ossoff leads Democrats at 17%, ahead of Ocasio-Cortez at 12%. Echelon's polling has Vance ahead 40 to 26, so the order depends on the pollster.

Emerson finds Democratic favourability up nine points and Republican favourability down nine over the past year.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

US intelligence reportedly shows Iran drafting options for a dirty-bomb or chemical attack on senior officials in Washington (CNN, seven officials). Officials stress these are options, not an imminent plot; the White House sees no credible homeland threat and Tehran calls it fabricated. Experts doubt the capability.

Reuters/Ipsos finds only 31% of Americans approve of the Iran war. Public opinion thus runs against escalation even as Israel lobbies for new strikes.

The IDF launched a rare operation in central Gaza west of the "Yellow Line" to dismantle a tunnel, after evacuation orders. Ministers called for full implementation of Trump's Gaza plan.

Iran's Foreign Ministry called chemical weapons allegations "fraudulent" on 19 November. Iran also denied the dirty-bomb plot while Gulf states report continued drone attacks (Breitbart framing).

Witkoff and Kushner told Russia and Ukraine that the current round may be the last real chance for a deal before Washington's politics shift. The Abu Dhabi talks produced only a prisoner exchange, and Putin has not dropped his Donbas demand.

Rubio is heading to a G7 gathering in Paris to set priorities for the administration's final years.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Caracas on 18 November, met interim leader Delcy Rodriguez and signed renewed counter-narcotics military cooperation. It is the highest-level US visit since Maduro's capture in January, and the Pentagon did not acknowledge it at first.

He then handed Colombia a C-130 transport aircraft in Bogota. It deepens the defence partnership in Latin America.

Hegseth survived an impeachment push earlier this month. The AP reported the House turned aside the resolution on 10 November, while another outlet reports a 211 to 204 referral to committee; he called it a "spectacle".

Defence funding is in limbo. Inside Defense reports Hegseth is elevating a special operations office with more budget influence, alongside a Senate GOP defence bill.

Critics cite the legality of anti-drug boat strikes and a ninth failed audit (Real News framing, left-leaning).

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Patel's skipped hearing was meant to cover outages that hit the NSA. Members of both parties said the absence undermined oversight.

The top US intelligence official said on Wednesday the outages were not coordinated or state-sponsored. Patel said the FBI stays on alert for cyber threats.

Intelligence on a possible Iranian dirty-bomb or chemical option has prompted protective preparations for diplomatic and intelligence staff in the region. Officials are divided on capability and intent.

Open source (unverified): Bellingcat found at least 46 of 54 southern Lebanese towns damaged or flattened, and earlier showed public maps expose details of US bases Iran targeted.

Open source (unverified): ISW's 15 November assessment cites geolocated footage of a Ukrainian strike near Mala Vovcha. A newsletter also claims satellite imagery of a PLAN carrier group east of Luzon (unconfirmed).

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Fed officials worry that inflation driven by tariffs and the Iran war is more persistent than expected. October CPI beat expectations, with headline up 0.4% and core up 0.3%.

RBC expects Fed rate hikes in December and March. Roughly half of CPI items run above 3% year on year and freight costs are spilling into core goods.

The labour market is holding with unemployment at 4.1% and October payrolls above expectations. That strengthens the case for tightening.

The US-China trade truce expires in January and the Shenzhen bilateral made little progress. Trump previewed farm purchases and says many deals are coming; the Philippines and Thailand are advancing.

Anthropic's record-breaking IPO is lifting Nasdaq futures.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin and ICE publicised "worst of the worst" arrests and attacked sanctuary policies in California and Massachusetts. These are agency characterisations, not independently verified.

Acting ICE Director David Venturella confirmed ICE will not use ships for deportations, only air and land. This ends months of controversy over a training vessel and ferries.

A judge accused ICE of making "a mockery" of her court by re-arresting a freed migrant.

Mullin and another official faced bipartisan grilling over the tech outages.

11. States and governors.

Democrats will hold 30 governorships and 22 trifectas in 2027, against 20 and 18 for Republicans. All 36 governor races are called.

Maryland voters approved Question 3 on 3 November with about 60%, loosening limits on redrawing House districts.

New York has advanced a mid-decade redistricting amendment that needs a second legislative vote and a referendum; Mississippi's Governor Reeves has signalled interest in redrawing.

Minnesota and Georgia legislative canvasses and recounts were still pending.

12. Media and public opinion.

CNN's 12 November poll had Trump approval at 34% and disapproval at 64%. The Economist tracker showed 32/62.

Social media fed outage panic as TikTok users urged stocking supplies for a blackout.

TechCrunch compares the outages to "Y2K, AI agent style".

Right-leaning outlets frame the Shenzhen trip as historic; Democrats call it an embarrassment.
