Here is your US briefing for Monday, 23 November.

Trump eyes renewed Iran strikes as Hormuz blockade standoff builds; Senate blocks war-powers bid; cyber chief fired after outages.

Compiled from sources dated 19 to 23 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump said on Fox News (22 November) that he is leaning toward resuming major strikes on Iran after the new year if no deal is reached. He called Iranian negotiators "professional tap dancers" but said indirect talks continue; the remarks set a rough deadline for a war that has now cost an estimated 48.3 dollarsbillion.

Iran reportedly gathered more than 14 oil tankers near Kharg Island, and Trump said any vessel trying to break the US blockade would end up "at the bottom of the sea." No order to sail has been confirmed, but the standoff over Hormuz is the most likely flashpoint this week.

The Senate rejected the House-passed Iran war-powers resolution 48 to 51 on 19 November, the first war vote since the midterms. Collins, Murkowski and Paul voted yes and Fetterman voted no; it was Democrats' 15th attempt, and the focus now shifts to the 11 December funding deadline.

Trump returned from Asia having met Xi and Putin in Shenzhen, with few concrete results. The main announced items were continued talks on nuclear risk reduction and "joint guardrails" on AI; Zelenskyy, excluded from the meeting, called that a "huge mistake."

Trump replaced National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross with Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies (acting) after a week of outages hitting flights, banks and the NSA. Officials have not linked the incidents or named a cause, so the dismissal looks as much political as technical.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump held a long White House press conference on 20 November, defending his relationship with Putin and accusing Iran of bad-faith negotiating. He also said he would stop US companies from building data centres abroad, a surprise industrial-policy signal.

Chief of staff Stephen Miller had long wanted Cairncross out, according to reporting; Trump said publicly he was not "good enough." The firing shows how outage-driven blame is landing on individual officials rather than agencies.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told lawmakers investigators had "seen nothing" indicating the outages are related. That caveat matters because opposing claims of a coordinated attack are circulating on partisan sites.

Trump is pressing ahead with White House renovations despite the midterm loss. The Washington Post framed it as a sign of an administration unchanged by the result.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

Congress left for Thanksgiving with funding expiring 11 December and no deal. Appropriators expect a short-term extension; the House has passed only three of 12 spending bills and the Senate none.

Senate Democrats are divided on using the funding fight to challenge Iran war spending. Neither party appears ready for its most confrontational approach, which makes a clean stopgap the base case.

The Senate confirmed two appeals court judges, including Daniel Desmond Domenico, and passed a bipartisan permitting bill. The lame-duck session is moving judges while the Republican majority lasts in the House's final weeks of control.

House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker 251 to 11 on 18 November, with Katherine Clark as majority leader and Pete Aguilar as whip. The full House votes when the new Congress convenes 3 January 2027; Democrats hold roughly 257 to 258 seats.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court agreed on 13 November to hear the administration's suspension of asylum at the southern border. Argument is set for the 2026 to 27 term, putting a core immigration power before the justices.

Judge Leonie Brinkema indefinitely halted construction of Trump's "triumphal arch" in Washington, and the administration filed an appeal this weekend. Judge Chutkan separately extended a restraining order to 3 December.

The administration withdrew its Supreme Court request on Venezuelan TPS after an appeals court extended its stay. The Solicitor General said it will seek relief again if it loses below.

A New York judge declined a long-term ban on taxpayer-funded administration ads, citing no ongoing harm after the midterms. A DNC case remains pending.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval sits around 34 to 38% depending on the aggregator. CNN's poll of polls shows 34% approve and 65% disapprove (19 November); other averages put it near 36 to 38%.

CNN's voter poll found Trump had the worst net approval of any president in a midterm in the exit-poll era. Harry Enten framed it as a record low at the ballot box.

House Republicans picked Jim Jordan over Steve Scalise 33 votes to lead the conference after Speaker Mike Johnson declined to seek minority leader. The shift moves the GOP caucus toward its hard-right wing.

Senate leaders are unchanged: Thune re-elected Republican leader; Schumer says he is confident of becoming majority leader. Axios noted a Democratic leadership revolt has yet to produce a challenger.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Iran's deputy foreign minister called the US blockade an "act of war" on 21 November while saying Tehran still wants talks via Qatar. Iran's demands include ending the war on all fronts, unfreezing assets and lifting oil sanctions.

CBS reported Israel and the US are weighing an attack on Iran in the near future (unconfirmed, single-source via Times of Israel). An IDF intelligence warning reportedly told Netanyahu a unilateral strike could trigger a multi-front war, while Avigdor Liberman called a strike politically motivated.

Israel ran a rare limited operation in central Gaza on 18 November to destroy a tunnel, and the Board of Peace warned it may hand Gaza's administration to the UN. That threat signals strain over Israeli compliance with the ceasefire framework.

Zelenskyy said the US proposed three-way talks with Russia before Christmas, possibly alongside the G20 in Florida (unconfirmed, single outlet). Earlier rounds in Abu Dhabi produced prisoner-swap talk but no breakthrough on territory or guarantees.

Treasury continues sanctioning Iranian "trustees" who collect oil revenue. Economic pressure runs alongside the naval blockade.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

The Washington Post reported Hegseth issued a classified memo restricting military cooperation with Canada. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said NORAD, NATO and defence-intelligence ties are unaffected; Canada's Defence Minister David McGuinty said he had not seen the document and it is business as usual.

A bipartisan group of US senators at the Halifax security forum said they had not seen the memo. The gap between report and denial leaves the scope unclear.

The US military disputed Iranian claims of successful strikes, saying it intercepted incoming drones; Bahrain also intercepted Iranian attacks. Active exchanges continue despite talk of negotiation.

Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies now doubles as acting national cyber director. It concentrates cyber leadership in the defence establishment.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

NSANet, the NSA's classified internal network, suffered an outage that reportedly was resolved with staff returning by 20 November. Start date and duration vary by report (12, 14 or 17 November), and no cause is confirmed; this is the main publicly reported intelligence-community incident.

Mullin told Congress investigators found nothing linking the outages. Treat claims of a cyberattack as unproven.

Open source, unverified: tracking sites and satellite imagery show more than 14 tankers around Kharg Island, and TankerTrackers says a reported Iranian tanker transit of Hormuz does not appear in imagery. Treat the convoy as a signal rather than a confirmed movement.

Open source: ISW/Critical Threats' 22 November Iran Update lists Iranian war aims including recognition of control over Hormuz and splitting the US and Israel from Arab states. The detailed text was not retrievable, so check the original.

Bellingcat published on how public mapping platforms expose US bases targeted by Iran (5 November). Older than the 72-hour window; cited for context.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Fed October minutes showed rates held at 3.75 to 4.00% on a 10 to 2 vote, with a couple of participants open to a larger-than-25bp December hike. A Reuters poll had all 101 economists expecting one more 25bp hike in December.

J.P. Morgan now expects further hikes in January and March. It argues the labour market is not driving inflation, a notable shift for a Fed tightening cycle.

Fed officials worry inflation driven by tariffs and the Iran war is more persistent than expected. An opinion piece argues tariffs had only a one-time effect; treat it as advocacy.

The Shenzhen summit produced no announced tariff cuts; Trump said trade agreements would be signed "soon." Rare-earth flows beyond a January deadline remain unresolved.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS said ICE carried out a record 483,200 deportations and 491,000+ arrests in fiscal 2026. CBS found more than half of those detained in one month had no conviction or pending charge, and Tom Homan said "elections don't change laws."

ProPublica's tracker counts 97,050 immigration habeas cases since January 2025 (as of 17 November). The scale shows the pressure on federal courts.

The Supreme Court asylum case and Venezuelan TPS fight are the live legal fronts. See section 4.

11. States and governors.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: the DOJ suit against California's Proposition 50 map and post-Callais redraws in Louisiana, Florida, Missouri and Tennessee remain in litigation, with no new rulings found.

12. Media and public opinion.

Outlets split on the Asia trip: NPR and NBC stressed thin results and called Putin an early winner; Trump and allies called it a success. A multi-outlet comparison showed 18 outlets framing it differently.

Pro-Trump outlets such as Breitbart played up the blockade threat; Israeli outlets stressed strike timing and the outage story ran on fringe sites with coordinated-attack framing. Check original reporting.

Fox's last poll (9 to 12 October) had Trump at 36% approve, 64% disapprove.
