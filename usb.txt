Here is your US briefing for Friday, 27 November.

ABC wins injunction against FCC, new tariffs on 14 partners, Iran closes Hormuz further.

Compiled from sources dated 24 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

A federal judge blocked the FCC's early licence review of eight ABC-owned stations on 25 November. Judge Loren AliKhan found Disney likely to win its First Amendment claim and wrote that FCC Chair Brendan Carr treated Trump's rhetoric as "marching orders"; the FCC calls the ruling "wrong on the facts and wrong on the law" and is appealing, a significant test of presidential pressure on broadcasters.

The administration proposed new Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the EU, Japan and India. China would face 20% on top of existing duties and the EU and Japan up to 15%, but Reuters-sourced reporting says the rates are unlikely to take effect before February or March.

Iran has ordered the Strait of Hormuz closed "further day by day" while Qatar tries to revive talks. The US blockade of Iranian ports remains in force, so the war that began on 28 February has no confirmed deal in sight.

A district judge struck down the administration's narrower birthright-citizenship restrictions. Judge Christopher Cooper held that affected children remain under US jurisdiction; the White House called it "just another ruling from an activist judge."

Funding expires on 11 December and Congress returns from the Thanksgiving break to a crowded lame duck. Democrats won both chambers on 3 November, so Republicans have a short window for any priorities.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump proclaimed Thursday 26 November a National Day of Thanksgiving, framed around the 250th anniversary of independence. The proclamation fits the administration's heavy semiquincentennial branding.

Trump awarded Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom, per the White House events page. Vance visited troops at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base on 25 November.

Trump said he pressed Putin to pull back military activity in Europe and negotiate a Ukraine deal. The remarks come as US envoys push security-guarantee talks.

Reports say Trump backs Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO and claims "victory at hand" in Iran (unconfirmed). This rests on a single secondary outlet, and the Iran claim sits uneasily with the closure of Hormuz.

A Wikipedia appointments table lists November staffing changes, including Stephen Miller as chief of staff in place of Susie Wiles (unconfirmed). I found no news report confirming this, so treat it with caution.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The Senate is out until 30 November, and the lame duck faces the 11 December funding cliff, the SAVE America Act and a bipartisan tax package. Reporting describes poor House GOP attendance since the election and doubts that big ambitions are achievable.

The Senate passed a bill called BAAJA 62 to 33 on 19 November, with House action expected under a rule (unconfirmed). The source is a single law-firm blog, and the final coalition was 47 Republicans, 14 Democrats and Angus King.

Leadership fights are brewing: Thune is seeking to remain Senate GOP leader while Ron Johnson, Mike Lee and Rick Scott pressed to delay the vote. On the Democratic side, Jeffries is the expected Speaker candidate and Van Hollen is challenging Schumer.

Rep. Thomas Massie filed impeachment articles against Hegseth over the Iran war, and Democrats plan war-powers action on the first day of the next Congress on 3 January. Rep. Gregory Meeks says he will introduce a joint resolution to end the war.

Congressional intelligence committees complained that a classified memo on possible Chinese surveillance of Alice Huang was withheld from a 25 November staff briefing. See the intelligence section.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

Judge AliKhan's preliminary injunction against the FCC also denied the agency's motion to dismiss. It does not bar future licence reviews, and the administration is appealing to the DC Circuit.

Judge Cooper's birthright ruling follows a Supreme Court loss for the administration, after which it tried narrower restrictions. A separate order protects the certified class in ACLU's Nathan v. Trump.

The Supreme Court granted the government's stay application in the Big Bend border-wall case on 9 November, with Justice Jackson dissenting. The case is Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church. I found no Court action dated 26 November.

An emergency-docket tracker shows the Court granting 9 and denying 82 of 91 applications it ruled on this term, with 14 pending. Trackers disagree on how to classify some matters, such as the 2 November California mail-ballot dispute.

A federal court order treating Abrego Garcia's exit to Spain as a deportation was reported on 25 November, via a partisan outlet (unconfirmed). DHS had called it "self-deportation."

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval averages 36 to 38% in late November, with a net rating around -22. The aggregates are from RealClearPolitics-style averages and PollWave; individual polls vary.

An American Research Group poll put Trump at 29%, the lowest of his second term, with 25% approving his handling of the economy (unconfirmed). Single-poll outliers should be weighed against the averages.

Gallup reportedly stopped publishing presidential approval ratings (unconfirmed). If accurate, it removes the longest-running benchmark series.

Democrats won both chambers on 3 November, with NBC projecting a 56 to 44 Senate split (election-night projection, not certified).

House Democratic committee-leadership races are set for 9 December.

6. Foreign policy (Middle East focus)

Iran's foreign minister said reopening Hormuz is "not difficult" if the US accepts Tehran's conditions, while its army chief ordered the strait closed further. Araghchi called the US blockade "completely illegal," and Qatar's prime minister urged Rubio and Vance to restart talks.

The IRGC claimed three tankers under US escort hit mines, which maritime monitors called unconfirmed. Escalation risk at sea is the main variable for any renewed US strikes.

The Gaza ceasefire from October 2025 persists but Hamas has not disarmed. The Congressional Research Service flags open questions about the next stage of the US 20-point plan.

Israeli politics: Yashar and UTJ reportedly agreed key principles of a Haredi draft law on 26 November. The deal bears on coalition stability in wartime.

ISW says US negotiators detailed Ukraine security guarantees but that the proposed approval process could block them, and that Washington is still holding up Western-funded Patriot interceptors. Reuters-type wire coverage of the Abu Dhabi talks produced only a prisoner-swap agreement.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

The Pentagon released a blunt fact sheet criticising Canada over defence spending and its F-35 review, after a classified Hegseth memo restricting cooperation. Ottawa says it has not seen the memo.

The Washington Post reported that Hegseth and others discussed using the Defense Production Act to pressure Nvidia's board, which the Pentagon denies. The denial leaves it a contested single-outlet report.

Inside Defense headlines put the Iran war's cost at 48.3 dollarsbillion and cite persistent munitions shortages. The White House also wants broad exceptions in the FY27 continuing resolution.

Former Pentagon officials say Hegseth is blocking senators from visiting Gulf bases, calling it a lack of transparency. Oversight friction is likely to sharpen once Democrats take the committees.

Hegseth has vowed to defend Pentagon policies as Democrats take Congress. He has also called for a 1.5 dollarstrillion defence budget.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

US agencies told congressional staff on 25 November that China's MSS may have surveilled Alice Huang, partner of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. CBS says no evidence shows she was directed to collect or passed substantial intelligence; both intelligence committees complained the classified memo was withheld.

A secondary report citing the New York Times says officials told Congress that Huang passed information to the MSS (unconfirmed). The accounts conflict, so her role is unsettled.

Two criminal export-control probes involving Megaspeed and Super Micro remain open. CBS also reported that two California buyers of Nvidia's top chips are Chinese-owned.

CBS's report refers to "then-Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard," which suggests a leadership change (unconfirmed). I could not find the successor or the circumstances.

Open-source, unverified: Bellingcat tracked the tanker Krasnodar at Sevastopol's Avlita terminal on 26 November with AIS switched off, and found EU Sentinel-1 imagery over Gaza and parts of the region is delayed 24 hours. ISW separately reports 112 Russian strike drones launched overnight 24 to 25 November.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge rose 3.8% year on year in October, and futures price a 98% chance of a 25bp hike in December. The tariff proposals add to the expectation of higher-for-longer rates.

Third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualised from 3.4%, the fastest in three years. Strong growth with hot inflation is what is driving the rate-hike pricing.

Stocks recovered as markets judged the tariff impact limited. A Capital Economics economist called the extra burden small but a marginal boost to prices.

The government is funded only through 11 December under the FY27 continuing resolution (P.L. 119 to 103). As of late November no new funding bill or extension had been enacted.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE made 483,200 removals and over 491,000 arrests in fiscal 2026, both records. This is still short of Trump's one-million-a-year goal, and the figures come from the agency itself.

The acting ICE director confirmed that removals will be by air and land only, ruling out deportation ships.

ICE arrested a Salvadoran man in Boston days after he won 1 dollarsmillion in the state lottery. It is a case likely to feed the debate over enforcement priorities.

Judge Cooper's birthright ruling is the main legal constraint on the administration's citizenship agenda.

11. States and governors.

D.C. Council member Brooke Pinto urged five newly elected Democratic governors (Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, Texas) to withdraw their 628 National Guard troops from Washington. The Pentagon has authorised the mission through January 2029, and incoming governors can recall troops but the administration may seek workarounds.

Georgia's governor-elect has criticised the mission without announcing a recall, and Iowa's wants to consult military leaders first.

Maryland voters approved Question 3 on 3 November with about 60%, and a 2027 to 28 redistricting overview lists Maryland and New York among states with new maps planned. I found no fresh redistricting action dated within 72 hours.

12. Media and public opinion.

The ABC ruling is the week's main press-freedom story, with ABC saying the review had a chilling effect. Coverage ranges from NPR and Time describing a "not a close case" defeat to conservative outlets such as RedState framing it as a Disney win from an activist bench.

NPR separately reported a Justice Department antitrust probe into the five networks in the White House video pool, including ABC and CNN. It is a second front in the administration's dealings with the press, though the report is not dated precisely.

A Newsweek-cited tracker shows Trump's net approval about -22 three weeks after the midterms, and only a quarter of respondents in one poll approve of his economic handling. Inflation at 3.8% is the likely driver.
