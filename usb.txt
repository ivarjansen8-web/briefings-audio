Here is your US briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Democrats sweep Congress as Miller replaces Wiles and Hegseth impeachment looms.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Democrats won both chambers in the 3 November midterms, with the AP count at roughly 254 House seats and 54 Senate seats and the national House vote about 12.8 points Democratic. Several races remain uncalled, so final margins may move; the result ends unified Republican control from January.

Trump replaced chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller, effective Monday 9 November. Axios's sources call the exit mutual but driven by the election result, and the Washington Post expects a further rightward tilt in policy.

Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said he is a "hard yes" on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Rep. Thomas Massie plans to force a House vote this week. Passage needs several Republicans; CNN reports GOP sources expect it could pass.

The Iran war is the central driver of the political fallout, with Vance blaming it for the losses and Trump calling that "largely true" while saying he would do it again. Strikes, mines and stalled talks continue in the Gulf.

A Venezuelan national was arrested near the White House on 5 November with several firearms and, per DHS, said he planned to kill Trump. The account rests on DHS and a criminal complaint; no one was hurt.

2. White House and the administration.

Miller takes over as chief of staff on 9 November, and Deputy Chief of Staff Richard Walters, a Wiles ally, is leaving. Axios calls Walters the first major departure of an expected shake-up.

Trump says he offered the press secretary job to UFC CEO Dana White, who says he will meet Trump on Monday. CNN earlier reported White had declined, so the outcome is open.

Trump signalled more Cabinet departures, then said he expects "very little" turnover. The mixed messages leave Hegseth's and others' futures uncertain.

Trump has spoken publicly twice since the midterms and both times dwelt on White House renovations and decor. Critics read this as avoidance of the election result.

Trump told ABC he has "every intention" of keeping Hegseth, and has said a replacement would be easier to confirm before Democrats take the Senate. That second remark is the reason Pentagon-watchers treat his support as conditional.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

A Hegseth impeachment vote is expected this week. Massie's resolution is described as "much gentler" than the Democratic version; his September attempt failed after Speaker Johnson called an early recess.

Speaker Mike Johnson has reportedly told staff he will not stay on as Republican leader, and Jeffries has formally launched his bid for Speaker. The Johnson report comes from RedState and is not confirmed on the record (unconfirmed).

Senate Republicans hold leadership elections on 10 November, and Thune is reported to be seeking the minority leader post. Schumer is positioned to return as majority leader in January.

The lame-duck session faces a 11 December funding deadline, a possible fourth shutdown of Trump's second term, plus the NDAA and farm bill. Reporting expects another stopgap, not a full-year deal; Thune has floated a debt-limit increase before Democrats take over.

Democrats plan a first-100-hours vote to curb tariffs, starting with Canada, but lack the votes to beat a veto. Expect oversight of Hegseth, ICE and the Iran war to dominate the new Congress. MLQ, recent (unconfirmed)

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The DOJ has reportedly opened a criminal investigation into the 2022 Dobbs draft leak, overseen by D.C. U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro. The NYT reportedly says grand jury subpoenas went out; DOJ declines to comment, and the story is mostly relayed by conservative outlets.

The Court granted review in Goldman Sachs Bank USA v. Brown on whether a bankruptcy court can refuse to enforce an arbitration agreement. It is a routine cert grant; more orders are expected Monday after Friday's conference.

Judge Ana Reyes (D.D.C.) is weighing an emergency motion alleging the administration is breaching her notice order by planning to deport 11 Iranians via Pakistan on the night of 8 November. She has threatened contempt, and DOJ wants the order dissolved; whether the flight left is unconfirmed.

The Court reportedly rejected the administration's emergency bid to make California segregate ballots from about 20,000 registered voters. The date is given as 2 November by one roundup (unconfirmed).

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval was 38% in voter polling, which CNN calls the worst midterm net rating for a president in the voter-poll era. Aggregators show about 38 to 39% approve, 59% disapprove; a Fox News poll in October had 36%.

Alaska's Senate race is unresolved: Mary Peltola led Dan Sullivan by 637 votes on Wednesday with about a quarter of ballots outstanding. Both campaigns claim victory; key counts are due 13 and 18 November, and ranked-choice tabulation may follow.

Mississippi's Senate race is also uncalled, with counties having until 13 November to finish counting.

Vance's post-mortem blamed the Iran war, cost of living and Israel ties, setting up a 2028 contrast with Trump. Fox frames it as a 2028 showdown; MS NOW calls it inconsistent given his earlier defence of the war.

Democrats face base pressure against Schumer and Jeffries, though RedState expects both to have the votes. The source is partisan; no senator has publicly launched a challenge.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

The US and Israel's war with Iran, begun on 28 February, is in a coercive stalemate: oil fell about 12% on the week on hopes of a deal, while ships keep being hit in and near Hormuz. Iranian state media claimed seven tankers were damaged overnight (AP could not confirm), and Oman is talking to Iran about joint control of Hormuz traffic.

Iran called the latest US proposal to end the war "insufficient," and Araghchi says Tehran's plan to reopen Hormuz remains on the table. Indirect talks run via Qatar, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia.

Trump said the US carried out "minor strikes" on Iran as missiles hit Qeshm Island, and called a return to full-scale war "highly unlikely." Officials said the strikes were not a resumption of major combat, and dates differ across outlets.

Times of Israel reports the military told Trump it favours conditions on 9 November for a new attack, even as Iranian negotiators review nuclear-curb proposals via Qatar. This is a single Israeli-outlet report (unconfirmed).

Trump said he met Putin's envoy Kirill Dmitriev and predicted a Ukraine deal that could cover "all of Europe," with trilateral talks set for 10 to 11 November in Abu Dhabi. Trump has predicted deals before; European capitals worry about troop-withdrawal hints.

On Gaza, the October 2025 ceasefire framework persists but Hamas has not disarmed, and Iran ties any deal to Lebanon and Gaza. Little fresh reporting surfaced in the window.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth defiantly vowed to keep fighting from the Pentagon and defended the Iran war, while spokesman Sean Parnell said he is "100 percent committed" through the end of Trump's term. Democrats promise sweeping Pentagon oversight from January.

CENTCOM created Task Force Iron Gate, about 20 civilian and military staff based in Florida, to monitor Iran's land borders and Caspian access and disrupt ground supply routes. Iran's military dismissed it as "wordplay."

US forces remain off Iran enforcing a naval blockade, and the IRGC warns it could hit US bases if Washington escorts tankers elsewhere in the strait. The risk to Gulf-based troops stays elevated.

A tabloid-leaning outlet reports an unnamed Pentagon source saying Hegseth is "looking for a way out," which the Pentagon denies on the record. Treat as (unconfirmed).

The Hill reports Hegseth's future is uncertain amid backlash over the war.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Quiet on leadership: I found no new CIA, FBI, NSA or ODNI personnel changes in the window. The last substantive public assessment found was Gabbard's spring testimony that Iran's regime is "intact but largely degraded," and her stated view that US and Israeli objectives differ.

The Secret Service and DHS arrest near the White House is the main security-service story, with Director Sean Curran citing rising danger from armed individuals. The suspect is alleged to have entered illegally in 2024.

Open-source: ISW's 6 November update cites BBC Verify satellite imagery showing Iranian excavation at at least two missile sites, Garmdareh and Shahroud, as part of rebuilding. It is open-source analysis and unverified by US officials.

Open-source: Bellingcat showed how public online maps expose US bases in the Middle East that Iran has targeted. It raises force-protection and information-security questions.

The reported DOJ Dobbs-leak probe could reach court staff records and raises separation-of-powers questions. The Court's own inquiry never named a leaker.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

October payrolls rose 165,000 against forecasts of roughly 59,000 to 65,000, and unemployment fell to 4.1%. Prior months were revised up by 84,000.

Markets price about a 78% chance of a quarter-point Fed rate hike on 9 December, and Mary Daly says there is room to keep fighting inflation. A hike would be a rare move in the opposite direction from the cuts Trump has demanded.

Crude fell to about 91 dollarsa barrel on 6 November, down roughly 12% on the week on hopes of an end to the Iran war. Prices rebounded slightly as ship attacks continued.

The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs 6 to 3 on 20 February, and about 126 dollarsbillion including interest had been refunded by 2 October. Section 232 and 301 tariffs remain, and the Section 122 surcharge expired on 24 July.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said "deportations will continue" after the Democratic sweep. ICE is reported at about 1,500 arrests a day; oversight will begin once committees are organised in January.

The planned removal of 11 Iranians to Iran via Pakistan is the live legal flashpoint, with a contempt threat from Judge Reyes. It tests whether notice orders can bind removals during wartime.

A federal judge in New York restricted deportation flights involving Afghan nationals through 7 November, while two Afghans were detained in Sacramento despite the order. Davis Vanguard, November (unconfirmed)

DHS's framing of the White House arrest stresses the suspect's illegal entry in 2024. The affidavit itself did not include the alleged remarks about killing Trump.

11. States and governors.

Democrats flipped six governorships, per MultiState: Alaska, Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio and Texas. NBC projects Keisha Lance Bottoms won Georgia with 54.3% and Gina Hinojosa won Texas, with the tally still shifting.

Democrats took full control of government in Arizona, Michigan, Minnesota, Pennsylvania, Nevada and Wisconsin. Republican mid-decade redistricting in Texas and Florida did not stave off House losses.

Kansas's governor race is extremely close, with Democrat Cindy Holscher ahead by about 7,000 votes.

Xavier Becerra leads California's governor race with 75% counted.

12. Media and public opinion.

Coverage of the White House is split between the shake-up and the optics of a president talking about decor. NBC and NPR stress the vacancy in the press secretary role.

Partisan framing diverges on the Dobbs probe and the Vance comments. Conservative outlets lead on the DOJ probe; MS NOW and CBC question Vance.

Pentagon staff reportedly launched a social media campaign posting images of Hegseth and Miller. The report comes via Yahoo/HuffPost (unconfirmed).

Voters' stated priorities in exit polling centred on the war and cost of living.
