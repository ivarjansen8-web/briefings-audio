Here is your US briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

Trump touts Xi-Putin summit as Senate blocks Iran war powers and shutdown clock ticks.

Compiled from sources dated 17 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Trump held a near-two-hour White House press conference on 20 November after a four-day Asia trip that included a Shenzhen meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin. He called it "one of the most successful presidential visits to Asia in American history," but NPR and CBS found few concrete results beyond a joint statement and no breakthroughs on Ukraine, Iran or AI.

The Senate rejected an Iran war powers resolution on 19 November, the first such vote since the midterms. Roll Call calls it the first war powers vote since the elections; reported tallies differ (48 to 51 or 51 to 48) and Fetterman was the lone Democrat opposed.

Congress faces an 11 December funding deadline with Iran war money at the centre. Thune says there are "some good conversations" but "no deals yet," and the dispute turns on a 350 dollarsbillion Pentagon request.

Israel and the US are reportedly weighing renewed strikes on Iran (unconfirmed). CBS reports Israel would prefer another joint operation, while Trump's intentions are unclear.

2. White House and the administration.

Stephen Miller became chief of staff on 9 November, replacing Susie Wiles, who resigned after the midterm losses. Miller was previously deputy chief of staff and a DHS adviser, so immigration hawks now hold the central gatekeeping role.

Trump said at the press conference that he fired a senior White House cyber official because "I didn't think that person was good enough." The Record separately reports National Cyber Director Cairncross is out as the White House resets cyber leadership.

Asked about reports he was unhappy with JD Vance for blaming the Iran war for GOP losses, Trump said "I have not lost confidence." Vance told Axios on 4 November the war cost Republicans the midterms, and Trump partly agreed it "drove the gasoline up a little bit."

Trump keeps pressing White House renovations despite the midterm loss, and signed a proclamation making the Oval Office a national monument. The ballroom and other projects face legal pushback.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The Senate voted down the Iran war powers resolution on 19 November; Democrats are pivoting to funding limits. Three Senate Democrats unveiled a bill barring funds for offensive operations in Iran, to be offered as an amendment to the next spending bill.

Republicans hold 53 Senate seats, so a funding bill needs at least seven Democratic votes to clear the filibuster. Democrats are split on risking a shutdown to constrain the war, and Thune hinted at trading Iran limits for munitions money.

The Senate held votes on 20 November on judicial nominees, including cloture on Courtney Coker for the Northern District of Texas. The floor is processing judges ahead of the handover to a Democratic-controlled Congress in January.

Incoming Homeland Security chair Bennie Thompson says his panel will investigate administration immigration policy. Democrats take control of Congress in January.

Eight Democratic senators demanded answers on reports that Hegseth ordered internet searches on officers up for promotion. MS NOW reported the searches blocked 17 promotions (single-source, unconfirmed).

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court agreed to hear Trump's suspension of asylum access at the southern border (Mullin v. RICELS). The D.C. Circuit rejected the executive's argument but the restrictions stay in effect; argument is expected in spring 2027.

The administration asked the Court to let it cancel about 16 dollarsbillion in Biden-era climate grants. It is a petition for review, not yet decided.

On 19 November the administration withdrew its emergency request over Venezuelan TPS because the Ninth Circuit has kept the ruling on hold, removing an immediate flashpoint.

The Court denied review in dozens of cases on 16 November, including a University of California research-funding challenge. No merits ruling dated 20 November turned up.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Democrats won both chambers in the midterms; The Hill reports 56 Senate seats and at least 257 House seats (figures from search summaries, unconfirmed). Polls underestimated Democrats by more than in 2016 or 2024, per Axios.

Fewer than half of Republican voters called the midterms "free and fair," a UC San Diego poll found, while 66% of all Americans did. It shows lasting distrust on the right despite GOP losses.

The 2028 GOP race is unsettled: Emerson has Rubio at 36% to Vance's 20%, while Echelon and YouGov show Vance ahead. Partisan-leaning outlets frame it differently; The Hill and Daily Beast lean Rubio, others Vance.

Redistricting fights continue after the election, with New York pursuing a constitutional amendment and Wisconsin's incoming Democratic governor open to a redraw.

6. Foreign policy (with a Middle East focus)

Israel and the US are weighing renewed Iran strikes, and Iran refuses nuclear talks unless the US lifts its naval blockade and meets six further conditions. Avigdor Liberman said there is no security justification for striking now and that a decision would be politically motivated.

The US, UK, France and Germany jointly accused Tehran of "stonewalling" at the IAEA board over inspector access to sites bombed last year.

Vance vowed the Iran conflict will not become a "forever war," while Israel's ambassador drew bipartisan criticism for saying US troops would stay until Iran is defeated.

Israeli tanks launched a ground operation in central Gaza despite the US-brokered ceasefire; the army said it ended 19 November. Israel has not yet legally authorised ISF deployment, and the Board of Peace's envoy is due in Russia and Japan.

Trump described the Shenzhen meeting with Xi and Putin as "primarily on Ukraine," yet Russia launched more attacks after Putin left. The Pentagon is also warning Kyiv that deep strikes in Russia are "counterproductive," which European allies find confusing.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Caracas on 18 November and signed a defence cooperation document with Venezuela. Delcy Rodriguez called it a "resumption of the technical-military relationship"; few details are public.

A secret Hegseth memo restricts the US-Canada defence relationship, the Washington Post reported on 20 November. The Pentagon says intelligence sharing, NATO and NORAD cooperation are unaffected; the memo is classified and the claim rests on anonymous officials.

Reports say Hegseth had staff search the internet on officers considered for promotion, blocking 17 (unconfirmed, single outlet). Left-leaning outlets are driving the story.

Pentagon spokesman Joel Valdez called ex-spokesman John Ullyot a "sour puss coward" after Ullyot's book alleged a makeup studio request near the briefing room, which the Pentagon denies.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

The NSA resumed normal operations on 20 November after internal network disruptions. DNI Jay Clayton attributed it to a routine PHP patching issue and said there was no evidence linking it to recent airline, bank and tech outages.

Palantir's Michael LoSapio, nominated to lead CISA, pledged to bolster defences and denied involvement in January 6. The agency has lost roughly a third of its workforce.

Sen. Ron Wyden urged Congress to secure lawmakers' phone, email and video communications from executive-branch access after reporting on hacking threats to senators and staff.

Open-source (unverified): Iraqi forces deployed in Kataib Hezbollah's Jurf al-Sakhar stronghold under a deal to curb the group.

ISW and Bellingcat: no relevant dated 2026 output surfaced in search this run. Check understandingwar.org directly.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

The Fed is holding rates amid persistent inflation, and some analysts see a possible December hike. Officials worry price pressure is spreading beyond tariff-hit sectors and the Iran war.

Job growth surprised to the upside while wage growth lags, and RBC flags freight costs spilling into core goods.

A New York Fed study estimates tariffs added 2.9 points to goods inflation by February 2026, contradicting Trump's claim that tariffs "cause wealth." Right-leaning op-eds argue the effect was one-time.

The Washington Times criticises Fed chair Kevin Warsh for much shorter post-FOMC statements.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE set records in FY2026 with more than 491,000 arrests and 483,000 deportations, though it is short of Trump's one-million-a-year goal.

Acting ICE Director David Venturella told Congress DHS will not use ships for removals.

The National Pulse reports over 41 dollarsbillion in enforcement contracts, including 1.28 dollarsbillion for CSI Aviation charter flights (advocacy-leaning outlet, unconfirmed).

The Supreme Court asylum case plus the incoming Democratic oversight agenda set up a clash over enforcement in 2027.

11. States and governors.

Governor Hochul says she will pursue New York's redistricting amendment "long term"; it must pass the next legislature and then go to voters.

Wisconsin Governor-elect David Crowley signalled he would sign a congressional redraw, and Mississippi's Tate Reeves is eyeing the state's only Democratic seat.

Quiet: no other significant state developments surfaced for 20 to 21 November.

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump did not formally take questions in China, so the 20 November press conference was his first extended Q&A on the trip. Collins's question on Vance came there.

Pentagon press access remains contested, with a Washington Post reporter publicly tracking Hegseth's Venezuela trip despite a Pentagon blackout.

Public trust in the midterms splits sharply by party, with 66% of Americans calling them fair.
