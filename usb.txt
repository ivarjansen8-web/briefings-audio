Here is your US briefing for Friday, 13 November.

Democrats' midterm sweep, Miller takes over as chief of staff, and Fed weighs bigger hikes as Iran standoff drags on.

Compiled from sources dated 10 to 13 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Democrats won the House by a wide margin and flipped the Mississippi Senate seat, leaving Trump at 32% approval. Scott Colom beat Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith roughly 49.2% to 48.7% on uncertified results, and Democrats hold about 255 House seats, with some races still uncalled. The result shapes every fight below, from oversight to tariffs.

Stephen Miller became White House chief of staff on 9 November, replacing Susie Wiles. Trump announced it on 6 November, reportedly angry about the midterm losses. It puts the architect of the immigration crackdown in charge of the building.

Fed officials are openly discussing faster rate hikes as inflation nears 4%. October CPI rose 0.4%, and San Francisco Fed president Mary Daly said on 12 November that more hikes are likely. Tariffs and the Iran war are both feeding prices.

The White House says it will keep its tariffs despite the midterm backlash, and Trump threatened on 12 November to double tariffs on Mexico over migrant caravans. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 62% support repealing his tariffs.

Trump says "we don't need a deal with Iran" while the US naval blockade and a new anti-smuggling task force continue. Tehran is reportedly weighing an exit from the nuclear treaty.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump authorised a third crackdown this year on companies diverting Nvidia chips to China, while standing by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. Axios reports officials believe Lutnick repeatedly stalled export-control action. Bipartisan lawmakers want answers, and Rep. Bill Huizenga wants Lutnick to testify.

Trump called the Nvidia/Jensen Huang saga "a horrible thing." Reports conflict on whether Huang has left the CEO role (unconfirmed).

White House counsel Will Scharf warned Rep. Robert Garcia that "unlawful" subpoenas could trigger criminal investigation. The letter, dated 9 November, became public on 11 November. Critics note the administration pursued its own opponents aggressively; Fox and conservative outlets frame it as a warning against overreach.

Trump ordered the DOJ to investigate Democrats over "threatening letters" to Republicans. Details are thin so far (unconfirmed beyond one report).

An armed man was arrested near the White House for allegedly threatening Trump. Details remain limited (unconfirmed).

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

The House voted 211 to 204 on 10 November to refer Rep. Thomas Massie's Hegseth impeachment articles to Judiciary, effectively killing them. Eighteen members were absent, including 11 Democrats, and several said they were denied a chance to vote. Democrats promise to revive it in the new Congress.

The House passed the Major Richard Star Act (H.R. 2102) on 12 November. It covers concurrent receipt of disability pay for veterans with combat-related disabilities; the reported tally differs by source (392 to 23 per C-SPAN, 392 to 18 per Congress.gov).

The Senate spent 12 November on judicial nominations, including Gregory Cook for the Northern District of Alabama. H.Con.Res.93 is due by 20 November.

Garcia, likely next Oversight chair, told Stephen Miller to preserve documents. Democrats vow investigations of the White House once they take control in January.

Rep. Huizenga and Sen. Elizabeth Warren separately pressed Lutnick over chip export controls. It is a rare bipartisan oversight thread.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court let the administration resume border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend sector, over Justice Jackson's dissent. The order came around 9 to 10 November. Conservative outlets cast it as a major border win.

Trump v. Thakur (No. 26 to 210) is pending at the petition stage. The government argues the Tucker Act bars district-court jurisdiction over grant-termination claims; the SG filed a reply on 27 October.

SCOTUSblog is tracking which Trump cases may return to the Court, including a ballroom dispute. No opinion was issued on 12 November.

Background: the Court struck down Trump's IEEPA tariffs in February. That limits how he can implement the new Mexico threat.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Trump's approval is 32% in Reuters/Ipsos (6 to 9 November), near the lowest of his career. Only 21% approve of his handling of prices versus 38% on immigration.

Young voters swung hard: Economist/YouGov has 30% of 18 to 29s approving versus 66% disapproving. Exit polls showed Democrats carrying under-30s by nearly 50 points.

Alaska's Senate race is close: Democrat Mary Peltola leads Sen. Dan Sullivan by about 4,000 votes with about 86% counted. A ranked-choice tabulation is set for 18 November if needed.

A Democrat won the Kansas governor's race. The Hill live blog carries the call.

Mississippi Republicans may challenge Colom's win. The state has no automatic recount (single-source analysis).

6. Foreign policy (Middle East focus)

Trump said "we don't need a deal with Iran," and Iran threatened US bases from the Gulf to Europe if attacked. The war began on 28 February with US-Israeli strikes; the April ceasefire collapsed in July. Turkey is urging a return to talks.

The US launched the "Iron Gate" task force on 7 November to restrict Iranian weapons flows by land and across the Caspian. Russia called it a "clear threat" and Iran and Russia vowed a joint response.

Trump announced on 3 November that roughly 4 dollarsbillion in annual military aid to Israel would end at year-end. Netanyahu's office has not confirmed it, and Congress controls appropriations, so experts expect a phase-out in 2027.

Gaza remains stalled over Hamas disarmament. Netanyahu rejected the Board of Peace roadmap in August, and Hamas objects to the disarmament clause while Israeli strikes continue. No new development in the last 72 hours.

Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow for over two hours on 12 November, then headed to Kyiv-related talks with Zelensky. Abu Dhabi talks produced no breakthrough, and Putin rejects foreign peacekeepers. Trump and Putin may meet at APEC in China later this month.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth survived the impeachment push and called Massie a "disgruntled and defeated congressman." Democrats say they will pursue accountability from January.

A security agent reportedly warned reporters at a 10 November Pentagon briefing they could be removed if they did not "maintain decorum." The Washington Post's Tara Copp reported it (unconfirmed beyond that account).

Hegseth gave a commendation medal to the soldier who scolded his entourage at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. A Pentagon official confirmed it to Fox.

MS NOW reports experienced officers were withheld from promotion under Hegseth's criteria; spokesman Sean Parnell says he is "reviving the warrior ethos." A single-outlet account (unconfirmed).

CENTCOM says the Hormuz blockade stays in force.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Open-source: CTP-ISW's 12 November update says it has seen no indicators Iran has re-mined Hormuz, despite Iranian claims that ships hit mines on the US-backed southern route. ISW reads the claims as an effort to erode confidence in US escorts and push ships into Iranian waters. Analyst assessment, not verified government reporting.

Open-source: ISW also flags a rise in attacks on ships' engine rooms. Damage is unclear.

Open-source: ISW/CTP assess that Iran and Russia are cooperating to counter US efforts on Caspian and land arms routes. A study cited by Euronews outlines joint sanctions evasion.

Officials reportedly suspect Iran in cyberattacks on military-base freezers and water-treatment equipment, and CISA/FBI issued a joint warning. Reporting on a DHS staff-data theft (40,000+ staff) and a Pentagon HR breach adds to the picture (single-outlet, unconfirmed attribution).

Axios reports Lutnick received intelligence about Jensen Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese spy and did not act. Single-source (unconfirmed).

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

October CPI rose 0.4% and annual inflation is about 4%, up from 3.7% in September. Core inflation is also rising, with services prices persistent.

Governor Waller floated a supersized hike next month, and Daly said more hikes are likely. Two officials said on 10 November that the Fed may need to move faster.

The White House says tariffs stay despite the midterm result. Reuters/Ipsos has 62% of Americans backing repeal.

Trump threatened to double Mexico tariffs unless it halts migrant caravans. Sheinbaum says no caravan has reached the border this term; the authority for such tariffs is legally constrained after the February IEEPA ruling.

The Lutnick/Nvidia dispute makes China chip export controls a live trade issue.

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS says ICE logged 491,000+ arrests and 483,000+ removals in FY2026, a record. DHS claims 1.2 million removals since January 2025; AP notes missing breakdowns, such as criminal histories.

AP ties the crackdown to the political blowback behind the Democratic midterm win.

About 1,500 migrants are in a caravan in southern Mexico, the third since September. Trump links it to an "invasion."

The Court's Big Bend ruling clears wall construction in Texas.

11. States and governors.

Several states are weighing new congressional maps. Wisconsin's governor-elect David Crowley says he would sign a redraw, and Mississippi's Tate Reeves has signalled interest in targeting the state's one Democratic seat.

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul remains open to a constitutional amendment for redistricting, possibly for 2028.

Maryland voters approved a redistricting-related ballot question with about 60% support on 3 November; Gov. Wes Moore faces obstacles to a redraw.

A Democrat has taken the Kansas governor's mansion.

12. Media and public opinion.

Karoline Leavitt, who left the press secretary role in August, is now a Fox News contributor and has commented on Miller. Trump has floated UFC chief Dana White as a replacement (unconfirmed).

Pentagon press access is under strain after the reported "decorum" warning.

Outlets diverge on the Scharf letter: CNN and MS NOW stress hypocrisy, while Fox frames it as a warning against overreach.

Public mood is sour on prices: only 21% approve of Trump's handling of them.

Coming days: Whether Trump formally acts on the Mexico tariff threat, given the IEEPA ruling.
