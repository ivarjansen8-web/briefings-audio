Here is your US briefing for Monday, 9 November.

House poised to impeach Hegseth as Miller replaces Wiles and Democrats take Congress.

Compiled from sources dated 6 to 9 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

House Republican leaders are bracing for enough GOP defections to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth this week. Rep. Thomas Massie is forcing a vote on an eight-article resolution (one article cites the War Powers Resolution), and Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries says he is a "hard yes"; removal would still need two-thirds of the Senate.

Stephen Miller takes over as White House chief of staff today, replacing Susie Wiles. Trump announced the change on 6 November, days after Democrats won both chambers of Congress, and said he may make "a few changes" elsewhere.

Democrats won the Senate (NBC projects 54 to 44 on one tally) and the House (237 seats called so far). Mississippi and Alaska Senate races were still uncalled, so final Senate numbers may shift.

The US struck Iranian air defences on Qeshm Island on 6 November while Trump claimed progress in Qatar-mediated talks. The naval blockade and the Hormuz closure remain, so the war is in a fragile limited-fighting phase.

Trump told "60 Minutes" that mass deportations were "too tough" and hurt Republicans in the midterms. DHS says enforcement will continue regardless.

2. White House and the administration.

Trump named Stephen Miller chief of staff and moved Susie Wiles to his political operation. CNN reads the pick as a signal that Trump will not fundamentally change course after the rout, and Steve Bannon had urged Wiles's removal.

Trump said he offered the press secretary job to UFC CEO Dana White, then said on 8 November he was "only kidding." White is still due at the White House today, and CBS said it was unclear whether he had accepted at the time of publication.

Trump is reportedly weighing whether Hegseth should stay, while publicly defending him. CBS reported he asked allies about a replacement; DeSantis as a possible successor is a single-source report (unconfirmed).

Trump said on 3 November the US will stop giving Israel about 4 billion dollars a year in aid after year-end. Congress appropriates that money, so he cannot end it unilaterally, and no cut has been reported.

3. Congress (House, Senate, key votes, investigations)

Congress returned today for a lame-duck session with a 11 December funding deadline. None of the 12 FY2027 spending bills has passed, and Thune reportedly put the shutdown odds at about 50 percent.

Massie's Hegseth resolution could be tabled or the session cancelled, but Axios reports GOP leaders expect it to pass. Jeffries said his conference will discuss it Tuesday morning.

Speaker Johnson reportedly will not seek the speakership again, with Scalise and Jordan reported as contenders (unconfirmed). Who leads Republicans shapes the December funding talks with Democrats.

A debt-limit increase and a reconciliation bill are on the lame-duck wish list, but Thune says the votes are not there for reconciliation. Some Democrats want to use the funding fight to defund the Iran war.

Democrats take control of both chambers on 3 January 2027. Expect oversight of Pentagon, DHS and Iran war policy from then.

4. Courts and the Supreme Court.

A federal judge issued a two-week temporary restraining order barring the DOJ from pressing Washington State officials to set aside ballots from voters on a federal list. The order covers the count and certification period.

Judge Boasberg called DOJ's lists of suspected noncitizen voters "flawed" and blocked their use to sequester ballots. A California court separately held that imposing ballot-counting procedures through prosecution threats is unconstitutional.

A divided Ninth Circuit panel found no private right of action under Section 11(b) of the Voting Rights Act in a suit over DOJ prosecution threats, and a judge called for an en banc vote. This leaves the legal route to challenge such threats unsettled.

A judge blocked construction of a planned arch near Arlington National Cemetery for 14 days.

No new Supreme Court rulings surfaced in the last 72 hours. The tariff background remains the 20 February 6 to 3 ruling in Learning Resources v. Trump, which held IEEPA does not authorise tariffs.

5. Parties, elections and polls.

Alaska is the closest uncalled Senate race, with ranked-choice results not due until 18 November. Reports differ on whether Sullivan or Peltola leads, so treat the margin as unsettled.

Mississippi's Senate race is at about 49 to 49 between Scott Colom (D) and Cindy Hyde-Smith (R), with county certification due 13 November. AP also calls it too close to call.

Trump's approval sits near 36 to 39 percent in aggregates, and Election Day exit polling put it at 37 percent. Silver Bulletin recorded an all-time low of 36.3 percent; Gallup no longer publishes approval.

Maryland voters approved a ballot measure enabling congressional redistricting (about 61 percent yes in an early count). Democrats framed it as a response to Republican redrawing elsewhere; Republicans called it gerrymandering.

6. Foreign policy (Middle East focus)

The US hit Iranian air defences on Qeshm Island on 6 November, and Trump called it "minor." CENTCOM had not published a strike summary, so details rest on Trump's remarks and Iranian state media.

Qatar says Iran talks have not failed, while Tehran reportedly set seven conditions including sanctions relief, an end to the blockade and compensation. Iran says it could reopen Hormuz within two days if they are met, but Iran's foreign minister reportedly said talks stalled after the midterms.

Trump pressed Netanyahu on 5 November to advance the Gaza plan now that Israel's 27 October election is over. A senior US official ties reconstruction to Hamas disarmament; opposition leader Eisenkot has the first chance at forming a government.

US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks open in Abu Dhabi on 10 to 11 November, led by Witkoff and Kushner, with France joining. Analysts such as the Atlantic Council expect little progress, and Zelensky warned of a Russian strike on energy sites.

7. Defence and the Pentagon.

Hegseth called the impeachment effort a "sham" and released a post-election video defending the Iran war and urging a 1.5 dollarstrillion defence budget. Watchdogs say the video violates federal law.

Thousands of US air and naval forces are reportedly poised to resume major combat against Iran, per unnamed Pentagon officials (unconfirmed). Trump has called a new offensive "highly unlikely" while talks continue.

The US naval blockade of Iran, reinstated 14 July, remains in force. A US strike disabled a ship trying to run it.

Breaking Defense says Republicans have a short window in the lame duck to pass defence priorities before Democrats take over.

8. Intelligence and OSINT.

Hegseth's directive orders Cyber Command, NSA, DIA and NGA to prioritise foreign election threats, as a "no-fail mission." Military.com notes it does not authorise military control of voting and the Pentagon would not say what is new.

NSA/Cyber Command chief Joshua Rudd reportedly said his forces were "postured and ready," and officials reported no evidence of tampering with voting infrastructure (unconfirmed, opinion-site source).

Open-source (unverified): ISW says Russian forces lost ground in October for a second straight month and suffered their highest monthly losses of the war. ISW also logged likely Russian drones entering Moldovan and Romanian airspace on 31 October.

Open-source (unverified): ISW reported a large Russian drone and missile strike on Ukrainian energy infrastructure on 2 to 3 November.

Quiet on CIA, FBI and ODNI leadership: no new November reporting surfaced. The latest oversight items found were the March 2026 threat hearings.

9. Economy, trade and tariffs.

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, versus about 65,000 expected, and unemployment fell to 4.1 percent. Wage growth was only 3.1 percent against inflation near 3.6 to 3.7 percent.

Futures put a December Fed hike at roughly 75 to 80 percent ahead of Tuesday's CPI report. The Fed raised rates in September under Chair Kevin Warsh and the FOMC meets 8 to 9 December.

Q3 GDP grew 3.4 percent, driven by consumer spending and AI investment. An AI-related selloff is reportedly spreading to credit markets.

Section 232 steel and aluminium duties stay at 50 percent, and Section 301 tariffs on China remain. IEEPA tariffs were struck down in February; refund and Section 122 appeals are pending at the Federal Circuit, and a Trump-Xi tariff suspension reportedly runs to 10 November (unconfirmed, low-grade trade-blog sources).

10. Immigration and homeland security.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said "Deportations will continue" after the midterm losses.

ICE is reportedly targeting about 2,700 arrests a day, roughly 19,000 a week, per CBS sources relayed by secondary outlets (unconfirmed).

Trump said deportation policy "might be too tough," a tension with Miller's elevation. A cited poll found 58 percent of voters think the crackdown has gone too far.

Courts continue to restrain DOJ voter-list efforts tied to noncitizen voting claims.

11. States and governors.

Maryland approved its redistricting amendment and elected Democrat Boafo to Steny Hoyer's seat.

Washington and California officials won court protection against DOJ pressure over ballots from voters on federal lists.

Utah's redistricting fight continues, with a Republican repeal petition reportedly failing after a signature-removal campaign. Dates are unclear in the source (unconfirmed).

Governor-race results were not retrievable in this run. Check NBC's results page.

12. Media and public opinion.

Trump's "60 Minutes" interview with Ross Douthat aired Sunday. Douthat pressed him that he "picked the candidates" in many lost races; Trump blamed "bad messaging."

Left-leaning outlets (Daily Beast, Raw Story) stressed his remarks on death and enemies, while some conservatives framed the interview as hostile editing. I found no Fox News or White House response (unconfirmed).

An AP-NORC poll from October found voters "overwhelmingly" want change, with 36 percent approving of Trump. No post-election AP-NORC reading was found.

Polls also show 52 percent of likely voters preferring Democrats on immigration.
