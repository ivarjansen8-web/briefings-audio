Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Somalia court says president's term expired as northern Ethiopia war deepens and Israel plants flag in Somaliland.

Compiled from sources dated 6 to 11 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term extension is unlawful, that officeholders' terms have expired, and ordered elections within 90 days. The ruling came a day after he dismissed the chief justice, and the opposition now says he used security forces to seize the court, so the legitimacy fight is moving from the ballot to the security services.

Northern Ethiopia is back at war: Critical Threats reports continued federal clashes with Tigrayan (TDF) and Fano forces, and federal troops reportedly killed TDF commander Col. Semere Teklu. Crisis Group dates the large-scale hostilities to 23 September, after the TPLF-led "Alliance for Survival" with Fano groups, and the Pretoria deal is effectively dead.

An RSF drone strike killed nine civilians in Bani Shako village, Kurmuk County, Blue Nile, per the local commissioner. Drone war now reaches the far south-east, while the UN envoy's talks with the RSF-allied bloc are not expected before late December.

Somalia and the Arab League condemned Israel's accreditation of its first ambassador to Somaliland and the laying of a cornerstone for an Israeli embassy in Hargeisa. Mogadishu calls it a flagrant violation of sovereignty, so the recognition question (Israel recognised Somaliland on 26 December 2025) is becoming concrete.

2. Sudan.

Fighting continues west and southwest of El Obeid, North Kordofan, with both sides claiming the same four areas. An army-allied joint force said it took them, RSF fighters posted videos claiming they still hold them, and neither side issued an official statement (unconfirmed).

Dar Hamar Emergency Response Room accused the RSF of abuses around Foga and Wad Banda, West Kordofan, and of closing the Wad Banda market. The area has seen heavy SAF-RSF fighting over two days, with claims about civilians coming from a local group.

A suspected RSF drone struck the Nawafil fuel station in Taqali al-Abbasiya, South Kordofan; Darfur24 says the toll has risen above 19 civilians, after Sudan Tribune's initial report of at least seven dead. Drones on civilian infrastructure, including power stations in the north, are now the war's defining tactic.

UN envoy Pekka Haavisto expects talks with the RSF and its allies in late December, focused on a transition to civilian rule. The Soufan Center notes this is not a ceasefire commitment and that army chief Burhan has repeatedly rejected truces.

The Quad (US, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE) roadmap, a three-month humanitarian truce leading to a civilian transition, remains unaccepted by the army. Port Sudan objects to the UAE's role over its alleged backing of the RSF, which the UAE has long denied.

3. South Sudan.

Riek Machar was due to resume testimony on 11 November at the Special Court in Juba, in a treason trial now in its 127th-plus session. He calls the case political and says he acted to prevent violence in Nasir and Ulang; prosecutors accuse SPLA-IO and the White Army of killing 257 soldiers in March 2025.

The Special Court said on 23 October that its probe into alleged threats against Machar and his co-defendants is complete. The matter was referred onward, with no outcome yet visible.

Presidential envoy Adut Salva Kiir called the 22 December election date "absolute". Crisis Group says the SPLM-IO split (Oyet Nathaniel versus Regina Kaba) has neutralised its ability to respond, and ACLED warns an election without Machar's release could deepen violence.

Gunmen abducted three aid workers on the Yei-Maridi road, Central Equatoria, on 9 November and demanded US8,000 dollars (police account). Insecurity in Equatoria is a further obstacle to a credible vote.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces and Fano (AFNM) kept fighting around the A2 highway between Dessie and Woldia; Fano reportedly captured Wuchale on 2 November (unconfirmed). Control of the A2 decides whether Addis can supply Tigray and the north.

In Tigray, fighting near Bizet from 1 to 3 November and the reported killing of Col. Semere Teklu show federal forces pushing in the city belt. Claims come from a monitoring group citing local reports, and TDF counter-claims (for example of 500 soldiers killed, per TRT) are unverified.

Crisis Group says the TPLF announced an Alliance for Survival with six groups on 20 September, including a major Fano umbrella organisation. This fusing of the Tigray and Amhara insurgencies is the main strategic change for Addis.

Wazema Radio reports clashes at Badme with Ethiopian, TPLF and reported Eritrean forces, but could not verify who fought whom (unconfirmed). It matters because it would link the internal war to the interstate standoff.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea severed diplomatic ties with Ethiopia on 1 October after Addis ordered its Asmara embassy shut and expelled ten Eritrean diplomats. Ethiopia accuses Asmara of hosting and arming opposition groups, which Eritrea denies; Addis Standard dates the embassy closure differently, so the sequence is disputed.

Eritrea's information minister accused Ethiopia on 9 October of an "unprovoked war of aggression" aimed at Assab. Abiy repeated on 5 October that Ethiopia needs Red Sea access, and Saudi Arabia has offered to mediate.

Analysts warn an Ethiopia-Eritrea war near Assab could open a second Red Sea front beside the Houthi threat. Sources disagree on whether direct confrontation is likely soon.

Eritrea joined Egypt, Somalia and Sudan at an October summit in New Alamein that said only coastal states should govern the Red Sea. The bloc is in effect an anti-Ethiopia alignment, and its leaders also rejected any recognition of Somaliland.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Supreme Court's expiry ruling comes with no UN mediator, since the UN political mission closed about a week earlier. Armed clashes over the dispute already occurred in Mogadishu in June, and the presidency had not responded by 6 November.

The Somali Future Council opposition accuses the president of using troops to take over the Supreme Court; Hiiraan reported soldiers deployed around it and the chief justice ordered out (AP could not verify). Puntland and Jubaland also contest his electoral changes.

Hassan Sheikh invited the opposition to national consultations in Mogadishu on 14 to 18 November, but opposition figures question their purpose. He wants direct elections and says al-Shabaab is on the back foot.

Israel's first ambassador presented credentials in Hargeisa and an embassy cornerstone was laid; the Arab League sided with Mogadishu. Somaliland is courting Washington on Berbera and Red Sea security.

Uganda has warned it could withdraw its troops if the political dispute persists, and says its AUSSOM exit date is 1 August 2027. The US ends UNSOS funding after 31 December per one report, versus a 12-month drawdown per Security Council Report.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April 2026 and the 24 September groundbreaking of a US160 dollarsmillion Damerjog fuel terminal with Ethiopia shows Djibouti's dependence on Ethiopian trade, while Guelleh has refused an Ethiopian corridor or naval base.

Egypt's foreign minister met his Somali and Djiboutian counterparts on 7 November in Tripoli on Red Sea security; no military or anti-Ethiopia measure was announced. It shows Cairo recruiting Red Sea states.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Uganda's leverage in Somalia is rising and falling at once: it is the largest AUSSOM contributor yet has set a withdrawal timeline. A report that the UAE offered about US500 dollarsmillion to induce the withdrawal is an unverified allegation.

Museveni urged a peaceful South Sudan election and cited about US700 dollarsmillion in annual Ugandan exports there. Uganda has strong commercial and security stakes in Juba's survival.

Turkana County reports hundreds of South Sudanese crossing weekly, most unaccompanied minors. Kenya carries the spillover from Juba's insecurity.

Uganda's NRM leadership is debating succession, with reports that Gen. Muhoozi eyes the vice presidency. Domestic transition affects Kampala's regional posture.

The US kept Level 4 advisories for Uganda, Somalia, South Sudan and DRC on 9 November.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Egypt is building a coastal-states bloc with Somalia, Eritrea and Sudan against Ethiopia and against Somaliland recognition. The October declaration created a permanent follow-up mechanism.

Israel's embassy steps in Hargeisa give it a foothold on the Gulf of Aden; a report mentions an Israeli presence at Berbera (unconfirmed). The UAE is reportedly building naval facilities at Berbera, while Mogadishu cancelled about US800 dollarsmillion in UAE port and security deals in January.

The Houthis declared a maritime blockade on Saudi Arabia in July and claimed a missile strike on a Bahri tanker; Reuters reportedly said they weighed transit fees, which they deny. ACLED expects a persistent rather than continuous threat to shipping.

Russia's Port Sudan base is reportedly frozen, with Burhan telling Saudi Arabia so, while another analysis says Moscow shifted support from the RSF to the SAF. China's only base remains Djibouti.

10. Humanitarian situation.

UNHCR lists about 11.3 million Sudanese forcibly displaced, including 6.41 million internally displaced.

The IPC warns of rising famine risk across Greater Darfur and Greater Kordofan; one al-Damazin camp's malnutrition cases rose from 1,800 to 2,116.

Kalma camp in South Darfur recorded 2,750 malnutrition cases in October.

OCHA reported over 2,000 suspected cholera cases and 170 deaths since May, mostly in Kordofan (late-July figure). Funding gap estimates range from 41 to 83 percent unfunded depending on source.

Northern Ethiopia, Jonglei and Somalia's funding cuts add further needs; no fresh figures found. (unconfirmed trend)

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan renewed objections to Ethiopia's unilateral GERD operation on 3 November, invoking the 1959 agreement and a joint damage committee. Ethiopia rejects the colonial-era basis, and talks remain stalled.

Ethiopia says it has documented 22+ projects Egypt allegedly obstructed. This hardens a legal-compensation dispute.

Djibouti handles most Ethiopian trade, over 75 percent of Djibouti's GDP per analysts, so a war near Assab would raise costs for the whole corridor.

Quiet on Ethiopian macro data (birr, IMF): nothing found in the last 72 hours.
