Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Monday, 16 November.

Ethiopia blames Eritrea and Egypt for TPLF attacks; Sudan army breaks El Obeid siege; Somalia court says president's term expired.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 16 November 2026 (some background items older). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Ethiopia's army chief accused Eritrea of directing a "fourth round" of TPLF attacks in Amhara and Afar, with Egyptian supplies, on 13 to 14 November. Eritrea and Egypt reject the charge; the claim links the Tigray war to the wider Ethiopia-Eritrea-Egypt rivalry.

Sudan's army and allied Darfur forces have reportedly broken the RSF siege of El Obeid and taken al-Khuwai in West Kordofan, according to Al Jazeera. A shift in Kordofan would change the balance in the war's central theatre, though battlefield claims are unverified.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's mandate has lapsed and ordered elections within 90 days. The opposition boycotted his 14 November talks, deepening the legitimacy crisis.

South Sudan's electoral commission moved its first national elections from 22 December 2026 to 11 February 2027 on 14 November. The delay is the latest sign the 2018 peace deal is hollow.

2. Sudan.

RSF drone strikes hit at least 15 power stations across Sudan within days, plunging cities into darkness. Al Jazeera counts at least 18 attacks on electricity infrastructure in nine-plus states between 20 October and 2 November; the cabinet blamed the RSF while both sides deny most strikes.

Local reporting says the army took al-Khuwai in West Kordofan on Friday 13 November, while the RSF and SPLM-N (al-Hilu) still hold al-Nuhud and parts of South Kordofan. El Obeid's reopened axis matters for army supply lines.

A local committee says RSF gunmen killed 10 civilians in al-Nuhud amid looting and a communications blackout (unconfirmed). Single-source, from an Italian briefing.

The Quintet (AU, IGAD, Arab League, EU, UN) condemned the drone escalation on 13 November, and the G7 backed the Quad on 12 November. Both urge a humanitarian truce, but neither the army nor the RSF has signed; Crisis Group says the Quad's 90-day truce text remains unagreed.

Amnesty says fewer than 70,000 people remain in El Fasher, where detentions and killings persist a year after the RSF takeover. North Darfur's governor claims 140 mass graves, a figure not independently confirmed.

Nine displaced people were killed in a drone strike in Blue Nile, a local official attributed it to the RSF, on 10 November. It shows the drone war reaching the south-east.

3. South Sudan.

The National Elections Commission set 11 February 2027 for the vote, with campaigning from 27 December. Spokesperson Josephine Achiro rejected the word "postponement"; about 90% of people lack state ID, complicating registration.

Riek Machar's treason trial continues, with the Special Court told on 11 November of a new security complaint involving his defence team. Machar calls the case political; the unity government was dissolved in September and replaced by a caretaker administration.

The UN told the Security Council on 10 November that 112,406 cholera cases and 1,728 deaths were recorded by 31 October, and about 10 million need aid. Famine risk is rising as Sudanese refugees and returnees arrive.

The Special Court said on 23 October its probe into alleged threats against Machar's team is complete. The next step is unclear.

4. Ethiopia.

Chief of staff Birhanu Jula says federal forces repelled the TPLF's fourth round of attacks in Amhara and Afar and blames Eritrea and Egypt. Eritrea and Egypt deny it; Afar authorities say TPLF fighters seized villages, a breach of the 2022 deal.

Federal forces retook Mekelle in early October and hold major northern Tigray cities, but residents report artillery and drone fire. A federally appointed six-month interim administration imposed a curfew and gun surrender; TPLF commander Fisseha Kidanu says Tigray remains at war.

A Tigray regional election is planned for April 2027, after the 1 June vote in which the Prosperity Party won 438 seats. Voting was skipped in Tigray and disrupted in Amhara and Oromia by Fano and other insurgencies.

No major new Amhara or Oromia incidents surfaced in the last 72 hours (Quiet). The Fano insurgency remains the background threat.

5. Eritrea.

Ethiopia and Eritrea severed relations on 1 October after Addis closed its embassy and expelled ten Eritrean diplomats. Eritrea says Ethiopia is preparing an "invasion"; the navy chief called sea access "inevitable" (BBC Amharic, 12 November).

G7 ministers meeting in Paris on 9 to 10 November noted "great concern" about the Horn, and five US senators warned of a multi-front conflict. Assab is the flashpoint.

Reuters and others reported Eritrean troops inside northern Ethiopia; Asmara denies deploying soldiers and says Addis is justifying a future attack.

Analysts warn fighting toward Assab could disrupt Red Sea shipping even without attacks on vessels.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Al-Shabaab captured the village of Coomaad in Hiiraan, about 30km from Mataban, on 14 to 15 November (outlets differ on the date). It sits near the Beledweyne-Galkayo road; WardheerNews links the loss to a split between federal troops and Macawiisley fighters.

The Supreme Court gave stakeholders 30 days to agree a framework and 90 days to hold elections, against an official timetable of 15 April 2027 (parliament) and 15 May (president). The government says its extension runs to May 2027 and pushes one-person-one-vote.

The Somali Future Council (Sharif Sheikh Ahmed, Puntland, Jubaland) boycotted the 14 November Villa Somalia talks. ACLED logged 12 election-related violence events in 1 to 10 November.

The court also voided the president's dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed. It raises the question of whether the executive will comply.

Somaliland's recognition by Israel (26 December 2025) remains contested; Hargeisa rejected Mogadishu's claims about Berbera in October. Somaliland says Israel is training its forces but has no base (no new development in 72 hours).

7. Djibouti.

Guelleh named Aden Ali Mohamed defence minister on 6 November, filling a post vacant since July. He was previously ambassador to Eritrea, which matters given Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions.

Guelleh toured the new international conference centre on 10 November. It signals Djibouti's bid to remain a regional diplomatic venue.

Guelleh won a sixth term in April with about 97.8% and turns 79 this month; no successor is named. Succession is the main open question.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours on their Horn role. Background: Kenya and Uganda are tied by the Kisumu-Malaba SGR extension launched in March.

Ruto denied a rift with Museveni over Dangote's Lamu refinery; Uganda's army chief Muhoozi has backed Ruto's 2027 re-election. Fuel and trade politics shape regional alignment more than the Horn's wars.

Sudanese and Somali refugees continue to reach the region (about 3.7 million Sudanese refugees and asylum seekers since April 2023).

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Al Jazeera reports the Houthis now control Yemen's whole Red Sea coast, including Mocha and Perim island, and RT reports Saudi-backed forces pulled back near Bab el-Mandeb. The advance toward Aden raises the stakes for Suez and Eritrean and Djiboutian ports.

Egypt has about 1,100 troops in Somalia under AUSSOM, per one report, and is deepening ties with Eritrea including Assab upgrades. Cairo's GERD dispute with Addis is the driver.

The US told Sudan's warring parties in Geneva (23 October) there is no military solution. Washington also urged Ethiopia and Eritrea to show restraint.

Turkey, China, Russia and the Gulf states: Quiet, no specific reports in the last 72 hours. Israel's Somaliland ties remain the main Gulf-adjacent flashpoint.

10. Humanitarian situation.

Somalia is near famine, with US support to aid agencies falling from 70 million dollars to zero, according to Doctors Without Borders, and total funding of about 160 million dollars by May versus 531 million dollars last year. The response cut its reach by 72%, to 1.3 million people, and over 400 health facilities closed.

The US reportedly suspended all assistance to Somalia's federal government over an alleged warehouse incident involving 76 tonnes of food aid (unconfirmed). Single secondary source; verify before relying.

UNICEF says drone attacks on Sudan are "unlike anything Sudan has seen before", and the UN warns the power-station strikes endanger civilians. Outages threaten hospitals and water.

Sudan's 2026 appeal had received only about 1.1 billion dollars of 2.9 billion dollars by late July, and UNHCR lists 11.35 million forcibly displaced (9 November). Cholera was concentrated in Kordofan.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's central bank held its policy rate at 16% on 3 November; inflation was 14.9% in September. Fuel costs are biting.

The birr traded near 159.7 per dollar at recent auctions, and IMF review talks are set for November with a Board decision in early 2027. The IMF has paid out about 2.65 billion dollars of 3.4 billion dollars.

Ethiopia rebased GDP on 4 November to 210.7 billion dollars, calling it Africa's fifth-largest economy. The figure is government data and shrinks debt and tax ratios on paper.

Suez traffic remains about 60% below normal in one gCaptain analysis, while Egypt lost about 7 billion dollars in 2023 to 24. Cairo's leverage against Ethiopia is tied to Red Sea fragility.

GERD and Djibouti/Berbera port news: Quiet in the last 72 hours. Analysts tie Egypt's Somalia deployment to the GERD's final filling.

What to watch.

Tigray/Afar front: further TPLF attacks or federal counter-moves this week, and any Ethiopian or Eritrean troop movements toward Assab.

Kordofan: whether the army pushes on from al-Khuwai toward al-Nuhud, and whether the RSF retaliates with more drone strikes on power infrastructure.

Somalia, by about 5 December: the Supreme Court's 30-day deadline for an election framework, and whether the Somali Future Council joins any new talks.

Ethiopia-IMF: sixth-review talks in November.

South Sudan: the Machar trial's next hearings and campaign preparations before 27 December.

Quad/Quintet diplomacy: any movement on the 90-day Sudan humanitarian truce.
