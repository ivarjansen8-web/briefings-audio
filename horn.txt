Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Somalia court says president's term expired; Sudan accuses Ethiopia over RSF drones; Ruweng clashes shadow South Sudan vote.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's mandate expired on 15 May 2026 and ordered elections within 90 days. The government disputes the ruling, and the chief justice says his guards were withdrawn, which turns an electoral-calendar dispute into a open clash between the executive and the judiciary.

Sudan's army says it took Iyal Bakhit in West Kordofan on 7 November and shot down an RSF long-range drone. The advance, south and west of El Obeid, shifts the Kordofan front while drone war spreads to Blue Nile and the South Kordofan fuel network (army claim; the RSF has not confirmed).

Sudan filed complaints at the UN Security Council and the AU Peace and Security Council accusing Ethiopia of letting the RSF launch drones from its territory. Addis Ababa denies it and accuses Khartoum of backing TPLF-linked rebels, so Sudan's war and Ethiopia's northern war are now formally entangled.

Fighting in Ruweng state, South Sudan, has killed at least 23 since early October, per the local information minister. The clashes between government forces and Riek Machar's SPLA-IO come six weeks before the 22 December election.

The TPLF claims 500 Ethiopian soldiers killed near Aksum in fighting around 31 October. The figure is a TPLF-side claim that Addis Ababa has not commented on and that nobody has verified.

2. Sudan.

The army's allied joint force and the RSF both claimed four areas west and southwest of El Obeid on 5 November. Neither side issued an official statement and the RSF released videos disputing the army claim, so control is contested (unconfirmed).

The army said it captured Iyal Bakhit and downed an FH-95 drone, the second drone it claims to have downed in Kordofan in 72 hours. Holding ground south and west of El Obeid matters because the city has been the RSF's main Kordofan objective since the UN Security Council warned of atrocity risk there in June.

An RSF drone strike on the Nawafil fuel station in Taqali al-Abbasiya, South Kordofan, has killed more than 19 civilians, and a Damazin strike on Tuesday killed at least two. Both sides use drones against civilians and infrastructure, per local reporting.

Sudan's Sovereignty Council counts 70 drone assaults involving 187 drones between 15 February and 27 October, and the UN says drone attacks have killed over 1,100 people this year. Guterres said he was "appalled" after a strike on a market in RSF-held Darfur reportedly killed more than 100 people.

Peace talks: Quiet, with no new truce meeting found in the last 72 hours. The Quad (US, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, UAE) roadmap exists, but Crisis Group says US attention has been pulled elsewhere and Sudan's army has rejected the plan.

3. South Sudan.

Government forces and SPLA-IO fighters have clashed in northern Ruweng since 4 October, killing at least 23 and wounding 17, per the state information minister. Who started the fighting is disputed by anonymous witnesses, and Machar's forces did not comment.

An official says over 100,000 people fled, but a police source says only "thousands" did (unconfirmed). The gap shows how little verified information exists on displacement from the area.

The election is set for 22 December and the SPLM-IO says unimplemented 2018 peace-deal provisions make the timetable unworkable. One analyst told Radio Tamazuj elections will not happen "unless a miracle occurs", and Machar remains on trial for treason.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces hold Mekelle since early October, and the TPLF says it moved its administration elsewhere, while fighting continues near Aksum. The TPLF, Fano and the OLA announced a joint alliance, with Fano leader Zemene Kassie as chair, to topple Abiy Ahmed.

The TPLF claims 500 soldiers killed and 800 wounded near Aksum, which is unverified and uncommented by the government. Treat casualty claims from either side with caution.

Ethiopia accuses Eritrea and Sudan of backing the rebels, and Egypt, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan jointly called for talks. Crisis Group and CRS analysts warn the war could draw in Middle Eastern powers.

Amhara: Quiet in new reporting beyond the alliance announcement; fighting in North Wollo toward Weldiya and Lalibela was the heaviest in late September.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea denies backing Tigrayan forces, and its information minister Yemane Gebremeskel called the claims false. Asmara asked the UN to intervene while Addis Ababa vowed self-defence in October.

Abiy said on 28 October he has no plan to fight Eritrea and called for international mediation, while sea access stays non-negotiable. Eritrea claims an Ethiopian general said operations would extend toward Assab.

No new border incident was found for 5 to 8 November. Reports of an Eritrean incursion near Zalambessa around 7 October were unverified.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Supreme Court said the mandates of parliament and government ended in April and the president's on 15 May, and told political leaders to agree an electoral framework within 30 days (reports also cite 90 days for elections). Puntland and Jubaland reject the government's timetable of parliamentary elections on 15 April and a presidential vote in May 2027.

A senior commander of NISA's Duufaan unit defected to al-Shabaab, per a report dated 8 November (unconfirmed, single source). Analysts quoted say it signals an intelligence breach.

Al-Shabaab is massing in Lower Shabelle as plans for reducing foreign forces advance (unconfirmed). This is the security risk behind any direct vote.

Somaliland rejected Mogadishu's claims about an Israeli base at Berbera and says its Israel ties are sovereign, after Israel's December 2025 recognition. Somaliland's foreign ministry also says it intends to join the Abraham Accords.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Guelleh and Abiy broke ground on a 160 dollarsmillion Damerjog petroleum terminal on 24 September, tying Djibouti's port economy to Ethiopia even as Ethiopia seeks other sea access.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Ruto has proposed an IGAD sub-committee on South Sudan, and Museveni urged peaceful December elections there.

Sudan's army refuses to cooperate with the IGAD Quartet led by Ruto until he is replaced, per a Wikipedia snapshot (unconfirmed). It limits Nairobi's mediation role.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

The Houthis run a transit-permission system in the Red Sea, with permission reportedly granted in about 24 hours, after a declared ban on Saudi-port traffic (7 November report). The Yemeni government urged ships not to engage with the Houthi centre, so shipping risk persists.

Saudi Arabia is reported to be building a military partnership with Egypt and Somalia to counter UAE influence. Egypt has about 1,100 troops in the AU mission in Somalia and a brigade on the way (reported).

Turkey's parliamentary foreign affairs committee approved its Somalia defence and economic agreement on 27 October; it awaits a plenary vote.

US envoy Massad Boulos on 5 October urged Ethiopia and Eritrea to show maximum restraint. The US says the conflict jeopardises cooperation with both.

10. Humanitarian situation.

Renewed fighting has forced Tigray returnees to flee again and displaced thousands in Amhara; the UN is preparing to scale up aid. FEWS NET expects crisis-level food insecurity in parts of South and East Tigray.

Sudan's 2026 appeal is about 40% funded (1.1 dollarsbillion of 2.9 dollarsbillion), with 8.6 million internally displaced and over 4.6 million refugees (as of late July). Cholera is concentrated in Kordofan.

In North Kordofan and Aj Jazirah, power cuts and fuel shortages disrupt clean water supply. Waterborne disease risk rises.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan issued a joint statement on 2 November invoking the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement and demanding a binding GERD framework. Ethiopia rejects the 1959 deal, and its water minister links Nile understanding to support for Red Sea access.

The birr traded around 162 per dollar in late September under the IMF programme, with a bondholder agreement in principle in July (explainer-site figures). War adds fiscal risk.

Red Sea risk and constrained Hormuz traffic raise the stakes of any fighting near Assab. Analysts say even a modest new risk would fall on one of the few export routes left.

What to watch.

Next days: whether the army holds Iyal Bakhit and whether the RSF retakes ground southwest of El Obeid.

Somalia, by about 5 December: the 30-day window the Supreme Court gave leaders to agree an electoral framework, and any move against the chief justice.

South Sudan, to 22 December: whether Ruweng clashes spread and whether the elections commission holds the date.

UN Security Council and AU PSC: any session on Sudan's complaint against Ethiopia and on Ethiopia-Eritrea.

Tigray: fighting around Aksum and any Ethiopian or TPLF statement on casualties.

Turkey's plenary vote on the Somalia agreement, and any Houthi action on Red Sea transits.
