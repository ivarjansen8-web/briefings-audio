Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Tuesday, 24 November.

Khartoum drone strikes spark Ethiopia row; Somalia election standoff; South Sudan peace deal 'collapsing'

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 24 November 2026 (most items 20 to 23 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

RSF drones struck Khartoum and Omdurman on 22 November, killing at least four people and damaging a UN building. Sudan's army told AFP the drones were launched from Ethiopian territory, an allegation Sudan has taken to the UN Security Council and African Union; it turns the war into a regional dispute.

Ethiopia's military chief accused Eritrea of directing a new TPLF offensive in Amhara and Afar, with Egyptian backing. These are government claims, not independently verified, but they show Addis Ababa framing the Tigray fight as a proxy war.

Somalia's election standoff is deepening after the opposition boycotted President Hassan Sheikh's talks on 14 November. Uganda is trying to mediate while the mandate dispute and a clash with the UAE escalate.

Crisis Group told the UN Security Council that South Sudan's 2018 peace deal is collapsing. Machar remains detained and on trial, and violence continues in Warrap.

2. Sudan.

RSF drone strikes hit Khartoum, Omdurman and the area around Khartoum airport from about 2 a.m. on 22 November. Local reports cited at least four civilians dead and nine injured; OCHA called the risk to UN staff "unacceptable".

Sudan renewed its claim that drones are launched from Ethiopia, and the UN and US condemned the strikes. The US adviser Massad Boulos blamed the RSF and said the flow of weapons, especially drones, must stop; Ethiopia's response was not found.

Amnesty says nine RSF drone strikes around El Obeid between June and August killed at least 37 civilians and should be investigated as war crimes. The RSF denied responsibility and blamed the army.

Fighting in West Kordofan and Blue Nile displaced thousands more, including more than 51,000 newly displaced in Blue Nile alone (figure as reported by Darfur24). The IOM also reported flight from Abu Qalb and other areas of Abu Zabad locality.

The Quintet (AU, IGAD, Arab League, EU, UN) condemned the surge in drone attacks on 13 November and urged support for the Quad's humanitarian truce effort. No truce is in force, and a Geneva consultation of 27 countries in late October ended without one.

The Security Council held closed consultations on Sudan on 20 November. The UN humanitarian coordinator warned that hundreds of aid trucks were stranded by fuel shortages.

3. South Sudan.

Crisis Group told the Security Council the 2018 peace agreement is collapsing as Kiir pushes toward elections the opposition rejects. It said Juba had denied envoys, including AU officials, access to Machar.

The election date is unclear: Crisis Group coverage cites 22 December, while Wikipedia and CFR report a postponement to 11 February 2027 (unconfirmed). The UN mission chief had warned on 10 November that the country was at a "critical juncture".

An attack in Warrap State's Tonj East on 22 November killed about 15 civilians, and one report put the toll at 24, including seven children. Local officials blamed fighters linked to Machar's SPLA-IO; that attribution is unverified.

At his special court hearing in Juba, Machar blamed Kiir's security decisions for the Nasir bloodshed. He said Kiir ignored his request to delay a troop deployment, which makes the trial a political stage.

4. Ethiopia.

Ethiopia's military chief said on 14 November that Eritrea is directing a TPLF offensive in Amhara and Afar with Egyptian support. This is the government's account only.

Addis Ababa installed a federal caretaker administration in Tigray for six months after the TPLF's 23 September offensive. Federal forces pushed the TPLF out of major towns, ending the Pretoria-era interim administration; the TPLF has retreated to the hills.

Fighting between Fano and federal forces continues across Amhara, with civilian casualties and drone activity in the 2 to 8 November update. Fano's claimed capture of Meragna is unconfirmed.

Western Tigray tension rose after the caretaker administration named a coordinator for the disputed area (unconfirmed, single report). Amhara claims the territory, so it is a likely flashpoint.

The Critical Threats security review of 17 November tracks the northern war and Ethiopia-Eritrea risks.

5. Eritrea.

President Isaias received the credentials of six new ambassadors on 19 November, from South Africa, Spain, Hungary, South Korea, Oman and Switzerland. This is routine, but it shows Asmara is still courting diplomatic ties as war fears grow.

Isaias met China's Horn envoy Hu Changchun on 5 November, and Beijing reiterated respect for Eritrea's sovereignty. The statement comes from Eritrea's information ministry.

Eritrea accuses Ethiopia of planning an "invasion" over Assab, and Ethiopia's navy chief said sea access is "inevitable" (BBC Amharic, as relayed). G7 foreign ministers and US senators have warned of war; analysts such as Brasidas judge full interstate war unlikely before year-end.

Egypt, Eritrea, Somalia and Sudan said in the 4 October El Alamein Declaration that Red Sea control belongs to coastal states. Ethiopia answered that it has "legitimate interests".

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Hassan Sheikh's election talks failed to open on 14 November after the main opposition alliance boycotted at Villa Somalia. Puntland and Jubaland also stayed away, so there is no agreed path to the 2027 votes.

The electoral commission has set parliamentary elections for 15 April 2027 and the presidential vote for 15 May 2027; a 5 November Supreme Court ruling reportedly said the mandates had expired and ordered elections within 90 days (unconfirmed, from one summary). The two timelines conflict.

Uganda is trying to convene talks, and Museveni reportedly agreed to travel to Mogadishu, but reports conflict on whether Hassan Sheikh accepts talks held abroad. Uganda also has troops in the country ahead of the 2027 exit.

Hassan Sheikh accused the UAE on 17 November of meddling and backing structures outside federal command, and Somalia reportedly expelled the UAE ambassador on 19 November (unconfirmed, single report).

Israel's first ambassador to Somaliland presented credentials in Hargeisa; Somalia called it null and void and the Arab League condemned it. Israel remains the only UN member to recognise Somaliland, since 26 December 2025.

A reported New York Times investigation says the UAE has turned Berbera and Bosaso airports into large military hubs (unconfirmed, via Somali-language Kaab TV). US officials reportedly said they plan no permanent base there.

7. Djibouti.

President Guelleh toured the new Djibouti International Conference Centre on 10 November. It signals a push for regional diplomatic standing; he won a sixth term earlier this year.

Ethiopia-Djibouti rail links to the Doraleh Multipurpose Port and HDTL oil terminal were targeted for November 2026. I found no report confirming that the deadline was met.

Guelleh has rejected Ethiopia's request for a corridor and naval base at Tadjourah. DP World also says Djibouti ignores arbitration rulings in its favour (older background).

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Uganda is pushing a Somalia settlement and Museveni has agreed to travel to Mogadishu. Kampala's troops make it the key outside broker, though the president rejects talks outside Somalia.

Ruto cut short a Western Kenya tour to attend a four-nation meeting in the Republic of Congo on 22 November. Kenya-DRC relations are strained.

Museveni briefed Dangote on a 60,000 bpd refinery, while commentators say Uganda is hedging between Kenyan and Tanzanian rail links. It shows economic rivalry inside the bloc.

I found no new Kenyan or Ugandan statements on Sudan in the period.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree told shippers on 20 November to leave Saudi Red Sea ports, naming Jeddah; the Saudi-led coalition intercepted three missiles aimed at Riyadh on 21 November. By 23 November no shipping company had publicly complied.

One analysis found no corroborated Houthi attack on a merchant ship in the Red Sea since 24 August, with transits at about three-quarters of the depressed 2024 to 25 level. It points to risk, not closure.

The US adviser Boulos met Egypt's foreign minister in the UAE on 13 November, with Sudan, Libya and the Horn on the agenda. No initiative was announced; he also met army chief al-Burhan in Egypt in October.

Egypt is aligned with Eritrea and Somalia in the El Alamein Declaration, and the Somalia-UAE fallout is a worry for Cairo.

Turkey held pre-talks between Mogadishu and the opposition earlier, but Puntland and Jubaland boycotted. I found nothing new on Russia or China beyond Isaias's meetings.

10. Humanitarian situation.

Sudan: 19.5 million people, about 41% of the population, faced crisis-level hunger or worse between February and May 2026 (IPC, via WFP/FAO/UNICEF). Famine was confirmed in El Fasher and Kadugli in 2025, and no newer update was found.

WFP pledged month-long food aid for 30,000 displaced people in Sudan's Northern State, per Dabanga's 17 to 18 November headlines.

Fuel shortages have stranded hundreds of aid trucks, the UN coordinator told the Security Council.

South Sudan: about 73,000 people in Jonglei and Upper Nile were classed IPC 5 in an earlier, undated analysis, and the UN warned of rising famine risk.

Tawila in North Darfur keeps receiving displaced people, with over 6,000 arrivals since 1 September as of an early-October report.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan renewed objections to Ethiopia's unilateral operation of the GERD on 2 November, citing the 1959 agreement and demanding a binding framework. Talks have stalled and Ethiopia is pushing three more Blue Nile projects.

Ethiopia rebased GDP on 4 November to 31.86 trillion birr (about 210.7 billion dollars), well above the IMF's earlier 127.1 billion dollars estimate. Critics note it changes neither tax take nor debt.

The central bank held its rate at 16% on 3 November and the birr trades near 161 per dollar. The IMF has disbursed about 2.65 billion dollars of a 3.4 billion dollars loan; a sixth review is expected but unconfirmed.

Dangote and Ethiopian Investment Holdings plan pipelines through Djibouti (approved in May).

What to watch.

Sudan, this week: whether Ethiopia answers Khartoum's drone-origin claim at the UN Security Council or AU, and any follow-up Quad truce move.

Somalia, coming days: whether Museveni's Mogadishu trip or a Kampala meeting happens, and whether Puntland and Jubaland attend.

Somalia-UAE, coming days: confirmation or denial of the reported ambassador expulsion.

Red Sea, coming days: whether any shipper leaves Jeddah after the Houthi warning, and further Houthi launches at Saudi targets.

South Sudan, 22 December or 11 February 2027: clarify which election date stands; Machar trial sessions continue.

Ethiopia, coming weeks: IMF sixth-review talks, and any Assab or Afar escalation.
