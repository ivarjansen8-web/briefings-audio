Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Friday, 20 November.

Somalia expels UAE envoy, Ethiopia-Eritrea troops mass near Badme, Blue Nile town contested in Sudan.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 20 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia ordered the UAE ambassador out within 72 hours on 19 November and recalled its own envoy from Abu Dhabi. Mogadishu accuses Abu Dhabi of violating its sovereignty and backing regional administrations against the federal government, which turns a long-running proxy rivalry into an open diplomatic rupture.

Ethiopia and Eritrea are reported to have massed troops near Badme, with fighting reignited along the border. Prime Minister Abiy has said Ethiopia could take the Red Sea port of Assab by force if peaceful means fail, while Asmara calls this a threat and denies its troops crossed over.

The Sudanese army and the RSF both claimed the Blue Nile town of Deraing on 19 November. Neither claim is independently verified, and fighting has displaced more than 1,400 families, according to Sudan Tribune.

South Sudan pushed its first national elections from December to 11 February 2027. The election commission cites registration and civic education, and the delay lands while Riek Machar is on trial and fighting continues in Jonglei and Upper Nile.

The US Embassy in Addis Ababa warned on 19 November against travel to Amhara, Tigray and Afar. It cited continuing violence in remote areas of Tigray near the Eritrean border.

2. Sudan.

The army and the RSF trade claims over Deraing in Blue Nile, with the army's 4th Infantry Division saying it destroyed 28 RSF combat vehicles and seized 18. The RSF separately claims Bao locality headquarters; all figures come from the parties and are unverified, and a Blue Nile front widens the war far from Darfur and Kordofan.

Army drone strikes hit RSF intelligence headquarters in Nyala, South Darfur, with dozens of fighters reportedly killed (unconfirmed). The figure rests on military sources and witnesses, and Nyala is the RSF's main hub in Darfur.

The army and allied forces have broken the RSF siege of El Obeid and retaken al-Khuwai in West Kordofan, according to Al Jazeera on 14 November. If it holds, this is the most significant shift on the Kordofan front this year.

Amnesty International on 19 November called for war-crimes investigations into nine RSF drone strikes around El Obeid between June and August that killed at least 37 civilians. Amnesty says it is also investigating army drone strikes that caused civilian casualties.

No truce is in place; the UN Security Council holds closed consultations on Sudan on 20 November. The African Union, IGAD, Arab League, EU and UN condemned drone attacks across at least eight states on 13 November, and UN envoy Pekka Haavisto expects talks with the RSF before Christmas.

3. South Sudan.

Elections are postponed to 11 February 2027, the electoral commission says. The first national vote since independence has now slipped again, which deepens the legitimacy problem around President Kiir's government.

Riek Machar told the special court in Juba that Kiir ignored his request to delay troop deployment to Nasir (reported 18 November). This is Machar's account at his treason trial, and the case sits at the centre of the collapsed 2018 peace deal.

Government forces and the SPLA-IO are still fighting in Jonglei and Greater Upper Nile, with intercommunal violence in Warrap and Lakes, according to Security Council Report's November forecast. The council also notes drought-like conditions in Upper Nile.

Funding cuts have left Bentiu and Malakal displacement camps without clean water, sanitation and healthcare, affecting more than 150,000 people, MSF says. This is relayed through the EEPA situation report of 5 November.

4. Ethiopia.

The US Embassy warned citizens on 19 November not to travel to Amhara, Tigray or Afar. It cited violence in remote Tigray near the Eritrean border.

Federal forces took Mekelle in early October and installed a federally appointed interim administration, which announced a night curfew and a firearms surrender order on 6 November. The TPLF has branded Tigrayans working with Addis "traitors" and urged continued fighting, and Abiy told AP the conflict is not over.

Heavy fighting between federal forces and Fano continued in West Gondar near the Sudan border, with businesses in Metema shut, Wazema Radio reports. Earlier fighting with heavy weapons and drones hit Wuchale in South Wollo, and the Dessie-Woldia road was reportedly closed (unconfirmed).

The Red Cross warned of a worsening humanitarian crisis in Oromia as conflict and displacement deepen. Addis Standard also reported on 16 November that Oromo intellectuals back a new OLA-Fano alliance, a claim that remains contested.

Ethiopia's Horn adviser Getachew Reda told a UN envoy that Eritrea and Sudan back armed groups and drone attacks. Neither Asmara nor Khartoum had responded in the reporting seen.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea's information minister rejected Ethiopian claims that Eritrean troops crossed the border and said the army is deployed within its sovereign territory. Asmara says Addis is using the conflict as cover to seize Assab.

G7 foreign ministers and five US Democratic senators have urged restraint and diplomacy over Assab. The G7 statement did not name the port, and no response from either capital was found.

President Isaias met China's Horn envoy Hu Changchun on 5 November, and the Eritrean readout says Beijing stressed respect for Eritrea's sovereignty. Chinese sources had not published their own readout, so this is single-source.

Isaias returned on 1 November from the Third Russia-Africa Summit, where Putin thanked Eritrea for its UN support. Asmara is leaning on Moscow and Beijing as the border crisis builds.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Somalia expelled the UAE ambassador on 19 November, and the UAE condemned a "hostile approach". Mogadishu says Abu Dhabi runs unauthorised military bases and backs rival regional administrations; Somaliland's foreign ministry rejected the claims.

Al-Shabaab took the Hiran area of Omad, about 30 km from Mataban, raising fears of an advance. The defence ministry says airstrikes killed 27 fighters in Lower Shabelle, and the intelligence agency NISA reports two militants killed in Hiran; all are government or single-source figures.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled this month that the president's term has expired and ordered elections within 90 days. The electoral dispute is unresolved and fed armed clashes in Mogadishu earlier this year (single-source summary).

Forces loyal to Puntland clashed with northeastern administration forces near Fliyad Yali in Sool, with no casualty figures released. The clash adds to the fragmentation of the federal system.

Somaliland's intelligence agency said it briefly detained five US nationals it considered a security threat. Israel recognised Somaliland on 26 December 2025, while the US has not, and a recognition bill sits in the House Foreign Affairs Committee.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Six survivors of a cargo boat that sank near Bab al-Mandeb reached Berbera on 17 November.

Djibouti is the port of choice for Somali officials, with the Somali Speaker touring Doraleh and the free zone on 4 November. Djibouti also trains 11 Berbera port staff.

A target to link the Doraleh multipurpose port and the HDTL oil terminal to the Ethio-Djibouti railway was set for November 2026; I found no confirmation it was met (unconfirmed). An Assab war would raise Djibouti's strategic weight further.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Kenya's AUSSOM Sector II commander met Jubaland's president in Kismayo this month amid worries about mission funding. Kenya is the main troop contributor in a sector covering Lower and Middle Juba, Gedo and parts of Lower Shabelle.

Uganda's army chief Muhoozi Kainerugaba said on 17 November he will run for president after his father leaves office. Uganda chairs the East African Community summit and is a key actor in South Sudan (single-source summary).

The US kept Uganda, Somalia and South Sudan at Level 4 travel advisories. Crisis Group says South Sudan's clashes have spread to Equatoria on the Kenyan and Ugandan borders.

Uganda eyes a January financing deal for the long-delayed railway to Kenya. The line was launched in March 2026.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Houthi drones and missiles hit Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, which carries about 4 million barrels a day to the Red Sea, according to Red Sea Futures. A Saudi-led coalition said on 19 November it destroyed a Houthi cruise missile fired at southern Saudi Arabia.

Turkey has reportedly ratified a Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan defence pact, and Egypt's next step is an open question (unconfirmed). Analysts frame a bloc of Israel and the UAE in Somaliland against Turkey and Egypt in Somalia.

Russia has no ratified naval base in Sudan, and Sudan's army chief offered a Red Sea free economic zone in late October. Analysts say the base is stalled while the war continues; a Turkish base is also mooted, on a single report (unconfirmed).

Egypt's foreign minister met US adviser Massad Boulos in Abu Dhabi on 13 November to discuss Sudan. The Quad (US, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE) remains the main mediation track despite the Somalia-UAE and Sudan-UAE strains.

10. Humanitarian situation.

The WFP warns funding shortfalls will force cuts to food aid for Sudanese communities facing famine, while pledging a month of food for 30,000 displaced people at El Dabba. Deliveries to the camp had stopped.

UN News reported on 16 November that funding cuts raise trafficking and exploitation risks for refugee children. Children are 52 per cent of Ethiopia's 1.1 million refugees.

Fuel shortages have stranded hundreds of humanitarian trucks in Sudan, the UN resident coordinator says. This hampers access just as El Fasher and Kadugli remain flagged for famine by earlier IPC findings.

UNHCR's chief reportedly says the agency has received only 3 dollarsbillion of the 8.5 dollarsbillion it needs this year (single-source). Renewed fighting in northern Ethiopia is adding displacement across Tigray and Amhara.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan renewed opposition to Ethiopia's independent management of the GERD around 3 November, demanding a binding legal framework. Ethiopia rejects binding limits and no date is set for new Nile talks.

Ethiopia's Finance Ministry published rewritten national accounts on 4 November putting 2025/26 output at 31.86 trillion birr (about 210.7 dollarsbillion). Officials say this makes it Africa's fifth-largest economy, an official claim not independently ranked; the market rate was about 160.8 birr per dollar on 12 November.

Egypt's irrigation minister said in August that Egypt will not allow new Ethiopian dams on the Nile. Ethiopia is reportedly preparing three more.

Saudi Arabia reportedly rolled over a 5.3 dollarsbillion deposit at Egypt's central bank (unconfirmed). Neither government had confirmed it as of 19 November.
