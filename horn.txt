Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

Sudan rejects Ethiopia as talks host as Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions and Somalia's mandate row deepen.

Compiled from sources dated 25 to 28 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Sudan's army rejected Ethiopia's offer to host peace talks on 25 November, accusing Addis Ababa of backing the RSF. The row turns a Sudan mediation offer into an Ethiopia-Sudan proxy dispute, and Ethiopia denies the charge.

Ethiopia and Eritrea remain on a war footing with no diplomatic ties since October. Think-tank monitoring reported Eritrean reinforcements near the Assab road on 20 November (unconfirmed, single source), and China sent envoys to four capitals to calm things.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that the president's mandate expired on 15 May and ordered elections within 90 days. The government says it can stay until May 2027, and the opposition rejects talks, so the standoff is feeding violence around Mogadishu.

Houthi strikes hit Saudi Red Sea ports Jeddah and Jazan on 25 November. The escalation across the water raises the risk to shipping and to Horn states that sit on Bab el-Mandeb.

2. Sudan.

A rights group says RSF attacks killed at least 14 people in East Darfur and West Kordofan over three days. Anadolu relayed the figure on 27 November; it is a rights-group count that is not independently verified.

RSF drone attacks killed at least 19 people in Blue Nile state, according to a rights group. Analysts say the RSF is advancing toward Damazin near the Ethiopian and South Sudanese borders, which is why Khartoum blames Ethiopia.

The army is reported to be pushing the RSF out of western Kordofan while the RSF tries to stretch its forces thin. Burhan insists on fighting until the RSF is defeated, so no decisive shift is visible.

RSF commander Ammar Mohammed Zein Rabih announced his defection at Khartoum airport and alleged Israeli trainers helped the militia. The Israel claim is his own and unverified (unconfirmed); the defection itself is a propaganda win for the army.

Tasis, the RSF-aligned bloc, met the Quintet in Addis Ababa on 25 November and asked it to clarify its stance on the Quad's September 2025 statement. The outcome was not reported (unconfirmed). The Quad's three-month truce plan is still not agreed.

UN envoy Pekka Haavisto said on 2 November that talks with the RSF and allies were expected before Christmas. Prediction-market odds of a ceasefire by 31 December are around 5%.

3. South Sudan.

Riek Machar's treason and murder trial is adjourned to 30 November, when he resumes defence testimony. He denies ordering Gen Majur Dak to surrender and says he sought helicopters to evacuate 219 soldiers from Nasir.

Machar calls the case a political trial between Kiir's SPLM-IG and his SPLM-IO. The prosecution alleges SPLA-IO and White Army forces killed 257 soldiers in Nasir; Machar denies it, and rights groups cite due-process concerns.

Kiir ordered security forces to remove illegal roadblocks on aid routes on 26 November. The order follows US and other diplomatic criticism of harassment and illegal taxation of aid groups.

OCHA's chief said she was appalled by the killing of an aid worker in South Sudan. Details were thin in the result seen (unconfirmed).

4. Ethiopia.

Abiy told parliament that Ethiopia will not invade Eritrea and seeks Red Sea access through dialogue. He also accused Eritrea of arming Ethiopia's adversaries, so the reassurance comes with a warning.

Abiy called for swift Tigray elections within six months under an interim government he named in mid-October. One report says the TPLF rejects the administration as illegitimate (unconfirmed, headline only).

Fano leaders say they formed a unified command and an alliance with the OLA aimed at removing Abiy. The EU and other actors criticised the alliance; Fano territorial claims in North Shewa are unverified (unconfirmed).

Addis Standard reports forced conscription resurfacing across Oromia, including near Sheger City. It shows the federal government drawing on manpower as several fronts open.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea reportedly cut diplomatic ties with Ethiopia on 1 October after fighting resumed in Tigray in September. Asmara denies arming the TPLF and accuses Addis of pursuing territorial ambitions through Red Sea diplomacy.

G7 foreign ministers and five US senators warned the Assab dispute could widen into regional war. No Eritrean response was found; I found no report of fighting at Assab itself.

China sent senior envoys to Ethiopia, Eritrea, Sudan and others to head off another war. Reports of troop concentrations near the border sharpen the urgency.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The opposition on 21 November rejected any talks with President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud. The core dispute is direct "one person, one vote" versus the clan-based indirect system.

Parliamentary elections are set for 15 April 2027 and presidential for 15 May 2027. The Supreme Court's 90-day order would imply early February, so a legitimacy clash is baked in. ACLED counted 12 election-linked violence events in the first 10 days of November.

The AU Peace and Security Council on 27 November praised Somalia's first direct local council elections. It welcomed Hassan Sheikh's outreach, which signals AU backing for the government line.

Al-Shabaab is pressing Lower Shabelle, reportedly pairing suicide car bombs with armed drones. Analysts say its control of the rural belt gives it leverage over Mogadishu.

Somaliland remains recognised only by Israel, whose first ambassador presented credentials in Hargeisa in late October. Washington has not followed, and on 17 November Somalia's president accused the UAE of meddling.

7. Djibouti.

President Guelleh proposed port and maritime cooperation with Madagascar on 25 November. It shows Djibouti marketing its port expertise while Ethiopia eyes Assab.

Guelleh received credentials from six new ambassadors on 24 November, including China's. Only the Chinese envoy will be resident, reflecting Beijing's base there.

Guelleh won a sixth term in April with 97.8% of the vote. Critics say the contest lacked real competition.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background only: Kenya's hosting of a parallel Sudanese government has strained ties with the army, and Museveni chairs the EAC.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

The Houthis struck Jeddah and Jazan on 25 November and threatened more Saudi ports. Strikes by both sides now hit airports and energy sites.

Analysts warn the Houthis could besiege Aden, putting a Houthi administration opposite the Horn. Houthi seizures of islands and Mokha are aggregator claims (unconfirmed).

Egypt is reported to be moving to support Saudi Arabia (unconfirmed, headline only). Cairo and Khartoum also issued a joint anti-GERD statement on 2 November.

Russia's Port Sudan naval base is described as halted by the war. Moscow's ambassador said progress is "currently halted", and the US had warned of serious consequences.

AFRICOM's 91st declared 2026 strike in Somalia hit an al-Shabaab target near Jamaame in early November.

10. Humanitarian situation.

A UN hunger analysis, reported 27 November, says 19.5 million Sudanese (41%) face crisis-level hunger or worse. Up to 13 areas in North Darfur and South Kordofan could face famine by January 2027.

More than 383,000 people have been displaced from Kordofan in a year, per IOM. Families are fleeing repeatedly.

The UN says 5.7 million Somalis will need aid in 2027. The 2026 plan (852 dollarsmillion) is 36% funded, with 306 dollarsmillion received.

Reports put the Sudan war at 59,000+ dead and about 13 million displaced. These are cumulative estimates by media and the UN.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's birr traded near 160.7 per dollar at the 24 November auction, where banks bid 160.8 million dollars for 140 million dollars. Demand exceeding supply keeps pressure on the currency.

Ethiopia's central bank held its rate at 16% and inflation eased to 14.6% in October. IMF sixth-review talks were set for November with a board date in 2027 (unconfirmed by the Fund).

Ethiopia rebased GDP on 4 November to about 210.7 billion dollars, which would make it Africa's fifth-largest economy.

Egypt and Sudan blame GERD releases for recent Nile floods; Ethiopia says its operations reduced the flooding. Sudan's government points to other factors too.

South Sudan opened 14 oil blocks to investors. The date was not confirmed (unconfirmed).
