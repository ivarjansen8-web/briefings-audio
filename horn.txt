Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

Ethiopia-Eritrea border clashes near Badme, army breaks El Obeid siege, South Sudan vote delayed to February.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 18 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Ethiopia and Eritrea are trading fire near Badme, and Eritrea has accused Ethiopia's army chief of laying claim to its territory. A situation report dated 16 November describes more than six days of clashes with Ethiopian drone strikes and heavy Eritrean artillery, and says the two states cut diplomatic ties in October; the risk is a state-on-state war layered on the renewed Tigray conflict.

Sudan's army says it has broken the RSF siege of El Obeid and is pushing west toward Al-Nahud. Al Jazeera reports gains in West Kordofan this week; if they hold, the army gets a route toward Darfur.

South Sudan has postponed its first national elections from 22 December to 11 February 2027. The delay, announced 14 November, comes with Riek Machar still facing treason charges and fighting continuing in Jonglei.

The UN Security Council met privately on AUSSOM funding on 17 November, with the US backing a one-year extension. The December renewal decides whether the Somalia mission survives 2026.

Israel laid the cornerstone of its embassy in Hargeisa and Somaliland's first Israeli ambassador presented credentials. It keeps the recognition question, which Somalia, China, Turkey and Saudi Arabia oppose, live.

2. Sudan.

The army (SAF) retook Al-Khuwai on 13 November and Ayal Bakhit on 7 November, according to one account, and is advancing on Al-Nahud under air cover. Sudan Tribune says the army's aim is to push into Darfur, which the RSF still largely holds.

At least 15 civilians, including four children, were reportedly killed in a drone strike on Abu Qalb, West Kordofan. The RSF-aligned Tasis alliance puts the toll at nine and denies RSF forces were the target; none of the figures is independently verified.

An RSF field commander, Ahmed Hasab Al-Rasoul, announced his defection to the army on 15 November (unconfirmed). He accused the UAE of financing the war; a single defection is a signal, not yet a trend.

The UN says 2,230 civilians died in ten months, over 80 percent from drone strikes. It also attributes 29 RSF drone strikes between 20 October and 4 November to power and fuel infrastructure in 11 states.

Peace diplomacy is stalled. The US-Egypt-Saudi-UAE Quad's roadmap (humanitarian truce, then ceasefire, then civilian transition) has not been accepted by the army, which prefers its own proposal; the AU and US co-chaired a Quad-Quintet meeting in September.

Sudan's bishops called on both sides to protect civilians and warned of threats against Christians.

3. South Sudan.

The National Elections Commission moved the vote to 11 February 2027, announced 14 November. It is a further slip for a transition that Kiir has run by decree since dissolving the government in September.

Riek Machar remains under house arrest and charged with treason (he denies the charges), so the 2018 peace deal is hollow. Rights campaigner Malong Deng was quoted saying "There are no elections in South Sudan".

Fighting between the army (SSPDF) and the SPLA-IO continues in Jonglei and Greater Upper Nile, with clashes along the Nasir-Ulang corridor displacing more than 35,000. The Security Council was due to hold consultations this month.

Intercommunal violence persists in Warrap and Lakes states and between Duk and Twic East youth.

4. Ethiopia.

The Tigray war that resumed on 23 September is now in its eighth week. Federal and TPLF forces reportedly clashed in the interior south of Axum; the TPLF is allied with Fano and the OLA in the "Alliance for Survival" (Fano fighting alongside the TPLF is unconfirmed, per Critical Threats).

Drone strikes in Amhara, Tigray and Oromia killed at least 62 civilians between 29 October and 6 November, per witness and media accounts. In Rama, Tigray, a hospital confirmed 11 dead and ACLED recorded 12; the army did not respond.

Ethiopia told a UN envoy that Eritrea and Sudan back armed groups and drone attacks, and it expelled six staff of Sudan's consulate. Sudan and Eritrea deny or dispute the claims.

Abiy met Erdogan in Antalya and is courting climate diplomacy ahead of hosting COP32 next year. The outreach shows Addis seeking partners while at war on several fronts.

Security forces are reportedly conscripting by force in Oromia and Addis Ababa (unconfirmed).

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea's information minister said on 17 November that Ethiopia's army chief made claims on Eritrean territory. Asmara says its forces stay inside borders set by the boundary commission and accuses Addis of planning an "invasion".

Ethiopian officials, including the air force commander in October, have named the port of Assab as an objective. Witnesses reported Eritrean troops near Zalambessa and Ethiopian drone strikes near Adigrat (single analysis, unconfirmed).

Eritrea's ambassador in Khartoum called Ethiopia's Prosperity Party the main source of regional instability on 13 November. The message reflects the Asmara-Khartoum alignment Ethiopia complains of.

The G7 foreign ministers voiced concern at escalation after meeting in Paris on 9 to 10 November.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Somalia's intelligence agency says two al-Shabaab members were killed and three commanders wounded in Hiraan operations on 17 November. It cited help from international partners; the group still holds large areas of central Somalia.

Somalia welcomed a US decision to resume support for the anti-al-Shabaab operations, ending a funding dispute.

President Hassan Sheikh's election talks failed to open when the opposition Somali Future Council boycotted, and Puntland and Jubaland also refused. The electoral commission has set parliamentary elections for 15 April 2027 and the presidential vote for 15 May; Hassan Sheikh says national talks must follow regional votes in Galmudug and Hirshabelle.

Mogadishu says it does not recognise the Jubaland election that returned Ahmed Madobe.

Somaliland: Israel reaffirmed recognition, first made on 26 December 2025, and says it would survive a change of government. The Times of Israel blog cites Israeli media reports; Somaliland remains unrecognised by any other UN member.

Somalia warned that instability in Ethiopia could weaken AUSSOM, since Ethiopian troops are a major contingent.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest item found is President Guelleh touring a new conference centre on 10 November, after a 97.81 percent provisional win in April.

Background: Guelleh has rejected Ethiopia's request for an extraterritorial corridor and naval base at Tadjourah. This leaves Djibouti an exposed party if Ethiopia presses for sea access.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Searches found no dated item for 15 to 18 November.

Background: Kenya's Ruto runs the Tumaini mediation for South Sudan, and Uganda's Museveni chairs an AU committee on Sudan. Both have strong stakes, and Uganda hosts nearly one million South Sudanese refugees; neither has visibly moved on the postponed vote.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Hapag-Lloyd resumed Suez and Red Sea transits on 16 November and Maersk is adding three services. Suez revenue in October was the highest since attacks began in late 2023.

One tracker claims the Houthis are pushing toward Aden after taking the Bab el-Mandeb heights (unconfirmed). Its last logged ship attack was on 4 October.

Diaspora groups protested at the US State Department on 17 November over the Ethiopian conflicts and alleged UAE involvement. Five Democratic senators wrote to Rubio on 10 November warning of a multi-front war.

Washington lifted sanctions on Eritrea and is trying to balance Asmara and Addis, but critics say it lacks a Horn strategy.

Ethiopia reportedly received 12 Russian Su-35 jets and plans four S-400 batteries (date unclear, unconfirmed).

Egypt and Sudan restated opposition to Ethiopia's unilateral management of the GERD on 2 November, and Ethiopia accuses Cairo of backing Tigrayan rebels.

10. Humanitarian situation.

WFP's new chief warned funding cuts are deepening Sudan's hunger crisis after a first field visit. WFP reached more than four million people, including 880,000 in famine-risk areas as of August, before scaling back.

UNHCR counts 11.35 million forcibly displaced in the Sudan situation (updated 9 November).

Sudan's 2026 appeal has received about 1.1 dollarsbillion of 2.9 dollarsbillion needed (about 40 percent), with cholera in all 18 states per WHO.

Tigray's health system is being pushed toward collapse again, and aid convoys and IDP camps have reportedly been hit by drones.

South Sudan: Nasir-Ulang clashes displaced more than 35,000; February's UN count was nearly 280,000 fleeing.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

The birr trades near 160 per dollar, with the central bank's 27 October auction at 159.70. Inflation was 14.9 percent in September and the policy rate was held at 16 percent on 3 November.

The IMF projects Ethiopian growth of 9.2 percent in 2026, slowing to 7.8 percent in 2027. War spending and fuel prices threaten that path.

Djibouti remains Ethiopia's main gateway; Djibouti, Ethiopia, South Sudan and Uganda created the DESSU Corridor Authority in March, and a rail link to Doraleh was targeted for November 2026 (completion unconfirmed).

Six crew reached Berbera on 17 November after a boat from Djibouti sank near Bab al-Mandeb, with two dead.

GERD: no new flood reports; the dispute is diplomatic.

What to watch.

Badme and Zalambessa fronts, days ahead: any Eritrean or Ethiopian statement on Assab, further drone strikes near Adigrat, or an AU/IGAD or G7 mediation move.

Al-Nahud, this week: whether the army takes the town and whether the RSF counterattacks in West Kordofan; watch for confirmation of the Abu Qalb toll.

Security Council readout on AUSSOM, this week, and the December renewal vote on UNSOS/AUSSOM, with the US pushing a 12-month drawdown.

Somalia's election dispute: whether the Somali Future Council, Puntland and Jubaland join any talks before the 15 April 2027 parliamentary date.

South Sudan's Security Council consultations later this month, and whether Machar's treason case moves before the 11 February 2027 vote.

Ethiopia's Tigray front: further drone-strike tolls and any sign Fano and TPLF coordinate (unconfirmed so far).
