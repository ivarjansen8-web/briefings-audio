Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

Somalia's election standoff, Ethiopia-Eritrea Badme clashes and South Sudan's delayed vote.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 17 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia's election standoff deepened on 14 November, when government-called talks at Villa Somalia failed to open because the opposition boycotted. The Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that the president's mandate expired on 15 May and ordered an election framework within 30 days, so the clock runs to early December.

Ethiopian and Eritrean forces are reportedly trading drone and artillery fire near Badme, with residents fleeing. Diplomatic sources told Addis Standard that Ethiopia used drones and Eritrea heavy artillery; neither army controlled the town as of 13 November, per ACLED. It is the most dangerous interstate flashpoint in the region.

South Sudan postponed its first national elections from 22 December to 11 February 2027. The date was moved on the day voter registration was due to start, and the UN has warned that voting under current conditions could trigger renewed fighting.

A drone strike on Abu Qalb in West Kordofan killed at least 15 civilians, four of them children, according to the Kordofan Human Rights Observatory. Dabanga could not verify the toll; the RSF-aligned Tasees alliance said nine died and blamed the army.

2. Sudan.

The Quintet (AU, IGAD, Arab League, EU, UN) on 13 November condemned drone attacks across eight Sudanese states and urged backing for the Quad's push for a humanitarian truce. It set no date for talks, and the Quad (US, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE) has produced no truce since its September 2025 roadmap.

Army airstrikes have hit RSF-held areas near El Khuwei and En Nahud in West Kordofan for about a week, according to activists. They report residents fleeing south from Abu Zabad, which points to a looming humanitarian emergency in the area.

The army is sending reinforcements to the Blue Nile front and replaced its regional commander with Maj. Gen. Mohamed Osman Hamad. The army says it repelled RSF and SPLM-North attacks on 2 November, while CFR logged an RSF capture of the Sali base on 3 November, so the front is contested.

A rights lawyer cited at least 360 civilians killed by drones in under three weeks. The figure is hers and Dabanga did not verify it; the strikes have also hit power infrastructure.

Sudan's foreign ministry accused Ethiopia of hosting RSF drone launch sites, and Ethiopia and Sudan accuse each other of backing armed fighters. These are government claims, not independently verified.

3. South Sudan.

The electoral commission moved the vote to 11 February 2027, with results expected on 15 February. The delay leaves President Kiir ruling by decree in a caretaker administration after he dissolved the transitional government in September.

Riek Machar, sacked as vice president, remains charged with treason and under house arrest. Analysts say this undermines the 2018 peace deal; one Arab Weekly commentary calls the elections effectively cancelled, which is opinion rather than the common reporting.

Fighting between the army and SPLA-IO in Jonglei and Upper Nile has run since late December 2025. The latest OCHA figures I found (March) counted over 267,000 displaced; I found no fresh battlefield reporting for this week.

4. Ethiopia.

Army chief Field Marshal Birhanu Jula on 14 November accused Eritrea of directing a new Tigrayan offensive in Amhara and Afar with Egyptian supplies. He also named Fano and the OLA in the alleged coalition. Asmara and Cairo have denied such claims, and these are government allegations.

Federal forces and allied Tigrayan fighters retook Mekelle in early October, and the TPLF says it will keep fighting from the mountains. The conflict has shifted to a rural insurgency, with clashes reported near Aksum on 3 November.

A TPLF-Fano alliance is emerging between former enemies, per analysts. Federal forces are now fighting in Tigray, Amhara and Oromia at once.

Analysts of the renewed war describe it as a wider sovereignty contest. Read the Second Tigray War analysis for how the political reality differs from 2020.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea's information ministry said it was preparing to stop a possible conflict and win it, and accused Addis Ababa of declaring an invasion. Ethiopia told the UN Security Council on 9 October it was taking self-defence measures against Eritrean deployments.

G7 foreign ministers urged maximum restraint, and Western governments fear a fight over Assab. An analyst called any move toward Assab "the point of no return".

Reports of Ethiopian drone strikes inside Eritrea remain unconfirmed (unconfirmed).

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Somali Future Council told the Quartet (US, UK, EU, Turkey) it would not attend talks on Villa Somalia's terms, and demands a credible mediator and a freeze on election activity. The government is deploying extra forces while opposition figures appeal to clan militias; ACLED warns of renewed conflict.

UN experts on 12 November condemned the dismissal of Somalia's chief justice and urged reinstatement. It bears directly on the court ruling that the mandate has lapsed.

Al-Shabaab reportedly seized Comaad in Hiiraan without resistance and is advancing on Mataban (APA); another outlet says government troops withdrew from Omad (unconfirmed). No casualty figures have been released.

Somali forces say they killed an al-Shabaab financier in Bakool, and partners struck weapons depots in Lower Shabelle on 16 November. Casualties from the airstrikes are undisclosed.

Sen. Ted Cruz renewed calls on 15 November for the US to move toward recognising Somaliland, tying it to an ambassador nomination. Israel remains the only recognising state, and reportedly told Hargeisa it will keep recognition whoever governs.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest items I found are from October, including a capsizing that killed 139 migrants off the coast.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Sudan lifted its restrictions on Kenyan tea on 11 November, ending a dispute over Nairobi hosting RSF events. Tea worth over Ksh3.1 billion had been caught; Ruto denies backing the RSF.

Aid agencies are struggling to serve more than 50,000 Ethiopian refugees in Kenya. It shows spillover from Ethiopia's wars.

Uganda: Quiet. I found no reporting on its regional role this week.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Rival blocs are hardening: Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Turkey back the army and Eritrea, while the UAE, and less openly Israel, are linked to Ethiopia and the RSF. Officials deny the UAE-RSF link; this is analysts' framing.

Riyadh's Red Sea maritime coalition, with Egypt, Djibouti, Sudan and Somalia, excludes the UAE and Ethiopia.

Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd reportedly resume Suez-Red Sea sailings (unconfirmed, single Substack source). Houthi bans on Israeli and Saudi-linked ships stand, and Suez traffic is reportedly about 60% below pre-crisis.

Egypt told the UN Security Council on 16 October it would coordinate between the Quad and Quintet on Sudan.

10. Humanitarian situation.

OCHA estimates 33.7 million people in Sudan need aid (secondary report of an October estimate). UNHCR counts about 6.4 million IDPs and 11.35 million forcibly displaced.

The WFP chief says aid is the "only hope" for millions as funding cuts deepen hunger. An IOM official reportedly said warehouses are nearly empty and aid reaches about one in ten in need (secondary).

IOM reported 1,515 newly displaced in North Darfur and Blue Nile, including about 5,035 fleeing Um Baru villages on 10 to 11 November (as cited).

OCHA says 71 aid workers were killed in Sudan in 2025.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's rebased accounts put 2025/26 output at 31.86 trillion birr (about 210.7 billion dollars), though the market rate was about 160.8 birr per dollar on 12 November. The two exchange rates give very different dollar figures.

The central bank held its rate at 16% on 3 November, and the IMF brought forward about 200 million dollars to cover dearer fuel.

Egypt and Sudan invoked the 1959 Nile agreement at "2+2" talks in early November; Ethiopia's water minister said on 5 November Ethiopia will not seek permission. No date is set to resume Nile talks.

Dangote is reported to be investing 660 million dollars in a fuel pipeline from Djibouti to Dewele (October).

What to watch.

Coming days: whether Badme clashes widen toward Assab, and any UN Security Council session on Ethiopia-Eritrea.

Coming days: the army's West Kordofan airstrikes and Blue Nile reinforcements; any Quad announcement of a humanitarian truce.

Late November: South Sudan voter registration and the reaction of the SPLA-IO to the 11 February date.

Ongoing: Houthi shipping restrictions and whether major carriers actually return to Suez.
