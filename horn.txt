Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Somalia's court says president's term expired; US senators press Rubio on Ethiopia-Eritrea; Sudan drone war spreads.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 12 November 2026 (the last 72 hours were thinly covered, so some items are slightly older and flagged). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term has expired and ordered elections within 90 days. The court said parliament's mandate ended on 14 April and the president's on 15 May, a day after he dismissed the chief justice; the presidency says the amendments let him stay until May 2027, so the ruling may go unenforced.

US Democratic senators sent Secretary of State Rubio a second letter urging action to avert a wider Ethiopia-Eritrea war. They criticised the lapse of the Ethiopia sanctions programme and the lifting of an arms-export ban, and argued Washington should oppose any forcible Ethiopian grab of Assab. This signals congressional pressure on an administration that has courted Addis Ababa.

An RSF drone strike on a Blue Nile village killed nine civilians on 10 November, per local officials. Sudan says such drones launch from Ethiopian soil, which Ethiopia denies, so the Sudanese war is fusing with the Ethiopia-Eritrea-Tigray crisis.

Machar's treason trial reached its 128th session, with Machar alleging an assassination plot against defendants. The vote is set for 22 December, with the transitional government dissolved since September, so the trial and the election are now one political problem.

2. Sudan.

The army and allied forces pushed against the RSF in West Kordofan in the past week, per Sudan Tribune. This is the army's side of the account; the RSF has not confirmed losses (unconfirmed).

RSF drones hit 14 power stations across Sudan in about two weeks, according to Sudan Tribune, and the cabinet ordered "maximum alert". Attacks on grid infrastructure deepen the humanitarian damage far from the front lines.

Sudan's foreign ministry told the UN Security Council and the AU on 1 November that Ethiopia lets the RSF launch drones from its territory, claiming 70 attacks and 187 drones between 15 February and 27 October. These figures are Sudan's own and unverified; both countries have reportedly expelled diplomats.

UN envoy Pekka Haavisto said on 2 November the UN expects talks with the RSF and allies before Christmas, while Burhan has repeatedly rejected ceasefire proposals. The Quad roadmap remains a proposal, not an agreement, with the army demanding RSF withdrawal first.

UN investigators warn El Obeid, the North Kordofan capital with roughly 500,000 civilians at risk, could become "another El Fasher" (older than 72 hours). Drone strikes and arbitrary detentions by army security cells are both reported.

3. South Sudan.

Machar told the Special Court on 11 November of a plot to assassinate him, co-accused and lawyers, calling the trial environment "toxic". Details of his urgent security complaint were not disclosed; he remains under house arrest and the court has sat 128 times.

Prosecutors allege SPLA-IO forces allied with the White Army killed 257 soldiers at Nasir in March 2025. Defendants deny the charges.

A UN briefing on 10 November counted 42 days to the 22 December election and said clashes in Jonglei still threaten civilians. The elections commission says it needs 250 dollarsmillion and faces severe shortfalls; Kiir dissolved the unity government and all five vice-presidents on 22 September.

AU envoy Kikwete warns poorly managed polls could trigger a fresh crisis and risk unravelling the 2018 deal.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces and allies took Mekelle in October, and Addis Ababa installed a six-month caretaker administration for Tigray with elections promised. The TPLF calls it illegitimate and accuses cooperators of treason, so a political settlement looks distant.

Renewed fighting in Tigray has pushed the health system toward collapse, with Mekelle hospitals overwhelmed within days. This follows fighting that resumed on 23 September.

At least 60, and by one count 94, people were killed in three attacks in 16 days on Orthodox Christians in Arsi, Oromia, the latest on 5 November. The rights commission blamed the OLA for 41 of 48 deaths on 24 October; no one claimed 5 November, and Oromia arrested 73 people.

Heavy fighting with drones between the army and Fano was reported at Wuchale, Amhara, around 3 November (unconfirmed, aggregator).

Abiy joined ministers in military drills on 31 October after announcing delivery of 12 Russian Su-35s and plans for S-400 batteries. The signal is militarisation of civilian leadership.

5. Eritrea.

Ethiopia closed its Asmara embassy and expelled ten Eritrean diplomats on 1 October, and Eritrea cut all ties the same day (older than 72 hours). It reverses the 2018 peace deal.

Abiy warned Eritrea on 20 October of "extremely severe" consequences if it keeps rejecting Ethiopia's peace calls.

Reports of an Eritrean incursion on the Assab-Bure front rest on Ethiopian state-linked sourcing (unconfirmed).

Isaias reportedly travelled to Egypt for security talks (unconfirmed, single source).

Crisis Group warns a war would spread to the Red Sea.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Somalia's Supreme Court ordered leaders to agree an electoral framework within 30 days and hold elections within 90. Troops were reported deployed around the court and security forces reportedly pursued the bench (unconfirmed).

The opposition Future Council backed the ruling, and Puntland, Jubaland and the opposition have threatened a parallel government. The UN political mission has closed with no replacement mediator, leaving no neutral broker.

A senior NISA Duufaan commander defected to al-Shabaab with laptops and files, per Somali Guardian on 8 November (unconfirmed). An analyst warns the intelligence agency may be compromised.

Israel's first ambassador to Somaliland presented credentials in Hargeisa and a cornerstone was laid for an embassy; Somalia and the Arab League reject it. No UN member besides Israel recognises Somaliland.

Somaliland pitched Berbera as a Red Sea security partner at a Washington conference on 7 to 9 November.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: more than 95% of Ethiopian imports transit Djibouti, so an Ethiopia-Eritrea war would hit it first.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Sudan's army chief of staff Yasser al-Atta alleged on 1 November that the RSF recruited "tens of thousands" of mercenaries from South Sudan. No evidence published; South Sudan had not responded (unconfirmed).

Ruto denies backing the RSF and says Kenya works with the Quad (May statement; background). Port Sudan's suspicion of Nairobi persists.

Uganda: nothing found in the last 72 hours.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Russia hosted the third Russia-Africa Summit, and Burhan met Putin on 30 October. Sudan offered a Red Sea free economic zone; the Port Sudan naval base is still unratified.

Suez Canal revenue hit 724 dollarsmillion, the highest monthly level since 2023, with traffic near two-thirds of pre-crisis levels. The Houthis have shifted toward Saudi Arabia, though Bab al-Mandab risk remains.

Rubio urged restraint on Ethiopia-Eritrea; the G7 warned of regional escalation.

Turkey, Gulf states and China: nothing found in the last 72 hours.

10. Humanitarian situation.

Renewed Tigray fighting is overwhelming health services.

RSF and allied militias reportedly killed more than 800 people over several days in Ardamata, west Darfur, in early November, per one account (unconfirmed).

About 50,000 people have fled RSF attacks in Blue Nile, per Sudanese officials (unverified).

The 2026 Sudan appeal was about 40% funded in July, with cholera concentrated in Kordofan (older figures).

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's central bank held its rate at 16% on 3 November while the IMF advanced about 200 dollarsmillion for fuel imports.

Egypt and Sudan invoked colonial-era Nile agreements and plan a joint committee on alleged GERD damage; Ethiopia set up a counter-committee. Talks are stalled.

Sudan's grid attacks continue to hit power supply in northern Sudan.

Suez recovery eases the Red Sea shipping squeeze.

What to watch.

Ethiopia-Eritrea, coming days: any State Department reply to the senators' letter and Assab-front reports.

Sudan, before Christmas: the UN's planned talks with the RSF; watch for Burhan's response.

Sudan-Ethiopia, coming days: UN Security Council or AU follow-up to Khartoum's drone complaint.

South Sudan, 22 December: election date; watch Machar trial sessions and funding.

Tigray, coming weeks: TPLF response to the caretaker administration.
