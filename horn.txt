Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Tuesday, 10 November.

Ethiopia's northern war and Eritrea standoff deepen as El Obeid front stays contested.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 10 November 2026 (a few late-October items are flagged as context). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Ethiopia's war in the north is now in its seventh week, with fighting around the A2 highway and a curfew in Mekelle. Critical Threats reports federal forces killed TDF commander Col. Semere Teklu and that the army and Fano are still clashing between Dessie and Woldia; this is the largest interstate-risk crisis in the region.

Ethiopia and Eritrea are in open confrontation after severing diplomatic ties in early October. Addis Ababa accuses Asmara of backing the TPLF and says Eritrean troops advanced up to 60 km into Tigray, while Eritrea denies this and asked the UN Security Council to act.

Sudan's army and the RSF are trading conflicting claims west of El Obeid. The army-allied joint force says it took four areas (Umm Samima, Kazqil, Al-Hamadi, Abu Qud), the RSF released videos claiming it still holds them, and neither side has confirmed.

Israel's first ambassador to Somaliland presented credentials, and Somalia calls it "null and void". The Arab League has joined Mogadishu's objection, which keeps the recognition question and the Red Sea rivalry at the centre of Gulf and Israeli policy in the Horn.

The UN says Sudan relief stocks are running dangerously low. Agencies cite 15.4 million people needing shelter and IOM's emergency warehouses empty (UN figures, via a single outlet).

2. Sudan.

Fierce clashes west and southwest of El Obeid (North Kordofan) left control of four localities disputed on 6 November. Both sides claim them and no official confirmation exists; the road to El Obeid decides whether the country drifts toward an east-west partition.

The army says it downed its fourth drone in a week while advancing in West Kordofan (unconfirmed). The claim comes from a single analytic digest, but it fits the drone-centred character of the war now.

Critical Threats has logged 21 sets of RSF strikes on fuel facilities or power stations in cities including Khartoum and Omdurman. Its tally covers drone campaigns from October 2024 to October 2026; infrastructure strikes are driving blackouts and displacement.

Port Sudan accuses Ethiopia of providing drone safe havens to the RSF, and the two states have retaliated diplomatically. This links the Sudan war directly to the Ethiopia-Egypt-Eritrea alignment.

A drone strike on a Nyala market (26 October) killed more than 100 according to the Emergency Lawyers group, which blames the army. The toll is disputed (one outlet reported at least 20) and the army has not commented on the market strike itself; it is context from two weeks ago.

No truce is in force and I found no direct talks. The AU and US co-chaired a first Quad-Quintet meeting in September, but the Quad's three-month humanitarian truce plan has not been implemented.

3. South Sudan.

Riek Machar's treason trial continues in Juba, with proceedings adjourned to 11 November for further testimony. He told the Special Court that he took steps to prevent violence in Nasir and Ulang and called the case a political trial.

The 22 December election is still officially on, but its feasibility is widely doubted. Juba told the UN it is time for "ballots, not bullets", while University of Juba academics say voter registration and security are incomplete.

An opposition alliance warns the dialogue could be used to postpone the vote or hold only the presidential race. Its chair, who is also a candidate, says he sees "indicators" of this; no official postponement has been announced.

Machar's eligibility is unresolved while he is on trial and the candidate lists are unpublished.

The US placed South Sudan, Somalia, Uganda and the DRC at Level 4 (do not travel) on 9 November.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces entered Mekelle on 3 October and set up a caretaker administration, with Abiy pledging Tigray elections within six months (single-source, unconfirmed). The TPLF calls it illegitimate and its commander vowed on 22 October to keep fighting.

Crisis Group dates the large-scale hostilities to 23 September, pitting the TPLF and Fano against the federal government. The TPLF and six groups announced an "Alliance for Survival" on 20 September, per Reuters as cited by Critical Threats.

Drone strikes on the Jari 2 displacement camp in Amhara killed at least 15 (Reuters sources) to 16 (Addis Standard) around 29 to 30 October. Nobody has been identified as responsible; Fano members were reportedly nearby.

WHO warns that violence is pushing northern Ethiopia's health system towards collapse.

Oromia and the general-election timetable: Quiet in my results. I found nothing dated in the last 72 hours.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea cut diplomatic ties on 1 October after Ethiopia expelled ten Eritrean diplomats and closed its Asmara embassy.

Eritrea's Information Ministry updated a list of Ethiopian officials' war statements on 3 November. It argues they show a plan to take sea access by force; Ethiopia's deputy army chief reportedly said the Tigray offensive "will not stop there" and that Assab is the destination. These are partisan, reported claims.

Critical Threats judges Eritrea is not actively pushing for full-scale war. That reading rests on the scale and duration of clashes, and it is an analyst's view only.

An Atlantic Council analysis argues an Assab offensive would strip troops from Amhara and Oromia. That is a main brake on escalation.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

A senior commander of NISA's Duufaan unit has defected to al-Shabaab, raising fears of an intelligence breach (single-source, unconfirmed). A Sahan Research analyst warned the agency may be severely compromised.

Heavy fighting near Baraawe followed the withdrawal of Ugandan troops, and al-Shabaab is reported moving in Lower Shabelle (unconfirmed, Kaab TV). This matters because the drawdown of foreign forces is the group's main opening.

New America counts 28 announced AFRICOM strikes on al-Shabaab between 6 May and 6 November and none on ISIS-Somalia.

Somalia's federal election row remains unresolved. The electoral commission set parliamentary polls for 15 April 2027 and the presidential vote for 15 May, but Puntland says the president's term expired in May 2026 and Jubaland has declared itself independent of the federal system; Hassan Sheikh has invited opposition and state leaders to talks this month.

Israel's ambassador Michael Lotem presented credentials in Hargeisa and a cornerstone was laid for an Israeli embassy. Somalia plans its own embassy in Hargeisa; Israel remains the only UN member to recognise Somaliland (since 26 December 2025).

Somaliland's US representative pitched Berbera's role in Red Sea security at a Washington conference on 9 November.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest item found is Guelleh's September meeting with Macron on Red Sea security.

Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April with about 98 percent. Djibouti's port-dependent economy and its role as Ethiopia's main outlet make any Assab move a direct concern.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Uganda's troop withdrawal from the Baraawe area is cited as a trigger for renewed fighting in Somalia (unconfirmed, Kaab TV).

Hassan Sheikh travelled to Uganda in late October amid the election crisis. Garowe Online questions what Kampala's role is.

Uganda is trialling an Ebola vaccine as the region tries to contain an outbreak (single aggregator, unconfirmed).

Kenya: no mediation on Ethiopia-Eritrea found. Ruto's earlier Sudan mediation record is a handicap, and Eritrea has reportedly left IGAD, shrinking channels for dialogue.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Analysts describe two loose blocs: a UAE-Israel-leaning axis backing Addis, and Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Turkey aligned toward Asmara, Mogadishu and Port Sudan. The framing is analysis, not a formal alliance.

Mogadishu reportedly cancelled about 800 dollarsmillion of UAE port and security deals (Berbera, Bosaso, Jubaland) in January. Saxafi (unconfirmed, secondary source)

The third Russia-Africa summit did not mark a major shift, per Critical Threats. Russia concentrates on defence, education, media and nuclear energy.

The US State Department urged restraint on Ethiopia and Eritrea. Wikipedia summary (secondary, unconfirmed)

Saudi Arabia shipped three to four million barrels a day via the Red Sea in April-May with Hormuz disrupted. This raises its stake in Red Sea stability.

10. Humanitarian situation.

Sudan's 2026 appeal is about 40 to 48 percent funded depending on source. Dabanga cites roughly 1.1 billion dollars of 2.9 billion dollars, and support reaches only about one in ten in need.

Cholera reached nearly 3,000 cases and 250 deaths across 20 localities in six states by late September, with numbers declining.

Yale's lab found almost no civilian presence in El Fasher a year after its fall.

Fighting has displaced returnees in Tigray again and thousands in Amhara, concentrated in Shire and Debre Birhan.

3.9 million people in Tigray, over half the population, needed emergency food aid in September. The source is the regional disaster commission.

Floods have affected more than 175,000 people in Somalia (single aggregator, unconfirmed).

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan issued a joint statement on 2 November invoking the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement and demanding a binding GERD framework. Ethiopia was not a party and rejects it.

They are setting up a joint committee to assess damage they attribute to the dam, with a view to compensation; Ethiopia formed its own Nile Compensation Committee.

Ethiopia's water minister linked understanding on the Nile to support for its Red Sea access. Kulu Media (single source, unconfirmed)

Port and currency data: Quiet. I found no dated reporting on Djibouti throughput or the birr in the last 72 hours.
