Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Sudan army claims Al-Khuwai; Somalia court voids president's term; Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions alarm G7.

Compiled from sources dated 11 to 14 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Sudan's army and allied Joint Force say they retook Al-Khuwai in West Kordofan on 13 November, a town the RSF seized in May 2025. The Joint Force claims it destroyed 20 RSF vehicles and captured 40; these are one-sided figures and the RSF had not responded. The push targets the En Nahud axis that links the RSF to Darfur.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh's term has expired and ordered elections within 90 days. The presidency attacked the ruling and tried to dismiss the chief justice, a move the court voided. Somalia now has two rival claims to legitimacy and no agreed electoral model.

G7 foreign ministers voiced "great concern" on 9 to 10 November over rising Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions. Diplomatic sources report low-intensity clashes near Badme, though no war has been declared. This is the most dangerous interstate flashpoint in the region.

An SPLM nominee, Lucy Veronica Gordon, was killed in an ambush on 12 November in Mundri East, South Sudan. It comes six weeks before the 22 December general election.

2. Sudan.

The army and Joint Force claim the capture of Al-Khuwai and the Sarif Ankoush area on 13 November. Outlets disagree on whether Sarif Ankoush lies in West or North Kordofan; the RSF was silent. Fighting in Kordofan has escalated since 25 October.

The army says it shot down a drone over Dandaro in Blue Nile state on 13 November. It was the fourth drone the army reported downing in a week. The drone war now reaches beyond Kordofan and Darfur.

UN rights investigators say El Obeid residents live under constant drone threat, and October saw the most drone attacks on civilians of the war. The UN says roughly 30% of El Obeid's hospitals still function.

No truce has been signed. The G7 on 9 to 10 November urged both sides to negotiate via the Quad (US, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt) toward a permanent ceasefire. The 90-day truce proposal remains unaccepted by the army, which objects to the UAE's role.

Sudan has accused Ethiopia of letting the RSF launch drones from its territory (unconfirmed). Addis Ababa makes mirror accusations against Khartoum. The Sudan war is merging with Ethiopia's conflicts.

3. South Sudan.

Gunmen killed SPLM women's-seat nominee Lucy Veronica Gordon and teacher Justin Akim on 12 November in Mundri East. Two others were wounded and several delegates fled into the bush. No motive or suspects were announced.

The election is set for 22 December, the first since independence in 2011. Envoys warned the Security Council on 10 November that the vote risks unravelling the fragile peace.

Riek Machar remains under house arrest on murder and treason charges, and Juba has refused AU envoys access to him. The US called dialogue "untenable" while he is detained. Kiir dissolved the government in September.

Clashes in Upper Nile have displaced more than 35,000 people. The figure comes via Security Council reporting.

4. Ethiopia.

Getachew Reda told UN envoy Guang Cong on 11 November that Eritrea and Sudan back armed groups and drone attacks in Ethiopia. He offered no evidence, and Asmara and Khartoum deny it. Both reportedly agreed dialogue beats confrontation.

War in Tigray resumed on 22 to 23 September; federal forces retook Mekelle on 4 October but fighting continues in rural central, northern and northwestern Tigray. Artillery and drones are in use near Sheraro, and the interim administration imposed a curfew and a weapons-surrender order in Mekelle. The TPLF calls its withdrawal tactical.

The TPLF, Amhara Fano and the OLA have formed an "Alliance for Survival" against Addis Ababa. Fano and the TPLF still dispute Western Tigray and Fano and the OLA have fought each other, so the alliance is fragile.

Prosperity Party holds 438 of 501 seats after the 1 June election, which excluded Tigray. 143 polling stations in Oromia and Amhara did not open.

5. Eritrea.

Ethiopia and Eritrea have had no diplomatic ties since 1 October 2026, and reports speak of artillery fire near Badme. No independent confirmation exists of Ethiopian troops inside Eritrea.

Eritrea has accused Ethiopia at the UN of planning to seize Assab. Asmara's information ministry also published a list of Ethiopian officials' war statements, updated 3 November. Ethiopia's sea-access push is the core grievance.

Analysts warn a war could open a second Red Sea front near Bab el-Mandeb. Freight costs could rise even without attacks on ships.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh's term ended on 15 May and ordered elections within 90 days, limiting the government to caretaker duties. The government is challenging the ruling. Puntland and Jubaland reject the federal electoral framework, and the commission's timetable is 15 April 2027 (parliament) and 15 May (president).

Uganda began withdrawing bilateral troops on 2 November; the roughly 4,500 AUSSOM troops follow in 2027. Sources conflict on the final date (end of 2027 per the UPDF; 1 August 2027 per Somali Guardian). The US said it will not back the December mandate renewal and UNSOS funding was cut 25%.

Somalia says it killed two al-Shabaab commanders in Lower Shabelle (unconfirmed). It is a government claim only. AUSSOM warns the drawdown leaves Lower Shabelle exposed.

A Mudulood clan elder accused Kenya of sheltering Abdiaziz Laftagareen and urged his extradition (unconfirmed). It adds friction to Nairobi-Mogadishu ties.

Israel remains the only state to recognise Somaliland, since 26 December 2025. President Trump said he opposed US recognition, though Senator Cruz and others push for it. I found no new recognition in the last 72 hours.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April with about 98% of the vote, and in September rejected Ethiopia's request for a Tadjourah corridor and naval base while offering ordinary port access. Djibouti handled 96.7% of Ethiopia's seaborne trade in 2025/26.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Ruto welcomed consultations on an EU election observation mission for Kenya's August 2027 election on 12 November. Domestic politics are shaping Nairobi's regional bandwidth.

Uganda is exiting Somalia after about 20 years, with its army saying the mission is complete. Financial pressure and US policy drive the exit more than battlefield success.

Museveni, EAC chair since April, is pushing an East African political federation. The push dates from August and is unlikely to move soon given the region's crises.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

The G7 on 9 to 10 November backed the Quad's Sudan efforts and flagged Horn escalation risks. The UAE sits in the Quad but Sudan's army rejects it.

Houthi threats to shipping have resurfaced, and operators are seeking transit assurances from a Houthi-run office. Suez transits average about 41 ships a day in 2026 against 74 in 2023. Saudi forces reportedly intercepted three Houthi cruise missiles on 11 November.

Egypt and Sudan, after a 2 November meeting in Cairo, warned against operating the GERD without a binding agreement. Egypt's stance is closely tied to its Sudan and Red Sea posture.

Reports on Turkey, China and Russia in the last 72 hours were not found. The EU's Aspides mission got Djibouti basing in July.

10. Humanitarian situation.

A 2026 multi-sector assessment found 68% of Sudanese households have unmet livelihood needs. Displaced households fare worse.

FEWS NET expects drought and conflict to push parts of Tigray, Amhara and Afar into Emergency (IPC 4) in early 2027.

Clashes in South Sudan's Upper Nile have displaced over 35,000.

Sudan's 2026 response plan sought 2.9 dollarsbillion and was only 5.5% funded earlier in the year (older figure).

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's water minister said on 8 November that Egypt should help Ethiopia gain sea access, which could ease the Nile dispute. He also said Ethiopia never sought permission for its water projects. Egypt says talks are at a dead end.

Egypt and Sudan invoked the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement (55.5 and 18.5 bcm). Ethiopia rejects colonial-era arrangements as non-binding.

Djibouti took 96.7% of Ethiopia's 17.6 million tonnes of cargo while Berbera handled 2.7%. Berbera runs at about 30% capacity, so a Red Sea war would hit Ethiopia's trade hard.

What to watch.

Coming days: Somali government and opposition reaction to the Supreme Court's 30-day window for agreeing an electoral model (about 5 December).
