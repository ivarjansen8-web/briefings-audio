Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Monday, 9 November.

Somalia court says president's term expired; Sudan fights over West Kordofan; Ethiopia-Sudan drone rift.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 8 November 2026 (search found nothing dated 9 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term expired on 15 May and ordered elections within 90 days. The government rejects the ruling, which sets up a constitutional standoff while al-Shabaab and clan militias exploit the gap.

Sudan's army says it captured Ayal Bakhit and Umm Marahik in West Kordofan, while the RSF claims it killed more than 600 allied fighters. Both sets of claims are partisan and unverified; the fight is over Al-Khuwei, the link between central Sudan and West Kordofan.

Sudan and Ethiopia shut each other's consulates around 4 November after Khartoum accused Addis Ababa of hosting drone launches. This links the Sudan war to the Ethiopian conflict and the Eritrea standoff.

At least 23 people died in clashes in South Sudan's Ruweng state, six weeks before the 22 December elections. The toll comes from a local information minister.

2. Sudan.

The army and RSF clashed heavily in West Kordofan on 8 November. Fighting resumed last week after the army began operations to retake RSF-held areas of North Kordofan.

The army says it shot down a fourth drone in a week over West Kordofan. Drones are the main escalation tool on both sides.

The army took control of a key area in West Kordofan on 8 November, per Middle East Monitor. No independent confirmation of control. Middle East Monitor, 8 November (unconfirmed)

The RSF reportedly captured the Sali military base in Blue Nile state on 3 November. It would open a second front away from Kordofan. Chimp Reports, early November (unconfirmed)

UN envoy Pekka Haavisto said on 2 November the UN expects talks with the RSF and allies before Christmas, on a transition to civilian rule. He said the RSF and Hemedti have not made clear commitments, and no truce is in force.

Army chief of staff Yassir al-Atta accused the UAE and Israel's Mossad of backing the RSF on 1 November. The UAE denies supplying the RSF, and no public evidence backs the Mossad claim.

3. South Sudan.

At least 23 dead and 17 wounded in northern Ruweng since early October, per a local information minister. He said SPLA-IO fighters entered from Unity state; Machar's forces did not respond to AFP.

Elections are set for 22 December, with voter registration due to open in early November. Analysts say less than two months is too short to prepare a credible vote.

Riek Machar remains detained and on trial for treason, which he denies. His detention is the core reason the 2018 peace deal has broken down.

Kiir dissolved the transitional government in September. Analyst Abraham Awolich says this violates the 2018 agreement.

4. Ethiopia.

Sudan accuses Ethiopia of allowing drone strikes on power infrastructure from Asosa, the Kurmuk-Gizen area and Bahir Dar. Sudan filed complaints with the UN Security Council and AU; Ethiopia denies it and accuses Sudan's army of backing Tigray rebels.

Federal and allied forces retook Mekelle in early October after the TPLF withdrew. The TPLF calls it a tactical retreat and signals guerrilla attacks; the Tigray Peace Force is to take over security.

The WHO says violence in northern Ethiopia is pushing the health system toward collapse (reported 2 November).

Crisis Group describes a multi-pronged offensive by armed groups in Tigray, Amhara and Afar. Fano and the OLA have not held major territory, per CSIS.

Quiet on federal politics: no new reporting in the last 72 hours. Abiy's Prosperity Party won 438 seats in the 1 June election.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea's Information Ministry posted a dossier of Ethiopian war statements, updated 3 November. Ethiopia had not replied by 5 November.

Eritrea asked the UN Security Council in mid-October to condemn Ethiopia's "declaration of war." It cites an Ethiopian general's remark that "our ultimate destination is Assab."

Ethiopia expelled Eritrean diplomats on 1 October, and Eritrea cut relations. Ethiopia alleges Eritrean troops entered Tigray around 7 October; Eritrea denies it, and no independent verification exists. Crisis Group via Al Jazeera, 11 October (unconfirmed)

A war near Assab could open a second Red Sea front and raise shipping costs.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Supreme Court ruled parliament's mandate ended 14 April and the president's 15 May, giving leaders 30 days to agree an electoral framework. The government says the amendments let Mohamud stay until May 2027 and disputes the court's jurisdiction.

Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed a day before the ruling, who then said he no longer felt safe in Mogadishu.

Security Council Report says al-Shabaab is using the political crisis to expand territory. A sanctions renewal vote is due before 30 November, and a US strike hit near Dhooble on 17 October.

Tensions in Beledweyne: armed Macawisley fighters and ex-intelligence chief Sanbaloolshe reportedly entered the city. This is a Hirshabelle election dispute, and the claim of a captured airport is unconfirmed. FTL Somalia (unconfirmed)

Israel's first ambassador, Michael Lotem, presented credentials in Hargeisa around 1 November. Israel recognised Somaliland in December 2025; Somalia, the AU and China object.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April with about 97 percent.

The EU signed a deal in July letting Operation Aspides use Djiboutian soil.

Analysts warn Djibouti's revenue depends on Ethiopian trade, which Addis wants to diversify.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Museveni urged Somali leaders to unite against al-Shabaab and welcomed a Ugandan coffee warehouse deal (28 October). Uganda's troops remain a pillar of the Somalia mission.

Museveni recalled Ssentongo to be foreign affairs permanent secretary. The ministry faces DRC, South Sudan and Somalia files.

Kenya-Sudan tension stems from Ruto hosting a parallel Sudanese government in Nairobi in 2025, which the army called hostile. Older context; nothing new this week.

Jubaland reportedly backs Puntland against Mogadishu, with Kenya a neighbour to the dispute. FTL Somalia (unconfirmed)

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

The foreign ministers of Egypt, Somalia and Djibouti discussed Red Sea security on the sidelines of a summit in Libya (7 to 8 November). Egypt says Red Sea littoral states should bear responsibility for its security.

One analysis frames the Horn as split between Israel and the UAE (Somaliland) and Turkey and Egypt (Somalia). It says Turkey landed F-16s in Mogadishu and Egypt has about 1,100 troops in the AU mission. This is analysis, not official position. Headtopics (unconfirmed)

Turkish Akinci drones reportedly fly against the RSF from Egypt's East Oweinat, per a February NYT report. The UAE is widely reported as the RSF's main supplier and denies it.

The US ending logistical support for AUSSOM beyond December puts the mission at risk (CFR).

Quiet on China and Russia: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

10. Humanitarian situation.

Nearly 900,000 displaced people shelter in Tawila, Darfur, with overstretched services (OCHA, 27 October).

Sudan's 2.9 dollarsbillion response plan is just over 46 percent funded (1.33 dollarsbillion).

WHO says millions in the greater Horn face acute hunger and flags cholera risk (page updated 2 November). I found no current IPC or case counts.

Sudan says Ethiopia-linked drone strikes killed or wounded hundreds, citing 484 civilians since February. The figure is Sudan's government's.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan issued a joint statement on 2 November calling for a binding GERD framework and invoking the 1959 Nile agreement. Ethiopia rejects the 1959 deal and was not a party.

Cairo and Khartoum reportedly agreed to quantify alleged damage from Ethiopia's dam management, opening a compensation track. See News (unconfirmed)

The birr traded at 161.05 per dollar on 3 September, down 12.6 percent in a year. No later rate found; IMF disbursements total about 2.18 dollarsbillion.

About 97 percent of Ethiopia's seaborne cargo goes through Djibouti, and a fuel pipeline launched in September. The IMF flags diversion risk.

What to watch.

By about 5 December: Somalia's 30-day deadline for leaders to agree an electoral framework; watch for opposition and federal member state moves.

Before 30 November: UN Security Council vote on renewing the Somalia 2713 sanctions regime.

Coming days: Al-Khuwei and the West Kordofan front, and whether the RSF follows Sali with more attacks in Blue Nile.

Coming days: Ethiopia's response to Eritrea's UN dossier and Sudan's complaint; any move at Assab or the Tigray border.

Mid-November onward: South Sudan voter registration and Ruweng clashes ahead of the 22 December vote.

Late December: UN talks with the RSF per Haavisto, and the end of US AUSSOM logistics support.
