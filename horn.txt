Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Monday, 23 November.

Ethiopia-Eritrea border clashes, Khartoum drone strikes and Somalia's mandate crisis.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 23 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Drone strikes killed at least four civilians in Khartoum and damaged a UN building with staff inside. The Sudanese military says the drones were launched from Ethiopia and blames the RSF, a claim that would internationalise the war further and that is not independently verified (unconfirmed).

Ethiopia and Eritrea are trading fire along the border near Rama and Badme, and the two have cut diplomatic ties. Ethiopia's army chief accused Eritrea of directing the TPLF-allied offensive with Egyptian support on 14 November; Eritrea and Egypt have rejected such claims.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that the president's mandate expired on 15 May 2026. It set 30 days to agree an election framework and 90 days to hold elections, while talks with the opposition have stalled.

The SAF has pushed west from El Obeid in Kordofan, retaking Al-Khuwai by 13 November. It matters because Kordofan is now the decisive front of the war.

2. Sudan.

Drone attacks hit Khartoum at dawn, killing at least four and injuring at least nine, according to ABC. UN humanitarian coordinator Denise Brown said a UN building was damaged while staff were inside; OCHA called the danger "unacceptable".

The army and allied forces say they took Al-Khuwai in West Kordofan by 13 November and are heading toward Al-Morkib and El-Nuhud. One report claims the RSF still holds 85 to 90% of West Kordofan; both sides' claims are unverified.

Amnesty International says RSF drone strikes killed 37 civilians in El Obeid between June and August 2026. It calls for war-crimes investigations, which adds to the evidence on RSF attacks on cities.

Fighting in Blue Nile has displaced more than 1,400 families from Geissan, Wad al-Mahi and Bau. It shows the war widening to a new southern front.

Diplomacy is stalled: a Geneva consultation ended without a truce in late October, and the Quintet condemned the drone strikes. The UN envoy expects talks with the RSF and allies in late December; the Security Council held closed consultations on 20 November ahead of the sanctions renewal.

3. South Sudan.

At least 24 civilians, including 7 children, were killed in an attack on a grazing area in Warrap State, according to local officials who blamed SPLA-IO fighters. An earlier report gave at least 15 dead; the SPLA-IO attribution is not independently confirmed.

Crisis Group warned the UN Security Council on 21 November that rushed elections risk a "decisive final blow" to the 2018 peace deal. It says Kiir's government has dismantled power-sharing with Machar.

Machar told his Juba trial on 18 November that Kiir ignored his request to delay troop deployment to Nasir. He has been under house arrest since March 2025, and the trial is central to the political crisis.

The elections commission reportedly postponed the vote from 22 December to 11 February 2027, and the SPLM-IO threatens a boycott (unconfirmed, secondary summary).

Juba opens 14 oil blocks to investors ahead of an oil conference on 24 to 25 November. It aims for over 200,000 barrels per day within two years, which matters for a budget starved of revenue.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces and TPLF-allied fighters have clashed repeatedly around Rama since 7 November. The TPLF offensive began on 23 September, and the army says it has pushed the fighters back; Critical Threats tracks the front.

Ethiopia's army chief accused Eritrea of leading the Tigray offensive with Egyptian support. Both deny it, and the US State Department urged restraint.

Fighting between Fano and federal forces continues across Amhara, with weekly trackers logging events in more than 29 districts. Fano alleges drone strikes killed dozens of civilians (unconfirmed, Fano claim).

Abiy called on 7 November for ruling and opposition parties to agree long-term goals. His Prosperity Party won 438 seats in the June vote, held without Tigray, so the call comes from a position of dominance.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea and Ethiopia cut diplomatic ties in October, and fighting returned near Badme in mid-November. Ethiopian artillery reportedly fired toward homes near Badme.

Trackers report widespread Eritrean military mobilisation (unconfirmed). Asmara has not commented, and its state media are silent on the border.

Isaias received credentials from six ambassadors on 19 November. The state-media routine contrasts with the border crisis.

The foreign ministers of Egypt, Sudan and Eritrea met in Aswan on 21 November. They stressed that coastal states should govern Red Sea security and rejected unilateral steps.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Somalia's Supreme Court declared the president's term extension unlawful on 5 November, a day after he dismissed the chief justice. It put the mandate expiry at 15 May 2026 and parliament's at 14 April.

A national forum planned for 14 to 18 November failed to open as the Somali Future Council refused to attend on Villa Somalia's terms. Puntland and Jubaland have cut ties with Mogadishu; the opposition fears a third-term bid.

Al-Shabaab shelled a Puntland camp near Afaroor in the Galgala mountains and ambushed a unit, reported 19 November. It followed a roadside bomb on a convoy and a Puntland announcement of an offensive (unconfirmed, single report).

US forces struck al-Shabaab positions on 17 November, about 40 km northwest of Kismayo. AFRICOM also announced strikes on 13 to 15 November.

Israel's first ambassador presented credentials in Hargeisa on 1 November, and Israeli sources say recognition will survive an Israeli change of government. Mogadishu calls the recognition an act of aggression; no other state has followed.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April, and Djibouti rejected Ethiopia's Tadjourah corridor and naval-base request.

Ethiopia and Djibouti broke ground in September on a 160 dollarsmillion Damerjog petroleum terminal tied to Dangote's 660 dollarsmillion pipeline. It deepens Ethiopia's dependence on Djibouti even as the Ethiopia-Eritrea crisis raises the stakes.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours on their regional security role.

Ruto said on 30 September that Museveni's backing helped advance the 16 dollarsbillion Dangote refinery in Lamu, and he dismissed rift reports over competing refinery plans. It shows the two leaders' competition and cooperation over regional energy.

Museveni chairs the East African Community since March 2026. The EAC's weak security role contrasts with the Horn's crises.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Egypt, Sudan and Eritrea met in Aswan on 21 November and rejected unilateral steps on Red Sea security and water. The bloc is aimed at Ethiopia's Assab ambitions, and Abiy has not ruled out taking the port by force.

An October policy paper says a Riyadh-Abu Dhabi split over Sudan and Yemen has weakened mediation, while Russia and China have blocked a wider UN arms embargo. The UAE denies backing the RSF.

Turkey has ratified the Mecca Defense Agreement, and Saudi Arabia reportedly rolled over a 5.3 dollarsbillion deposit at Egypt's central bank (unconfirmed; neither government confirmed as of 19 November).

Turkey supplies the Sudanese army with Bayraktar TB2 and Akinci drones, according to Georgetown analysts. It shows how external supply sustains the war.

10. Humanitarian situation.

The WFP said on 20 November that more than 19.5 million people in Sudan face acute hunger and over 4 million women and children are acutely malnourished.

UNHCR puts the forcibly displaced from Sudan at 11.4 million as of 16 November. Al Jazeera cites roughly 13 million.

Blue Nile displacement is raising fears of "near-total humanitarian collapse".

OCHA reported in July over 2,000 suspected cholera cases and 170 deaths since May, mostly in Kordofan, and a 2.9 dollarsbillion appeal about 40% funded. The figures are dated and no November update was found.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

The birr has weakened to about 162.7 per dollar (25 September), while the central bank held its rate at 16% on 3 November and inflation eased to 14.6% in October. The IMF programme is worth 3.4 dollarsbillion.

Egypt and Sudan set up a joint committee to document alleged damage from GERD operation, and Ethiopia answered with its own committee. No date is set for resuming Nile talks.

South Sudan's oil conference opens in Juba on 24 November.

Port Sudan, Assab and Djibouti remain the contested outlets. Ethiopia says its access claim is "irreversible".

What to watch.

24 to 25 November: South Sudan Oil and Power conference in Juba; watch for election-timetable statements.

Around 5 December: The 30-day window set by Somalia's Supreme Court for an election framework expires.

Coming days: Further clashes at Rama and Badme, and whether Eritrea or Ethiopia escalates.

Coming days: Whether the SAF reaches Al-Morkib or El-Nuhud, and any new drone strikes on Khartoum.

Late December: Planned UN talks with the RSF and allies; the Security Council sanctions renewal.

Date to be confirmed: Sixth IMF review for Ethiopia; no date announced.
