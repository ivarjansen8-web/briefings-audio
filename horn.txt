Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

China steps up Ethiopia-Eritrea mediation as Sudan army presses in Kordofan and Houthis threaten Saudi ports.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 25 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

China is stepping up diplomacy to stop a new war between Ethiopia and Eritrea over Assab and sea access. Al Jazeera's 24 November analysis says Beijing is working on Egypt, Ethiopia, Eritrea and Djibouti, but that money may not settle disputes over territory, sovereignty and water. Crisis Group says the two neighbours look closer to frontal combat than at any time since 2000.

Sudan's army says it has retaken more than 30 villages and towns in North and West Kordofan since early November. ACLED researcher Ali Mahmoud Ali, quoted by The National, puts the army about 60 km from En Nahud, the RSF's main western base, as of 17 November. A push there would change the balance of the war west of the Nile.

Ethiopia's prime minister has offered to host inclusive Sudan peace talks in Addis Ababa. No date or guest list is set and neither the army nor the RSF has replied. The offer puts Ethiopia in the Quad-Quintet diplomacy even as it faces its own war.

The Houthis threatened on 25 November to hit Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports if Riyadh attacks Hodeidah. The Saudi-led coalition then struck Hodeidah, Saada and Amran, according to a news aggregator (unconfirmed). Escalation next to Bab el-Mandeb raises risks for Horn ports and Red Sea trade.

South Sudan has postponed its first national elections from December to February. Crisis Group has warned the UN Security Council that a rushed vote risks a "decisive final blow" to the 2018 peace deal.

2. Sudan.

Fighting in Kordofan and Blue Nile displaced thousands more people in the days to 23 November. Darfur24 reports more than 51,000 people fled in Blue Nile alone and that the army took Al-Khuwai and advanced toward Abu Zabad. These are single-outlet figures (unconfirmed).

The army and allied Darfur forces have broken the RSF siege of El Obeid, per Al Jazeera. The city had been under partial siege for almost two years and is the key highway hub of North Kordofan.

Amnesty says nine RSF drone strikes on El Obeid between June and August killed 37 civilians and injured 10. It wants them investigated as war crimes; the RSF has denied responsibility and blamed the army without evidence, and Amnesty says it is also examining army drone strikes.

The RSF still holds most of Darfur, including El Fasher, which it took in October 2025, and runs a parallel administration from Nyala. Any army advance in Kordofan therefore does not yet threaten that hold.

Diplomacy remains stalled. The Quintet (AU, IGAD, Arab League, EU, UN) condemned drone strikes and urged a humanitarian truce; UN envoy Pekka Haavisto said on 2 November he expects talks with the RSF and allies before the end of December. One report cites a prediction market at 8% for a ceasefire by 31 December (unconfirmed, a market price rather than reporting).

3. South Sudan.

The electoral commission has moved the first national elections from December to February. The delay follows deadly clashes between President Kiir's forces and followers of detained Riek Machar.

Crisis Group says Kiir's administration dismantled the power-sharing deal. It notes Machar was placed under house arrest in March 2025 and the unity government dissolved in September, so an election now would lack the consensus the 2018 deal assumed.

Machar told a Juba special court on 18 November that Kiir ignored his request to delay troop deployment to Nasir. Machar says the president did not convene the National Security Council; this is the defendant's account in a trial seen as political.

OCHA describes South Sudan's cholera outbreak as the largest on record. It adds pressure on a country where Nasir and Upper Nile have seen aid access blocked for long periods.

4. Ethiopia.

The Alliance for Survival and Amhara Fano leaders asked the UN envoy on 18 November to facilitate inclusive political dialogue. The alliance groups armed movements from Tigray, Amhara and Afar against Addis Ababa; the government has not signalled openness.

Fighting between Fano and joint federal and Oromia forces continued across several Amhara zones in the week of 9 to 15 November. The figures come from an advocacy group's weekly update, not independent verification (unconfirmed).

Federal forces took Mekelle in early October after the TPLF said it had "temporarily moved out". Fighting goes on in central, northern and northwestern Tigray with artillery and drones, and the federal side is advancing alongside the ex-TPLF Tigray Peace Force.

A Tigray administration named a chief coordinator for disputed Western Tigray, reigniting tension with Amhara. The report is a single-source summary (unconfirmed).

Abiy began a new term on 5 October after the Prosperity Party won 438 seats in the June election. Voting did not take place in Tigray and was disrupted by violence in Amhara and Oromia.

5. Eritrea.

On 17 November Eritrea's information minister accused Ethiopia's army chief of making territorial claims and threatening to seize Assab by force. Asmara says its troops remain inside recognised borders, rejecting Addis Ababa's claim that they crossed.

The G7 foreign ministers and five US Democratic senators warned that the Assab dispute could spread into regional war. I found no Ethiopian or Eritrean response in the reporting.

Abiy says sea access is vital to Ethiopia's future, and officials say it needs a sovereign seaboard with a naval base. That demand, not just port access, is what Eritrea reads as a threat.

Troop movements and drone attacks are reported, but I found no confirmed interstate fighting. Treat specific claims as unconfirmed.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Somalia and Uganda's presidents called on 24 November for a stronger offensive against al-Shabaab and sustainable AU mission funding. It comes as troops are due to withdraw by 2027, and Somalia's interior minister claimed more than 285 militants killed and plans to recruit over 20,000 soldiers (government claims).

Somali security sources claim 61 militants were killed near Leego. Nobody has verified this and AFRICOM gave no figures (unconfirmed).

The Soufan Center says al-Shabaab is escalating in Lower Shabelle and encircling Mogadishu. It describes a crisis of political legitimacy in the capital, and notes an AFRICOM strike near Jamaame in early November.

Parliament's speaker accused Kenya, Ethiopia and Uganda of colluding with al-Shabaab. The claim is made without evidence in the report and strains ties with troop contributors.

Opposition boycotted election talks the president called, as reported on 14 November. The Somali Future Council, with Puntland and Jubaland, rejects a process it says prolongs expired mandates; the commission has set parliamentary elections for 15 April 2027 and presidential for 15 May (Substack analysis, unconfirmed).

Somaliland: Israel remains the only state to recognise it, and no new recognition has followed. Israel's ambassador presented credentials in Hargeisa on 1 November; the US says its policy is unchanged.

7. Djibouti.

Djibouti and Madagascar signed three cooperation agreements on 24 November. Guelleh offered port management expertise, training and logistics digitisation, conditional on a joint port committee.

Guelleh received credentials from six new ambassadors, including China's. It shows Djibouti keeping a wide set of partners while hosting foreign bases.

Guelleh won a sixth term in April with about 97 percent, running to 2031. The result gives him a stable platform as Ethiopia's sea-access push raises Djibouti's stakes.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Ruto flew to Oyo on 21 November for a four-party summit on Congo security. It shows Nairobi's mediation role beyond the Horn; I found no outcome.

Uganda's president joined Somalia's in calling for more pressure on al-Shabaab. Uganda is a main AU troop contributor facing the 2027 exit.

Museveni chairs the East African Community since March. Kenya and Uganda are tied by the fuel import route and pipeline stake, with Ruto saying Kenya has no problem with a Ugandan refinery beside Lamu.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Houthis claimed an attack on the Rabigh refinery on 23 November, and Britain sanctioned the movement and four officials. Reports say the Houthis took two districts near Bab el-Mandeb, per six Yemeni sources cited by AFP on 15 November.

The US maritime authority advises ships in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden to stay alert. Traffic through Bab el-Mandeb remains below normal.

Sudan has offered Russia a Red Sea free economic zone as a compromise on a naval base. Reports on the base's status conflict, from frozen to approved, so this is unsettled.

Analysts describe a Gulf split: the UAE with Israel and Ethiopia, against Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Somalia. Turkey works with both Mogadishu and Addis. The framing is analytical and varies by source.

China is acting as a mediator and its base in Djibouti gives it a stake. Beijing walks a "thin line" between partners.

10. Humanitarian situation.

The UN human rights office said on 23 November that drones are now a main cause of civilian deaths in Sudan. It warns disrupted water supply raises cholera risk.

OCHA recorded at least 71 aid workers killed in Sudan in 2025 and over 180 health facilities losing funding. About 2.7 million people are affected.

Sudan's appeal was about 41 percent funded in September, with 1.2 dollarsbillion of nearly 2.9 dollarsbillion. Cholera, mostly in Kordofan, passed 2,000 suspected cases by July.

Displacement keeps growing in Kordofan and Blue Nile, with over 51,000 reported in Blue Nile. That is a single-outlet figure (unconfirmed).

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's minister told Egypt on 24 November to modernise irrigation and consult before Nile projects. He pointed to the 2015 Declaration of Principles as a model.

Egypt and Sudan, after Cairo talks on 2 November, warned that running the dam without a binding deal threatens downstream water security. They cited the 1959 agreement that excluded Ethiopia.

Ethiopia's inflation was 14.9 percent in September and the central bank held its rate at 16 percent on 3 November. The birr cleared near 159.70 per dollar at the 27 October auction.

The IMF sees Ethiopian growth slowing to 7.8 percent in 2027 from 9.2 percent in 2026. It projects inflation rising.

What to watch.

Any Ethiopian or Eritrean reply to the G7 warning and China's mediation, in the coming days. Border incidents near Badme or Assab would signal escalation.

Whether the army reaches En Nahud in West Kordofan. Watch for RSF counterattacks and drone strikes on El Obeid.

Sudan's response to Abiy's offer to host talks, and UN envoy Haavisto's planned contacts with the RSF before the end of December.

A Saudi-Houthi exchange around Hodeidah and Jeddah, and any shipping line changes through Bab el-Mandeb.

South Sudan's February election timetable, with further UN Security Council discussion and the Machar trial.

Somalia's election talks, with the opposition boycott and the 15 April and 15 May 2027 dates.
