Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Ethiopia blames Eritrea for Tigray offensive; SAF breaks El Obeid siege; South Sudan delays vote to February.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Ethiopia's army chief, Field Marshal Birhanu Jula, accused Eritrea on 14 November of directing a new Tigrayan offensive, with Egyptian weapons and logistics. He said federal forces repelled the attack and that the attacking commanders fled to Eritrea; these are one-sided government claims, and Eritrea and Egypt have rejected earlier accusations of this kind.

The Sudanese army's Kordofan offensive has reportedly broken the RSF siege of El Obeid. Al Jazeera's analysis says the gains open routes toward South Kordofan and North Darfur, which could shift the balance of the war.

South Sudan's electoral commission postponed the first national elections to 11 February 2027. It is at least the third delay, and it came on the day voter registration was due to start.

Somalia's election talks failed to open on 14 November because the main opposition alliance boycotted them. The boycott follows a Supreme Court ruling that the president's term extension is unlawful.

2. Sudan.

The army and allied forces say they have taken Umm Sumayma and are advancing toward Al-Nuhud in West Kordofan. Sudans Post reports the operation began on 5 November; Al-Nuhud is an RSF stronghold, so its fate would show whether the RSF's Kordofan line is collapsing.

RSF fighters reportedly killed at least 10 civilians in West Kordofan, according to a Sudanese relief group. A communications blackout hinders verification (unconfirmed).

The death toll from an RSF drone strike on displaced people at Bani Shako, Kurmuk county (Blue Nile), has risen to 16, according to the county governor. On 14 November the army also said it shot down a long-range drone over Dandaro that it alleged came from Ethiopia; the RSF has not commented.

Sudan Tribune reports that RSF drones have hit 14 power stations over two weeks, and the cabinet ordered maximum alert. The attribution to the RSF comes from the government and local sources.

Peace diplomacy is stalled: the G7 on 12 November backed the Quad (US, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE) and urged both sides to negotiate. UN envoy Pekka Haavisto expects talks with the RSF and allies in late December, while army chief Burhan rejects the Quad as biased toward the UAE.

3. South Sudan.

The election delay to 11 February 2027 came after more than 3,100 registration centres had been set up. The commission gave no clear reason; rights campaigner Malong Deng said "there are no elections in South Sudan".

Riek Machar remains under house arrest and is on trial with seven co-defendants for treason and crimes against humanity, which he denies. Kiir dissolved the unity government in September, which UN Commission chair Yasmin Sooka called a "constitutional coup".

UNMISS head Anita Kiki Gbeho told the Security Council on 10 November the country is at a "critical juncture". The briefing noted rising SSPDF-SPLA-IO fighting in northern Jonglei and more than 35,000 displaced along the Nasir-Ulang corridor in Upper Nile.

The US said on 12 November that the Council must make clear UNMISS needs no permission to carry out its mandate. This points to continuing friction over access.

4. Ethiopia.

Jula alleged an Eritrea-led coalition of the TPLF, Fano, the OLF, Sudan and Egypt. He described a fourth round of TPLF attacks on military positions in Amhara and Afar; none of this is independently verified.

The 2022 Pretoria Agreement is effectively broken: Eritrean troops have reportedly been seen inside northern Ethiopia, and the two states have cut diplomatic ties. Ethiopia wrote to the UN calling the Eritrean action an invasion, while Eritrea's information minister calls it a pretext for aggression.

Fighting between the army and Fano continues in Amhara, with an insurgency in parts of Oromia. ACLED reports Fano attacks on Oromo civilians; a WHO warning cited by Al Jazeera says northern Ethiopia's health system is near collapse.

Sudan keeps accusing Ethiopia of hosting RSF drone launch sites and has complained to the UN. Addis Ababa says Khartoum backs TPLF-linked rebels; Khartoum denies it.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea denies directing any offensive and calls Ethiopian claims a pretext for war. Information Minister Yemane Gebremeskel has said Ethiopia has waged an "unprovoked war of aggression" for over three years; I found no new Eritrean statement dated 14 November.

Asmara rejects Ethiopia's claim to "reclaim" a sea route, saying Ethiopia never owned the ports outside the illegitimate federation and annexation periods. Assab remains the flashpoint.

G7 foreign ministers and five US Democratic senators have warned of a wider Ethiopia-Eritrea war. Egypt, Somalia, Eritrea and Sudan have reportedly agreed that only Red Sea littoral states should govern the sea, a line Ethiopia rejects.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term ended on 15 May 2026 and ordered elections within 90 days. It also gave 30 days to agree an electoral framework; the attorney general's office rejects the ruling.

The opposition Somali Future Council accuses the president of using security forces to seize the Supreme Court after he removed the chief justice. The court declared that dismissal invalid, so there is now a dispute over who leads the judiciary.

The president's 14 to 18 November consultations opened without the opposition, which includes Puntland and Jubaland. The electoral commission's timetable (parliament 15 April, president 15 May 2027) conflicts with the court's deadline.

The Somali army claims it killed several al-Shabaab members in Lower Shabelle on 12 November. No figures or independent confirmation; analysts warn the political split opens security gaps as foreign troops draw down.

Somaliland: Quiet on 14 to 15 November. Israel recognised Somaliland on 26 December 2025, and in August President Irro offered base access at Berbera; I found no confirmed US recognition.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The latest item is President Guelleh touring the new International Conference Centre on 10 November, in his sixth term after the April election.

Djibouti has said Ethiopia's request for a Tadjourah corridor and naval base is off the table. This keeps Djibouti's port monopoly a live issue as the Assab tension grows.

A target of November 2026 was set for linking the Ethio-Djibouti railway to Doraleh and the HDTL terminal. I found no confirmation it was met.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Ruto denied a rift with Museveni over rival refinery projects, after both attended the Dangote refinery groundbreaking in Lamu on 30 September. The competition shows how regional integration and rivalry run together.

Museveni urged regional integration on 13 November; the Kenya Times carries a headline about opposition leader Kalonzo and "Ruto's Sudan deal" (headline only, unconfirmed). I could not verify what the deal is.

Quiet on Somalia and South Sudan diplomacy: I found no Kenyan or Ugandan moves on either in the last 72 hours. Uganda's ruling party has reportedly taken a position on Muhoozi succeeding Museveni (headline only).

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Turkey's Defence Ministry denied on 12 November a report of 15,000 troops going to Somalia. It said its existing task force and air component continue counter-terrorism support under bilateral agreements.

Egypt is preparing a bilateral deployment to Somalia before moving about 1,000 personnel into AUSSOM. No arrival date is announced; Ethiopia has recently withdrawn some troops from Somalia.

Lloyd's List says Red Sea risk remains "substantial" with no recent incidents, while Italy says Houthi attacks have intensified. The sources conflict; the Suez Canal Authority reports October revenue up 94% year on year.

Saudi Arabia is recasting its security priorities around the Houthi threat and the Iran war. It seeks equipment rather than foreign troops (single source).

I found no new China or Russia moves in the Horn in this window: Quiet. The UAE's role is contested in Sudan and at Berbera, where Emirati construction is reportedly meant for US and Israeli use.

10. Humanitarian situation.

WFP's new chief warned on 12 November that funding cuts are deepening Sudan's hunger crisis. WFP helped more than 4 million people in August, including 880,000 in areas at risk of famine, and has had to scale back.

UNHCR counts 11,351,393 forcibly displaced people from the Sudan crisis, as of 9 November. The total includes new arrivals in neighbouring countries.

Sudan's 2026 appeal has received about 1.1 dollarsbillion of 2.9 dollarsbillion needed (around 40%). OCHA also reported over 2,000 suspected cholera cases and 170 deaths since May, mostly in Kordofan, though that figure dates from late July.

Fighting in northern Ethiopia is pushing the health system toward collapse, according to WHO as cited by Al Jazeera. Over 35,000 are displaced in Upper Nile, South Sudan.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

IMF talks on Ethiopia's sixth loan review are set for November, with a board decision expected in early 2027. Around 2.65 dollarsbillion of the 3.4 dollarsbillion programme has been paid out.

Ethiopia's central bank held its key rate at 16% on 3 November, and inflation eased to 14.9% in September. A Middle East war fuel shock pushed inflation back into double digits; the IMF projects growth of 9.2% in 2026.

Egypt says it keeps the right to defend itself under international law if Nile flows are harmed, as Ethiopia revives plans for three more Blue Nile dams. Cairo's stated approach is diplomacy and cooperation with Sudan; this is August-dated context.

Suez traffic is recovering: 1,338 transits in October, according to Lloyd's, and a war near Assab could raise Red Sea shipping costs. Ethiopia remains mostly dependent on Djibouti.

What to watch.

14 to 18 November: the president's consultations in Mogadishu, and whether Puntland, Jubaland and the opposition engage; the court's 30-day framework deadline falls around 5 December.

Next few days: whether the SAF takes Al-Nuhud or moves from El Obeid toward South Kordofan and North Darfur, and whether the RSF answers with more drone strikes.

Now: whether the UN Security Council renewed the UNISFA (Abyei) mandate, which expired on 15 November (not confirmed in my results).

Coming days: any Eritrean response to Jula's 14 November accusations, and any Ethiopian move on Assab or Egyptian reaction.

Late December: the UN's planned talks with the RSF and allies; the Quad truce remains deadlocked.
