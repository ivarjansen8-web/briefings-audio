Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Friday, 27 November.

Abiy warns Eritrea over arming foes as RSF drones hit Blue Nile and Houthis threaten Saudi Red Sea ports.

Compiled from sources dated 24 to 27 November 2026 (a few older items are flagged as context). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Abiy told Eritrea in parliament on 26 November to stop arming Ethiopia's enemies and warned of "destruction" if Asmara persists. He denied planning to invade Eritrea for a coastline and said Red Sea access would be pursued through dialogue, which makes this the sharpest public statement yet in a standoff that Crisis Group says risks open war.

RSF drone attacks killed at least 19 people in Blue Nile state, according to a rights group. The RSF has opened a new front there and is reportedly 65 to 70 km from the state capital Damazin, while the army pushes it back in western Kordofan.

Houthis threatened on 25 November to strike Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports if Saudi forces attack Hodeidah. With Hormuz effectively closed since March, Saudi Arabia leans on Red Sea ports, so the threat raises the stakes for the whole Bab el-Mandeb corridor next to the Horn.

Somalia's election deadlock nears a 5 December Supreme Court deadline for an agreed electoral framework. The opposition Somali Future Council boycotted the president's talks, and al-Shabaab is pressing in Lower Shabelle amid the legitimacy dispute.

2. Sudan.

The RSF killed at least 19 in drone strikes on Blue Nile state overnight on 24 to 25 November, according to a rights group. RSF and allied rebels have taken small towns near the Ethiopian and South Sudanese borders, which stretches the army across several fronts.

Drone attacks on Khartoum and Omdurman on 22 November killed at least four and damaged a UN building with staff inside. The strikes show the RSF can still hit the capital region even as the army holds it.

The army is using El Obeid as a launchpad for a westward offensive in Kordofan, and its fighters are reported to have entered West Kordofan. The front line is still close to the city, though markets are reopening, according to The Africa Report.

Amnesty on 18 November called for war-crimes probes into RSF drone strikes on El Obeid that killed dozens, and said it is also examining army strikes. Both sides now face documented civilian-harm allegations in Kordofan.

A senior RSF commander has reportedly defected to the army and claimed Israeli experts are training RSF officers in Nyala (unconfirmed). The claim comes only from the defector, so treat it as propaganda-prone until corroborated.

Diplomacy is stalled: Burhan insists on defeating the RSF, and the US has said neither general should have a future role. The Quad's three-month truce plan has no takers, and Khartoum rejected Abiy's offer to host talks in Addis Ababa, citing Ethiopia's lack of neutrality.

3. South Sudan.

Riek Machar's treason trial in Juba was adjourned to Monday 30 November, when he is due to resume his defence testimony. He and seven co-defendants face charges including murder, treason and crimes against humanity, and he calls the case political.

Machar testified he sought two helicopters to evacuate 219 soldiers from Nasir, saying they arrived on 6 March 2025. His account is meant to rebut the prosecution's narrative of the Nasir violence, and it is his own version of events.

Elections are nominally set for December 2026, and one analysis says Kiir wants them as early as February 2027. The African Union's C5 committee has called for Machar's release to enable dialogue and a ceasefire, so a credible vote looks remote.

RSF-linked advances near the South Sudan border in Blue Nile add a cross-border security risk. Juba's own stability is already fragile.

4. Ethiopia.

Abiy's 26 November parliamentary statement accused Eritrea of arming groups hostile to Ethiopia and using its territory as a supply route. He said Eritrea rejected offers of electricity at domestic rates and port and road cooperation, and traced the breakdown to Eritrean forces entering Tigray.

Northern Ethiopia is again at war: the TPLF and Amhara Fano are fighting jointly against federal forces, and Abiy announced a six-month caretaker administration for Tigray on 16 October. Federal forces entered Mekelle on 3 October with drone support, per Crisis Group's monthly update summarised by a 16 November analysis.

Amnesty said on 23 November that army shelling of Alamata in September should be investigated as possible war crimes. The army had not responded.

A Fano group alleged on 23 November that weapons for Sudan's RSF pass through Gondar (unconfirmed). The claim ties Ethiopia's internal war to the Sudanese one, but Addis has rejected such allegations.

Abiy was reappointed for five years after the 1 June election, which analysts called among the least competitive since 1991. Addis Standard's licence was revoked before the vote, so domestic reporting is constrained.

5. Eritrea.

Ethiopia's army chief said on 14 November that Eritrea led the Tigray offensive with Egyptian logistical support; Eritrea and Egypt have rejected such accusations. These are Addis's claims and have not been independently verified.

Eritrean troops reportedly crossed near Zalambessa around 7 October, and Addis called it a 60 km incursion. Both governments accused each other of launching war on 9 October, and the two states are not at declared war but ties are cut.

China sent envoys to both capitals; Hu Changchun went to Asmara, and Beijing warned against turning the Horn into a geopolitical battleground. Neither side has publicly accepted AU mediation, and Saudi Arabia reportedly offered to mediate (unconfirmed).

Eritrea's official response to Abiy's 26 November speech was not found in the last 72 hours. Asmara typically replies slowly and through state channels, so watch for a statement.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

A Supreme Court ruling found President Hassan Sheikh's mandate expired in May, and the court set 5 December for an agreed electoral framework; the government disputes the ruling. The electoral commission has set parliamentary elections for 15 April 2027 and the presidential vote for 15 May.

The opposition Somali Future Council boycotted the Villa Somalia talks but said it would join talks mediated by the US, UK, EU and Turkey. Puntland and Jubaland reject direct elections, and Puntland's Deni accused Hassan Sheikh of abandoning consensus.

Al-Shabaab is expanding its offensive in Lower Shabelle and claimed hundreds of soldiers killed in an ambush near Leego on 26 October using car bombs and drones. The claim is the group's own, and the Soufan Center ties the push to Mogadishu's legitimacy crisis.

Israel remains the only state recognising Somaliland, since 26 December 2025, and its first ambassador reportedly presented credentials in Hargeisa in late October. Washington has not recognised it, though a State Department report says it is exploring more engagement.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Egypt signed a deal in late 2025 to develop Doraleh, and China's envoy Liu Xianfa visited Djibouti in early November.

Ethiopia's road link to Djibouti reportedly held through a month of northern fighting (unconfirmed). This keeps Ethiopia's import dependence on Djibouti central to any escalation with Eritrea.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Museveni chairs the East African Community since March and is pushing a political federation, including integrating Somalia and South Sudan.

Ruto and Museveni say they share positions on Sudan, South Sudan and Somalia, but no new joint initiative was found this week. Nairobi and Kampala remain secondary actors compared with the Gulf and Egypt.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

The Houthis told shipping firms to leave Saudi Red Sea ports and threatened Jeddah if Hodeidah is attacked; one report says Bab el-Mandeb traffic fell after strikes on Saudi oil sites (unconfirmed date). Fighting between the Saudi-led coalition and the Houthis has escalated since September.

China is trying to head off an Ethiopia-Eritrea war, with envoys in Addis Ababa, Asmara, Djibouti and Cairo. Analysts say Beijing must balance ties to all four capitals.

Sudan was left off an African seat on the OPCW Executive Council amid scrutiny of alleged army chemical weapons use. The US determined in 2025 that the army used chemical weapons.

Egypt is accused by Addis of backing the Tigray offensive, while Cairo and Khartoum jointly invoked 1959 Nile rights on 2 November. Egypt's Horn strategy now links the Nile, ports and the Sudan war.

No new US action on Sudan sanctions was found; the US pushed a UN arms-embargo resolution in August. Massad Boulos presented the draft to the Security Council.

10. Humanitarian situation.

UNHCR's portal, updated 16 November, counts about 11.37 million forcibly displaced in the Sudan situation. Sudan remains the world's largest displacement crisis.

OCHA figures from July reported more than 2,000 suspected cholera cases and 170 deaths since May, mostly in Kordofan. This is older data, and no November update was found.

Funding is thin: one source puts the 2026 Sudan appeal at about 1.1 dollarsbillion of 2.9 dollarsbillion, roughly 40%; CFR cites a lower figure from another date. Figures conflict.

The EU's 2026 Ethiopia humanitarian funding is 61 eurosmillion, down from 79.4 eurosmillion in 2025, citing fears of renewed northern conflict. Somalia and South Sudan are listed as forgotten crises.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan on 2 November demanded a binding legal framework for the GERD, and Ethiopia's water minister replied on 14 November that the dam benefits both and urged negotiation. Ethiopia has tied Nile talks to support for its Red Sea access.

Ethiopian inflation was 14.9% in September, and the central bank held its policy rate at 16% on 3 November. The bank blames supply shocks after the Middle East conflict and expects inflation to ease by December but stay in double digits.

The birr trades near 160 per dollar, and the IMF projects growth of 9.2% in 2026 and 7.8% in 2027. An IMF programme review date has not been announced.

Saudi reliance on Red Sea ports since the Hormuz closure raises the shipping stakes near the Horn. Sudan's Port Sudan and Eritrea's and Djibouti's ports sit on the same corridor.
