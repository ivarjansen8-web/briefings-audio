Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

Ethiopia-Eritrea clashes near Badme, Sudan army presses Kordofan, South Sudan delays vote.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Ethiopia and Eritrea are trading fire around Badme, and each says the other started it. Ethiopia told the UN Security Council that Eritrea launched an "all-out invasion", while Asmara calls it Ethiopian aggression cloaked in a demand for sea access; this is the most dangerous interstate crisis in the region.

Sudan's army is pushing through North and West Kordofan after breaking the RSF siege of El Obeid. Al Jazeera-cited analysis says it has retaken more than 30 villages and towns since early November and is about 60 km from RSF-held Al Nuhud; the claims are largely army-sourced and contested by pro-RSF media.

South Sudan has postponed its first national elections from 22 December to 11 February 2027. The delay comes with Riek Machar still on trial in Juba and Kiir having dissolved the unity government in September, which leaves the 2018 peace deal largely hollow.

The Houthi-Saudi confrontation is widening along the Red Sea. After a Saudi-led coalition strike on Hodeidah, the Houthis told ships to leave Saudi Red Sea ports; this directly raises risk for the African shore of the Red Sea.

The UN is cutting food aid targets for 2027. WFP says its 13 billion dollars appeal is half funded and it will reach fewer people, with Sudan rations cut up to 70% from January.

2. Sudan.

The army says it has recaptured areas of North Kordofan from the RSF and advanced toward Al Nuhud. ACLED's analyst counts 30+ localities retaken since 1 November, and the army has taken Al Khuway; this would turn the tide in the war's central front, though the figures are army-side claims.

Drone war on civilians continues on both sides. Amnesty says RSF drone strikes on El Obeid killed dozens and should be investigated as war crimes, while Radio Tamazuj reports seven civilians killed in Buram, South Kordofan, by a drone allegedly belonging to the army (unconfirmed attribution).

Sudan's cabinet ordered maximum alert after RSF drones hit power stations in several states including Khartoum. Strikes on infrastructure undercut the government's return to the capital.

Blue Nile and the Ethiopian border are a new front. The army reports shooting down a drone near the Ethiopian border and a rising toll from an RSF strike in Blue Nile; Ethiopia in turn accuses Burhan of backing armed groups inside Ethiopia.

Diplomacy is stalled. The Security Council held closed consultations on Sudan on 20 November and the Quintet (AU, IGAD, Arab League, EU, UN) called on 13 November for de-escalation; no humanitarian truce is signed.

North Darfur clashes at Kernoi killed 3 civilians and injured 15 on 20 November (unconfirmed, single source).

3. South Sudan.

Elections are pushed to 11 February 2027. The electoral commission's delay, from 22 December, reflects the collapse of the transitional arrangements and envoys' warnings that a rushed vote could unravel the peace.

Machar told his Juba court on 18 November that Kiir ignored his request to delay troop deployments to Nasir. He remains under house arrest on murder and treason charges he denies, so the trial doubles as a political forum.

Kiir dissolved the unity government, vice presidents and assemblies in late September. Security Council Report dates it 22 or 23 September; one-man rule now fills the gap.

Upper Nile clashes have displaced more than 35,000 people, per UN reporting, and the Security Council discussed rising violence this month.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces are reported to hold Mekelle after October fighting, with a government-aligned Tigray Peace Force alongside them. Tracker sources claim all major Tigray towns are federal-held (unconfirmed); the TPLF-aligned camp contests this and talks of a second Tigray war.

Clashes and bombardment near Badme on 19 November sent residents fleeing. Drone and artillery fire was reported amid a buildup on both sides (unconfirmed, diplomatic sources).

G7 foreign ministers and US senators have warned of regional escalation. A second senators' letter urges Rubio to act, and a US lawmaker proposed trade incentives to steer both capitals away from war.

Amhara Fano and the Alliance for Survival asked the UN envoy to facilitate inclusive dialogue. The Amhara-Tigray rapprochement is new; Fano claims of captured towns such as Meragna are unverified.

The US Embassy alert of 20 November advises against all travel to Amhara, Tigray and Afar. The Red Cross separately warns of a worsening crisis in Oromia.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea's information minister said on 17 November that its army is deployed inside sovereign territory, rejecting Ethiopian claims of a crossing; Asmara frames Addis's sea-access push as cover for seizing Assab.

Eritrea accused Ethiopia's army chief of territorial claims and says Abiy is "actively destabilising" the Horn. Both statements signal Asmara is preparing the diplomatic ground for war.

Analysts warn a fight toward Assab could open a second Red Sea front and push shipping away even without attacks.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Al-Shabaab has intensified its Lower Shabelle offensive, using suicide car bombs and armed drones. The Soufan Center ties it to a legitimacy crisis in Mogadishu and says the group is trying to encircle the capital.

NISA said on 17 November that two militants were killed in Hiran, and AFRICOM struck positions about 40 km northwest of Kismayo. Both are government-side claims.

The opposition "Future Council" of Puntland, Jubaland and the national opposition met in Nairobi and warned Hassan Sheikh against term extension and election delays. The post is undated in results, so timing is uncertain (unconfirmed).

A Mogadishu-UAE dispute is reported, including an unverified claim that the UAE ambassador was expelled. Abu Dhabi backs Puntland, Jubaland and Somaliland (unconfirmed).

On recognition, Israel remains the only state to recognise Somaliland (since December 2025), and Washington has not followed. Foreign Policy argues the Iran war could be Hargeisa's chance; no new recognition was found this week.

Somaliland jailed a would-be Somali presidential candidate and the journalist who interviewed him (reported 16 November).

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April with about 97 percent, is 79 this month and has named no successor.

Analysts say Ethiopian port diversification and the Ethiopia-Eritrea crisis put Djibouti's main revenue source at risk.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours on their Horn roles. Kenya hosted the Somali opposition Future Council in Nairobi (undated, unconfirmed).

Ruto and Museveni discussed oil infrastructure and Kenyan investment in Uganda's refinery at a Nairobi summit (date uncertain), which signals a deepening bilateral economic axis.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

The Houthis threatened Saudi Red Sea ports and told ships to leave after Hodeidah strikes a day after hitting Riyadh's tallest tower, amid the wider US-Israeli war on Iran. With Hormuz largely closed, Saudi exports lean on Yanbu.

Houthi forces have consolidated Ras Menhali and Perim Island and are pushing toward Aden, which they could use to pressure Bab el-Mandeb.

Rubio and UAE Foreign Minister Abdullah bin Zayed discussed ending the Sudan war on 20 November, per a State Department readout carried by a less established site (unconfirmed).

Turkey is Mogadishu's closest security partner, and a Turkey-Pakistan-Saudi "Mecca" pact still awaits a plenary vote in Turkey's parliament. Saudi Arabia also hosted 14 states in July to form the Multinational Maritime Defence Alliance.

Abiy and Erdogan discussed trade, energy and regional peace in Antalya.

10. Humanitarian situation.

WFP will cut Sudan food rations by 70% from January for communities facing famine, and by 50% for those at risk. It also pledged a month of aid for 30,000 displaced people at El Dabba.

UNHCR lists 11.38 million Sudanese forcibly displaced (updated 16 November).

Hundreds of aid trucks are stranded in Sudan by fuel shortages, UN official Denise Brown said.

South Sudan faces its largest recorded cholera outbreak, per OCHA; Sudan's 2026 appeal was only about 40% funded as of July.

Funding cuts have roughly halved alternative care for unaccompanied refugee children in Ethiopia, and the US reversed an end to UNSOS support for Somalia.

Moyale conflict has displaced nearly 700,000 in southern Ethiopia, per Islamic Relief (unconfirmed, headline only).

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan said on 2 November that their water security is "indivisible" and urged Ethiopia to return to binding GERD talks, invoking the 1959 agreement Ethiopia rejects.

Ethiopia is preparing three more Nile hydropower projects (August report), which will sharpen the dispute.

Djibouti handles about 96.7% of Ethiopia's seaborne trade while Berbera takes under 3%. Berbera sits near 30% capacity, so war around Assab or Bab el-Mandeb would hit a very concentrated corridor.

Ethiopian export earnings reportedly rose to 5 billion dollars in H1 2026 from 3.3 billion dollars (unconfirmed, secondary press release).

What to watch.

Badme front, next days: whether UN Security Council or G7 statements lead to Ethiopia-Eritrea de-escalation or further strikes.

Al Nuhud, West Kordofan: whether the army reaches the RSF-held city in the coming week.

South Sudan, ahead of 11 February 2027: Machar court hearings and any new SSPDF deployments to Upper Nile.

Hodeidah and Saudi Red Sea ports: whether Houthi threats against Saudi ports lead to attacks on shipping.

Mogadishu: al-Shabaab pressure in Lower Shabelle and any formal rupture with the UAE.

January 2027: WFP ration cuts in Sudan take effect; watch for donor reversals in December.
