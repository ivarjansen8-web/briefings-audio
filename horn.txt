Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Somalia expels UAE ambassador as Ethiopia-Eritrea border tensions rise and South Sudan delays vote.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 22 November 2026 (mostly 17 to 21 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia ordered the UAE ambassador out within 72 hours on 19 November and recalled its own envoy from Abu Dhabi. Mogadishu accuses Abu Dhabi of violating its sovereignty and backing forces outside federal command; the UAE calls the move escalatory and denies interference. It is the sharpest break yet in the Gulf contest over the Horn.

Ethiopia and Eritrea have massed forces along the border, with Badme clashes reported. Reuters cited conflict monitors and Tigrayan militia-linked sources and could not verify the clashes; Eritrea's information minister called the reports "propaganda". The risk is a second, interstate front on top of the Tigray war.

South Sudan postponed its first national elections from 22 December to February 2027. Insecurity, funding gaps and readiness were cited. Crisis Group warned the UN that rushing a vote risks a "decisive final blow" to the 2018 peace deal.

Amnesty urged war-crimes investigation of RSF drone strikes on El Obeid, North Kordofan. It documented nine strikes between June and August 2026 that killed at least 37 civilians.

Uganda has begun withdrawing troops from Somalia, with a full exit planned by the end of 2027. US funding cuts to the African Union mission are the main driver.

2. Sudan.

The army struck RSF headquarters in Nyala, South Darfur, on 19 November, a day after hitting RSF fuel trucks and reinforcements there. The strikes target the RSF's main Darfur hub.

A landmine on the road toward El Obeid reportedly killed at least 22 civilians on 21 November. Single report; it shows the cost of the army's push along the El Obeid highway. (unconfirmed)

A rights monitor accused the RSF of killing civilians near Abu Qalb, West Kordofan, and said army warplanes bombed villages around Al-Nuhud, Ghubeish, Abu Zabad and Wad Banda. Monitor claims, not independently verified; Kordofan is now the main front.

Fighting in Blue Nile has displaced more than 1,400 families from Geissan, Wad al-Mahi and Bau, according to local reporting, showing the war spreading along the southeastern border.

Diplomacy remains stalled. The Quintet urged support for the Quad's humanitarian-truce effort on 13 November; UN envoy Haavisto expects talks with the RSF and allies in late December, and Khartoum rejects initiatives it sees as violating sovereignty.

The UN says drone attacks killed at least 1,120 civilians in Sudan in the first half of 2026.

3. South Sudan.

Elections moved to February 2027 after earlier delays, with the vote originally due 22 December; the postponement reflects insecurity and funding shortfalls.

Crisis Group told the Security Council that Kiir's administration has dismantled the power-sharing deal, including by dissolving the unity government in September.

Riek Machar, detained since March 2025 and on trial for alleged crimes against humanity (which he denies), told the Juba special court on 18 November that Kiir ignored his request to delay troop deployment to Nasir. Tensions in Upper Nile remain high.

4. Ethiopia.

Federal forces hold Mekelle and most major Tigrayan towns since early October, but the TPLF fights on as a rural insurgency. A Horn Review analysis of 19 November describes the federal reconsolidation; clashes around Aksum and Sheraro continue.

The federally appointed Tigray caretaker administration named a coordinator for disputed Western Tigray, reportedly provoking fresh Amhara anger. Single report. (unconfirmed)

Fano forces in Wollo and North Shewa claim recent victories on the Raya and Ambasel fronts. These are Fano claims that I could not verify, and reports of Fano-TPLF coordination suggest a widening anti-Abiy front. (unconfirmed)

Abiy Ahmed began a new five-year term on 5 October 2026, with the war and Red Sea access as his central themes.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea accused Ethiopia's army chief on 17 November of making territorial claims and of threatening to seize Assab by force. Asmara frames Addis's sea-access argument as a pretext for war.

ACLED reports direct Ethiopian-Eritrean clashes near Badme on 13 November and an Eritrean crossing at Zelambesa on 7 October; Asmara denies sending troops into Ethiopia, and a pro-government militia source disputed the clash reports.

Diplomatic ties are cut: Ethiopia expelled 10 Eritrean diplomats on 1 October and Eritrea severed relations the same day.

G7 foreign ministers urged "maximum restraint"; five US Senate Democrats wrote to Rubio on 10 November asking for more mediation.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The UAE row is now a full rupture: the president had accused Abu Dhabi on 17 November of backing regional administrations and forces outside federal command, after Somalia cancelled UAE port and defence deals in January.

The election dispute is unresolved. The opposition Somali Future Council boycotted talks at Villa Somalia, says Hassan Sheikh's mandate expired on 15 May 2026, and rejects the amended constitution; the government says his term runs to May 2027, with parliamentary elections set for 15 April 2027.

Al-Shabaab is stepping up attacks in Lower Shabelle, including a reported October ambush near Leego where it claimed hundreds of soldiers killed, a figure from the group itself. Uganda's drawdown worries AUSSOM planners.

Israel recognised Somaliland in December 2025 and has exchanged ambassadors with Hargeisa; Somalia, Turkey, Egypt and the AU reject it, and the Houthis call any Israeli presence a target.

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Guelleh won a sixth term in April with 97.81% and has rejected an Ethiopian naval base or corridor at Tadjourah.

Guelleh toured a new international conference centre on 10 November.

Djibouti is exposed to an Ethiopia-Eritrea war, because it is Ethiopia's main trade outlet; a November target to link Doraleh and the HDTL terminal to the railway is unconfirmed as completed.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Uganda is exiting Somalia: up to 6,000 AUSSOM troops and 2,500 bilateral troops will leave by late 2027, and AUSSOM warned Lower Shabelle could be left vulnerable.

Claims that Abu Dhabi compensated Kampala for the withdrawal are unverified. (unconfirmed)

Kenya: nothing new in the last 72 hours. Quiet on Nairobi's mediation role; its ties with the RSF remain a sore point with Khartoum.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

Gulf rivalry is the common thread: Somalia has moved toward a Saudi-Egypt-Turkey alignment, while the UAE cultivates Somaliland and other regional administrations.

Egypt is reportedly coordinating air and drone strikes with Turkey on RSF supply lines near the Libya tri-border area (reported earlier in 2026; unconfirmed).

The US pushes a Quad truce and has proposed widening the Darfur arms embargo. The Security Council extended the 1591 sanctions to 11 December; Russia opposes more sanctions and China favours diplomacy.

Red Sea shipping remains cautious: Suez container transits passed half of 2023 levels, but Houthi threats persist; a report that the Houthis seized Bab al-Mandab is uncorroborated. (unconfirmed)

10. Humanitarian situation.

WFP says more than 19.5 million Sudanese face acute hunger and over 4 million women and children are acutely malnourished, and plans ration cuts of 70% from January in famine-hit areas.

WFP pledged one month of food for 30,000 displaced people at El Dabba camp, Northern State.

Sudan's 2026 appeal was about 41% funded in September, with 1.2 dollarsbillion received against nearly 2.9 dollarsbillion needed.

OCHA reported more than 2,000 suspected cholera cases and 170 deaths since May, mostly in Kordofan (July figures).

Ethiopia and South Sudan: no fresh figures found in the last 72 hours.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt's foreign minister said in August that Cairo could defend itself under international law if Nile flows were disrupted, and Ethiopia links Nile talks to Egyptian support for its sea access.

Ethiopia is reportedly preparing three more Nile dams (August report from an opinion-leaning outlet). (unconfirmed)

Ethiopia-Djibouti rail and port links were targeted for completion by November 2026; no confirmation found.

Quiet on birr, IMF and Somali economic news: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

What to watch.

22 November: the 72-hour deadline for the UAE ambassador to leave Somalia expires; watch for UAE retaliation or Gulf mediation.

Coming days: any confirmation or denial of Ethiopian-Eritrean fighting around Badme and Zelambesa, and whether Addis or Asmara escalates rhetorically on Assab.

Late November: the El Obeid-Kordofan front and RSF drone strikes; also whether the Quad advances a truce.

11 December: expiry of the UN 1591 Sudan sanctions renewal, with US arms-embargo expansion contested by Russia and China.

Late December: UN envoy Haavisto's expected talks with the RSF and allies; the original 22 December South Sudan election date passes without a vote.

January 2027: start of the 12-month AUSSOM transition plan as Uganda draws down.
