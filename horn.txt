Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

Ethiopia-Eritrea border build-up, Somalia's mandate crisis and the army's El Obeid breakthrough.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 19 November 2026 (a few earlier items are flagged for context). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Ethiopia and Eritrea are massing forces along their shared border, and skirmishes near Badme are reported but unverified. Reuters cites conflict monitors and Tigray militia-linked sources, and says it could not confirm clashes; a pro-government Tigray Peace Force source denied any. ACLED says shelling around Badme began last week and that Eritrea retook the town after being forced out last month. A direct state-on-state war would be the biggest shift in the region this year.

Sudan's army and allied forces have broken the RSF siege of El Obeid, according to Al Jazeera's 14 November analysis. The RSF still holds al-Nuhud and parts of South Kordofan. The shift matters because Kordofan links Darfur to the centre of the country.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's mandate expired on 15 May, and ordered an election framework within 30 days. The opposition boycotted the president's talks on 14 November. Critical Threats warns the standoff gives al-Shabaab room to operate.

The UN human rights office says at least 2,230 civilians were killed in Sudan in the first ten months of 2026, most by drone strikes. The figure is the UN's own count and probably understates the toll.

2. Sudan.

A Joint Force allied with the army says it repelled an RSF attack on Kornoi, North Darfur, on 18 November, destroying 31 combat vehicles and capturing 19. These are the force's own claims, with no independent confirmation (unconfirmed).

Amnesty International says RSF drone strikes on El Obeid between June and August killed 37 civilians and injured 10, and should be investigated as war crimes. The strikes hit homes and civilian infrastructure in a city the army now holds.

One person was killed and more than 45 injured in El Obeid on 15 November when celebratory gunfire followed a false report that the army had taken En Nahud. Rumours of battlefield gains are circulating fast, so treat claims about En Nahud with care.

The army and allies advanced on Al-Khuwai in West Kordofan and say they took Serif Ankoush from the RSF. The push west and southwest of El Obeid is aimed at En Nahud and the road toward Darfur.

A reported RSF field commander has defected to the army as it gains ground in Kordofan (unconfirmed). Defections would hurt RSF cohesion if they continue.

Peace talks remain stalled. The Quintet (AU, IGAD, Arab League, EU, UN) condemned drone attacks across eight states and urged a humanitarian truce; US adviser Massad Boulos said there is no military solution. The army still demands RSF withdrawal from civilian areas before any truce.

3. South Sudan.

South Sudan has pushed its first national elections from 22 December 2026 to 11 February 2027. Riek Machar remains detained and on trial for treason and murder, which he denies, so a credible vote looks unlikely. Radio Islam (unconfirmed, single secondary source)

The Special Court says its probe into alleged threats against Machar and his defence team is complete. The Machar trial continues at Freedom Hall in Juba amid repeated adjournments. Sudans Post (dates not confirmed; background only)

Quiet: no verified battlefield or political developments in the last 72 hours were found. The Jonglei fighting that displaced nearly 280,000 earlier this year remains the backdrop.

4. Ethiopia.

Ethiopia's army is clearing Tigrayan rebels from near the Eritrean border, and ACLED says opposing forces now sit in close proximity on a volatile front. Crisis Group's Magnus Taylor warned the fighting could trigger direct interstate conflict.

Addis Ababa announced an interim administration for Tigray and promised elections within six months. This is from 16 October, so it is context, but it frames the current fighting.

The Prosperity Party holds a supermajority after the 1 June election, but Fano and OLA insurgencies persist in Amhara and Oromia. Neither group holds major territory, yet voting was disrupted in parts of both regions. CSIS (background)

Quiet: nothing new verified on Amhara or Oromia in the last 72 hours.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea denies any incursion and accuses Ethiopia of seeking a pretext to invade; the two have had no diplomatic ties since October. Eritrean troops are reportedly positioned near Gemhalo and Sembel.

Eritrea's information ministry published a compilation on 3 November of Ethiopian officials' remarks on Assab, calling it a declared war agenda. The rhetoric centres on Ethiopia's demand for sea access. Shabait, 2 November (state-affiliated source; context)

Ethiopia has accused Eritrea and Sudan of backing armed groups, including through drone attacks near Badme (unconfirmed). Addis had not commented on the reported drone strikes.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

Al-Shabaab has advanced in Hiiraan, seizing Coomaad and moving toward Matabaan. The group has clawed back ground once held by federal troops and clan militias.

Somalia's intelligence agency NISA says strikes in Hiiraan killed two al-Shabaab members and wounded three commanders, one reportedly a finance chief. These are government claims.

A US spokesperson said Washington has returned with funding but there is no "indefinite commitment" to Somalia. Support is conditional while the mandate dispute runs.

Puntland and Jubaland, in the Somali Future Council, reject the March 2026 constitutional changes and the president's terms for talks. Two competing constitutional frameworks now exist, with no court to settle them.

Somaliland: no new recognition moves verified this week. Israel remains the only UN member state to recognise it, and the US has not followed. New Lines Magazine (background)

7. Djibouti.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

Context: Guelleh was re-elected in April with 97.81% by the interior ministry's count, and has refused Ethiopia's request for a Tadjourah corridor and naval base. An Ethiopia-Djibouti rail link to Doraleh and the HDTL terminal was targeted for November, with no completion report found.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

Context: Uganda hosts over 88,000 Sudanese refugees and Kenya about 210,000 South Sudanese, and a commentary this year argues Nairobi is used by sanctioned RSF financiers.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

A Turkish naval delegation visited Port Sudan on 2 to 5 November, and Sudan has proposed a Red Sea free economic zone to Russia in place of a naval base. No Russian base deal has been ratified, and a senior US official warned of "serious consequences" if one is granted.

Houthi forces reportedly seized Yemeni coast overlooking Bab el-Mandeb after Saudi-backed troops withdrew, and declared a blockade of Saudi ports (unconfirmed). The reports are inconsistent on dates; Houthi missiles reportedly targeted Riyadh airport.

Alignments are hardening: Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Turkey back the army in Sudan and Somalia's government, while the UAE sponsors the RSF and holds bases in Berbera and Bosaso. Analysts say an Ethiopia-Eritrea war would draw these rivalries in.

10. Humanitarian situation.

The WFP agreed exceptional one-month food aid for 30,000 people displaced from Darfur and Kordofan to Northern State on 18 November. It warns rations will be cut by 70% from January for communities facing famine and 50% for those at risk, because of funding gaps.

The UN counts 2,230 civilian deaths in Sudan in ten months, over 1,630 of them from drones.

Somalia: about six million people face acute hunger, with US aid suspension cutting the food pipeline, per Crisis Group's July brief. The figure is dated and a November Atlantic piece reports a looming famine (secondary, unverified).

An Ethiopia-Eritrea war would add a new displacement emergency to an already overstretched appeal. Sudan's 2026 response plan was only 5.5% funded at the last update found.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Egypt and Sudan have invoked the 1959 Nile treaty and demanded a binding legal framework for the GERD, and no date is set for talks with Ethiopia. The renewed rhetoric coincides with Cairo-Khartoum coordination.

Ethiopia's rebased national accounts, published 4 November, put 2025/26 output at 31.86 trillion birr (about 210.7 dollarsbillion), which officials call Africa's fifth-largest economy. Critics say the revision adds output on paper only.

The birr trades near 160 per dollar, the central bank held its rate at 16% on 3 November, and September inflation was 14.9%. An Eritrea front would pressure the IMF-backed programme.

Analysts warn that fighting near Assab could raise freight and oil costs through Bab el-Mandeb even without attacks on ships.
