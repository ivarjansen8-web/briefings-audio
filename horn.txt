Here is your Horn of Africa briefing for Friday, 13 November.

Somalia court voids president's term, SAF pushes west in Kordofan, G7 warns on Ethiopia-Eritrea.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 13 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Somalia's Supreme Court ruled on 5 November that President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's and parliament's terms have expired, ordering a framework within 30 days and elections within 90. The presidency disputes the court's jurisdiction and analysts expect the ruling will not be implemented, so a legitimacy standoff with Puntland and Jubaland is deepening.

Sudan's army and allied forces are pushing west along the central Sudan highway to clear the RSF from the Kordofans, while the RSF opens a second front in Blue Nile. The Blue Nile push could stretch the army away from its main axis.

G7 foreign ministers meeting in Paris on 9 to 10 November warned of regional escalation as Ethiopia-Eritrea tensions worsen, and urged an immediate ceasefire in Sudan. It is a rare joint Western signal on two linked Red Sea-facing crises.

South Sudan's UN envoy told the Security Council on 10 November that the country is at a "critical juncture", 42 days from the 22 December election. Funding and security gaps make a credible vote doubtful.

WFP's new chief says it urgently needs 520 dollarsmillion to feed 5.4 million people in Sudan through April 2027. Funding cuts are deepening hunger in a war with no ceasefire.

2. Sudan.

The army and allied forces are advancing in West Kordofan, reportedly seizing the Iyal Bakhit area near El-Khwei and dozens of villages, and say they shot down a third long-range drone in the region within 72 hours. These are army-side claims and the RSF has not been heard from in these reports.

An RSF drone strike killed nine civilians in the village of Bani Shako, Blue Nile, according to the Kurmuk commissioner. The attack fits the RSF's widening Blue Nile campaign.

Drone strikes across Sudan killed at least 14 civilians in early November, including at least four at a fuel station in a South Kordofan market, and hit electricity infrastructure, causing wide power cuts. Attacks on fuel and power compound the humanitarian toll.

The G7 backed the Quad (US, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE) track and called on both SAF and RSF to accept an immediate ceasefire. Sceptics, such as the Washington Institute, argue Saudi-UAE rivalry has left the Quad ineffective, so the statement does not show an agreement is near.

El Obeid's bakeries went on a general strike over bread prices, in a besieged city WFP calls the new epicentre of displacement and hunger. Reporting is from recent days and the exact date is not stated. (unconfirmed)

3. South Sudan.

UNMISS chief Anita Kiki Gbeho said the question is not only whether the 22 December vote happens but whether the transition can keep peace and public confidence. She said the government is determined to proceed.

The National Elections Commission has reportedly received only 21 dollarsmillion of the 276 dollarsmillion it requested. That shortfall bears directly on whether voting can be credible. Kenya Today, undated (unconfirmed)

Riek Machar is still detained and on trial on treason-related charges his allies deny, after Kiir dissolved the transitional government in September. Wire coverage says many fear the election is premature.

The Security Council Report November forecast says the AU and IGAD are still seeking consensus, and that Sudan has objected to including Abyei in the elections. This opens a possible Khartoum-Juba dispute.

South Sudan rejected claims that it obstructs UNMISS operations. The denial signals friction with the mission as the vote nears. (unconfirmed)

4. Ethiopia.

Ethiopia has told the UN that Eritrea and Sudan back armed groups and carry out drone attacks, while Abiy again said on Monday that Ethiopia must have Red Sea access. Eritrea says it faces a "war of aggression"; none of these claims is independently verified.

FEWS NET warns drought and conflict could push parts of Tigray, Amhara and Afar to IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) in early 2027. Clashes have intensified in several Tigray zones and in eastern and northern Amhara.

Crisis Group's October CrisisWatch says federal forces drove the TPLF from major Tigray towns, shifting the conflict toward a rural insurgency, with Eritrean forces briefly crossing into Ethiopia. Crisis Group also notes closer TPLF-Fano coordination.

Fano forces claim to have captured Meragna in North Shewa. This is a partisan claim I could not verify. (unconfirmed)

Oromia: an insurgency persists in parts of the region, with no specific incident found in the last 72 hours.

5. Eritrea.

Eritrea severed diplomatic ties with Ethiopia in October and denies backing the TPLF. Asmara fears Ethiopia means to seize Assab.

The Africa Defense Forum and Small Wars Journal warn that a war near Assab could add a second Red Sea front alongside the Houthi threat. One forecast cited puts a coastal war at about 25 percent.

The US State Department urged both sides to show restraint, and analysts say any settlement would need AU and Gulf mediation plus a long-term port deal. No mediation round has been announced.

Quiet inside Eritrea: no domestic news in the last 72 hours. Closed information environment limits what outsiders see.

6. Somalia and Somaliland.

The Supreme Court ruling, brought by ex-president Sharif Sheikh Ahmed, came a day after Hassan Sheikh replaced the chief justice. The court put the end of parliament's term at 14 April and the president's at 15 May.

The electoral commission's timetable has parliamentary elections on 15 April 2027 and a presidential vote on 15 May, well after the court's 90-day deadline in early February. Puntland and Jubaland, with the Somali Future Council, oppose the president's election changes.

Al-Shabaab killed eight Kenyans in an attack in Mandera County, Kenya, and Mogadishu condemned it. Analysts see pressure on Nairobi over its troops in Somalia; the date was not given. (unconfirmed)

US airstrikes reportedly hit ISIS positions in Puntland around 10 November. Single outlet. (unconfirmed)

On Somaliland, Israel recognised it on 26 December 2025 and Washington has not followed; in August its president offered Israel and the US base access at Berbera. No new recognition step was found this week.

7. Djibouti.

Djibouti's ports face rising operating costs from Red Sea and Hormuz disruption, though cargo volumes rose slightly, according to the port authority head in October. The port is Ethiopia's main outlet, so Addis's Assab push touches its revenue base.

Analysis dated 27 October calls Djibouti a crossroads of the Red Sea power struggle as Ethiopia pivots to other ports. It is commentary, not an event.

A August investigation alleges Djibouti is a Houthi smuggling hub; the government has not confirmed this. (unconfirmed)

Guelleh, re-elected in April with about 97 percent, toured a new conference centre on 10 November. Nothing else significant in the last 72 hours.

8. Kenya and Uganda.

Ruto asked EU ambassadors on 13 November to back a new Stabilisation and Mediation Unit under envoy Luis Franceschi, citing stalled efforts in South Sudan and Sudan. The Tumaini initiative reportedly stalled over reconciliation steps.

Kenya is directly exposed to al-Shabaab after the Mandera attack and a Somali clan elder's claim that Kenya shelters ex-South West leader Laftagareen, whom Mogadishu sanctioned in October. The claim is one elder's view. (unconfirmed)

Uganda's Museveni discussed roads, power and border security with a South Sudan delegation in October, and the AU sees him as a possible Sudan mediator. Ugandan influence in Juba matters for the election period.

9. Red Sea and outside powers.

A US envoy told the UN Security Council on 13 November that the Houthis threaten to close Bab el-Mandeb, have seized Mocha and islands at the strait's entrance, and fired on ships and tankers. This is the US account.

A Houthi strike on Riyadh on 8 November reportedly damaged an Aramco office tower. Houthi pressure on Saudi interests raises the stakes for Gulf mediation in the Horn.

Sudan proposed a Red Sea free economic zone to Russia after Burhan met Putin in Moscow on 30 October; the naval base deal remains unratified. Moscow has not publicly accepted.

Egypt and Sudan held a third "2+2" round in Cairo in early November and reasserted Nile rights. Egypt has also reportedly floated backing Ethiopian Red Sea access if the Nile dispute eases (single report).

Turkey and China: no relevant development found in the last 72 hours. Quiet.

10. Humanitarian situation.

WFP needs 520 dollarsmillion to reach 5.4 million people in Sudan through April 2027. Executive Director Luke Lindberg called aid the "only hope" after his first mission.

UN agencies said in May that 14 areas of Darfur and Kordofan are at risk of famine, with nearly 20 million facing acute hunger. That is the latest formal assessment I found.

South Sudan faces its largest recorded cholera outbreak, according to OCHA. Figures were not in the snippet reviewed.

El Obeid's camps are over capacity with families arriving daily, WFP says. The siege and drone attacks on fuel are cutting water and transport.

FEWS NET warns of Emergency-level hunger in Tigray, Amhara and Afar in early 2027.

11. Economy, ports and the GERD.

Ethiopia's finance ministry rebased GDP on 4 November to 31.86 trillion birr (about 210.7 dollarsbillion), claiming Africa's fifth-largest economy. The IMF projected 127.1 dollarsbillion, so the jump is mostly a statistical revision, and the country remains in debt distress.

IMF staff were expected to hold talks on the sixth review of Ethiopia's programme this month; about 2.65 dollarsbillion of 3.4 dollarsbillion has been paid. The timing is unconfirmed and the birr is around 151 to 161 per dollar depending on source.

Egypt and Sudan are pressing for a binding legal framework on the GERD; no date is set for resuming Nile talks.

Maritime risk is costing ports: the US advisory covering 23 September 2026 to 22 March 2027 flags higher risk for Israel-, US-, UK- and Saudi-linked ships. Operators are reportedly seeking Houthi assurances.
