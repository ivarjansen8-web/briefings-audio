Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

Russian missiles come down in Poland · Houthis fire on Riyadh again · Wall Street enters correction.

1. World Politics.

Russian missiles in Poland: Two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in south-east Poland on Friday during a major barrage on Ukraine; no casualties. PM Tusk called it a likely intentional provocation timed to European drills for a post-war Ukraine mission.

Putin invites Europeans to Moscow: After the Trump–Xi–Putin summit in Shenzhen, Putin invited European leaders to Moscow; France says they would go only with a "full role" in talks, while Zelensky called a Moscow trip a "mistake."

Denmark expels Russia's defence attaché: He has seven days to leave, and Copenhagen is halting non-essential visas for Russians. FM Rasmussen: "enough is enough." Al Jazeera reports the case involves an alleged plot to kill a Ukrainian rapper. Anadolu ·.

Markets: An AI-led sell-off pushed the S&P 500 down 3.7% (6,910) and the Dow down 949 points, and both closed in correction. The Nasdaq fell 5.3% and is now negative for 2026. The S&P lost 8.6% on the week, its worst since April 2025. AP via Yakima Herald ·.

2. US Politics.

Trump presser after Asia trip: In nearly two hours, Trump touted the Xi/Putin meetings, which produced few concrete results; defended his relationship with Putin; and praised ousted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. He also said he will stop US firms from building data centers abroad. NPR ·.

Cyber chief out: Trump fired National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross ("not good enough") amid a wave of US tech, banking and air-traffic outages. Pentagon cyber chief Kirsten Davies is tapped to replace him. Trump called the outages "separate events." White House ·.

Vance–Iran split: Trump denied anger at JD Vance over Vance's Iran-war distancing. CNN read the presser as Trump boosting Rubio over Vance.

3. Congress.

Lame-duck dynamics: Trump vowed to use the veto to force Democrats into deals but praised Hakeem Jeffries as "streetwise." Democrats take control of Congress in January. CNN ·.

Air traffic control: The administration is seeking 50 billion dollars from Congress for a new ATC system after recent ground stops.

Nvidia probe: House Foreign Affairs released Nvidia/AMD China chip-sale requests; roughly 900k advanced chips were approved by May. Lawmakers are still pressing Jensen Huang for a closed-door briefing on alleged spy Alice Huang. NPR ·.

Senate floor: This week the Senate confirmed Anna St. John to the 5th Circuit (52 to 46) and Daniel Domenico to the 10th Circuit (51 to 46).

4. US Foreign Policy.

Iran sanctions: State sanctioned Iranian regime insiders profiting from oil revenue (20 November) and targeted Southeast Asian cyber-scam enablers (19 November). State Dept ·.

"Dirty bomb" warning: Trump warned Iran would "pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid" over a reported plot against officials in DC. Tehran calls the reports fabricated. He also says he is "not rushing" his next Iran move, possibly into 2027. CNN ·.

Philippines: Trump claimed Manila will buy US farm and energy goods "100%"; Marcos clarified.

Central Asia: Deputy Secretary Landau is touring Tajikistan, Türkiye and Turkmenistan (16 to 24 November).

5. Middle East Politics.

Israel coalition talks: PM-designate Gadi Eisenkot laid out three routes to a government, including openness to Arab-party backing; Liberman refuses any arrangement with Netanyahu. Netanyahu stays caretaker PM. JTA ·.

Gaza aid: The UK, Australia, Canada and Italy urged Israel to let in 150,000 tents held at the border after floods; the EU issued a similar call.

Qatar: Merz called for a "360-degree partnership" with Qatar, praising its mediation.

Syria: The EU agreed to revive its cooperation agreement with Damascus.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Iraq: Under a deal brokered by Shi'ite leaders, government forces deployed into Jurf al-Sakhar, the stronghold of Kataib Hezbollah, with no clashes. PM Ali al-Zaidi accuses the group of oil smuggling, and nine security and oil officials have been dismissed. Reuters via Al-Monitor ·.

Context: Quds Force chief Qaani reportedly flew to Baghdad to defuse the standoff.

Israel: Eisenkot is pushing a fast "national emergency government" and has floated a minority-government option; his 28-day mandate runs to early December.

7. Middle East Defense News.

Saudi–Houthi escalation: The Saudi-led coalition intercepted three ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh early Saturday, the third straight night of alerts. This follows coalition strikes on Hodeidah port and Sanaa, and a 19 November hit on Riyadh's PIF tower. The Houthis threaten Jeddah and other Red Sea ports and a power station. Al Jazeera ·.

Lebanon: The IDF struck a former Hezbollah missile site at Janta in the Beqaa, its second Beqaa strike this week.

Iran: CBS sources say the US and Israel are weighing a near-term new assault despite Trump's "no rush" line. Iran continues to claim tankers are hitting mines in Hormuz. CBS ·.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Conscription protests: Pupils and students protested new military-service plans in 16 cities; ministers defended them.

Tata Steel trial: The trial opened, with prosecutors alleging 598 intentional pollution incidents at IJmuiden.

Hospitals: Several Dutch hospitals have frozen intake of new Zilveren Kruis patients over budget caps.

Kyiv embassy: A Russian drone crashed behind the Dutch embassy in Kyiv, causing damage.

FvD: Six FvD members were expelled as police investigate neo-Nazi cage fighting.

Housing: Q3 home building rose but remains short of the 100,000 target.

9. European Union News.

EU–UK reset: A deal was sealed in Brussels covering food trade (SPS), youth mobility and linked carbon markets; tuition-fee talks get six months. Farage opposes it.

Ukraine: At the Berlin Coalition of the Willing meeting, the EU readied 45 billion euros for Ukraine's 2027 needs and the UK pledged 10 billion pounds. Europe's team for talks is the E5 plus the EU institutions. Irish Times ·.

Visas: Denmark will push Schengen partners, especially France, on restricting Russian visas.

10. NATO News.

Poland: Rutte called the missile crash a "grave violation"; NATO jets scrambled. He is expected in Poland this weekend, and Warsaw is asking for extra alliance air-defence assets. Washington Times/AP ·.

Exercise: Thousands of European troops are drilling at Nowa Dęba, south-east Poland, for a possible post-war Ukraine mission.

11. Elections.

Upcoming.

Victoria (Australia): State election on 28 November; early voting is open.

The Gambia: Presidential vote on 5 December; Barrow seeks a third term, and 8 of 9 candidates signed a peace pact.

El Salvador: Bukele was due to register by 19 November for a third-term run in the February 2027 election.

US midterms: Democrats take Congress in January.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: The army is running an intensive air and drone campaign on RSF headquarters in Nyala and on convoys bound for El Fasher, ahead of a planned ground push. This follows SAF gains in West Kordofan, where the Heglig/Balila oilfields produce about 90% of Sudan's oil. The RSF still holds all of Darfur. Sudan Tribune ·.

Ethiopia–Eritrea: ACLED and Crisis Group report troops massing on the border and say Ethiopian drones hit likely Eritrean positions near Badme on 12 November, followed by clashes. Eritrea calls this propaganda, and the clashes are unconfirmed.

Senegal: The ruling Pastef party expelled President Faye on 17 November after his feud with Sonko. Pastef holds 130 of 165 seats, and the rift threatens IMF support.

Nigeria: All 36 states approved state police forces; Tinubu's signature is still needed. Separately, Nigerian raids are being enabled by US intelligence support. Rio Times ·.

Mozambique: Opposition leader Mondlane's terrorism trial is under way, with no live broadcast allowed.

Sahel: ACLED reports JNIM launching "unprecedented" strikes on the Burkinabe military and IEDs on the RN24. JNIM–ISSP turf war is spreading into Niger and Nigeria's Kebbi. ACLED ·.

13. Asia/India (brief)

Myanmar: The ICJ ruled Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time the court has found a state responsible; Myanmar calls it unfair.

India: Cyclone Akvan made landfall on the Odisha coast near Gopalpur after more than 120,000 were evacuated.

Philippines: Duterte's defence is seeking to exclude a key witness's testimony at the ICC.

14. OSINT Updates (analyst assessments, partly unconfirmed)

ISW on Poland: ISW assesses the Russian airspace violations were intentional, meant to deter the Coalition of the Willing and test NATO. Two likely Russian strike drones also briefly entered Moldovan airspace.

Crash sites: Polish security secured additional sites near Rudnik nad Sanem and Mołodycz; reports cite NATO AWACS patrols and Patriot alerts. Developing.

Kaliningrad plot: ISW reports a Russian false-flag plan, unexecuted, to stage an attack from Poland into Kaliningrad and blame Ukraine. Unconfirmed.

Kerch Bridge: Struck for the third time in three days. Ukraine's "Operation Bear Trap" reportedly retook more than 200 km² in northern Kharkiv.

Ethiopia–Eritrea: ACLED-tracked drone strikes near Badme. Unverified.

Hormuz: Iranian claims of mined tankers are largely unverified by independent monitors. UKMTO and Vanguard did confirm a projectile strike on the tanker ON PHOENIX off Oman.
