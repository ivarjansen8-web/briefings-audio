Daily News Briefing — 27 November 2026: Kuwait drone attack kills two Italians; EU budget summit breaks up early.

Kuwait drone attack kills two Italians · EU budget summit breaks up early · Trump presses Putin on "aggression across Europe"

1. World Politics.

Iran-linked drone attack in Kuwait kills two Italians. The two Rheinmetall technicians were servicing Kuwaiti air-defence detection gear; Kuwait and Italy blame Iran. Tehran denies it, calls it a false flag, and FM Araghchi has offered a joint probe. Kuwait and Bahrain have taken the brunt of recent Iranian strikes. —.

US trading partners reject the justification for new Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies. Together the 14 make up about half of world GDP. China faces 20% (possibly 40%) and reserves the right to retaliate; Japan, Singapore, Malaysia and the EU also dispute the findings. —.

Russia launched another ballistic missile and drone barrage on Ukraine. At least 3 were killed in Kharkiv and 7 wounded in Kyiv; Ukraine says it downed 87 targets. —.

Ukraine's FM Sybiha expressed regret over the "unintended consequences" of Ukrainian drone strikes in Libya last weekend, which killed two Libyan naval officers. —.

Armenian PM Pashinyan says Russian border guards will leave Armenia "in the coming years." —.

AFP and FIFA both hit by cyberattacks. AFP took its content platform offline; FIFA confirms limited data was accessed. —.

2. US Politics.

Thanksgiving nor'easter: New York declared a state of emergency, more than 800,000 customers lost power, and more than 1,900 flights were delayed. The Macy's parade was postponed to Saturday for the first time in its 100-year history. —.

On a Thanksgiving call with troops, Trump said victory over Iran is "at hand" and that the outcome is "whatever I want it to be." He also played down Russian and Chinese help to Tehran. —.

Trump claimed "fake economic numbers" were released before the midterms, keeping up his attacks on official statistics after the GOP's losses. —.

Trump awarded Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom (White House video, 26 November). —.

3. Congress.

Quiet week: both chambers are out for Thanksgiving. The House has about 12 voting days left in the 119th Congress, and absenteeism has more than doubled since the midterms. —.

The 11 December funding deadline is the next flashpoint. Senate Democrats (Kaine, Bennet, Duckworth, Sanders, Van Hollen) want an Iran-war funding cutoff attached to any stopgap. DeLauro has floated a short CR to February, which would push the fight into the Democratic-controlled Congress. —.

Still pending: the NDAA, the farm bill, FISA reform, and Trump's 1.5 trillion dollars defense request. —.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Trump says he urged Putin in Shenzhen to pull back "all military aggression" across Europe. This follows Russian missile and drone incursions into Polish airspace and a joint Trump–Rutte statement warning Moscow not to widen the war. Trump named Putin–Zelensky animosity as the main obstacle to a deal. —.

Iran–US talks: Araghchi says Qatari and Pakistani mediators are still passing messages between Tehran and Washington, though diplomacy appears stalled. —.

The White House has reportedly reached out to Gadi Eisenkot about a meeting if he forms a government (Al-Hadath, citing an Israeli source). —.

Kushner says Israel must drop its "constant wartime mindset." He says Netanyahu has blocked the Board of Peace's Hamas-disarmament plan and warns that bucking it could hand Gaza to the UN or the PA. —.

Washington warned the EU against reviving a digital services tax ahead of the budget talks. —.

5. Middle East Politics.

Gaza: an Israeli strike killed Khaled Abu Nada, Hamas's deputy education and economy chief, and his wife; at least 10 died in the latest strikes. Netanyahu says around 80 Hamas operatives were killed in the past month. Hamas chief al-Hayya says the group will consult factions on a response. Israel now controls about two-thirds of Gaza. —.

Defense Minister Katz threatened a fresh Gaza offensive if Hamas won't disarm, cutting against the Board of Peace push. —.

Saudi Arabia, Türkiye and Pakistan meet in Islamabad today and tomorrow on the mutual-defense pact they signed in August. —.

Türkiye rejected a European Parliament resolution on its 2019 maritime deal with Libya. —.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel coalition talks.

Yashar (Eisenkot) and UTJ have reportedly agreed key principles of a Haredi draft law.

Yashar–Democrats talks resumed after a freeze; the dispute is over portfolios, with Golan wanting a top-three ministry.

UTJ reportedly vetoes Reform rabbi MK Gilad Kariv as education minister.

Bennett denies a Haaretz report of a secret unity-government channel with Netanyahu.

Shas is splitting over whether to back an Eisenkot government. Deri reportedly said it would be "very difficult" for him to stay in politics if the rabbis force it. Shas fell from 11 seats to 6 in October. —.

The Knesset passed a first reading, 51 to 32, repealing Karhi's July broadcasting law. Separately, ex-AG Mandelblit testified at Netanyahu's trial, and Haredi protesters clashed with police in Jerusalem over jailed draft evaders. —.

Iraq has begun closing all unofficial border crossings with neighbours, including Syria and Iran, on PM Ali al-Zaidi's directive. —.

West Bank: settlers are accused of torching a car in Beita, and a new settlement road is under construction near Tuqu. —.

7. Middle East Defense News.

The Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed 50 Houthi "military targets" in Sanaa, Hodeidah and Saada. The Houthis have been targeting Saudi ports. —.

A strike on a refinery in Saudi Arabia's Jazan region killed one worker and injured several, per the Saudi Energy Ministry. —.

Strait of Hormuz: the IRGC Navy says two tankers hit mines and caught fire north of Khasab, Oman, while "secretly" transiting. UKMTO separately reports a projectile exploding near a tanker 50nm west of Dubai, with no damage. —.

IDF struck Hezbollah rockets and launchers in Lebanon's Beqaa Valley. —.

Kan documentary: IDF soldiers' testimonies describe widespread use of Palestinian civilians as human shields early in the Gaza war; the IDF says "we will learn from this." —.

9. European Union News.

The special MFF summit broke up early. Merz says positions on the 2028 to 34 budget remain "far apart," and leaders will try again at the 17 December summit. Costa's compromise is 1.781 trillion euros; Berlin reportedly wants about 1.59 trillion euros and no new borrowing. Macron called the cuts a "strategic mistake." —.

Ukraine funding: Merz wants all 27 states behind a 45 billion euros 2027 loan, while Hungary, Slovakia and Czechia resist. He also wants future Ukraine aid moved to majority voting. —.

Eurozone leaders backed Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Lagarde (from 7 February), the first Spaniard in the role. Eurozone inflation is 4%, and a December rate hike under Lagarde is possible. —.

EP President Metsola says Parliament won't "rubber-stamp" the MFF; consent needs 361 votes. —.

Farmers rallied with tractors in Brussels to defend CAP subsidies. —.

10. NATO News.

Trump and Rutte issued a joint statement urging Russia to avoid actions that widen the war, after Russian missile and drone incursions into Polish airspace. Poland has raised NATO intercepting Russian missiles over Ukraine. —.

Rutte to El País (26 November): Russia has "no say or veto" on Ukraine's membership, but unanimity is required and the US is among the opponents. —.

The UK has contacted about 100,000 ex-service personnel about recall availability, the first such move since the Cold War. They are being invited to Steadfast Defender in spring 2027. —.

German FM Wadephul urged Russia to show good faith with a Black Sea ceasefire. —.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan.

RSF drone strikes killed at least 19 to 20 in Blue Nile state, and more than 7,000 families are fleeing toward Ed Damazin. SIHA reports 28 women and girls abducted.

The army (SAF) has gained ground in Kordofan, and RSF defections are rising. These are the first signs the war's momentum may be shifting, even as WFP flags new famine hotspots in Darfur and Khartoum.

Egypt and Sudan agreed to prioritise post-war reconstruction and reject "militarisation" of the Red Sea.

DR Congo: Ebola has reached 12,021 confirmed cases and 5,904 deaths across 8 provinces, the fastest-growing outbreak on record. It is the Bundibugyo strain, which has no approved vaccine. Fighting between FARDC and the CRP militia in Ituri's Djugu territory is blocking contact tracing; only 15% of new cases come from known contacts. —.

Horn of Africa: Abiy demanded that Eritrea stop arming Ethiopian rebels and denied plans for an offensive to secure Red Sea access. Analysts warn of a widening Ethiopia–Sudan proxy war. —.

South Sudan: MSF suspended operations in Yei after an abduction, and 21 civilians were killed in an attack on a Warrap cattle camp. —.

Sahel and North Africa: Boko Haram killed 11 soldiers in Niger, and Algeria has expelled 31,734 migrants to Niger in 2026. —.
