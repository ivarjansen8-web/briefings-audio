Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Trump versus new Democratic Congress; Israel coalition shift; Russia hits Kyiv ahead of Abu Dhabi talks.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 8 November. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. World Politics.

Russia pounded Kyiv and Ukraine's grid overnight 6 to 7 November (36 ballistic missiles, 172 drones; Northern Bridge in Kyiv damaged), days before US-brokered trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 to 11 November.

Iran war continues: US forces disabled a commercial ship trying to run the US blockade of Iran; Qatar says its US–Iran mediation continues.

Spain heads to snap election on 29 November after PM Sánchez called it amid a housing crisis and protests.

APEC leaders' meeting in Shenzhen 18 to 19 November; Taiwan is protesting an invitation that omitted "Chinese Taipei."

2. US Politics.

Post-midterm blue wave: Democrats hold 254 House seats to Republicans' 163 as of CNN's count, with 20 races still uncalled; Senate races in Mississippi and Alaska remain too close to call.

Trump digs in: he says he has ordered the DOJ to "look into" Democrats pushing investigations and impeachment votes against his officials.

White House shake-up: Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as chief of staff (Trump's sixth); Trump says he offered the press secretary job to UFC CEO Dana White, who reportedly declined.

Democratic 2028 chatter: Sen. Jon Ossoff won a Crooked Con straw poll with 38% (AOC 17%, Buttigieg 14%).

Mississippi count controversy: most of 233 provisional ballots from Jackson State students were rejected; county board reviews next week.

3. Congress.

Hegseth impeachment: Rep. Thomas Massie says he'll trigger a floor vote next week; several GOP sources expect it to pass. Hegseth told troops to "ignore the noise."

Senate Democratic leadership: Schumer says he'll stay leader, but incoming senators (incl. Van Hollen, Slotkin comments) suggest it's not settled.

Iran war powers: a report says Trump's advisers told him he has until the new Congress is seated to restart major Iran operations.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Iran: naval blockade continues and the US military is looking to restrict Iranian land commerce too; CENTCOM offers a 20 million dollars reward for information on Iran's nuclear program; Trump described recent US strikes as "minor."

China channel: Treasury Secretary Bessent reportedly reached out to Beijing for help ending the Iran war after the midterm defeat.

Ukraine: Washington plans to ask NATO allies for more money for the PURL weapons program, possibly this month; a 2.1 billion dollars Patriot sale was approved on 30 October.

5. Middle East Politics.

Israel: President Herzog will task Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) on 8 November with forming a government; Eisenkot is pushing a "national emergency government" without "extremists," while Netanyahu calls for national unity.

Iran: hardliners are using Trump's midterm losses to demand a steeper price for any exit; the IRGC says its self-imposed ballistic missile range limit "has been lifted."

Qatar says its US–Iran mediation is continuing to prevent regional escalation.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel coalition math: Shas reportedly won't oppose an Eisenkot-led government that includes UTJ; Yair Golan is open to Haredi partners if they enlist; Likud is preparing to appeal the election results over alleged illegal voter tracking by Arab parties.

Tel Aviv protests (7 November) against the Shin Bet election probe and for a government that includes Arab parties.

Gaza: Hamas says about 70,000 tents were flooded by rain in 12 hours; sewage reported mixing into displaced families' tents.

West Bank: video shows settlers beating a Palestinian man with clubs in an overnight attack in the south.

Syria denied reports it would send thousands of troops to Yemen/Saudi Arabia, but said military cooperation with Riyadh could expand.

7. Middle East Defense News.

Hormuz/Gulf: CENTCOM says a strike disabled a ship attempting to break the blockade; an oil tanker reportedly caught fire after hitting a naval mine; UAE rescued 22 from a burning cargo ship in the Sea of Oman.

Iranian media report two missiles hit Qeshm Island (damage unconfirmed) and explosions on another Hormuz island.

Yemen: Saudi strikes on Houthi-held Sanaa killed at least 22 per Houthi media; Houthis claim attacks on Riyadh airport and Aramco sites; the Saudi-led coalition says it downed a Houthi cruise missile aimed at Riyadh.

Iran–Russia: Iran is said to be negotiating for the S-400 air defense system (unconfirmed).

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Box 3: the cabinet (PM Rob Jetten) will postpone its Box 3 wealth-tax plans by a year and set aside 350 million euros in tax relief; the coalition is still squabbling over taxing the wealthy.

ECB: the Netherlands nominated Klaas Knot to lead the European Central Bank.

Inflation rose to 3.7% in October on energy and fuel prices.

Cyber: the attack on Albert Heijn's parent Ahold Delhaize is estimated to have cost about 250 million euros; the Dutch security agency urges firms to prepare for faster AI-driven attacks.

Schiphol: bomb threats against KLM flights (Czech man arrested 7 November over a Prague flight threat); France is probing possible foreign-power links to airline bomb threats.

Weather: first official autumn frost; weekend 5 to 10°C.

9. European Union News.

This week: ECOFIN on 9 November (tobacco taxation package, stability pact decisions); budget conciliation with Parliament on the 2027 EU budget on 10 November.

Summitry: European Political Community 12 November; informal leaders' meeting 13 November; Foreign Affairs Council 16 November (Ukraine, Syria); EU–UK summit in Brussels 20 November with UK PM Andy Burnham.

Enlargement: the Commission published its candidate-country progress assessments on 4 November.

France: students and teachers called a new strike for Tuesday amid ongoing budget turmoil.

10. NATO News.

PURL funding push: the US is expected to ask allies for more money for NATO's Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List as soon as this month.

Coalition of the Willing exercises begin 9 November; Moscow calls any foreign force deployment to Ukraine "categorically unacceptable" and says Europeans have no place at the table.

Poland: a shooting in Krakow's main square killed 5; police say the shooter was fascinated by far-right ideologies.

Cyber: Italy's foreign minister says pro-Russian hackers leaked his data over his Ukraine support.

11. Elections.

Upcoming.

Spain snap general election, 29 November.

Somalia: Supreme Court ordered elections within 90 days after ruling the president's term expired.

Israel: coalition formation under way; Likud may challenge the result.

Just happened.

US midterms (3 November): Democrats take the House with a large majority; Mississippi and Alaska Senate races uncalled.

South Africa local elections: ANC at about 31% with 76% counted (45% in 2021), trailing in Johannesburg, Tshwane and eThekwini; coalitions expected.

Israel (late October): Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats; opposition 48, Arab parties 19 (per one report).

12. Africa In-Depth.

Somalia constitutional crisis: the Supreme Court ruled President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's term ended 15 May and parliament's on 14 April, voided measures taken since, left the cabinet as caretaker, and gave parties 30 days to agree an electoral framework and 90 days to hold elections. The government had argued he could stay until May 2027; a standoff or non-compliance is the key risk.

Sudan: a confidential UN report says a UAE-linked security firm moved Colombian mercenaries into Sudan to support the RSF via Libya and Somalia; Australia sanctioned Burhan, Hemedti and two RSF figures.

Horn of Africa: Germany warns against escalation as Ethiopia–Eritrea tensions rise (troop movements, Red Sea access) and Ethiopia–Sudan ties deteriorate.

Tanzania: opposition leader Tundu Lissu got four years for incitement, a charge not in the indictment, after treason charges were dropped.

Nigeria: military rules out a ceasefire with Boko Haram/ISWAP as attacks surge in the northeast; the army is expanding from 8 to 12 divisions.

DR Congo: WHO says cholera has killed about 1,800 this year (60,000 cases) alongside the country's deadliest Ebola outbreak; spread toward Kinshasa is a concern.

South Africa: Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla lost her bid to drop incitement charges tied to the 2021 unrest.

13. Asia / India.

China: the Fifth Plenum confirmed 33 expulsions; only 2 of 7 Central Military Commission seats are filled (Xi and Zhang Shengmin).

Hong Kong jailed Joshua Wong for 10 years under the national security law.

Markets: Nikkei and KOSPI each fell about 6% on 4 November; Australia's RBA raised its cash rate to 4.85%.

Japan: magnitude 5.5 earthquake in central Japan hurt at least 22.

India: no major item surfaced in today's sources.

Ukrainian deep strikes: General Staff claims hits on two Iskander launchers and a Pantsir in Bryansk and a Neptune strike on the Proton-Electrotex plant in Oryol (developing, not independently verified).
