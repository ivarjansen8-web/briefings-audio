Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Headline: Trump–Xi–Putin summit looms as Iran talks stall; Eisenkot races to form Israel's next government.

1. World Politics.

Trump, Xi and Putin are expected to meet in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC (leaders' meeting 18 to 19 November). A US official says Iran and nuclear arms control are on the agenda, not Ukraine. Beijing has said talks are underway but had not formally confirmed the meeting in the latest report I found (11 November).

Ukraine: after trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on 12 November, ISW assesses Putin still holds to his original territorial demands and rejects meaningful Western security guarantees. Russia launched about 100+ drones nightly this week.

The US–Israel war on Iran is in its ninth month. Hormuz remains largely closed and the US naval blockade continues.

2. US Politics.

Democrats won both the House and the Senate in the 3 November midterms, per AP tallies. Republicans stay in charge of both chambers until January.

Speaker Mike Johnson said he will not seek to remain GOP leader, setting up a Scalise versus Jordan race.

Trump's approval averaged about 37% in polls through 1 November. A Reuters/Ipsos poll (9 November) found 71% favor legislation to halt the Iran war.

Trump warned of "devastating" retaliation if Iran targets senior US officials; Treasury Secretary Bessent was reportedly moved to a military base after Iranian threats.

Pence is leaving the door open to a 2028 run, saying voters rejected Trump's brand of Republicanism.

3. Congress.

The five-week lame-duck session began 9 November. The agenda includes a government funding deadline of 11 December, the farm bill, defense and FISA. Trump is threatening vetoes of anything off-agenda.

Senate Republicans plan to confirm nominees before the mid-December break, since Democrats are unlikely to advance them next year. Jeffries, Schumer and Thune are expected to keep their leadership posts.

Fox's Chad Pergram flags risk that the 120th Congress opens in January with a shutdown fight, as in 2018 to 19.

I did not locate any Iran war-powers floor votes this week.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Trump hosted Qatar's prime minister on 9 November and cited "positive momentum" toward ending the Iran war, saying new attacks are unnecessary.

The administration looks split: Rubio is backing the blockade while Vance is weighing direct talks with a senior Iranian security official (WSJ report).

Reuters reports Trump told advisers he may hold off on new Iran strikes unless Iran or the Houthis provoke the US, despite Israeli pressure.

The US keeps pressing for European participation in Ukraine peace talks, which Moscow resists. Kyiv reportedly rejected a US idea to redraw Donbas administrative borders.

US–China trade truce runs to January 2027, and Xi is slated to visit for the G20 Miami summit in December.

5. Middle East Politics.

Iran says it is waiting for a US answer to seven conditions: lifting sanctions and the blockade, an end to fighting on all fronts (Lebanon first), and more. Security chief Rezaei denies any direct talks.

Parliament speaker Ghalibaf says Hormuz will never return to its pre-war status. Trump claims the war is already won but wants "total victory."

Netanyahu is pressing Trump for a "third round" on Iran, and Mossad's chief was in Washington on Friday.

The UN says more than 1 million people in Lebanon face acute food insecurity. No major Hezbollah–Israel exchange was found for 11 to 12 November.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel: President Herzog gave Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government on 8 November (67 Knesset recommendations versus 46 for Netanyahu). He has 28 days, extendable by 14. Netanyahu stays on as caretaker.

The opposition is courting haredi parties, and Liberman is accused of stalling talks, which he denies. The new Knesset was sworn in 10 November.

Herzog urged the next government to intensify the fight against violent crime in Arab communities.

Palestinian legislative elections are officially set for 28 November, but factions and officials have discussed delay. I could not confirm the latest status.

Iran: Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei reportedly granted the government powers to speed up talks with Washington (IRNA, 2 November).

7. Middle East Defense News.

Defense Minister Katz said Israel's military mission in Iran is unfinished and threatened Hezbollah with further territorial loss if it joins an Iranian attack.

The IRGC rejected the US claim that Hormuz mines have all been cleared.

Iran's Rezaei threatened strikes on US bases "far away," and Iranian officials warned of targeting US bases in Europe if used against Iran.

Iran claims a strike on a US early-warning radar in Bahrain. This is unverified, and some of my results on similar Bahrain claims date from June–July.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

PM Rob Jetten is skipping the COP31 climate summit in Türkiye to secure support for the 2027 tax plan. His minority cabinet holds 66 of 150 seats, and he said support was close but not final on 6 November.

A 19-year-old was shot dead in Schiedam in a case of mistaken identity over a 500 eurosdebt. A teenager was arrested and two suspects are at large; four explosions followed in the Rotterdam area.

A fourth "Red Line" protest in Amsterdam on 8 November, with former mayors Cohen and van Aartsen taking part. Cohen criticized Jetten's leadership.

Trade unions walked out of talks with ASML over its reorganization, and a roadside-assistance strike hit motorists.

9. European Union News.

EU leaders meet again 26 November in Brussels, with Costa tasked to find a compromise ahead of a December summit (the EU's 2028 to 34 budget is the open file). The UK–EU summit is 20 November.

The October summit ended with no budget deal and a new 70 billion euros Ukraine funding gap on top of the 90 billion euros loan. A 22nd Russia sanctions package is in preparation.

The Commission plans to present a "DiversifyEU" supply-dependence mechanism in December. The EU–Western Balkans summit follows in Brussels in December.

Spain's 29 November snap election is being watched in Brussels because a PP-led government could shift EU migration and climate policy.

10. NATO News.

I found no major NATO decision for 9 to 15 November. Context: Rutte's "NATO 3.0" line, European allies and Canada reaching about 4% of GDP in defense-related spending, and a US force-posture review briefed by Colby in August.

ISW says Russia is widening demands to dismantle the post-Cold War security architecture in Europe, a direct challenge to NATO.

Rutte visited the 1st German-Netherlands Corps in Münster with Merz and Jetten. I could not confirm the date from the report.

New Zealand (7 November): no clear winner. Luxon's National (about 29%) and Labour (about 29%) both fell, a new entrant surged, and special votes decide the result on 27 November.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: the army and allied Joint Force claim gains in West and North Kordofan (including Al-Khuwei and Ayyal Bakhit, 7 to 8 November), but these are unconfirmed independently. Al Jazeera framed the army as gaining momentum in Kordofan in October, after the RSF's takeover of El-Fasher cemented its hold on Darfur. No confirmed humanitarian truce exists.

DR Congo: the Doha process with M23 is still alive (roadmap agreed 23 August, ceasefire monitors deployed 24 August), but fighting persists and the east also faces an Ebola outbreak.

Niger: a coup attempt was reportedly thwarted in early September, with Algeria backing the junta (CTP).

Watch: South Sudan's 22 December election amid ongoing instability and Gambia's 5 December vote. Coverage this week was thin: I found no fresh Mali/Sahel reporting from the last week.

13. Asia / India (brief)

APEC Shenzhen (18 to 19 November): Japan's PM Takaichi is seeking at least a greeting from Xi; a formal Japan–China bilateral is seen as unlikely.

Taiwan holds local elections 28 November.

India: no fresh headlines retrieved for this week.

14. OSINT Updates (unconfirmed / developing)

ISW assesses the Kremlin's request for a temporary localized ceasefire at Kostyantynivka would let Russia reinforce its southern flank before renewed pushes on Kostyantynivka and Dobropillya. This is analyst judgment, not confirmed.

Ukrainian officials report a sixth day of speed restrictions on SpaceX's Starshield network over parts of Ukraine, hampering defense against jet-powered drones.

An IED reportedly injured a former adviser to ex-Ukrainian PM Azarov in Moscow City on 13 November, and a 12 November incident in St. Petersburg reportedly targeted a Russian Air and Air Defense Forces colonel. Both are unconfirmed.

ISW notes Russia is using seized Western company assets to fund its war.

IRGC denial that Hormuz is mine-free conflicts with the US position; no independent imagery confirmation found. Iran's Bahrain radar claim (see §7) is likewise unverified.
