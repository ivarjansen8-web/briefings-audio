Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Headline: Iran stand-off drags on, Dutch coalition on the edge, Democrats line up their leaders.

World Politics.

Iran says it is still waiting for a US answer to its seven conditions for talks and denies negotiations are under way, while the IRGC says at least three ships hit mines in Hormuz. Trump, after meeting Qatar's PM on Monday, claims "positive momentum".

US-brokered Russia-Ukraine talks opened in Abu Dhabi on 10 November (Witkoff and Kushner mediating). No outcome had been reported in what I found; Russian strikes on Ukraine's grid continued beforehand.

Poland has opened a terrorism investigation after a 24-year-old gunman killed six people in central Krakow on 7 November.

Bangladesh's ex-PM Sheikh Hasina has asked for a new passport in what looks like preparation to return, despite a death sentence in absentia.

US Politics.

Democrats won both chambers on 3 November, with at least 54 Senate seats. They are leaning on cost of living, the Iran war and corruption as their agenda.

Trump told 60 Minutes that voters disliked the Iran war and higher gas prices, and that his immigration enforcement was probably too tough. He said he is open to a broader immigration deal.

Stephen Miller will replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff, announced 6 November.

Trump ordered the DOJ to look into "criminal acts" by Democratic lawmakers. AG Todd Blanche would not rule out prosecuting members over oversight activity.

Alaska Senate race is still too close to call: Democrat Mary Peltola leads Sen. Dan Sullivan by roughly a couple of hundred votes, with ranked-choice counting over the next two weeks.

Congress.

House Democrats hold their leadership vote on 18 November; Hakeem Jeffries is the frontrunner for Speaker (floor vote 3 January). Senate Democrats vote in early December.

Schumer says he has the votes to lead Senate Democrats again, though Chris Van Hollen has left the door open to a challenge.

Speaker Mike Johnson will not seek the House GOP leader post; Republican leadership elections are expected in mid-November.

Axios reports House GOP leaders expect enough defections to impeach War Secretary Pete Hegseth; Rep. Thomas Massie plans to force a vote this week. Removal would need 67 Senate votes.

The Senate has released a manager's amendment to the bipartisan permitting-reform bill (BAAJA); backers hope to pass it within two weeks.

US Foreign Policy.

Trump says Iran is "effectively over" militarily and that renewed strikes are highly unlikely if talks progress, but says 38 bridges are identified as targets if they resume.

Rubio reported no progress in talks and described US actions in Hormuz as defensive; in Paris he said Washington is open to diplomacy.

Kushner and Witkoff briefed partners (Ukraine, EU, NATO, Germany, France, UK, Italy) before the Abu Dhabi talks.

Middle East Politics.

Israel: President Herzog gave Gadi Eisenkot the first shot at forming a government (28 days plus a possible 14). Netanyahu's bloc has 53 seats, Eisenkot's 48, Arab parties 19, and Netanyahu alleges unsubstantiated foreign meddling.

Iran: Pezeshkian says Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered an end to the "no war, no peace" limbo. Araghchi says Hormuz could reopen within two days if demands are met.

Parliament speaker Ghalibaf says Hormuz will never return to its pre-war status and Tehran awaits an official US reply.

Iran hanged three men on 8 November over January protests.

Middle East Local Politics.

Gaza: the first winter storm flooded about 70,000 tents; a sand-cliff collapse in Deir al-Balah killed five, four of them children. Save the Children says shelter supplies are held up at the borders.

Gaza's Health Ministry says several transport firms have suspended services over unpaid dues and a lack of spare parts, threatening hospital operations.

Iran's FDA says US strikes damaged 15 drug makers and eight distributors, leaving 37 of about 700 essential medicines hard to supply.

Iraqi Kurdistan: a drone attack knocked the Khor Mor gas field offline, causing widespread power cuts; nobody has claimed it.

Middle East Defense News.

US forces hit Qeshm Island again on the night of 8 to 9 November; Iranian officials say outside residential and commercial zones. Explosions were also reported around Bandar Abbas, Sirik and Mahshahr.

The Pentagon counts 23 US service members killed since the war began in late February.

IRGC rejects the US claim that Hormuz is mine-free and warns ships off "unauthorised" routes.

Yemen: airstrikes blamed on Saudi Arabia killed at least 44 in a village near Sanaa, including 21 children. Houthis say they hit Riyadh, and debris damaged Aramco's HQ.

Dutch Local Headlines.

Cabinet crisis: D66 and CDA struck an income and assets tax deal with PRO and Volt that includes a millionaire's tax. VVD leader Yeşilgöz says her party was ambushed, will not vote for it, and the coalition's future depends on Thursday's debate. PM Jetten cancelled a trip to Ireland.

More than 200,000 people marched in Amsterdam on 8 November demanding a halt to arms sales to Israel.

Hundreds of Dutch sports clubs reported a cyberattack exposing members' personal data.

The Supreme Court ordered the government to reassess the security situation in Syria, which bears on asylum decisions.

Petrol prices hit their lowest since March.

European Union News.

EU-UK summit in Dublin next week; an EU-UK carbon market deal is reportedly close, and talk of an EU envoy for Ukraine peace talks is expected to dominate.

The Commission is hosting security talks after a week of bomb hoaxes that hit 20 airports and dozens of flights.

Member-state envoys are haggling over "Made in Europe" and low-carbon definitions in the industrial law; the Irish presidency wants a deal by year-end.

The Commission's Digital Fairness Act proposal targets stealth influencer ads and manipulative app design.

A watchdog report finds patchy customs controls leave loopholes in Russia trade sanctions.

NATO News.

Quiet day for formal NATO announcements in what I found. Rutte's side of the Ukraine track: US envoys consulted NATO and key allies before the Abu Dhabi talks.

Poland is reported to be reinforcing security on its border with Russia (headline only; details not retrieved).

Elections.

Israel (27 October): no bloc has a majority; Eisenkot now has the mandate to form a government.

Alaska Senate race still being counted (see US Politics).

The Gambia presidential election, 5 December; South Sudan general election, 22 December (the latter has a history of delays).

Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: On 8 November the army (SAF) and allies fought heavy battles with the RSF at Abu Minaiqir in West Kordofan, about 34 miles north of El-Khuwei, and shot down a long-range drone near Wad Banda, its third interception in Kordofan in 72 hours. The push west aims to secure routes for future Darfur operations.

The SAF also detained Ahmed Haroun, chair of al-Bashir's former ruling party and wanted by the ICC over Darfur; why now is unclear, since his Islamist movement has fought alongside the army.

Context: ACLED said in August that the RSF encirclement of El Obeid had collapsed after coordinated SAF offensives, but CTP reported the RSF countering via Blue Nile and pressing Khartoum-El Obeid supply lines.

Health: WHO approved vaccine trials for the Bundibugyo Ebola strain (reported 5 November).

Migration: a migrant boat capsized off Senegal on 7 November, killing at least 34.

Sahel: the latest CTP assessment I found (24 September) has JNIM intensifying pressure on Malian and Russian forces in central Mali; nothing fresher retrieved.

Asia / India.

Hasina passport request (see World Politics).
