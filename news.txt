Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Abu Dhabi talks open, Hegseth impeachment looms, Eisenkot tasked in Israel.

World Politics.

US-mediated Russia–Ukraine talks opened in Abu Dhabi on 10 November (first in nine months, about 4 hours); Zelensky called day one "constructive and quite substantive", Rubio said "very productive" but urged patience and has said no breakthrough is imminent.

G7 foreign ministers met in Paris 9 to 10 November (moved from Marseille on security grounds after weeks of protests); Europeans pressed Rubio for "no Ukraine deal without Europe" and no new Iran escalation.

Canada added 38 entities and 24 individuals to its Russia sanctions list on the G7 sidelines.

New Zealand's 7 November election produced no clear winner; weeks of coalition talks ahead between Luxon's National bloc (ACT 13 seats, NZ First 12) and Hipkins' Labour.

Ex-Bangladesh PM Sheikh Hasina asked for a new passport in New Delhi, the clearest sign yet she may return (she was sentenced to death in absentia).

Poland is treating Saturday's Krakow shooting (six killed) as terrorism.

US Politics.

Democrats won both chambers on 3 November; NBC's tracker had the Senate at 54 to 44 for Democrats, with Alaska and Mississippi not yet projected by ABC.

Trump (60 Minutes) blamed the loss partly on the Iran war and gas prices and said his immigration enforcement was "too tough", open to a comprehensive deal.

Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff.

Trump ordered DOJ to look at "criminal acts" by Democratic lawmakers; AG Blanche would not rule out prosecuting them over oversight.

Alaska Senate: Peltola leads Sullivan by roughly "a couple hundred votes" (48.2% versus 47.9%) with ranked-choice tabulation still to come.

Schumer's aides say he has the votes to stay Senate Democratic leader; Van Hollen says "the door is not shut".

Congress.

Lame-duck session began 9 November. Rep. Massie is expected to force an impeachment vote on Defense Secretary Hegseth this week; GOP leaders expect enough defections for it to pass the House, Trump says he will keep Hegseth, and removal would need 67 Senate votes.

Speaker Johnson will not seek the GOP leader post; Scalise versus Jordan was set for a Tuesday (10 November) vote. I did not find the result.

Government funding lapses 11 December when the current CR expires.

A bipartisan permitting-reform bill (BAAJA) got a manager's amendment; backers hope to pass it within two weeks.

Epstein files: Massie's discharge petition is reportedly two signatures short.

US Foreign Policy.

Rubio's first trip abroad since the midterms (Paris G7): pressed on Ukraine security guarantees, pushed UN reform and told allies the US wants action against the ICC.

Witkoff and Kushner lead the US team in Abu Dhabi; Europeans (France, Germany, UK, Italy, NATO, Commission) were also present in a side meeting.

Trump after meeting Qatar's PM: Iran war "militarily effectively over", further strikes "quite unlikely" but US is "locked and loaded"; Rubio earlier reported no progress.

NYT reports Rubio oversaw a classified memo warning of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence recruit.

Middle East Politics.

Iran: Pezeshkian says Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered an end to the "no war, no peace" limbo; Araghchi says Tehran's two-day Hormuz proposal stands, while security chief Rezaei denies planned talks and demands its seven conditions (end of blockade, unfrozen assets).

Israel: President Herzog tasked Gadi Eisenkot (not Netanyahu) with forming the government: 28 days plus a possible 14-day extension.

Lebanon is France's top diplomatic priority at the G7.

Middle East Local Politics.

Israel: Likud is the largest party (27 seats) ahead of Eisenkot's Yashar (21); Netanyahu's bloc has 53 and Eisenkot's 48, short of 61; Arab-led parties won a record 19. New MKs are sworn in this evening and Netanyahu stays caretaker.

Netanyahu's fraud claims against Arab parties were rejected by the election commission; Degel HaTorah backed neither candidate.

Gaza: winter storm flooded about 70,000 tents; five killed in a sand-cliff collapse in Deir al-Balah; transport firms suspended services over unpaid dues.

Iran hanged three men in Karaj over January protest-related charges.

Middle East Defense News.

US forces hit Qeshm Island (Strait of Hormuz) again overnight 8 to 9 November; Trump called earlier strikes "minor" and said targets incl. 38 bridges are identified if fighting resumes. Pentagon count: 23 US service members killed since late February.

Yemen: strikes blamed on Saudi Arabia killed at least 44 (21 children) in al-Shariya near Sanaa; Houthis say they hit Riyadh and Aramco's HQ was damaged by debris.

Drone attack on Iraqi Kurdistan's Khor Mor gas field (8 November) knocked it offline and caused regional blackouts; no claim.

Gaza: three killed in 24h under the ceasefire; 1,613 killed since 11 October 2025 per the Health Ministry.

Dutch Local Headlines.

200,000+ marched in Amsterdam ("Red Line", 8 November) demanding an arms embargo on Israel; PM Jetten skipped it, said it was "not his place" and ruled out an embargo or a "genocide" declaration; ex-mayor Job Cohen criticised him.

Little else fresh surfaced for NL today; the Jetten minority cabinet (66 of 150 seats) stays under pressure.

European Union News.

Commission preliminarily found TikTok in breach of the Digital Services Act over its "rabbit hole" recommender effect (6 November).

Von der Leyen announced 50 million euros for a Black Sea Maritime Security Hub (Constanța/Varna) on 10 November.

This week: EU Drone Summit (9 to 10 November), COP31 in Antalya (9 to 20 November), European Parliament plenary (11 to 12 November); the Commission published its 2027 work programme and enlargement assessments on 4 November.

NATO News.

No major NATO decisions found today. NATO and SACEUR representatives took part in the Abu Dhabi side meeting; allies at the Ankara summit (July) pledged 70 billion euros for Ukraine in 2026.

Russian strikes cut power for 600,000+ in Dnipropetrovsk and 250,000 in Kharkiv region ahead of the talks.

Elections.

Upcoming: Cabo Verde presidential, 15 November (six candidates; incumbent Neves strong favourite, runoff 29 November if no majority).

Taiwan local elections 28 November; Gambia presidential 5 December; South Sudan general 22 December (often postponed). IDEA also lists Spain's general election for 29 November, which I could not confirm, so treat it with caution.

Just happened: US midterms (3 November): Democrats took the House and Senate; Alaska Senate still being counted. New Zealand (7 November): hung result. Israel (27 October): no bloc majority.

Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: SAF is pushing west in Kordofan: heavy fighting at Abu Minaiqir (about 34 miles north of El-Khuwei, West Kordofan) and a third long-range drone downed near Wad Banda in 72 hours, to secure routes toward Darfur. SAF also detained Ahmed Haroun, Bashir-era NCP chair wanted by the ICC; motive unclear, as his Islamist movement has fought alongside the army. Context: RSF besieged El-Obeid from June; the army reopened the Khartoum–El-Obeid road in late July.

Sahel: al-Qaeda's JNIM and Islamic State Sahel Province are fighting each other across the Mali–Burkina–Niger borderlands, spreading into western Niger and NW Nigeria; ISSP claimed 400+ JNIM dead on 12 October (unverified). JNIM and the FLA also launched offensives across Mali in July.

DRC/Ethiopia: Critical Threats (15 October) assesses M23 has rebranded with Kabila's camp as the "FRN", likely a political relabel of the existing alliance; it also reports Ethiopian forces reaching the Eritrean border.

Senegal: at least 34 migrants died when a pirogue capsized off Kébémer.

Asia / India (brief)

Hasina's passport request is the main South Asia item (see World Politics).

A single-source macro note flags a Trump–Xi meeting at APEC in Shenzhen "next week"; unverified.

Quiet otherwise; searches for fresh India/China news returned mostly stale items.

OSINT Updates (unconfirmed or developing unless noted)

Unconfirmed: OSINT group AviVector (via Defense Express/UNITED24, 8 to 9 November) reports 47 Russian Tu-160/Tu-95MS bombers massed at Ukrainka airbase in the Far East, possibly a record concentration; nine recently relocated bombers may have joined the 3 November missile strike, not independently confirmed.

Contested: the IRGC says two tankers burned near southern Hormuz on 10 November (one on a mine, one allegedly under US fighter cover); CENTCOM has not confirmed.

Reported: BBC satellite analysis shows new construction at two Iranian missile sites and repairs at other bases; separate unconfirmed vessels were noted near subsea cables after Iranian cable threats.

Developing: a Ukrainian OSINT source says Russian forces are using mannequin decoys along roads and in dugouts to fool automated drone targeting (ISW, 8 November).
