Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

World. Russia hit Kyiv and Ukraine's power grid overnight with 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones, damaging the Northern Bridge, just days before US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi on the 10th and 11th. Spain heads to a snap election on 29 November. And APEC leaders meet in Shenzhen on the 18th and 19th.

United States. After the midterms, Democrats hold 254 House seats to 163 on CNN's count, with about 20 races uncalled. The Senate races in Mississippi and Alaska are still too close to call. Trump says he has told the Justice Department to look into Democrats pushing investigations. Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as chief of staff. In Congress, Thomas Massie says he will force a vote next week to impeach Defense Secretary Hegseth, and several Republican sources expect it to pass.

US foreign policy. The naval blockade of Iran continues, and US forces disabled another ship trying to run it. Treasury Secretary Bessent reportedly asked Beijing for help ending the Iran war. Washington also plans to ask NATO allies for more money to buy weapons for Ukraine.

Middle East. President Herzog is due to give Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form Israel's next government. Eisenkot wants an emergency government without extremists, while Likud is preparing to appeal the election result. Qatar says its mediation between the US and Iran continues, and the Revolutionary Guards say Iran's self-imposed missile range limit has been lifted. In the Gulf, a tanker reportedly hit a mine and caught fire. In Yemen, Saudi strikes on Sanaa killed at least 22 according to Houthi media, and the Houthis claim attacks on Riyadh airport and Aramco sites. In Gaza, heavy rain flooded around 70,000 tents.

The Netherlands. The cabinet is postponing its Box 3 wealth-tax plans by a year. The Netherlands nominated Klaas Knot to lead the European Central Bank. Inflation rose to 3.7 percent in October. A Czech man was arrested at Schiphol over a false bomb threat. And the first autumn frost has arrived, with weekend temperatures of 5 to 10 degrees.

Europe and NATO. EU finance ministers meet on Monday, followed by a European Political Community summit on the 12th and an EU–UK summit on the 20th. Coalition of the Willing exercises begin on Monday, which Moscow calls unacceptable. In Poland, a shooting in Krakow's main square killed five people.

Elections. Besides Spain, Somalia's Supreme Court ordered elections within 90 days after ruling the president's term had expired. In South Africa's local elections, the ANC is at about 31 percent with most votes counted.

Africa and Asia. A UN report links a UAE-connected firm to Colombian mercenaries fighting for the RSF in Sudan. Tensions are rising between Ethiopia and Eritrea. Hong Kong jailed Joshua Wong for 10 years. And Asian markets fell about 6 percent on Wednesday.

Open-source intelligence. Analysts say Russia is upgrading its long-range drones with jet engines, while Ukraine's stock of interceptors runs low.
