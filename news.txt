Daily News Briefing — 25 November 2026: Iran leadership-strike debate, Houthis hit Riyadh, Baghdad moves on Kurdish oil.

1. World Politics.

The US is preparing new Section 301 tariffs on 16 economies (China, EU, Japan, Mexico, India and others) over "excess capacity", to replace the tariffs the Supreme Court struck down; China is expected to face the steepest rise. Fiscal Times ·.

Ukraine hit the Syzran and Kuibyshev refineries overnight. Putin admits the refinery campaign is a heavy burden; ISW estimates it has cut Russian refining by about 30% since late October. The Global Eye ·.

Zelensky confirmed that Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in Benghazi, Libya, and hit the sanctioned cargo ship Angara, which takes the war far beyond the Black Sea.

China has put Shanghai party chief and Politburo member Chen Jining under investigation. He is the fourth member of the 2022 Politburo to be probed and had been tipped for the Standing Committee. Xinhua ·.

Russia arrested Murmansk Governor Andrei Chibis, the first sitting governor arrested since the full-scale invasion.

The COP31 climate talks collapsed after the EU and others rejected the final deal.

2. US Politics.

Trump's role in reinstating Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is drawing Republican criticism. Huang had resigned over an affair with a woman allegedly linked to Chinese intelligence. Trump says a pardon is "always an option", and his Nvidia stake rose by up to 15 million dollars. NBC · Democracy Now ·.

A US jury convicted Huawei of conspiring to steal trade secrets and of bank fraud.

Trump gave Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom and pardoned the Thanksgiving turkeys.

States are suing HUD over a rule that would end housing aid to mixed-status families.

Joe Kaspar, Hegseth's former chief of staff, says the Defense Secretary should resign over his handling of the Pentagon and the Iran war.

3. Congress.

Congress is out for Thanksgiving. Funding expires December 11 and there is no deal yet. Several Senate Democrats (Kaine, Van Hollen, Duckworth, Bennet, Sanders) want limits on Iran-war spending in any stopgap, and a stopgap needs 60 Senate votes. Harlem Times ·.

The White House sent a list of "anomalies" for a stopgap running to March 31, including 5 billion dollars for Golden Dome and 2 billion dollars for Artemis. US Brief ·.

The Nvidia affair is raising concern in both parties; Newt Gingrich urged Congress to investigate it as a national-security issue.

Lame-duck context: Democrats take both chambers on January 3. Republicans are weighing a debt-limit increase and a party-line reconciliation package before then.

4. US Foreign Policy.

NBC reports that officials are debating whether to target Iran's senior leaders to end the nine-month war. No decision has been made, and some military leaders warn of retaliation risks. The naval blockade and Task Force Iron Gate continue. Times of Israel ·.

Rubio and the Saudi foreign minister condemned the Houthi attacks and also discussed freedom of navigation in Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb, Sudan, and Hezbollah's disarmament. CBS ·.

Rubio and Vance met Qatar's PM during the 8th US–Qatar Strategic Dialogue; Doha is trying to revive US–Iran talks. Just Security ·.

Rubio joins Trump in Palm Beach today, where Trump meets NATO chief Rutte at Mar-a-Lago. State Dept ·.

Zelensky says Witkoff and Kushner are pushing for a Ukraine peace deal by January. He warns against concessions "at Ukraine's expense" and wants more aid and sanctions if the talks fail.

The Pentagon publicly rebuked Canada as falling short on defending North America and on its NATO commitments.

The administration is again threatening unilateral military action inside Mexico over cartels.

5. Middle East Politics.

Iran: chief negotiator and parliament speaker Qalibaf says there will be no talks with the US until Iran's "seven conditions" are met. The IRGC claims more tankers hit mines in Hormuz on "unauthorized routes"; CENTCOM has disputed earlier claims of this kind. AP via Click2Houston ·.

Israel: Eisenkot's Yashar signed its first coalition deal, with Lieberman's party, which gives the pair 48 seats. The left-wing Democrats suspended talks because of Eisenkot–Likud unity talks. His mandate runs to December 6, and Netanyahu remains caretaker PM.

Kushner warned Israel of "global-pariah status" as the Gaza plan stalls. Huckabee cast doubt on ending the 3.8 billion dollars in annual US military aid this year. Times of Israel ·.

Israeli Defense Minister Katz claims Iran is developing chemical weapons.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Iraq: PM Ali al-Zaidi ordered the federal Oil Ministry to take over Kurdish-secured oilfields in Kirkuk, Nineveh, Diyala and Salahuddin, and federal authorities to take over the Kurdistan Region's border crossings, after an oil-smuggling probe. KRG PM Masrour Barzani rejects the order, and the KDP and PUK call it a "red line". Al Jazeera ·.

Iraq separately accuses Kataib Hezbollah of running an oil-smuggling network from Jurf al-Sakhar; last week Baghdad threatened to send forces in.

Israel: Katz is seeking to censor a Kan report on the IDF's use of human shields in Gaza, and Netanyahu's outreach to UTJ lawmakers has reportedly failed.

Lebanon: President Aoun rejects direct Israeli supervision of Hezbollah's disarmament but would accept an international verification mechanism. Tension is also reported inside the government over how fast disarmament is going.

7. Middle East Defense News.

A Houthi drone hit the PIF tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, injuring 11. It was the second strike on the district in under a week. The Saudi-led coalition then struck Hodeidah port and targets in Saada and Amran, and today intercepted 4 drones over Ras Tanura. Aaj/Reuters ·.

Israel killed Hamas internal-security chief Naim Abu Nahal in Gaza City; at least 9 others died in separate strikes. Washington Post ·.

Yemen's army says it killed 27 Houthis in Taiz. Israeli forces raided homes and detained a civilian in southern Syria.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Prosecutors are seeking life imprisonment over the Tarwekamp explosion in The Hague that killed six.

New figures show the Dutch poor getting poorer and more workers living below the poverty line.

Police in Utrecht found a body in a freezer on the street and are investigating a possible crime.

A regional public-transport strike disrupted trains, buses and trams for a second day. The country is heading for its coldest November in 30 years, with frost and fog.

Politics: the Jetten minority cabinet (D66/CDA/VVD) is still recovering from a near-collapse on November 17 and has no broad majority for its tax plan. NL Times ·.

The Commission will introduce provisional import quotas on hybrid passenger cars in December.

Brussels warned Spain it may face an excessive-deficit procedure in 2027.

The EU is among the targets of the expected US Section 301 tariffs; reporting suggests a 15% cap in line with the earlier deal.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: the drone war is escalating. The UN counts at least 2,230 civilians killed from January to October, more than 1,630 of them by drones. Dawn strikes on Khartoum and Omdurman killed four and damaged a UN building; the UN and US condemned them. Khartoum says the drones are launched from Ethiopia (it names Bahir Dar), says it has documented 187 one-way attack drones since February, and has complained to the UNSC and AU. Ethiopia denies backing the RSF. On the ground, an ACLED analyst says the army has retaken more than 30 villages and towns in North and West Kordofan since early November. In Blue Nile, renewed fighting has displaced thousands, and SIHA reports 28 women and girls abducted. About 19.5M people face crisis-level hunger or worse. Addis Standard · Rio Times ·.

Sudan–Ethiopia: after reciprocal expulsions of diplomats, Sudanese opposition figure Yasir Arman is urging IGAD and the AU to mediate; Crisis Group warns of a widening proxy war. Amnesty separately reported abuses in Tigray. Sudan Tribune ·.

Somalia: after expelling the UAE ambassador on November 19, Mogadishu's break with Abu Dhabi is tied to a domestic power struggle with UAE-aligned regional leaders. Al Jazeera ·.

DR Congo Ebola (Bundibugyo): 11,504 confirmed cases and 5,630 deaths as of November 17, the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record. Cases have also been reported in Kenya and Nigeria, and funding pledges are not arriving.

Hormuz: IRGC claims of mine strikes cannot be checked independently and CENTCOM has called earlier ones false. Reports that around 14 Iranian tankers are massing off Kharg to run the blockade are unconfirmed. Euronews ·.

Iraq: a classified Interior Ministry report, revealed by the press, reportedly used satellite imagery of tapped federal pipelines and tanker movements behind the Kurdish oil order. It has not been independently verified.
