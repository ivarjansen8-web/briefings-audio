Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Headline: Russian missiles land in Poland as Iran war talks stall and Hormuz attacks mount.

1. World Politics.

Trump, Xi and Putin held a first-ever three-way leaders' meeting at APEC in Shenzhen (19 November). Their joint statement calls for UN reform and more fossil-fuel output, warns the ICC not to "abuse" its jurisdiction, and says "there can be no winners in a nuclear war". No breakthroughs on Ukraine or Iran.

Denmark is expelling Russia's defence attaché after its intelligence service PET said Russian intelligence planned the killing of US-Ukrainian rapper Kyivstoner, using gang-linked hitmen, before a September concert. Moscow denies it.

A Russian strike on Kryvyi Rih, Zelenskyy's hometown, killed 2 and wounded about 60. Kyiv was also hit again.

The ICJ ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, and the EU welcomed the judgment.

2. US Politics.

At a White House presser with Vance and Rubio (20 November), Trump said Iran "wants a deal" but its terms are unacceptable. He wants a "permanent" deal and called Vance's claim that the Iran war cost the GOP the midterms "very accurate".

Trump ordered federal contractors to consult the White House before answering congressional inquiries and to withhold communications deemed privileged. The order comes ahead of Democratic-led oversight in January.

Trump and outgoing Sen. John Cornyn are feuding after Cornyn blocked his Dallas judicial nominee.

3. Congress.

The Senate rejected the concurrent resolution H.Con.Res. 93 by 48 to 51 on 19 November. The House passed H.R. 4464 by 401 to 0 the same day.

Both chambers are on recess until 30 November. Senate Democratic leadership elections, with a challenge to Schumer, are set for 1 December, and House Democratic chair races for 9 December.

A bipartisan bill on sexually explicit deepfakes is still stalled despite rare cross-party support.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Trump said a next move on Iran may not come until early 2027. US envoys met Qatari mediators with no progress reported, and Treasury added sanctions on Iranian "trustees" handling illicit oil revenue.

The State Department said it "expects" Iraqi PM al-Zaidi to hold Iran-aligned militias accountable.

The US imposed new sanctions on Hezbollah.

5. Middle East Politics.

US–Iran talks remain stalled. Tehran's FM set a red line on enriched uranium while saying a "fair solution" is possible.

On Independence Day, Lebanese President Aoun demanded Israel's withdrawal from the south and criticised Iranian interference. UNIFIL keeps handing positions to the Lebanese army ahead of its mandate ending on 31 December.

Turkish FM Fidan said Ankara, a member of the Mecca Defense Alliance with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, prioritises defending Saudi Arabia. He said no decision has been made on sending troops to Yemen.

A Board of Peace adviser warned the board could hand the UN "the keys" to Gaza if Israel doesn't honour its commitments, blaming a "standstill" on Netanyahu-government politics.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel: PM-designate Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) invited Likud to "marathon" coalition talks. Bennett backs a unity government under Eisenkot but rules out Netanyahu "under any circumstances". Likud demands an end to "boycotts" of Netanyahu. About 29 days remain on the mandate, and Eisenkot says he will form a minority government by 6 December if talks fail.

Iraq: Under a compromise deal, security forces deployed into Kataib Hezbollah's Jurf al-Sakhar stronghold after PM al-Zaidi accused the militia of large-scale oil smuggling. Zaidi: "From Jurf al-Sakhar, we begin asserting state authority."

Iraq: A fire at Basra's Hartha power station killed at least 4 and injured 35.

West Bank/Israel: Israel issued demolition orders for two buildings in Hebron's Area A. Arab-sector crime violence continued, including a car bomb in Tira and a shooting in Reineh.

7. Middle East Defense News.

The IRGC said it struck US air-defence radars at Bahrain's Al-Rafa and Awali bases. Bahrain reported a fire at an industrial facility in Sitra and no injuries.

Hormuz: one tanker hit a mine east of the strait, according to Iran's navy, and UKMTO says two tankers were hit by "unknown projectiles" near Khasab. Iran also claims it downed US drones.

The Saudi-led coalition intercepted 3 Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and 2 at Jazan, after coalition strikes on Houthi depots in Sanaa and Hodeidah.

Israeli strikes continued in south Lebanon (Nabatieh, Bint Jbeil, Marjayoun) and Gaza, where a strike on a food-aid vehicle killed 3.

Trump is reportedly weighing another Iran offensive as Netanyahu pushes a 4 to 7 day campaign.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

PM Jetten voiced support for Denmark over the alleged Russian murder plot.

The D66–VVD–CDA minority cabinet carries on after a confidence crisis. Parliament is sceptical that the cabinet's "restart" will change much.

Nearly 60% of Dutch municipalities no longer require residents to separate waste at home.

A report says employees of Dutch arms firm Destinus are taking part in the Ukraine war. FvD expelled members over neo-Nazi cage-fighting events.

9. European Union News.

At the EU–UK summit in Brussels (20 November), PM Andy Burnham and Costa/von der Leyen agreed a reset package: an agri-food (SPS) deal, emissions-trading links and a youth mobility scheme. Farage accuses Burnham of "betraying Brexit".

Costa presented a revised negotiating box for the next MFF (long-term budget) on 21 November.

The EU and Indonesia signed trade and investment agreements.

Euro-area inflation is reported at 4.0%. Fisheries ministers meet Monday (23 November) on 2027 quotas.

10. NATO News.

Two Russian missiles, including a Kh-101, and a suspected jet drone came down in SE Poland (20 November), about 35 km from European drills for a possible post-war Ukraine mission. There were no injuries. Tusk called it an "intentional provocation".

Rutte called it a "grave violation", visited Warsaw and pledged to defend "every inch". The North Atlantic Council discusses it Monday, and Poland seeks more Eastern Sentry support. Article 4 has not been formally invoked.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Ethiopia–Eritrea: War fears are rising around Badme. ACLED reports Ethiopian drone strikes on likely Eritrean positions (12 November) and Eritrean artillery fire. Both sides are massing forces as Ethiopia's ENDF pushes Tigrayan TDF fighters toward the border. Eritrea accuses Addis of an "expansionist agenda". A US lawmaker is floating trade incentives to de-escalate.

Sudan: The army is advancing in Kordofan as RSF defections grow. Rights groups accuse the RSF of killing civilians, including children, in West Kordofan (Abu Zabad, Abu Qalb) and 30 villagers in North Kordofan. The RSF and SPLM-N are pressing in Blue Nile toward Ed Damazin. The UN counts at least 2,230 civilians killed Jan–October, more than 1,630 of them by drones, and Türk says this may amount to war crimes.

Elsewhere: South Africa summoned Nigeria's envoy amid strained ties. Uganda's Besigye treason bail hearing is set for 25 November.

13. Asia/India (brief)

After hosting APEC, China gained diplomatic stature, while Trump said Beijing's nuclear buildup is "hot and heavy".

Trump says US tech firms that build AI data centres abroad will be penalised, which affects Asian and Gulf projects.

Myanmar: the ICJ's genocide ruling on the Rohingya (see World) puts new pressure on the Min Aung Hlaing government.

14. OSINT Updates (developing / partly unconfirmed)

Kerch Bridge: Vantor WorldView-1 imagery (19 November) shows a destroyed road span and fresh Neptune impact points on the rail span. The rail section appears damaged but not cut.

Badme: Trackers disagree on who controls the town. ACLED says Eritrea regained it by 15 November, CTP has logged no fighting there since, and an AFP source claims a brief Ethiopian incursion into Eritrea. Unconfirmed by either government.

Poland impact sites: Geolocated sites near Nowy Lipowiec (Kh-101), Rudnik nad Sanem and Mołodycz (jet drone) line up with official statements. Analysts debate whether the strikes were deliberate, and some link the barrage to Putin's promised retaliation for the Kerch strikes. Intent unestablished.

Baltic: The Russian cargo ship Sparta II is burning in the Gulf of Finland after a drone strike, and its crew was evacuated. Attribution is unclear.

Hormuz: Iran's claim of downed MQ-9s is unverified. Tanker strike patterns point to a growing mine/projectile threat east of the strait.
