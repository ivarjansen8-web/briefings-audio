Here is your daily news briefing for Monday, 23 November.

Headline: Trump eyes post-New Year Iran strikes as Gulf attacks mount; COP31 collapses; Democrats hit 258 House seats.

1. World Politics.

COP31 ends without a deal. Turkish COP31 president Murat Kurum closed the Antalya summit with no overarching decision, blaming some countries for a lack of "flexibility"; Guterres said he was "deeply disappointed."

Turkey media crackdown sparks protest. Several thousand marched in Istanbul on Sunday after police raided Sözcü TV and installed a government trustee, the third such takeover in a year after Halk TV, Tele1 and T24.

Ukraine: Russia launched 186 drones overnight on 21 to 22 November; a strike on an apartment block in Dymer (Kyiv region) killed 11, per Ukrainian officials. Zelensky says about 2,700 drones and 165 missiles were fired over the past week.

Libya drawn into the Russia–Ukraine war. A Russian vessel at Benghazi naval base and a commercial ship offshore were hit by drone boats. Eastern Libya's government filed a UN complaint and demanded that Ukraine explain its "military activities in Libya"; UNSMIL has called for an independent probe.

2. US Politics.

Trump's first post-midterm news conference: he vowed to veto the new Democratic Congress's bills but left room for bipartisan deals, including on health care, after returning from a summit with Xi and Putin in Shenzhen.

Trump weighs in on the Senate Democratic leadership race, posting that it would be "hard" to work with Schumer, without endorsing Van Hollen.

Thanksgiving week: the White House receives its Christmas tree today, and the turkey pardon is Tuesday at 2 p.m. ET.

3. Congress.

Democrats reach 258 House seats, their largest majority since 1992 (a net gain of 44), after Chuck Hubbard ousted Rep. Virginia Foxx (NC-5) by 330 votes following a recount.

Both chambers are out for Thanksgiving during the lame-duck session; they return 30 November.

Last week's votes: the House passed the Earth MRI Reauthorization Act (396 to 14) and the Preventive Health Savings Act (401 to 0); the Senate approved a permitting overhaul, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act.

Schumer versus Van Hollen for Senate Democratic leader is set for a secret ballot on 1 December. Schumer says he has the votes; Sanders backs Van Hollen.

Incoming leader Jeffries says ending the "reckless, costly" Iran war will be a Democratic priority.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Iran: Trump told Fox he is "inclined" to resume strikes after the New Year if no deal is reached. He called Iranian negotiators "professional tap dancers" and warned that ships testing the US blockade would end up "at the bottom of the sea."

Rubio meets Qatari PM/FM Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani today (2 p.m. ET) in Washington. Qatar is the main channel to Tehran.

Ynet reports that Washington has put off any strike by at least a week despite pressure from Netanyahu.

Sudan: the US urged a halt to the flow of weapons and a "humanitarian truce" after RSF drone strikes on Khartoum.

5. Middle East Politics.

Lebanon: on Independence Day, President Aoun demanded Israel withdraw from the south and denounced Iranian "interference," saying the time for weapons outside the state is over.

Iran's position: Tehran says diplomacy is still open but conditions any Hormuz reopening on its demands: an end to the war on all fronts, unfrozen assets, lifted oil sanctions and an end to the US blockade.

Gulf solidarity: the UAE, Qatar and Saudi Arabia condemned Iran's attacks on Bahrain.

Libya: Egypt denies withdrawing its military experts from Benghazi, and Libya's parliament rejected the country's use as a foreign battleground.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel coalition talks: Eisenkot's Yashar party and Likud met Sunday and agreed to further talks on a unity government; Bennett's Together party did not attend. Eisenkot rules out a rotation with Netanyahu, threatens a minority "national emergency government" and faces a 6 December deadline.

West Bank: settlers torched vehicles in four villages (Kafr Malik, Deir Abu Mashaal, Deir as-Sudan, Al-Tuwani). Israeli police opened a probe, and Sa'ar condemned the "rampage."

Haredi draft protests blocked roads and the light rail in Jerusalem over jailed yeshiva students.

Lebanon: PM Salam condemned an Israeli influencer's video shot at a school in the south as a "violation of the sanctity of schools."

7. Middle East Defense News.

Iran–Bahrain: the IRGC claims missile and drone waves on the US Riffa, Awali, Isa and Juffair bases. Bahrain says it intercepted several and that an industrial site was hit, with no casualties; the US denies a radar was destroyed.

Hormuz: two more tankers were hit on Saturday, one by an "unknown projectile" (per UKMTO) and one by a mine, according to Iran. Iran also claims it downed a US drone over Qeshm, where US jets struck an IRGC missile site on 17 November.

CENTCOM is at "maximum readiness" and pushing for a joint strike with Israel, i24NEWS reports, citing Israeli officials (unconfirmed).

Houthis versus Saudi Arabia: a missile struck Riyadh's 80-storey PIF Tower on 19 November, the second hit on the financial district in 11 days. The Saudi coalition says it destroyed a launcher in Dhamar.

Iran unveiled a new missile launcher as commanders touted wartime capabilities.

Israeli Defense Minister: no withdrawal from the "security zones" until Gaza and Hezbollah are disarmed. Israeli strikes continue in Gaza, including near Mawasi.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Regional public transport strike today: fewer buses, trams and regional trains are running.

PM Jetten hosted the UK PM and partners at the Rijksmuseum to discuss Ukraine.

Jetten cabinet steadied: D66, VVD and CDA say trust was restored after a four-day coalition crisis (16 November).

Climate minister Van Veldhoven called the COP31 outcome "disappointing."

Also: 8 people were injured when an escalator ran out of control at Bijlmer ArenA; bird flu at an Overijssel farm brings this season's cull to 250,000; a delay in the Rotterdam CO2-storage project is costing the government 400 million euros; KNMI warns of frost and icy roads.

9. European Union News.

The Commission disbursed 7.9 billion euros to Ukraine (20 November) and proposed up to 45 billion euros for 2027 to help close an estimated 70 billion euros financing gap.

The second EU–UK summit was held in Brussels on 20 November, with a joint statement issued.

The Commission approved 9.5 billion euros in Dutch State aid for two North Sea offshore wind farms. A provisional deal was also reached on faster permitting for grids and clean energy.

10. NATO News.

Rutte addresses the NATO Parliamentary Assembly in Berlin today (11:35 CET), with a press conference alongside Merz at 15:35.

Coalition of the Willing (19 November, Berlin): more than 30 leaders attended, plus US envoys Witkoff and Kushner; Rutte praised the latest Ukraine Defence Contact Group pledges.

Allied intelligence chiefs met in Brussels (18 November) on Russia's war and its hybrid activity.

Eastern flank: a Russian jet drone twice violated Moldovan airspace on 21 November.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: drone strikes hit Khartoum on Sunday, damaging homes and a UN building, and killed at least 3 to 4 people across Greater Khartoum and North Omdurman. A Sudanese army source claims the drones came from Ethiopia, which Addis Ababa denies. UN figures say drones killed more than 1,100 people in H1 2026 and cause about 75% of civilian deaths. Separately, the RSF reportedly raided Abu Qalb in West Kordofan, and the army struck RSF headquarters in Nyala (20 November). The war has displaced 14M+ people, and Washington is now openly pressing for a truce and an end to the arms flow (widely seen as aimed at the UAE).

Ethiopia–Eritrea on the brink: both armies have massed near Badme. ACLED logged Ethiopian drone strikes and Eritrean artillery on 12 to 13 November, and residents report overnight strikes around 19 November. Addis accuses Asmara of backing the Tigrayan/"Alliance for Survival" offensive since September, while Eritrea alleges an Ethiopian invasion. The G7 warned of "regional escalation." The risk is a wider Horn war drawing in Egypt, Sudan and Somalia (the Cairo-hosted "quadrilateral" bloc).

Libya: the Benghazi drone-boat attacks pull Libya's east–west divide into great-power rivalry. Hammad's eastern government (aligned with Haftar and Russia) is demanding that Ukraine account for any presence in western Libya.

Senegal: the ruling Pastef party expelled President Faye after his split with PM Sonko, a major rupture in the governing project.

13. Asia/India (brief)

North Korea's Kim says the country seeks "security, not supremacy."

Pakistan PM Shehbaz Sharif will visit Russia on 30 November, then Iran for the ECO summit.

The Lowy Asia Power Index shows India's gap with China at its narrowest since 2018.

South Korea says its naval deployment is anti-piracy, not participation in the US–Iran war.

Philippine lawmakers seek full disclosure of US deals; Manila and Beijing reaffirm ASEAN ties.

14. OSINT Updates (unconfirmed / developing)

Russian corvette "Mercury" likely sunk at Benghazi. Satellite imagery shared by OSINT analysts (via ASTRA) shows extensive damage and flooding. The second vessel hit may be the sanctioned bulk carrier ASCALON. ISW assesses that Ukrainian forces struck both, destroying at least one.

Baltiysk (Kaliningrad) air defence activity, 21 November: possibly the first drone attack on the Baltic Fleet base. Dnipro Osint geolocated the footage to 54.6501, 19.9053. No damage is confirmed, and the object reportedly came from the direction of Lithuania.

Ethiopia front: trackers Sajid Nadeem and Ethiopian Conflict Map report a broad Eritrean mobilization and federal capture of Adi Arbate near the border.

Kharkiv/Donetsk: Russia claims Sosnovyi Bir; ISW notes advances by both sides southwest of Druzhkivka and Dobropillia, and the Azov Brigade reports renewed Russian mechanized assaults.

Sudan: trackers flag reports of UAE IL-76 cargo flights resupplying the RSF and Yale HRL-confirmed SAF bombardment of En Nahud.
