Daily Briefing — Friday, 20 November 2026.

1. World Politics.

Trump–Xi–Putin summit in Shenzhen: The first-ever trilateral summit produced a "Declaration toward Peace," committing the three to talks on reducing nuclear risk and on non-proliferation. No breakthroughs were announced. China says Ukraine, the Middle East and the Korean Peninsula were discussed.

Coalition of the Willing in Berlin: Merz, Macron and UK PM Burnham hosted 30+ leaders, including Zelensky, to discuss security guarantees and peace efforts. US envoys Witkoff and Kushner attended.

ICJ rules on Myanmar: The court found that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya.

Somalia–UAE rift: Somalia gave the UAE ambassador 72 hours to leave. The UAE called the decision "irresponsible."

Greenland: Denmark's parliament unanimously approved the Denmark–Greenland–US defence agreement, which expands the US military presence on Greenland.

2. US Politics.

Trump returns from Asia: He holds a White House news conference at 12:00 ET today and has disclosed little about the summit so far. Schumer called the trip a "historical embarrassment."

Tech outages: After a week of outages, the administration says they are unrelated and not malicious, and Trump told Americans to "relax." National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving, and Pentagon CIO Davies takes over in an acting role.

NSA network: NSA Net, the agency's classified internal network, was reported partly down for a third straight day. Officials cite "routine maintenance" and say there is no evidence of a cyberattack.

Trump stock trades: Disclosures show 700+ trades in September, including sales of Nvidia, Meta and Microsoft.

Rubio and 2028: Allies say Rubio is more seriously weighing a 2028 presidential run.

3. Congress.

Iran war powers: The Senate rejected the House-passed Iran war powers resolution (H.Con.Res. 93) 48 to 51. Collins, Murkowski and Paul voted yes; Fetterman voted no.

Permitting bill: Cloture on H.R. 2347 was invoked 65 to 31, and post-cloture debate continues.

Judicial confirmations: Anna St. John was confirmed to the 5th Circuit (52 to 46) and Daniel Domenico to the 10th Circuit (51 to 46) earlier this week.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Iran: Trump claims there is an "indication" that Iran is pursuing chemical and biological weapons and says "we'll see what happens after January 1." The White House is reportedly weighing strike options that would not trigger attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure. Tehran calls the claims "fraudulent."

New sanctions: The State Department announced sanctions on a Hezbollah front organisation and an Iran-backed finance network (November 18), on drug cartels linked to foreign terrorist organisations, and on enablers of Southeast Asian cyber-scam centres (November 19).

Asia trip deals: Outcomes include a US–Thailand reciprocal trade agreement and a Quad leaders' joint release. Deputy Secretary Landau is travelling to Tajikistan, Türkiye and Turkmenistan through November 24.

5. Middle East Politics.

Iran-war spillover: The fighting continues to spread across the region. A Pentagon official puts the cost of the Iran war at more than 48 billion dollars.

Gaza: The UK, Australia, Canada and Italy jointly urged Israel to allow unimpeded aid into Gaza after floods. The UK sanctioned eight Israeli settlement-construction firms ahead of the E1 tender deadline.

EU–Jordan: The EU and Jordan launched a 3 billion euros package for their strategic partnership at a Dead Sea investment conference.

Syria: Damascus welcomed a UN resolution recognising its post-war progress.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel coalition talks: PM-designate Eisenkot (Yashar) and Netanyahu's Likud remain deadlocked, and Likud says it will hold new talks. Eisenkot is still trying to build a "change" coalition.

Mosque arson in Israel: A mosque in the Lower Galilee village of Dahi was set alight and sprayed with pro-settler graffiti. Lapid said the attack "brings shame." Separately, settlers have reportedly set up a yeshiva campus in a West Bank area meant to be under PA control.

Iraq versus Kataib Hezbollah: Baghdad announced it would take over the militia's stronghold, and Kataib Hezbollah vowed not to leave. Reports late Thursday indicate a compromise was reached.

Syria: Authorities arrested a former general accused of a 2014 Eastern Ghouta massacre that killed 150.

Iraq trial: 47 French nationals accused of ISIS membership are on trial in Iraq.

7. Middle East Defense News.

Houthi strike on Riyadh: A Houthi missile damaged a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, reportedly the PIF tower. There were no injuries. The Houthis also claim cruise-missile strikes on King Khalid Airport.

Saudi defence support: CENTCOM chief Adm. Brad Cooper met Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman, and Britain is stepping up air-defence support for Saudi Arabia.

US carrier drawdown: The USS George Washington has left the region after four months, leaving two US carriers supporting Iran operations.

Hormuz: Iran's IRGC says two oil tankers hit mines in the Strait. This is not independently confirmed.

Israel–Hezbollah: The IAF bombed entrances to an underground Hezbollah missile site in eastern Lebanon. The IDF also wrapped up a one-day operation against a Hamas tunnel that crossed Gaza's Yellow Line.

Türkiye: The homegrown Kizilelma unmanned combat jet has entered the Turkish Armed Forces' inventory.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Kyiv embassy hit: A Russian drone crashed behind the Dutch embassy in Kyiv, damaging windows. Russia says it was "not a targeted attack."

Cabinet survives, for now: The Jetten cabinet continues as a minority government after the tax-plan crisis. Mediator Sybrand Buma has until mid-December to secure budget majorities.

FvD expulsions: Forum voor Democratie expelled members linked to neo-Nazi cage-fighting tournaments.

Destinus in Ukraine: A report says employees of Dutch arms firm Destinus are taking part in the Ukraine war.

Syria returns: The asylum minister maintains Syria is safe despite a Council of State ruling.

NS fares: MPs want NS to scrap its planned 4.7% fare increase.

HIV: New HIV infections in the Netherlands rose again.

9. European Union News.

General Affairs Council: Ministers reviewed the Commission's 2027 work programme and held rule-of-law dialogues on Cyprus, Lithuania, Luxembourg and Hungary. The EU also held its 31st accession conference with Montenegro.

Methane rules delayed: Parliament and Council agreed to postpone some EU Methane Regulation requirements for importers by two years, citing energy-security strain from the Middle East war.

Commission cyberattack: A cyberattack on the European Commission took HR and payroll systems offline for about 10 days, and personal data may be affected.

Greece referred to court: The Commission referred Greece to the CJEU for failing to implement aircraft identification rules.

10. NATO News.

Rutte in Berlin: At the Coalition of the Willing meeting, Rutte stressed continued support for Ukraine and praised allies' pledges at this week's Ukraine Defence Contact Group (Ramstein-format) meeting in Brussels.

Intelligence chiefs: Rutte met civilian and military intelligence chiefs from all 32 allies (November 18). Russia's war and its hybrid activity topped the agenda.

Space data: NATO launched a Global Data Marketplace for Space (November 17).

11. Elections.

Upcoming.

The Gambia: The presidential vote is on 5 December, and the campaign runs to 3 December. Barrow is seeking a third term against a split opposition.

South Sudan: General elections have been postponed from 22 December to 11 February 2027.

Israel: Government formation continues after the October election.

Just happened.

Cape Verde: President José Maria Neves was re-elected on 15 November with about 63.8%, and challenger Joana Rosa conceded. Turnout was very low, at about 32%.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan (war).

A Port Sudan court sentenced RSF leader Hemedti and 13 commanders to death in absentia. It is his second death sentence.

The SAF captured Abu Haraz, south-west of El Obeid, on 18 November and deployed five combat units to Ed Damazin in Blue Nile, near the Ethiopian border.

The UN says at least 2,230 civilians were killed from January to October, more than 1,630 of them by drones and over two-thirds in Kordofan.

Factional splits are resurfacing ahead of the Nyon peace talks on 25 to 28 November.

Ethiopia–Sudan: Relations are deteriorating amid mutual claims of interference, and Sudan alleges Ethiopian backing for the RSF. The UN is pushing diplomacy to prevent a military escalation.

Somalia–UAE: The ambassador's expulsion follows Somalia's January cancellation of all port, defence and security pacts with the UAE. This adds a Gulf proxy rivalry to Horn of Africa tensions.

Egypt: Egypt secured a 2.3 billion dollars IMF deal.

Zimbabwe: A constitutional court challenge to the July amendments over presidential succession and the election timetable is under way.

Migration: A record number of migrants reached Spain's Canary Islands, and another deadly boat disaster occurred off Senegal.

DR Congo: The Ebola outbreak in Ituri continues.

13. Asia/India (brief)

Imran Khan: In an open letter to Pakistan's top judges, the jailed former PM pleads for medical treatment for himself and Bushra Bibi: "I may not return alive."

Pakistan security: The army (ISPR) says 22 militants were killed in operations in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Pakistan and Qatar signed a security MoU.

Bangladesh: Paramilitary units were deployed amid unrest ahead of Hasina's planned visit.

14. OSINT Updates (developing / partly unconfirmed)

Kerch Bridge: Vantor WorldView-1 imagery from 19 November shows impact points on the rail span after a Ukrainian Neptune strike. The road span cut on 16 to 17 November is also visible.

North Darfur: Planet imagery analysed by Rich Tedd shows the RSF digging a new about 28 km earthen berm around al-Kuma, 76 km east of El Fasher, in early November. That suggests a possible new siege position.

US bases: Bellingcat found that public mapping platforms exposed details of US bases in the Middle East that Iran reportedly targeted, alongside Russian-supplied imagery.

Hormuz mines: The IRGC claim that two tankers hit mines is not yet corroborated by maritime trackers or independent imagery. Treat it as unconfirmed.

Yemen: OSINT reporting flags Houthi advances toward Aden, which raises the prospect of a Houthi administration across the Red Sea. This is unconfirmed.
