Daily Briefing — Thursday 19 November 2026.

1. World Politics.

Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen. Trump held a bilateral with Xi of just over an hour on the APEC sidelines and is heading into the first-ever three-way summit with Xi and Putin. The White House says Ukraine is not the focus; North Korea's Kim was spotted on his own east coast, so he is unlikely to appear.

Ukraine brings down part of the Crimean Bridge. Two strikes on November 16 to 17, using drones plus FP-5 Flamingo cruise missiles, collapsed the road arch into the Kerch Strait and halted traffic. Russia's Investigative Committee has confirmed structural damage. Russia had hit Kyiv's Northern and Southern bridges the night before.

Ukraine's allies meet in Berlin. Trump envoys Kushner and Witkoff are due to join Ukrainian, German, French, UK, Italian, Polish, EU and NATO officials, as allies worry the Shenzhen summit will look like a carve-up of spheres of influence.

COP31 in Antalya closes November 20. Talks are strained over the draft deal, with publication of the IPCC's seventh assessment report a sticking point.

Pacific islands declare an El Niño drought crisis. Separately, a US jury convicted a pastor over the 2021 assassination of Haiti's president.

2. US Politics.

Peltola flips Alaska's Senate seat. Former Rep. Mary Peltola (D) has defeated Sen. Dan Sullivan (R) by 50.1% to 45.8%, according to NPR and Democracy Now. She becomes the first Indigenous woman from Alaska elected to the Senate, and Democrats reach 56 Senate seats. These figures come from web reporting; the AP results lookup was unavailable.

NSA network outage. The NSA blames a "temporary degradation" for an outage of its classified internal network. It follows a run of federal tech outages, including one that stopped air travel.

AP: migrant children held for weeks. Children in CBP custody have been held in cold, sunless rooms for up to a month, far beyond the 72-hour legal limit.

SNAP work rules widen. Adults up to age 65 must now work or volunteer to receive SNAP benefits. Separately, a judge is keeping East Potomac Park open for now.

3. Congress.

Senate moves permitting reform. The Senate adopted the Capito substitute to H.R. 2347, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, by 69 to 27, then began a cloture vote.

House Democrats pick their leadership. They nominated Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker, with Katherine Clark running for majority leader and Pete Aguilar for whip. In the Senate, Chris Van Hollen is challenging Schumer for leader in an early-December vote.

Oversight preservation letters. Incoming Oversight chair Robert Garcia told 13 Trump allies, including Don Jr., Eric Trump, Kushner and Stephen Miller, to preserve their records ahead of investigations.

Outages hearing. The Homeland Security secretary (Mullin) faced scrutiny over the outages. Separately, Cornyn blocked a top DOJ official nominated to a Texas judgeship.

Recent votes. The House passed H.R. 9600, the Common Sense 250 Act, by 365 to 36 (November 17). The Senate confirmed Daniel Domenico to the 10th Circuit by 51 to 46 (November 16).

4. US Foreign Policy.

Rubio in Asia. Rubio is travelling with Trump in the Philippines and China through November 19. Deputy Secretary Landau is in Tajikistan, Türkiye and Turkmenistan through November 24.

US–Philippines trade deal. Trump is touting a "new" deal, but details remain vague.

Hegseth in Venezuela and Colombia. Hegseth made a surprise stop in Caracas, the highest-level US visit since January's ouster of the president, then flew to Bogotá. There he delivered a C-130 and discussed armed groups' drones and Colombia joining the "Shield of the Americas".

Hezbollah sanctions and Gulf warnings. Treasury added new Hezbollah sanctions, and the US reissued security warnings to Americans in the Gulf after Iranian strikes.

5. Middle East Politics.

US–Iran "frozen war". Iran's drone attacks on Kuwait (a US-used airbase) and Bahrain drew condemnation across the Gulf. Qatar warned that escalation is making dialogue harder. Analysts describe a low-grade conflict that keeps shipping and energy exposed.

Gaza ceasefire at risk. The US-led Board of Peace warns the ceasefire faces imminent collapse without action, citing continued fighting and delays in implementing the 2025 deal.

Settler sanctions. Australia sanctioned 3 Israeli individuals and 4 organisations, and the UK sanctioned 8 settlement firms. A 72-year-old Palestinian woman was killed by settlers near Jericho.

Nabeel Rajab dies. The Bahraini human rights activist died at 62.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel's new Knesset speaker. The Knesset elected Matan Kahana (Yashar, an Eisenkot ally) speaker by 66 to 52, with backing from Arab parties, after a six-hour filibuster by Netanyahu's bloc.

Judicial overhaul repeal advances. In preliminary readings, the Knesset backed repealing the law that weakened the attorney general (63 to 47) and the media-control law (63 to 49). Bennett met Netanyahu, who keeps calling for a unity government.

Iraq: standoff at Jurf al-Nasr. PM Ali al-Zaidi deployed troops around the Kataib Hezbollah stronghold over alleged oil smuggling, with a reported Friday deadline for the group to leave. The Quds Force's Qaani is in Baghdad to mediate. KH vows it will not withdraw.

Yemen. Southern separatists are trying to regain power to defend Aden, and Saudi Arabia dismissed its coalition commander.

7. Middle East Defense News.

US strikes Qeshm. US jets hit an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island in "self-defense". The IRGC claims a drone strike on a US radar at Sheikh Isa Air Base; CENTCOM says it was intercepted.

Third US carrier. USS Theodore Roosevelt has joined the George Washington and George H.W. Bush in the region.

Yemen: Houthis push toward Aden. The Saudi coalition hit 60 Houthi targets in Sanaa, Hodeidah, Amran and Saada, and intercepted 2 cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh. Government forces had already pulled out of Dhubab and the Bab al-Mandeb area.

Lebanon, Gaza and Syria. Israeli forces are demolishing homes in south Lebanon and issued evacuation orders in Nuseirat (Gaza). Israeli armour is also blocking roads in Syria.

Kuwait drone strike claim. A whistleblower says the drone strike in Kuwait that killed six US soldiers could have been avoided.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Cabinet survives. The Jetten I cabinet stays on with new ground rules and "less bickering", but parliament doubts the restart will change much.

Russia questions Dutch role. Russia asked whether the Netherlands is joining the war after a Dutch arms maker said Ukraine used its missiles on the Crimean Bridge.

Transport strikes next week. Regional public transport workers announced nationwide strikes, and MPs want NS to scrap its planned 4.7% fare rise.

Syria still "safe". The asylum minister reaffirmed that Syria is safe despite a Council of State ruling.

Crack addiction. Amsterdam and Groningen urged The Hague to tackle it. Petrol is down 12.5% in six weeks, and 1,100+ creditors are claiming 26 million euros from the bankrupt crypto platform Knaken.

9. European Union News.

More money for Ukraine. The Commission proposed 45 billion euros in additional financial support for Ukraine in 2027.

Faster permitting. A political agreement was reached on faster, simpler permitting for grids and clean energy projects. The EU also endorsed the Global Electrification Pledge at COP31.

Migrants to Rwanda. Five EU countries plan to send migrants to Rwanda, and critics warn the "partnership buys silence".

Aviation blacklist. The EU kept bans on airlines from 12 African countries, 87 airlines in total.

10. NATO News.

Ukraine Defence Contact Group. Rutte chaired the meeting at NATO HQ (November 17) and urged new pledges before year-end, with air defence, drones and PURL purchases as priorities. He warned that Russia may intensify its winter strikes.

Ukrainian delegation. Rutte met Ukrainian PM Koretskyi and Rada chair Stefanchuk (November 16) and publicly defended Ukraine's Crimean Bridge strike.

NATO in Berlin. NATO officials are expected at today's Berlin meeting with the US envoys on Ukraine.

11. Elections.

Just happened.

Cape Verde: Neves re-elected in the first round with about 63.8%; Joana Rosa conceded; turnout about 32%.

Alaska Senate: Peltola reported as winner.

Brazil: Lula won the October 25 runoff for a fourth term.

Upcoming.

Taiwan local elections, November 28. Taipei poll: Chiang Wan-an leads Puma Shen 44.1% to 37.1%.

US runoffs: Georgia and Mississippi on December 1, Louisiana general on December 12.

South Sudan postponed its elections to February 11, 2027.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Ethiopia–Eritrea: build-up on the border. ACLED and Crisis Group report both armies massed within striking distance. ACLED cites Ethiopian drone strikes on likely Eritrean positions near Badme (November 12) and clashes the next day. Reuters could not confirm the skirmishes. The backdrop is an alleged Eritrean incursion into Tigray in October and Abiy's push for sea access. Badme was the trigger of the 1998 to 2000 war.

Senegal: ruling party expels the president. Pastef expelled President Faye and 31 others, cementing his split with Ousmane Sonko, now Assembly speaker. Pastef holds 130 of 165 seats. The deadlock threatens IMF aid and the 2027 budget.

Nigeria: state police step closer. All 36 states approved the constitutional amendment, which now awaits Tinubu's assent. Critics fear governors could misuse the forces.

Sudan. The UN says 2,230+ civilians were killed Jan–October, 1,630+ of them by drones. Khartoum has filed UN complaints against Ethiopia over drone attacks. HRW urges the transfer of ICC suspect Ahmed Haroun, re-arrested by the SAF on November 8.

Guinea and the DRC. Guinea's crackdown on illegal mining has displaced tens of thousands, with Mali reporting up to 160,000 arrivals. In eastern DRC, rival peace efforts are failing to halt the fighting.

13. Asia/India.

APEC in Shenzhen. The Trump–Xi meeting comes amid a tenuous trade truce, and new US AI-chip rules are rattling Southeast Asia.

Philippines. Police arrested 11 Chinese nationals in a raid on an alleged scam hub in Batangas.

India. Delhi's air quality remains "very poor" (AQI about 351). Prince William's visit has highlighted India–UK ties.

14. OSINT Updates (unconfirmed/developing; treat with caution)

Crimean Bridge damage. Geolocated footage (Exilenova+) shows the road arch over the navigation span gone, while the parallel rail arch appears to be standing. Defense Express estimates about 270 m collapsed. Debris may be blocking the shipping channel, which is not verified.

US patrol aircraft. A US Navy P-8A Poseidon was tracked along NATO's Black Sea coast on the night of the bridge strike.

Ethiopia–Eritrea. ACLED event data flags Ethiopian drone strikes near Badme and Eritrean artillery fire. A pro-government Tigray militia source disputes that any clashes took place.

Sahel (ACLED, 7 to 13 November). IS Sahel attacks in Niger's Dioundiou and Tera departments, and a JNIM attack in Kirtachi commune that displaced thousands.

Strait of Hormuz. Social-media imagery verified by Reuters shows a tanker on fire off Oman (November 17). Ships are reportedly transiting with limited US protection.
