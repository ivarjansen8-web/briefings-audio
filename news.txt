Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

World. Russia pounded Kyiv and Ukraine's power grid overnight from the 6th to the 7th, with 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones, damaging Kyiv's Northern Bridge, days before US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi on the 10th and 11th. The Iran war continues: US forces disabled a ship trying to run the blockade, and Qatar says its mediation goes on. Spain holds a snap election on 29 November.

US politics. Democrats hold 254 House seats to 163 for Republicans, with 20 races uncalled, and the Mississippi and Alaska Senate races too close to call. Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff.

Congress. Representative Thomas Massie says he will force an impeachment vote on Defense Secretary Hegseth next week, and several Republican sources expect it to pass. Chuck Schumer says he will stay Senate Democratic leader, though that may not be settled.

US foreign policy. Washington is looking to restrict Iran's land trade as well. Treasury Secretary Bessent reportedly asked Beijing for help ending the war.

Middle East. President Herzog tasks Gadi Eisenkot with forming Israel's government today. Shas reportedly won't oppose him, while Likud prepares to appeal the election result. Iran's Revolutionary Guards say their missile range limit has been lifted. In Gaza, Hamas says about 70 thousand tents flooded in heavy rain.

Middle East defense. An oil tanker reportedly hit a mine near Hormuz. Saudi strikes on Sanaa killed at least 22, according to Houthi media, and the Houthis claim attacks on Riyadh airport and Aramco sites. Iran is said to be negotiating for Russia's S-400 air defense system, unconfirmed.

The Netherlands. The cabinet will postpone its wealth tax plans by a year and set aside 350 million euros in tax relief. The Netherlands nominated Klaas Knot to lead the European Central Bank. Inflation rose to 3.7 percent in October.

Europe. Finance ministers meet tomorrow, budget talks follow on the 10th, and an EU and UK summit is set for the 20th.

NATO. Coalition of the Willing exercises begin tomorrow, which Moscow calls unacceptable. A shooting in Krakow killed five.

Elections. Somalia's Supreme Court ruled the president's term has expired and ordered elections within 90 days. In South Africa's local elections, the ANC is at about 31 percent with most votes counted.

Africa. A confidential UN report says a Emirati-linked firm moved Colombian mercenaries into Sudan to support the Rapid Support Forces. Cholera has killed about 1,800 people in Congo this year. Tanzania's opposition leader Tundu Lissu got four years for incitement.

Asia. Hong Kong jailed Joshua Wong for 10 years. There was no major item from India.

Open source intelligence. Russia is reportedly upgrading its long-range drones, and an intercepted Russian plan, unconfirmed, aims to cut Ukraine's nuclear plants off the grid.
