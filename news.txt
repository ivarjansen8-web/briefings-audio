Daily News Briefing: Friday, 13 November 2026.

My searches turned up confirmed reporting through roughly 9 to 11 November; little from 12 to 13 November was available yet. Items marked (unconfirmed) are unverified.

1. World Politics.

US-mediated Russia–Ukraine talks resumed in Abu Dhabi on 10 November, the first in nine months. Rubio called them productive but urged patience, and Russia wants Europeans kept out; I could not confirm what the 11 November session produced.

Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv overnight on 6 to 7 November as part of its winter energy-strike campaign.

New Zealand's 7 November election produced no clear winner; Luxon stays caretaker PM until final results on 27 November.

2. US Politics.

Democrats won both the House and Senate on 3 November and take control in January; NBC's tally had them at 54 Senate seats as of 4 November.

Trump replaced chief of staff Susie Wiles with Stephen Miller (effective 9 November); AP reads it as a sign he will not take a conciliatory line with the incoming Democratic majority.

3. Congress.

The House voted 211 to 204 on 10 November to send Rep. Massie's Hegseth impeachment articles to Judiciary, effectively ending the effort this Congress. Jeffries has said Democrats will pursue Hegseth next year; Trump is standing by him.

Lame-duck agenda includes Senate permitting reform, Republican minority-leadership elections, and the 11 December funding deadline set by the September stopgap.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Witkoff and Kushner front the Ukraine track; Trump met Kremlin envoy Dmitriev at the White House on 5 November.

On Iran, Trump told CNN renewed strikes would be highly unlikely if Tehran agrees to a strong nuclear deal and fully reopens Hormuz; he also met Qatar's prime minister.

Turkey's intelligence chief said Vance and Iran's Mohsen Rezaei would meet in Doha next week; Iranian state media denies it (unconfirmed).

Trump's remark that roughly 4 billion dollars a year in aid to Israel would end within two months caught Israelis off guard, per a Jerusalem Post editorial.

A Trump–Xi meeting is expected at APEC in Shenzhen on 18 to 19 November.

5. Middle East Politics.

Israel: President Herzog tasked Gadi Eisenkot with forming a government (67 MKs recommended him versus 46 for Netanyahu); he has 28 days. Final seats were Netanyahu's bloc 53, Eisenkot's 48, Arab parties 19, so neither side has 61. Some outlets, for example JNS, still describe Netanyahu as PM-designate.

Iran says it will only reopen Hormuz once the US meets its seven conditions; Qatar's 48-hour plan front-loads IAEA inspections.

Lebanon: Israel–Lebanon talks are stalled over how far Israel's withdrawal extends, per Israeli broadcaster KAN; a seventh round was held in Rome on 5 November.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel: the new Knesset was sworn in on 10 November. Eisenkot is courting Haredi parties to replace Speaker Ohana, but UTJ declined before a government is formed.

Iraq: PM Ali al-Zaydi threatened to arrest militia members who join a US–Iran war, cancelled three Mosul airport contracts over armed-group ties, and let army commanders act on militia violations without his sign-off.

Yemen: government-aligned forces are advancing against the Houthis in Jawf, Marib and Lahij.

7. Middle East Defense News.

US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island reportedly hit radar, launchers and mine-laying systems; US media reported CENTCOM had further strike plans ready with three carriers in the region.

The IRGC Navy claimed three tankers hit mines in Hormuz; ISW reports the claim.

Saudi Arabia struck three Houthi missile-production sites near Sanaa and Hudaydah airport on 9 November; the Houthis likely hit the East-West pipeline near Medina, with minor damage.

Syria plans to send about 600 special forces to Saudi Arabia after a 4 November defence deal; Damascus rules out deploying to Yemen.

Gaza: Israeli strikes killed five people on 10 November.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

The Jetten minority cabinet (D66–VVD–CDA, 66 of 150 seats) was still seeking a Tweede Kamer majority for the 2027 Tax Plan days before the 12 November deadline; a no-confidence motion with Wilders as co-signatory was tabled on 10 November. I could not confirm the vote result.

NL Times: the State Secretary called a scenario in which Moerdijk village is not dissolved feasible (10 November); a 19-year-old shot dead in Schiedam was reportedly mistaken for a friend over a 500 eurosdebt (9 November).

9. European Union.

The Commission is exploring an extra 45 billion euros Ukraine loan for 2027 on top of the existing 90 billion euros scheme. Leaders are due to meet 26 to 27 November, with 17 to 18 December as fallback.

Von der Leyen said the EU will be represented in the Ukraine talks together with partners; Moscow objects.

Sanctions: the 21st package (July) was watered down and froze the oil price cap; the EU agreed record listings of about 1,600 targets on 12 October, and a 22nd package is in preparation.

10. NATO.

I found no major NATO decisions in the last 48 hours. Polish authorities blocked a Russian provocation at the border on the night of 2 to 3 November, and European services responded to bomb threats against commercial flights in the week of 2 November (origin unconfirmed).

Hegseth's six-month review of US forces in Europe, announced 18 June, would land around mid-December if on schedule.

11. Elections.

Upcoming

15 November: Cabo Verde presidential election (runoff 29 November); incumbent José Maria Neves is the heavy favourite per a prediction market.

27 November: official New Zealand results; 28 November: Taiwan local elections; 29 November: Swiss referendums.

A Spanish general election on 29 November and a Palestinian legislative vote on 28 November appear on IDEA and Socialist International calendars; I found no news corroboration (unconfirmed).

December: Gambia 5 December, Guinea-Bissau 6 December, Haiti 13 December (tentative), South Sudan 22 December.

Just happened

US midterms (3 November): Democrats took both chambers.

Bulgaria (1 November): Iliana Iotova, who favours dialogue with Moscow, won the presidential runoff and becomes the first elected woman head of state.

Israel (27 October) and New Zealand (7 November): both produced no outright majority (see sections 1 and 5).

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: the RSF has struck fuel depots and power stations across SAF-held territory since 22 October (CTP counts 21 sets of strikes, including Khartoum and Omdurman). The army's own drone strikes in RSF areas killed more civilians in October than in the previous three months combined, including an Abyei market strike on 19 October that killed between 153 and 226 people.

Sudan–Ethiopia: Khartoum alleges about 70 RSF drone attacks launched from Ethiopian territory between February and October and complained to the UN on 1 November. Ethiopia closed Sudan's consulate in Asosa on 4 November, and Sudan reportedly retaliated against Ethiopia's consulate in Gedaref.

Ethiopia–Eritrea: unverified reports describe border clashes between 30 October and 1 November, raising the risk of a low-intensity border war. Tigrayan forces retook Rama on 22 October.

Russia–Africa summit (Moscow, 28 to 29 October): 48 delegations but only 21 heads of state. Ethiopia took delivery of its first two Su-35s and announced a purchase of four S-400 batteries; Kenya and Ghana say Putin promised to halt recruiting their nationals for the Ukraine war. CTP sees no major shift in Russia's Africa role.

Sahel: Algeria has begun deploying to Niger as part of a more assertive Sahel strategy.

13. Asia/India.

Japan is lobbying Beijing for equal protocol treatment for PM Takaichi at APEC (18 to 19 November, Shenzhen); China still wants her to retract her Taiwan remarks.

Trump and Xi are expected to hold their third meeting of the year on the APEC sidelines.

India: I found no confirmed major India headline for the past two days.

14. OSINT Updates (all unconfirmed or analyst-assessed)

ISW assesses, without confirmation, that Iran-backed Iraqi actors attacked the Emirati-owned Khor Mor gas field on 8 November to deter PM al-Zaydi; no group has claimed it.

Conflict trackers on X and a Tigray Peace Force official reported Ethiopian–Eritrean border fighting (30 Oct–1 November); Critical Threats could not verify it.

OSINT analyst Rich Tedd reports October had the most Emirati-linked flights to Ethiopia this year, tied by CTP to RSF weapons shipments.

Satellite imagery analysed by OSINT researchers (reported 17 October) shows new T-90M tanks, BTR-82As and BMP-3s arriving at a Russian reserve base in Kostroma Oblast, about 500 km from the Estonian and Latvian borders.

A Russian milblogger claimed Ukrainian strikes hit a bridge on the Rostov–Crimea M-14 highway on 8 November; ISW cites the claim without confirming.
