Daily News Briefing — Saturday, 28 November 2026.

1. World Politics.

EU budget summit ends without a deal. More than 12 hours of talks on the 2028 to 34 budget and a new about 45 billion euros (49 billion dollars) Ukraine loan broke up with no agreement. Macron set an end-of-year deadline, and leaders meet again 18 to 19 December.

Zelensky likens Trump calling Putin a "friend" to befriending Hitler. He said it in a BBC interview, after Trump posted a photo from the Shenzhen summit captioned "My Friend."

Russia keeps up pressure on Ukraine's power grid. It launched 155 drones overnight on 26 to 27 November; 27 sites were hit, and one person was killed and 18 injured in Kharkiv region. Zelensky warned another large strike is coming, and Ukraine has stopped exporting electricity.

Ukraine hits Russia's Tula region. Its strikes hit a ballistic-missile fuel plant there and the Novoshakhtinsk refinery. Al Jazeera puts Russia's lost refining capacity at about one-third.

Nine Ukrainian children returned from Russian control. Zelensky credited Melania Trump's mediation.

Costa Rica turns away UN rapporteur Francesca Albanese. It cited an "international alert over links to terrorist acts"; she called the ban "a disgrace."

UN warns hunger will worsen in 16 countries. The FAO/WFP Hunger Hotspots report puts Sudan, Yemen, South Sudan, Somalia and northern Nigeria at highest concern, citing war, fuel costs and aid cuts.

2. US Politics.

Trump's Thanksgiving posts attack the government's own GDP figures. He accused the Commerce Department of issuing "fake Economic Numbers" before the midterms, after Q3 growth was revised up from 3.4% to 4.3%, and called for an investigation without evidence.

The same 461-word post targeted Somali immigrants, liberals and ballroom critics. The Hill reports it also called for the mass expulsion of Somalis.

Trump told troops victory over Iran is "at hand." On a call from Mar-a-Lago he said "it's going to be whenever… I want it to be," and called the blockade "the most successful blockade in naval history."

3. Congress.

Congress is out for Thanksgiving; the government shutdown deadline is 11 December. Another stopgap funding bill is expected. The annual defense bill (NDAA), the farm bill and permitting reform are still pending in the lame-duck session.

Senate Democratic leadership race. Schumer faces Chris Van Hollen in a vote expected in early December; The Hill's count has Schumer 15, Van Hollen 5, with 34 undeclared. Jeffries is already the Democrats' nominee for Speaker.

Incoming Democrats say ending the Iran war is a priority. Jeffries said Democrats must "move aggressively" to end it, but neither he nor Schumer has said how.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Trump rewrote a joint statement with NATO's Rutte after it was published. In his own posted version, the warning against expanding the war applies to Ukraine as well as Russia. NATO told Politico the version on its website was the agreed text.

Trump played down Chinese and Russian intelligence help to Iran. "Involvement to me would mean shooting," he said, adding that both countries are "helping me" end the war.

Reuters: the US pushed Venezuela and Iraq to consider leaving OPEC. Ten sources say this happened earlier this year and that the US blocked Venezuela's dues payment. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman reportedly talked Washington down.

Trump on Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site: "If we do something, we will definitely hit Pickaxe Mountain."

5. Middle East Politics.

Iranian drone attack in Kuwait kills two Italians. The technicians were servicing air-defense equipment. Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador; Iran denies responsibility. ISW says the attack fits Iran's recent pattern of targeting air defenses.

Tehran openly discusses forcing an end to the US blockade. Options aired by officials include sending tankers through it and striking assets that help enforce it. IRGC aerospace chief Mousavi said enemies should "expect action in air and at sea."

Israel's security cabinet is reportedly set to discuss advancing Trump's Gaza plan, under US pressure.

Iran warns South Korea over its naval plans. Seoul's parliament is weighing extending its Cheonghae navy unit's operating area to waters off the Gulf of Oman. FM Araghchi said foreign forces would "heighten tensions"; Seoul says it will not join the war.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel coalition talks: Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas is due to say publicly tonight (Channel 12) that Hamas is a terror group. That could clear Ra'am's path into a government led by Gadi Eisenkot, whose mandate to form one expires in about 8 days. Likud, Katz and Smotrich call the move deception. Netanyahu is reportedly preparing to offer Eisenkot the top spot in a rotation government.

Knesset Speaker Matan Kahana accuses Netanyahu of stalling to force another election while he stays on as caretaker PM.

Iraq: PM Ali al-Zaydi moves on Kurdish-held borders and oil fields. After a Federal Supreme Court ruling on 25 November, federal forces closed two KRG-run border crossings, and Zaydi ordered a takeover of Kurdish-controlled oil fields in four provinces. The stated aim is to stop oil smuggling by Iran-backed groups.

Lebanon: Hezbollah's Naim Qassem implicitly threatens the government. He demanded the "immediate" release of five members being prosecuted for arming illegal groups in Bcharre.

Iran arrests a journalist who warned protesters faced execution; rights groups say he was tortured.

7. Middle East Defense News.

CENTCOM disables a blockade runner. A US aircraft disabled the Palau-flagged bulk carrier Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman after it left Bandar Abbas; CENTCOM alleges it carried "precision munitions." It is the first announced disabling since 7 November.

Iran says it fired warning shots to drive a US F-16 away near Hormuz. The IRGC also claims two oil tankers hit mines in the Strait.

Houthis hit Aramco's Jizan refinery, killing one worker and briefly halting operations. Saudi Arabia says it destroyed 50 Houthi targets. Houthi media say a Saudi strike in Taiz killed 11, mostly children.

Turkey completes ratification of the Mecca defense pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan. Under the pact, an attack on one is an attack on all. The three countries' foreign and defense chiefs met in Islamabad on 27 to 28 November.

IDF strikes dozens of Hezbollah rocket sites and a Radwan Force camp in Western Bekaa. It also says Hezbollah uses fishing boats to spy on the Israeli navy, and it announced four Hamas operatives killed in central Gaza.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

Record cold: −8.1°C overnight, the coldest 27 November ever recorded in the Netherlands.

Klaas Knot loses the ECB presidency race to Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos.

The Dutch army is buying dozens of Swedish-made armored vehicles, and drone pilots get more airspace around five airports.

Amsterdam's OLVG hospital will close the emergency room at its Oost location.

The 2027 Tax Plan is now in the Senate (Eerste Kamer). The Jetten minority cabinet will defend it there after a coalition rift in which the VVD voted against it in the House. At the EU summit, Jetten said the EU budget must be "modernized and downsized."

9. European Union News.

The special EU budget summit failed to agree. Leaders could not settle the roughly 1.6 trillion euros (1.76 trillion dollars) 2028 to 34 budget. Hungary, the Czech Republic, Slovakia and Bulgaria oppose a joint loan for Ukraine. Merz links a budget deal to all members helping fund Ukraine; Meloni said the sides were "light-years away."

Bulgaria is blocking the 22nd Russia sanctions package to extract concessions.

The Kremlin says EU money for Ukraine will make Kyiv "less inclined toward a peace process."

10. NATO News.

Trump–Rutte meeting at Mar-a-Lago (25 November). They discussed Ukraine, Iran, US troop levels in Europe and defense spending, and the joint readout called the talks "outstanding." The meeting was then overshadowed by Trump's unilateral rewrite of the statement.

NATO foreign ministers meet soon in Brussels. Ukraine's FM Sybiha will meet Rutte on the sidelines. Rutte says NATO sees no immediate Russian military threat.

Allies worry the war is spreading geographically. Concerns follow Russian airspace violations over Poland and Ukraine's sinking of the Russian corvette Merkury in Benghazi, Libya (22 November). Zelensky admits the Libya strike was not coordinated with European partners.

11. Elections.

Upcoming.

Victoria, Australia (today, 28 November): state election. Polls favour the Liberal–National Coalition, with One Nation surging and Labor third on primary votes in some polls.

Switzerland (29 November): national referendums on four proposals.

US runoffs: Georgia and Mississippi (1 December), New Jersey (8 December), Louisiana and Texas (12 December).

The Gambia (5 December): presidential election.

Serbia (27 December): early presidential vote. The student movement has named chemistry professor Radomir Saičić as its candidate; Vučić's SNS may boycott.

Guinea-Bissau: the junta revoked the 6 December election date. South Sudan: elections postponed to 11 February 2027.

Just happened.

US midterms (3 November): Democrats won both chambers, with around 258 House seats, their most since 1992, after the last races were settled.

Israel (October): coalition talks are ongoing (see section 6).

12. Africa In-Depth.

South Africa: a court halts Ramaphosa's impeachment process. On 25 November the Western Cape High Court set aside the Section 89 panel report on the Phala Phala affair, ruling that the panel "went beyond its lawful remit," and voided every parliamentary step based on it. This removes the biggest threat to his presidency for now. The ATM party, which brought the original motion, says the motion itself still stands, so appeals are likely.

Sudan: the army is pushing west from El Obeid. Egypt's Sisi and Sudan's PM Kamal Idris agreed to prioritise post-war reconstruction and jointly rejected "militarization" of the Red Sea. Idris also called for an inclusive national dialogue.

DRC–Uganda row. Kinshasa summoned Uganda's top diplomat over rebel-related remarks by Uganda's army chief Muhoozi Kainerugaba, despite the two countries' joint operations against the ADF. AU envoy Sahle-Work Zewde is visiting Beni and Bunia in eastern DRC.

Nigeria: armed raids kill at least 46 in total. At least 30 were killed in one attack and 16 in a night raid in Plateau State. Separately, a military aircraft crashed near Igbokoda.

Kenya: cult leader Paul Mackenzie was found guilty of terrorism. Togo: the opposition alleges a campaign to eliminate its leaders. Central Africa: the Ebola outbreak is growing, and Africa CDC's leadership faces internal revolt.

13. Asia/India (brief)

Pakistan's first astronaut flew to China's Tiangong space station aboard Shenzhou-24.

Islamabad locked down its Red Zone for the Saudi–Turkish–Pakistani Mecca pact meetings.

India is upbeat about its 2036 Olympic bid after its presentation.

Malaysia's PM Anwar: diplomacy is the key to South China Sea disputes.

14. OSINT Updates (unconfirmed or developing; treat with caution)

New Iranian underground site at Behjatabad. It sits about 62 km from both the Isfahan site and Pickaxe Mountain. Vantor imagery from 24 November, annotated by ISIS, shows at least four active tunnel entrances, checkpoints, a perimeter wall and a probable explosives storage area; the security perimeter could cover about 12,000 acres. The site's purpose is unknown, and a nuclear purpose "cannot be ruled out."

UK MoD imagery (taken 22 November) shows two Russian Pantsir air-defense systems near the Kerch Bridge that failed to stop Ukraine's mid-November strikes. Russia is moving to criminalize unofficial reporting of strikes.

North Korea has reportedly paused military aid to Russia, though its missile crews appear to remain in Russia.

OSINT count of Ukraine's Flamingo missile: Dnipro Osint logs only 12 hits out of 53 known launches. Other analysts dispute some entries, including imagery suggesting all four missiles hit the Skif-M plant.

Ukraine says it hit a new Russian Buk-M3 air-defense system in occupied territory, and Azov's "Hell-3" operation destroyed a hidden fuel depot in Donetsk. Neither is independently verified.

Yemen: drone footage from government forces shows strikes on Houthi movements along the Taiz–Aden road. A local journalist reports residents of Bani Hashish near Sanaa demanding the Houthis remove launch sites from their area.
