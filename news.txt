Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Iran–US standoff persists as Hormuz mine claims and a Bahrain drone incident test talks; Dutch coalition survives tax rift.

1. World Politics.

Novaya Gazeta, Russia's last major independent paper, has ended operations after pressure from authorities.

Hungarian police arrested Viktor Orbán's brother, and France raised its minimum wage 1.6% to calm protests (both November 10 roundups, single-sourced).

G7 foreign ministers in Paris said China's increasingly assertive actions east of Taiwan threaten regional stability and freedom of navigation.

2. US Politics.

Democrats win both chambers after the November 3 midterms and take control on January 3; Republicans keep their majorities through the lame duck.

Trump reportedly removed VP Vance from Iran negotiations after Vance's critical remarks about the war (NYT, via CFR; single-sourced).

Treasury Secretary Bessent has moved onto a military base over Iranian threats, per NYT.

California voters rejected the proposed "billionaire tax"; Jeffries says he is "confident" of being elected Speaker.

Unverified: Mississippi Senate race still uncalled in my sources (Democrat Scott Colom reported ahead as of November 9 to 10).

3. Congress.

The House voted 211 to 204 on November 10 to refer the Hegseth impeachment resolution to Judiciary, shelving it; 11 absent Democrats made the difference, and several have since apologized. Al Jazeera ·.

Next funding deadline is December 11, when the current CR expires.

Senate Republicans are racing to confirm about two dozen Trump judges before the gavel changes hands; Thune is keeping the chamber in on Friday to start the clock.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Rubio and Wang Yi spoke November 11 to 12 (reports differ on the date) about Trump's APEC trip to Shenzhen and Xi's G20 visit to Florida, and agreed on the importance of leader-level talks on peace and denuclearization, including any trilateral with Russia.

In Abu Dhabi (November 10 to 11) Ukraine rejected a US proposal to redraw Donbas borders; Russia reportedly claims control of about half the Ukrainian-held part of the region.

Trump says the Iran war is "militarily effectively over" while keeping the blockade; a third carrier has arrived in the region.

5. Middle East Politics.

Iran says it sent Washington a "clear response" and is awaiting a reply; Qalibaf insists seven conditions must be met before Hormuz reopens and talks resume, while Rezaei denies any negotiations are under way. Anadolu ·.

Qatari and Pakistani mediation continues despite rising military tension.

Iran's security chief Rezaei threatens that distant US bases, including some in Europe, will be hit if US attacks resume.

Day 258 of the war by one tracker; Brent about 93 dollars.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel: after the October 27 election Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats and the anti-Netanyahu Jewish bloc 48, so neither has a majority.

Herzog gave Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) the mandate on November 8; Netanyahu keeps calling for a unity government under his leadership.

Lebanon: an official source says no more Israel–Lebanon meetings are expected until Israel has a new government; the last round, in Tampa, made little progress.

Lebanon asked Syria to extradite two suspects in the killing of former Iraqi MP Ahmed al-Alwani, an Iran critic found dead in Beirut.

Gaza: Israeli strikes continue under the October 2025 ceasefire (about 1,614 killed since, per Gaza health figures); West Bank settler attacks reportedly more than doubled in October.

7. Middle East Defense News.

CENTCOM says it shot down two Iranian one-way attack drones headed for Bahrain and denies the IRGC claim that it destroyed a US early-warning radar there (unconfirmed; the IRGC framed it as retaliation for a US strike on a telecom tower in Sirik). Euronews ·.

The IRGC says tankers hit mines in Hormuz (November 10 to 13); AP could not verify, and CTP-ISW saw no indicators Iran has re-mined the strait.

Saudi air defenses intercepted a Houthi drone near Riyadh and two in the Eastern Province on November 12; Houthis captured the al Shatt junction in Lahij, Yemen.

The GCC approved an advanced joint military HQ in Kuwait; France accuses the IRGC of October cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

PM Rob Jetten's cabinet survives for now: the VVD stays in after D66 and CDA joined PRO and Volt to shift about 1 eurosbillion in annual tax onto higher earners; talks continue over the weekend.

The amended tax plan passed the Tweede Kamer by a single vote; the Box 3 rates become 38.5% above 60,000 eurosof asset income and 35.5% below.

Petrol prices are near their lowest since March as oil falls (November 10).

9. European Union News.

Von der Leyen, at the European Political Community summit in Dublin, said the Commission will present a 22nd Russia sanctions package and 2027 Ukraine financing proposals before the November 26 to 27 European Council.

She called Europe's seat in Ukraine peace talks "self-explanatory"; per ISW, Moscow would admit Europeans only to talks on a new European security architecture, not on ending the war.

Politico reportedly finds the EU split into three camps over who should represent it in talks with Russia (headline only; not independently read).

10. NATO News.

Rutte told the Atlantic Future Forum in Oslo (November 11) that European Allies and Canada will add more than 250 dollarsbillion in core defense spending across 2025 to 26, and welcomed the UK's path to 3% of GDP by 2030.

Rutte urged closer transatlantic defense-industry cooperation at a Rome forum and attended the Dublin EPC summit on November 12.

Unverified: Bild reports Russia demanded Finland leave NATO, and the Kremlin has responded; I could not confirm the original report.

11. Elections (Upcoming / Just Happened)

Just happened: US midterms (November 3) flipped both chambers to Democrats; a few races remain uncalled.

Just happened: Israel (October 27), with government formation still open.

Tomorrow, November 15: Cabo Verde presidential election; incumbent José Maria Neves is the clear favorite, and a runoff would be November 29 if nobody tops 50%.

November 29: Spain's snap general election, called by Sánchez after a housing-decree defeat.

December 22: South Sudan general election is scheduled; Cameroon's legislative vote and Gambia's presidential vote are expected by year-end.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan: the army and allies are advancing against the RSF in West Kordofan (Al-Khuwei, November 7 to 8); gains are claimed, not independently confirmed. More than 2,500 people reportedly fled Darfur for Chad (November 10). Sudan Tribune ·.

Burkina Faso: ISSP and JNIM are fighting each other as the state loses ground; casualty claims (200 to 400+ JNIM fighters) are unverified. Crisis Group notes JNIM now targets major cities in Mali. Al Jazeera ·.

DR Congo: Rio Times reports Ebola above 11,000 cases (low-reliability source, treat with caution), journalists covering it face threats, and the Senate passed a law easing constitutional-referendum rules. Rio Times ·.

Nigeria: gunmen killed a guard and 10 relatives and abducted 13 in the northwest.

Also: Uganda's succession row, South Africa's hung councils and Cameroon's undecided election date.

13. Asia/India (brief)

China and the US are preparing for a Trump–Xi meeting at APEC in Shenzhen, November 18 to 19; Wang called the Rubio call "positive and constructive."

South Korea's foreign minister visits China November 13 to 14.

Japan is unlikely to match NATO's 5%-of-GDP spending target; Taiwan is walking a diplomatic tightrope over APEC.

Pakistan freed two of Imran Khan's sisters; a third remains jailed.

India: nothing notable verified today.
