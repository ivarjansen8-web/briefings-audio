Daily News Briefing — Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

1. World Politics.

Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral set for Thursday (19 November) in Shenzhen on the sidelines of APEC, the first meeting of its kind. Trump wants to talk nuclear arms reduction and the Iran war; Ukraine is reportedly not on his agenda, though the Kremlin says it will be raised.

Ukraine hits the Kerch (Crimean) Bridge during what Russia called the biggest drone barrage of the war (Russia claims 1,300 to 2,000+ drones intercepted across 15 to 16 regions). A second strike overnight reportedly brought down a road span.

UN chief Guterres says a US–Russia–China commitment to "meaningful denuclearization" would be a major achievement. Separately, UN talks on autonomous weapons are deadlocked.

2. US Politics.

Fallout from the Democratic midterm sweep continues. Democrats won both chambers (about 55 Senate seats projected), and Scott Colom's Mississippi Senate win was called by AP on 11 November. Vance blames the Iran war for the GOP losses.

CNN: US intelligence says Iran is developing plans for a dirty-bomb or chemical attack targeting senior officials in Washington. Seven officials describe this as options-planning, not an imminent threat, and are split on how credible it is. Iran denies the chemical-weapons claims.

Nvidia/Jensen Huang spy scandal: a closed-door House briefing is requested for 18 November, and the FBI and Nvidia's board are investigating.

3. Congress.

The lame-duck session faces a 11 December shutdown deadline. Appropriators have no deal yet. Democrats want a clean stopgap into 2027, and some Republicans want to use their last window to fund the Iran war. The NDAA, farm bill and debt limit are also pending.

Hegseth impeachment shelved: the House voted 211 to 204 (10 November) to refer Massie's articles to committee. Jeffries says "this is not the end."

Senate Democratic leadership fight: Sanders and Slotkin oppose or condition support for Schumer, and several freshmen are uncommitted. The vote is expected in early December.

4. US Foreign Policy.

Trump lands in Manila today. He holds the first Quad leaders' meeting in two years, a US–ASEAN special summit and a US–Thailand trade signing, then meets Marcos on Wednesday before flying to Shenzhen. Rubio is travelling with him.

Trump floats meeting Kim Jong Un in China, saying it's "up to Xi."

Rubio condemned Iranian attacks on Bahrain in a call with FM Al Zayani. Deputy Secretary Landau is touring Tajikistan, Türkiye and Turkmenistan (16 to 24 November).

UN Ambassador Mike Waltz was in Beirut pushing a framework deal, a post-UNIFIL force and "voluntary" Hezbollah disarmament.

5. Middle East Politics.

US–Iran war, day about 261: Vance insists it won't become a "forever war," while Trump says the US "doesn't need a deal" and has pulled Vance off the talks. Tehran is reportedly still sending Araghchi to Muscat today.

EU agrees to revive its cooperation deal with Syria and to open talks on a new association agreement.

Gaza: Israeli strikes killed at least 37 people in the past week, including a strike on Nasser hospital. Gaza's health ministry counts 1,650 killed since the October 2025 ceasefire. Germany's Wadephul urges much more winter aid.

6. Middle East Local Politics.

Israel: Knesset votes today on replacing Speaker Amir Ohana (Likud) with Yashar's Matan Kahana. Eisenkot holds the mandate (67 recommendations) but no coalition, and UTJ says it won't back Kahana. Liberman now chairs the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee.

Israeli attorney general probes whether Netanyahu's office engineered election-day arrests of Ra'am activists. Eisenkot says Netanyahu "can't remain for one more minute."

Iraq: PM Ali al-Zaidi ordered the army into Jurf al-Sakher, a Kataib Hezbollah stronghold, and gave the faction a deadline to leave amid an oil-smuggling crackdown.

Lebanon: Speaker Berri says he "takes orders from no one." Lebanon demands a UN-backed force to replace UNIFIL. Jordan blocks Telegram, citing extremism.

7. Middle East Defense News.

Third US carrier, USS Theodore Roosevelt, is on station, joining the George Washington and George H.W. Bush in enforcing the Iran blockade (about 20,000 personnel). US and Israel are reportedly drawing up plans for possible new strikes.

Gulf flashpoints: Iran claims a drone strike on a US radar in Bahrain, a tanker near Hormuz was hit and its engine room caught fire (24 Indian crew safe), and the UN has logged 130+ vessel incidents in Hormuz since the war began.

Yemen: the Houthis seized the coast from Dhubab to Bab al-Mandab and are within about 60 km of Aden. The Saudi-led coalition intercepted Houthi drones aimed at Makkah and Riyadh.

Lebanon: the IDF struck a Hezbollah weapons shipment in the Beqaa near the Syrian border. ISW reports that Hezbollah is redeploying fighters south.

8. Dutch Local Headlines.

The Jetten cabinet survives, for now. After days of crisis talks over the D66/CDA tax deal with PRO and Volt, which the VVD voted against, the RVD says the minority coalition will continue. Details go to the ministers today. Yeşilgöz had called the minority cabinet a "failed experiment."

GPS jamming around Schiphol disrupted at least seven flights. Experts see a Russian hybrid-war link.

Other headlines: a fourth bird-flu outbreak in Gelderland led to about 160,000 birds being culled, and the Tweede Kamer received a proposal for mandatory e-bike helmets for under-18s.

9. European Union News.

The Foreign Affairs Council (16 November) approved 145 new Russia sanctions designations. A 22nd sanctions package is being finalised this week. Several ministers warn it is premature to discuss Europe's security architecture with Moscow before a ceasefire.

Politico: Kallas has been left off the EU team for Ukraine–Russia negotiations, which are being run at national security adviser level. Lithuania and Spain object.

France and the UK will host a conference on Russia's shadow fleet on 4 December.

10. NATO News.

NATO drops the 2027 Albania leaders' summit and cuts back on ministerials. Rutte says the alliance will hold "summits when we need them."

Rutte tells industry that "demand still exceeds supply" and says five allies are projected to hit 5% in 2026. He also reassured Ukraine's Rada speaker of continued NATO support.

11. Elections.

Just happened.

Cabo Verde (15 November): President José Maria Neves (PAICV) was re-elected with about 64% to Joana Rosa's 31.6%, so no runoff is needed. Turnout was under one-third.

New Zealand (7 November): the result is a hung outcome on preliminary counts. National–ACT–NZ First has 67 of 131 seats, Opportunity holds 7, and final results come 27 November.

Upcoming.

Northern Mariana Islands governor (20 November), Taiwan local elections (28 November), Spain snap general election and Swiss referendums (29 November), Gambia presidential (5 December), Fiji referendum (9 December).

South Sudan's elections are postponed from 22 December to 11 February 2027.

12. Africa In-Depth.

Sudan / Ethiopia: risk of a wider war. Sudan's army (SAF) says it shot down a long-range drone over Blue Nile on 13 November that it alleges came from Ethiopia, and Reuters imagery earlier showed an RSF camp on Ethiopian soil. The UN envoy Haavisto is stepping up diplomacy to prevent an Ethiopia–Sudan clash. Meanwhile SAF retook Al-Khuwai in West Kordofan, but an SAF "double-tap" drone strike in Abu Qalb reportedly killed 15 people, including children playing football. The RSF alliance is fraying: the Third Front (Tamazuj) threatens to quit. WFP says 900,000 people are cut off from food aid.

Sahel: JNIM versus ISSP infighting. Clashes between the two groups have intensified since September; ISSP claims it killed 200+ JNIM fighters. JNIM nonetheless keeps gaining against Burkina Faso's state forces and exerts economic control in Mali's Kayes region. ACLED flags signs of a coming FLA offensive in northern Mali.

South Sudan's delay to February comes amid violence between Kiir loyalists and supporters of the detained Machar, raising fears of a renewed civil war.

13. Asia / India.

Japan protests after two suspected Russian aircraft violated its airspace near Okinoshima (Fukuoka). South Korea scrambled jets as Russian planes entered its air defence zone.

India: in the "vote chori" row, the INDIA bloc is seeking the Supreme Court's permission to question Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over irregularities in the electoral roll revision (SIR).

ASEAN ended without a South China Sea code-of-conduct text.

14. OSINT Updates (unconfirmed or developing; treat with caution)

Kerch Bridge: Vantor/Maxar imagery and geolocated footage indicate a road-vehicle span has dropped, and Ukraine's General Staff released video of an FP-5 "Flamingo" cruise missile in the final moments before impact. The full extent of structural damage and the status of the rail line are unconfirmed.

Qeshm Island, Iran: several OSINT channels report explosions and surface-to-air missile/anti-aircraft fire coinciding with the Roosevelt's arrival. Neither CENTCOM nor Iranian state media has confirmed it.

Hezbollah repositioning: ISW assesses that fighters and weapons are moving back into south Lebanon, possibly in preparation for action if the Iran war resumes. Israel reportedly judges intervention "highly likely."

Iran's missile range: Tasnim outlines how Mojtaba Khamenei's revocation of the 2,000 km missile range cap could enable missiles able to reach US bases in Europe. This is an analysis piece, not evidence of a new capability.

Sudan: the drone SAF says came from Ethiopia is a claim only. No imagery has independently verified its launch point.
