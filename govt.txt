Tuesday, 24 November 2026: Daily Government Briefing.

United Nations.

Yesterday the Security Council held a high-level briefing on Ukraine titled "Protecting Critical Energy Infrastructure and Black Sea Lifelines of Global Food Supply." Latvia, which holds the November presidency, called the meeting. Under-Secretary-General Rosemary DiCarlo, WFP Executive Director Luke Lindberg and UNDP Administrator Alexander De Croo all briefed. DiCarlo said OHCHR recorded 164 attacks on commercial vessels in the Odesa and Mykolaiv regions and the Black Sea between June and October. She warned that attacks are spreading beyond Ukrainian and Russian territorial waters. Russia's deputy ambassador Anna Evstigneeva blamed Kyiv ("We are not the ones who opened Pandora's Box"). The US called on both sides to stop striking energy infrastructure, ports and commercial shipping. The race to succeed António Guterres is still deadlocked. In the ninth straw poll on 20 November, Rebeca Grynspan (Costa Rica) kept her lead with 10 encourage, 4 discourage and 1 no opinion, down from 12 to 3 on 5 November. Rafael Grossi had 8 to 6 and Lana Nusseibeh 7 to 6. Every one of the eight candidates drew "discourage" votes from at least two permanent members, and Guterres's term ends on 31 December. Separately, the UN said COP31 in Antalya "collapsed" on 22 November after two days of overtime without a deal.

European Union.

The European Parliament is meeting in plenary in Strasbourg from 23 to 26 November. Today MEPs vote on speeding up environmental assessments. The S&D group says it will back no text that weakens the Birds and Habitats Directives. MEPs also debate the worsening humanitarian situation in Gaza today, the first such debate in several sessions. Wednesday's main item is a debate on European Council President António Costa's revised negotiating box for the 2028 to 2034 Multiannual Financial Framework, including new own resources. The Commission's College holds its weekly meeting today. In the Council, agriculture and fisheries ministers met in Brussels on Monday under Ireland's Martin Heydon. They discussed farm-market strains from drought and the war, the 2040 fisheries vision and 2027 fishing opportunities ahead of the December quota talks. Today is a day of preparatory bodies (PSC, Antici, Trade Policy Committee) ahead of Thursday's special European Council.

US House of Representatives.

The House is in Thanksgiving recess. It adjourned on Friday despite Speaker Mike Johnson urging members to work through the lame-duck session, and it returns on 30 November. After that it has only 12 legislative days before breaking on 17 December. Johnson's letter said he had to postpone votes last week because absent members left Republicans short for party-line votes, and Bloomberg Government reported that leaders trimmed the week's schedule for the same reason. The FY2027 NDAA (H.R. 8800) was on the floor schedule last week. Earlier in the lame duck, the House blocked an effort by outgoing Rep. Thomas Massie to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, 211 to 204. The main item waiting is government funding, which expires on 11 December. Appropriations Chair Tom Cole is still pushing for all 12 full-year bills, while Ranking Member Rosa DeLauro favors full-year bills where possible plus a "short" stopgap.

US Senate.

The Senate is out for a state work period from 23 to 27 November, so there is no floor business today. Last week it worked on the bipartisan permitting overhaul, using H.R. 2347 with the Capito substitute as the vehicle. It also confirmed Daniel Domenico to the Tenth Circuit and other Trump nominees. The main political story is the Democratic leadership contest, with the caucus vote set for 1 December. Chris Van Hollen is challenging Chuck Schumer to be majority leader in the incoming 55-member Democratic majority, and 28 votes are needed to win. Schumer says he has the votes locked down, and his allies predict more than 40. Van Hollen said on 22 November that he has support from "north of 15" senators, but only a handful, including Bernie Sanders and Jeff Merkley, back him publicly. Senate Republican appropriators say a short-term CR is the likely answer to the 11 December deadline.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is between argument sessions, with no arguments this week. Its next session opens on 30 November with Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia. The most notable recent order came on 16 November, when the justices declined without noted dissent to hear the Trump administration's appeal over roughly 150 dollarsmillion in canceled University of California research grants. That leaves in place a Ninth Circuit ruling that the DEI-based terminations likely violated the First Amendment. Earlier in the November sitting, the Court let the administration resume border-wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region, with only Justice Jackson noting she would deny. It also heard Nielsen v. Watanabe (liability of federal officials) and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy (pipeline just compensation). Justice Sotomayor's public remarks on term limits drew coverage yesterday.

White House.

President Trump and First Lady Melania Trump host the 79th National Thanksgiving Turkey pardon today at 2:00 p.m. ET at the South Lawn helipad. This year's birds, Freedom and Feathers, were raised in West Virginia, and both will retire to Texas A&M. Palm Beach has announced road closures and flight restrictions starting today, so Trump is expected to leave for Mar-a-Lago for the holiday. The White House is publicizing a "Transportation Technology Transformation Tiger Team" of FAA, DOT, DoD and ODNI officials. It was formed after this month's nationwide flight grounding and is working to prevent air-traffic outages during Thanksgiving travel. On foreign policy, Trump told Fox News on Sunday that any Iranian vessels trying to run the US naval blockade would be sunk. Secretary of State Rubio is hosting Qatar's prime minister, who is trying to revive US–Iran talks. The First Lady received the White House Christmas tree on Monday.

What's Coming Up.

EU leaders meet in Brussels on Thursday and Friday (26 to 27 November) for a special European Council on the 2028 to 2034 MFF and Ukraine's 2027 financing. Parliament's Strasbourg session ends Thursday. Spain holds a snap general election on Sunday, 29 November. Polls put Alberto Núñez Feijóo's PP ahead of Pedro Sánchez's Socialists, with PP and Vox together near or above the 176-seat majority. On 30 November the House returns and the Supreme Court opens its December argument session. On 1 December Senate Democrats vote on Schumer versus Van Hollen. That starts a compressed stretch before the 11 December funding deadline, with the NDAA, a possible debt-limit increase and a possible CR into early 2027 all competing for floor time. At the UN, the Security Council faces growing pressure to recommend a Secretary-General before Guterres's term ends on 31 December. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot's 28-day mandate to form a government runs to 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension.

International Politics Analysis.

The Gulf is the most dangerous theater right now. The US naval blockade of Iran has passed 130 consecutive days, and a third US carrier group has arrived in the region. Iran ended a two-month lull with drone strikes on a US radar site in Bahrain on 17 November and also hit Kuwait. A US jet then struck a Revolutionary Guard missile site on Qeshm Island. Egypt, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE all condemned the Bahrain attack, which weakens Tehran's diplomatic cover. Iran's foreign ministry says it will use "all available tools" against the blockade, and its parliament speaker has set seven preconditions for talks. The US is investigating possible Iranian cyberattacks on about 50 energy tankers, and Treasury has sanctioned Iran's shadow banking network. Qatar is still trying to restart negotiations, with its prime minister in Washington this week. Foreign Minister Araghchi says Tehran still wants a diplomatic way out, but Trump calls Iran's terms unacceptable and has threatened new strikes in the new year. Israeli military intelligence reportedly warned Netanyahu that a unilateral Israeli strike could set off a wider war on the Lebanon, Syria and Gaza fronts. That matters more because Israeli politics is in flux: after the 27 October election, President Herzog asked former army chief Gadi Eisenkot rather than Netanyahu to form a government. Eisenkot is expected to present a coalition plan within days.

On Russia and Ukraine, diplomacy and escalation are running side by side. On 19 November in Shenzhen, Trump, Xi Jinping and Putin held the first-ever trilateral summit of the three leaders on the sidelines of APEC. They issued a four-point "Declaration toward Peace" covering UN reform, more refined-fuel supply, ICC jurisdiction and the principle that nuclear war must never be fought. It said nothing on Ukraine, Iran or Taiwan and contained no arms-control commitments. US envoys then met Zelensky and European leaders in Berlin on security guarantees, and Europe's "Coalition of the Willing" said Ukraine's security cannot be decided without Europeans. Meanwhile the war is spreading at sea and along NATO's edge. Suspected Ukrainian drones struck a Russian cargo ship in the Baltic and a Russian warship in a Libyan port, and Zelensky confirmed the Libya attack. Finland's prime minister said the war is "spilling over into our immediate vicinity." Poland reported that two likely missiles and a drone violated its airspace, and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte pledged to defend "every inch" of Polish territory.

Elsewhere, Spain's election on Sunday could produce the first government in years that depends on Vox. That would shift the EU Council's balance on migration and climate files just as the MFF fight peaks. US–China competition continues despite the warm tone in Shenzhen: a Commerce Department rule in force since 19 November now requires licenses for advanced AI chips sent to overseas data centers that serve Chinese, Russian or Iranian firms. New satellite imagery shows North Korea expanding a uranium-enrichment plant. Taken together, great-power summitry is producing statements but no settlements, while the Iran and Ukraine wars keep expanding their geographic reach.
