Here is your government briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

It's a Sunday, so most institutions are not sitting. Here's where things stand.

United Nations. No major meetings today. The race to succeed Secretary-General Guterres is under way, and Gaza remains on the agenda, with the EU worried about slow progress on the Security Council's Gaza resolution.

European Union. Brussels is quiet. Last week MEPs voted on new CO2 standards for cars and vans. The Commission is preparing a tool to let member states limit market access during surges of cheap imports. And Europe is watching, with alarm, a proposed summit between the US, China and Russia that would leave out Ukraine and the EU.

House of Representatives. Not in session. Democrats have won the House, with a few races still uncalled, and say their mandate is about the economy. Republicans are weighing one last partisan budget bill before January, possibly including Iran war funding and a debt limit increase.

Senate. Also out. NPR's count gives Democrats 52 seats plus 2 independents, against 44 Republicans. Alaska and Mississippi are still uncalled. Trump is moving to put a defeated Texas judge on the federal bench, a sign the lame-duck session will be used for confirmations.

Supreme Court. No rulings today. Justice Sotomayor said she hopes the Court will reverse some of its recent decisions, including on presidential immunity. Pressure is growing on conservative justices to retire while Republicans still hold the Senate.

White House. Trump spent the weekend in Florida. Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as chief of staff, and Trump says he offered the press secretary job to UFC boss Dana White, who reportedly declined. In tonight's "60 Minutes" interview, Trump says he could stop insulting Democrats for six months to get a deal.

Coming up. The APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18 and 19 November, where a Trump, Xi and Putin meeting is being discussed. Russia and Ukraine may resume talks in Abu Dhabi after 10 November. And Congress returns for the lame-duck session.

The big picture. The proposed three-way summit would let Moscow push for a Ukraine settlement without Kyiv at the table. Beijing is likely to keep things vague. The Democratic win weakens Trump's hand abroad, and analysts warn Russia and China may see the next two years as their moment. In the Middle East, the war with Iran continues to drive oil prices, while Europe looks sidelined on both Ukraine and the Middle East.
