Here is your government briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

It is a Sunday, so most institutions are not in session, and coverage today was thin.

The United Nations. No Security Council or General Assembly meetings of note today. The race to succeed Secretary-General António Guterres is under way, and experts warn the UN faces a crisis as major powers act on their own.

The European Union. Brussels is quiet. Last week parliamentary committees voted on revised carbon dioxide standards for new cars and vans. The Commission plans a tool letting member states quickly limit market access during surges of cheap imports, and Europe is watching with alarm a proposed US, China and Russia summit that would leave out Ukraine and the EU.

The House. Not in session. Democrats have won control of Congress, though some races are still uncalled. Republicans are weighing one last partisan reconciliation bill in the lame-duck session, possibly with Iran war funding and a debt limit increase, though lawmakers doubt it will pan out.

The Senate. Also out. Democrats have 52 seats called plus 2 independents, against 44 for Republicans, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled. Trump is moving to nominate a Texas Supreme Court justice who lost her election on Tuesday to a lifetime federal judgeship, a sign the lame duck will be used for confirmations.

The Supreme Court. No opinions or orders. Justice Sotomayor said she hopes the Court will reverse some of the crazy things it has done, including the 2024 immunity ruling. Republican losses are expected to raise pressure on conservative justices to retire while a Republican Senate can still confirm a successor.

The White House. Trump spent the weekend in West Palm Beach. He said he offered the press secretary job to UFC chief Dana White, who reportedly declined,. Stephen Miller replaces Susie Wiles as chief of staff. In a 60 Minutes clip airing today, Trump says he could go six months without insulting Democrats to get a deal done.

Coming up. The Asia Pacific summit in Shenzhen on the 18th and 19th, where a Trump, Xi and Putin meeting is being discussed; China has confirmed talks but nothing is final. Russia agreed to resume US-mediated talks with Ukraine after the 10th, possibly in Abu Dhabi. Congress returns for the lame duck, and watch for retirement signals from the justices.

The big picture. The proposed three-way summit dominates. Putin floated it as a way to settle Ukraine on Moscow's terms without Zelenskyy at the table. Beijing is expected to keep any outcome vague, while Moscow gains standing just by being there. The midterm result weakens Trump's leverage abroad, and a Democratic Congress could start sending money to Kyiv. Meanwhile the war on Iran continues to drive fuel prices and politics, and Gaza faces a worsening humanitarian crisis as winter nears. Together, these threads show a Europe sidelined while Washington, Moscow and Beijing negotiate directly.
