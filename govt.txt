Daily Government Briefing — Friday, November 27, 2026.

United Nations.

The Security Council has a quiet day at the end of Latvia's November presidency. The main event this week was Tuesday's high-level open debate on "Conflict resolution in an era of rapid technological change," chaired by Latvian President Edgars Rinkēvičs. Secretary-General António Guterres told ambassadors that "killer robots must have no place in our future," and the ICRC's Mirjana Spoljaric Egger and Microsoft's Frederick Humphries also briefed. On Monday the Council heard that Russian strikes on Ukraine's power grid and attacks on Black Sea shipping are disrupting grain exports and pushing up food prices worldwide, and its Libya sanctions committee added a vessel to its list on November 24. The bigger story is the succession: after nine straw polls since July, Rebeca Grynspan keeps topping the field, but the US and Russia are expected to veto her. Experts warn that Guterres could leave office on December 31 with no successor in place, which would be a first for the UN.

European Union (Commission, Parliament, Council)

The special European Council in Brussels ended in the early hours of Friday after about 15 hours, with no deal on the 2028 to 2034 Multiannual Financial Framework. The "frugal" group (Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden, Austria, Denmark, Finland, and now Belgium) largely refused to join Costa's small-group sessions. Merz said even Costa's box, about 204 billion euros below the Commission's proposal, needs further cuts, and Meloni called a deal "light-years away." Macron warned that without agreement by December 31 he could not decide on the MFF later because of France's presidential election calendar. On Ukraine's 2027 financing, Bulgaria won an opt-out from the military part of the proposed 45 billion euros loan, while Hungary, Slovakia and Czechia still oppose taking part at all. The one clear result came on the ECB: euro-area leaders backed Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde when she steps down early in February, with Laurence Boone and Markus Brunnermeier going to the Executive Board. Formal appointment is expected at the December summit, and Costa will table a revised budget proposal in the coming days. In Strasbourg, Parliament rejected Costa's compromise, which its co-rapporteurs put at about 0.97% of GNI against its own 1.27% target. Siegfried Mureșan (EPP) said Parliament "cannot support this." MEPs also adopted their negotiating mandates for Horizon Europe FP10 (458 to 190 to 14) and the European Competitiveness Fund (386 to 252 to 23). Earlier in the week the Commission proposed a new Quantum Act, and Parliament gave final approval to the pharmaceutical overhaul.

US House of Representatives.

The House is on its Thanksgiving recess and holds only a noon pro forma session today. Leaders cancelled last Friday's votes and left early, despite Speaker Mike Johnson's letter asking for "full attendance." Members return Monday, November 30, and the House is scheduled to sit for just 12 more days before adjourning December 17. That is the last stretch of a Republican majority (219 seats in a 433-member House with two vacancies) before Democrats take control in January. The main unfinished item is government funding, which runs out December 11. Some Democrats say they will not back a stopgap while the Iran war continues, and Appropriations Chair Tom Cole is still pushing for full-year bills instead of a CR.

US Senate.

The Senate is in recess and meets only for a pro forma session at noon today, with no business expected. It returns Monday, November 30, at 3:00 p.m., with a roll-call vote at about 5:30 p.m. on confirming Kyle Reardon as U.S. District Judge for Alaska. Cloture has already been invoked on four more district-court nominees: Daniel Ballou (E.D. Ky.), Robert Long (M.D. Fla.), Peter Mansfield (E.D. La.) and Jesus Osete (W.D. Mo.), so judicial confirmations will take up much of next week's floor time. Senate Republicans on Appropriations say a short-term CR is the likely way through the December 11 deadline. Sen. Mike Rounds said he is hearing talk only of a stopgap running "until sometime in February or March." The incoming Senate will have 56 Democrats (including independents who caucus with them) and 44 Republicans, based on AP calls through November 17.

US Supreme Court.

The Court has no arguments or scheduled opinions today, and I found no orders or emergency rulings over the holiday. The December sitting opens Monday with Wassily v. Blanche and Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia, followed by Jouppi v. Alaska on Tuesday. The headline case comes Wednesday, December 2: the consolidated challenges to bans on AR-15s and other semiautomatic rifles, Viramontes v. Cook County and Grant v. Higgins. The justices also meet in conference on Friday, December 4.

White House.

President Trump is spending the holiday at Mar-a-Lago. His public schedule today shows only executive time at 7:00 a.m. and a policy meeting at 3:00 p.m. On Thanksgiving he played golf at Trump International in West Palm Beach, and in the evening he and the First Lady called U.S. service members. In remarks to reporters during that call, Trump said his Wednesday-night meeting with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte was "mostly" about Russia and Ukraine. The joint statement from that meeting urged Russia to avoid actions that "expand the war," discussed Iran, and called for more defense spending across NATO. Trump's own version on Truth Social later added Ukraine to that warning, and a NATO official told Politico the change was made unilaterally. Secretary of State Rubio has no public appointments today.

Both chambers return, the Senate votes on the Reardon nomination, the Supreme Court opens its December sitting, and EU trade ministers meet at the Foreign Affairs Council (Trade). The big US test is the December 11 funding deadline, with only eight legislative days to pass a CR or full-year bills before a partial shutdown. At the UN, Liberia takes the Council presidency on December 1, and President Joseph Boakai chairs a high-level debate on maritime security on December 3. Beyond next week, watch for Costa's revised MFF box ahead of the regular December European Council, and the G20 leaders' summit Trump hosts at Doral on December 14 to 15.

International Politics Analysis.

The great-power picture is still shaped by the November 19 trilateral summit between Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of APEC. The leaders met for about 80 minutes and issued a joint declaration repeating that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought." They reached nothing on Ukraine, Iran or arms cuts and announced no follow-up meeting. Most analysts saw Xi as the main winner, gaining standing as convener, and Putin as gaining legitimacy, while European officials called it a "Putin dream." Poland's Donald Tusk responded: "God, protect us from such friends." Washington is now pressing for a Ukraine–Russia deal by early January 2027. Envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff have held rounds in Moscow, Dublin and Berlin, and US officials have floated leader-level talks at the Miami G20. Zelensky wants to join a trilateral with Trump and Putin there. Moscow, however, appears to be relying on an airstrike campaign to build leverage rather than hurrying toward a deal.

On the ground, the war is spreading geographically. On November 22, seven Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in Benghazi, Libya, and disabled the cargo ship MV Angara. Ukraine also hit a Russian military-linked cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland. The Kremlin has seized on these strikes to accuse Kyiv of widening the conflict, the same framing Trump added to the Rutte statement. Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv just hours after the Mar-a-Lago meeting, and Ukraine answered with strikes on an oil pipeline in Bashkortostan and a refinery in Samara. In Europe, the deadlocked budget summit shows how money, Ukraine aid and national election calendars are now tangled together. Bulgaria's partial opt-out and the continued refusal of Hungary, Slovakia and Czechia show the coalition backing Kyiv's 2027 funding is fraying at the edges.

In the Middle East, the US–Iran war has moved into a tense, mostly maritime standoff. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said late Thursday that Pakistan and Qatar are still passing proposals between Tehran and Washington and that "a way forward could be found." He also repeated Iran's conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz: an end to the US naval blockade, an end to fighting on all fronts including Lebanon, sanctions relief, and the release of frozen assets. Washington wants guaranteed safe passage and insists Iran's nuclear program be part of any first-stage talks. Iran and Oman are separately working on a temporary safe-route arrangement for the strait. Pakistan hosts the Saudi and Turkish foreign ministers today and Saturday for talks on the Makkah defense pact, a sign that regional powers are hedging toward their own security arrangements while the US-led diplomacy stalls.
