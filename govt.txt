Sunday, 22 November 2026: Daily Government Briefing.

It's a quiet Sunday across all six institutions. This briefing covers the week that just ended and the week ahead, which is short because of Thanksgiving.

United Nations.

Nothing new is on the UN's public calendar this weekend. The big story is still the race to succeed António Guterres, whose second term ends on 31 December. The Security Council keeps holding secret straw polls. Rebeca Grynspan led the eighth round, but none of the seven candidates yet has the backing of all five veto-holding powers. On the security side, the Council held closed consultations on Sudan on 20 November and a Libya/ICC briefing on 18 November. These followed a 13 November session, requested by Bahrain, on Gulf maritime security. At that session, UN officials cited IMO figures: 134 incidents and 25 seafarers killed around Hormuz and the wider region, about 1,600 vessels disrupted, and more than 23,000 seafarers stranded. Guterres used his last ASEAN-UN summit, in Manila on 16 November, to say that suffering in Myanmar had reached "epic proportions." The US-China-Russia joint statement from Shenzhen (see White House) also included a pledge to pursue UN reform.

European Union (Commission, Parliament, Council)

The EU institutions are off for the weekend, and a busy week starts tomorrow. Parliament meets in plenary in Strasbourg from 23 to 26 November. On the agenda are the joint text for the 2027 budget (rapporteurs Ušakovs and Hadjipantela), the Omnibus IV digitalisation package, Omnibus VIII to speed up environmental assessments, a vote on the EU pharmaceutical reform, and a debate on the 2028 to 2034 long-term budget. The Council is still acting on the 13 November informal summit in Dublin. There, leaders decided that Europe will be represented in Ukraine peace talks by France, Germany, the UK, Italy and Poland together with the EU institutions, rather than by a single envoy. European Council President António Costa said leaders will return to Ukraine's financing needs for 2027 and beyond on 26 to 27 November, working from new Commission proposals. The Kremlin has not yet accepted European participation in the talks.

US House of Representatives.

The House is not in session. It ended a shortened lame-duck week ahead of a one-week Thanksgiving break. On 18 November, Republican leaders under Speaker Mike Johnson pulled a procedural rule vote that would have brought up the Direct Seller and Real Estate Agent Harmonization Act (H.R. 3495) and the American Franchise Act (H.R. 5267). They also cancelled Friday votes because not enough Republicans, many of them outgoing members, would be in town to pass a party-line vote. The House's main job now is government funding, which runs out after 11 December. Lawmakers are scheduled to be in session for only about 10 days before that deadline.

US Senate.

The Senate has also left for Thanksgiving. Its biggest vote of the week came on 19 November, when it rejected a war powers resolution (H.Con.Res. 93) aimed at blocking further US strikes on Iran, 48 to 51. This was the first test of Republican support for the war since the GOP lost Congress in the midterms. Three Republicans voted for it, Democrat John Fetterman voted against, and Chris Murphy, who is recovering from a stroke, did not vote. Earlier in the week the Senate confirmed Daniel Domenico to the Tenth Circuit (51 to 46) and Anna St. John to the Fifth Circuit (52 to 46). It also advanced H.R. 2347, a tax bill that excludes damages received for sexual abuse from gross income, adopting the Capito substitute 69 to 27 and invoking cloture 65 to 31. On funding, Majority Leader John Thune reports "some good conversations" but "no deals yet." Democrats are split over whether to tie spending to limits on the Iran war, and Trump is asking for an extra 350 dollarsbillion for the Pentagon.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is between sittings and has nothing scheduled today. The most active emergency matter this week fizzled out. On 18 November, Solicitor General D. John Sauer asked the Court to block a lower-court order that would bring back Temporary Protected Status for Venezuelans (Mullin v. National TPS Alliance, 26A714). A day later the government withdrew the request, because the Ninth Circuit had extended its own administrative stay until seven days after it issues a judgment. The Court's 16 November order list turned away DEI-related disputes and a Second Amendment challenge to Maryland's ban on guns in "sensitive places." It also agreed to hear a case on the administration's asylum ban. At the next private conference, on 4 December, the justices will consider United States v. Talbott (transgender service members), E. Jean Carroll's jury award, and the legality of Trump's acting U.S. attorney appointments.

White House.

The President is in Washington with no public events today: the schedule shows only executive time at 8:00 a.m. ET and an 8:00 a.m. pool call. He is back from a three-stop Asia trip. In Manila (17 to 18 November) he attended a special ASEAN-US summit and signed a trade deal with Thailand. In Shenzhen (18 to 19 November) he attended APEC, met Xi Jinping one-on-one, and held the first-ever three-way meeting with Xi and Vladimir Putin. The meeting produced a four-point "declaration toward peace" with a pledge to hold talks on reducing nuclear risks and on non-proliferation, but nothing concrete on Iran or Ukraine. At a long news conference on 20 November, with JD Vance and Marco Rubio behind him, Trump called the trip a resounding success. He defended his relationship with Putin, accused Iran of negotiating in bad faith, said he would stop US companies from building data centers abroad, and confirmed he had fired National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross. On 21 November he signed an executive order telling federal contractors to notify the White House of any information request from Congress and to withhold material the government considers privileged. The order is a move to get ahead of the oversight investigations expected when Democrats take control of Congress in January.

What's Coming Up.

Europe has the busiest calendar this week. Parliament's Strasbourg plenary runs 23 to 26 November, an EU–Western Balkans summit meets in Brussels on 25 November, and EU leaders take up Ukraine's 2027+ financing on 26 to 27 November. In Washington, both chambers are out for Thanksgiving, and the Supreme Court is closed on 26 November. The Court's December argument session opens on 30 November with Crowther v. Board of Regents and the consolidated Second Amendment cases Viramontes v. Cook County and Grant v. Higgins. Senate Democrats choose their leader on 1 December (Chuck Schumer versus Chris Van Hollen), and the justices hold their next conference on 4 December. Government funding runs out after 11 December, so a shutdown on 12 December is a real risk. The US hosts the G20 at Trump National Doral on 14 to 15 December.

International Politics Analysis.

The Iran war, now nearly nine months old, is still the issue shaping world politics. It began with US and Israeli strikes in February. A 60-day ceasefire mediated by Pakistan ran out in August without a deal. Iran has all but closed the Strait of Hormuz, with transits about 65% below pre-war levels, and October brought the most attacks on shipping since the war began. Tehran says it will reopen the strait only if seven conditions are met, including an end to the war on all fronts, the lifting of the US naval blockade, and the release of frozen assets. Washington wants limits on Iran's nuclear program in any deal from the start, and Rubio has called Iran's demands "unacceptable." The risk of escalation remains. The USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the region on 16 November, and Iran warned it might strike first. Bahrain and Kuwait reported intercepting attacks this week. The IAEA says it is "increasingly concerned" about the enrichment plant at Isfahan, and Iran's Assembly of Experts has called for a review of defense doctrine that could lead to dropping its ban on nuclear weapons. In Washington the war is now as much a domestic problem as a foreign one. Republicans largely blame it for their midterm losses, and Democrats are weighing whether to use the December funding deadline to limit it.

Talks on Ukraine are moving forward but have produced little. On 10 to 11 November, Russia and Ukraine held their first direct talks in nine months, in Abu Dhabi with US mediation. They agreed to a large prisoner exchange but made no political breakthrough. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner then met Putin in Moscow on 12 November. Kyiv still rejects Moscow's demand that it give up all of Donbas. The Kremlin is also sending mixed signals about whether Europeans will be at the table: Witkoff said Putin had agreed, but aide Yuri Ushakov said on 16 November that Russia had made no such commitment. The Shenzhen summit showed how the great powers line up. Hosting the meeting raised Xi's diplomatic standing. For Putin, attending APEC for the first time in nine years and sitting as an equal next to the US and Chinese leaders was a gain in prestige, even though the meeting produced little. China's foreign ministry had already called trilateral disarmament negotiations "neither reasonable nor realistic," so the "nuclear risk" dialogue is better seen as a channel for talks than as the start of arms control.

Elections are adding uncertainty in Europe. Spain votes in a snap general election on 29 November, which Pedro Sánchez called after parliament voted down his housing decrees. Nearly every private poll shows Alberto Núñez Feijóo's PP and Santiago Abascal's Vox close to or above a combined 176-seat majority. Tomorrow, 23 November, is the last day polls may be published. A government that includes Vox would move one of the EU's largest center-left governments to the right, just as Brussels negotiates its 2 eurostrillion 2028 to 2034 budget and its Ukraine financing. In Southeast Asia, ASEAN's Manila summit again left Myanmar's junta out despite lobbying from Thailand, and Trump's 24-hour stop reflected how far Washington's attention has shifted toward China.
