Daily Government Briefing — Monday, November 23, 2026.

United Nations.

The Security Council meets this morning (10:00 New York time) under Latvia's November presidency for a briefing on Ukraine, with UN political affairs chief Rosemary DiCarlo expected to brief as Kyiv presses members to demand that Russia halt attacks on civilians. Secretary-General António Guterres spent the weekend in Barbados with Prime Minister Mia Mottley. From there he called the collapse of COP31 in Antalya "deeply disappointing": the talks ended Sunday with no overarching outcome after two days of overtime, because countries could not agree on fossil-fuel language, and several objected that the final text weakened earlier commitments. The Secretariat is also warning about escalation in Yemen. Deputy spokesman Farhan Haq said the UN is "deeply concerned" after Saudi-led coalition strikes on Hudaydah port, a lifeline for millions of Yemenis. Last week the US-led Board of Peace told the Council that the Gaza ceasefire risks collapse unless it is implemented in full.

European Union.

The European Parliament opens its Strasbourg plenary (23 to 26 November) at 17:00 today. President Roberta Metsola meets Commission President Ursula von der Leyen at 16:15 beforehand and chairs the Bureau at 18:00. The week's agenda includes a debate on Tuesday about restoring the rule of law in Hungary after its April elections, a Gaza humanitarian debate (the first in several sessions), votes on the EU ETS market stability reserve, and a debate on Thursday about the COP31 outcome. The main fight is the 2028 to 2034 budget. On Saturday, European Council President António Costa unveiled a revised MFF "negotiating box" that cuts the Commission's proposal by about 10% overall and external action by about 21%, while keeping 158 eurosbillion for Horizon Europe. Germany, the "frugal" states, France and Parliament all criticised it, and Dutch Finance Minister Eelco Heinen said "either the EU budget must come down or our rebate… must go up." In the Council, the Agriculture and Fisheries Council and a General Affairs Council on cohesion meet today.

US House of Representatives.

The House is in its Thanksgiving recess and holds no votes this week. It left Thursday, a day early, after GOP leaders pulled a procedural rule vote and cancelled Friday's session because too many members were absent: 32 lawmakers, including 21 Republicans, missed Thursday morning's votes. Speaker Mike Johnson sent his conference a letter on Friday saying that "barring any serious medical issue, we need every Republican to be here." The lame-duck majority is 219 seats in a 433-member House with two vacancies. Members return November 30 and have about 12 legislative days before the scheduled December 17 departure. Outstanding work includes government funding, the NDAA, the Senate-passed permitting reform bill, the farm bill and a proposed 95 dollarsbillion reconciliation package.

US Senate.

The Senate is in a state work period for Thanksgiving (November 23 to 27) and has no floor business scheduled. Its biggest recent action was passing a large permitting reform bill on Thursday night, which now goes to the House. Government funding is the main unresolved issue. Appropriators in both chambers now say a short-term extension, probably into the new year, will be needed to avoid a shutdown at the December 11 deadline. The House has passed three of the 12 appropriations bills and the Senate none, and the administration's request for a record 1.5 dollarstrillion for defense is the central dispute with Senate Democrats.

US Supreme Court.

The Court has no arguments this week. The November sitting is over, and the building closes Thursday for Thanksgiving. The most notable recent docket development is the Venezuelan TPS case. On November 19, Solicitor General D. John Sauer withdrew emergency application 26A714, which sought to block restoration of Temporary Protected Status for Venezuelans, after the Ninth Circuit extended its stay until seven days after it rules. The government had warned that restoring TPS could require releasing about 1,800 detainees, and it says it will return to the justices if it loses in the Ninth Circuit. The December sitting opens November 30 with Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia.

White House.

President Trump's one public event today is a 4:30 p.m. ET speech on "Protecting American Families" at the Justice Department. Before it he has executive time, his intelligence briefing at 11:00 and closed-press policy meetings at 12:30 and 3:00. At noon First Lady Melania Trump receives the official 2026 White House Christmas tree on the North Drive. At 1:30 she and the Second Lady assemble Red Cross care packages with military spouses at Joint Base Andrews. Trump returned last week from the APEC summit in Shenzhen. In a Fox News interview he said Washington is working to expand earlier localized humanitarian arrangements between Ukraine and Russia into a "larger-scale" ceasefire, and the First Lady has said she hopes for a deal by Christmas.

What's Coming Up.

EU business dominates this week. MEPs debate Costa's MFF proposal on Wednesday morning, the EU–Western Balkans summit is also Wednesday (November 25), and a Euro Summit and a special European Council on the 2028 to 2034 budget and Ukraine funding for 2027 follow on November 26 to 27. Costa is aiming for a final deal at the December European Council. Thanksgiving (November 26) keeps Washington quiet until November 30, when both chambers of Congress return and the Supreme Court opens its December sitting. EU trade ministers also meet that day. The hard deadline is December 11, when government funding lapses, and a stopgap CR is now widely expected. Beyond that, Trump hosts the G20 leaders' summit at Trump National Doral in Miami on December 14 to 15. Putin has been invited, and there are reports Trump wants a Russia–Ukraine deal signed there.

International Politics Analysis.

Great-power diplomacy is still digesting last week's APEC summit in Shenzhen (November 18 to 19), where Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin held the first-ever trilateral meeting of the three leaders. They issued a joint declaration that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and agreed to boost global oil supplies. On trade, the summit produced little else: there were no changes to US–China tariffs, rare-earth and technology disputes stayed unresolved in public, and the US and China openly disagreed over who should govern AI. Trump reportedly repeated that the US does not support Taiwan independence. Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi instead called the Quad summit the week's "major outcome," pointing to its opposition to changing the status quo by force. The joint statement did not mention the Iran war, which Trump had said he wanted to discuss.

On Ukraine, there is more diplomatic activity but little to show for it yet. A third round of US–Ukraine–Russia talks opened in Abu Dhabi on November 10. The White House is now trying to widen the localized truce arrangements, starting with the Oleshky evacuation deal brokered with the First Lady's involvement, into a broader ceasefire. Zelensky says the next four-party round, which would include Europe, must produce an "all-for-all" prisoner exchange and concrete steps, and he accuses Moscow of stalling. Strikes continue. Brussels is meanwhile trying to secure Ukraine funding through the MFF and has just held its second EU–UK summit with Prime Minister Andy Burnham, which shows Europe working to keep its own role while Washington deals with Moscow directly.

The Middle East is getting worse on two fronts. In Yemen, the Saudi-led coalition launched a "special operation" against Houthi-held Hudaydah port on November 20 after an attack on the tower housing Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund headquarters in Riyadh. The Houthis responded by firing three ballistic missiles toward Riyadh, which were intercepted, and threatening Jeddah and all of Saudi Arabia's Red Sea ports. Houthi authorities report 41 people killed by coalition strikes in a single 24-hour period. In Gaza, the Board of Peace's latest report says the ceasefire "that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely." More than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the truce began, the International Stabilization Force has still not deployed, and a senior board adviser has warned that the board may hand "the keys" to the UN if Israel keeps blocking the interim administration. Together with COP31 ending without a deal, these developments add to the strain on multilateral institutions ahead of the Miami G20.
