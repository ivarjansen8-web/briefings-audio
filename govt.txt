Here is your government briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Lame-duck Congress, Abu Dhabi talks and a Dublin summit ahead.

Wednesday, November 11, 2026.

United Nations.

The Security Council has had a busy week around the edges of today. It met on Ukraine on 9 November and on the UN Mission in South Sudan on 10 November, and the General Assembly was scheduled to hold a plenary on 10 November under its veto initiative after Russia and the US both vetoed a Western Sahara text on 30 October. Today I found no specific Council or Assembly meeting reported, so the day looks quiet at UN Headquarters. Ahead of 15 November the Council is expected to vote on renewing the Abyei force (UNISFA), whose mandate and border-monitoring support expire that day, and it has a Middle East meeting set for 13 November. Latvia holds the Council presidency this month and has flagged civilian protection and Ukraine's energy infrastructure as priorities.

European Union.

Ukraine dominates the EU agenda. The US-mediated Ukraine-Russia talks run 10 to 11 November in Abu Dhabi, and Ukraine's chief negotiator said representatives of Britain, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission would be in the city on the same days, although Europeans are not formal parties to the talks and the Kremlin calls them trilateral. Separately, the Commission sent its 2027 work programme to the Council on 4 November, and it has opened negotiations on integrating six Western Balkan countries into parts of the EU system. I found no reported Parliament plenary vote or Council ministerial decision for today. Attention is on Dublin, where leaders gather tomorrow.

US House of Representatives.

The House returned on 9 November for a lame-duck session of about six weeks after Democrats won control of both chambers in the midterms. Speaker Mike Johnson is reported to be stepping aside from leadership, with Majority Leader Steve Scalise among those expected to seek posts. Rep. Thomas Massie, who lost his primary, is pushing a privileged resolution to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and reportedly has 216 signatures, and he is also pressing another Epstein-related vote. Today is the Veterans Day federal holiday and I found no reported floor votes, so the substantive action resumes later this week. Government funding runs out after December 11, and the NDAA, farm bill and FISA reform are also pending.

US Senate.

The Senate also returned on 9 November, with a procedural vote that day meant to show whether pre-recess funding talks would hold. A growing number of Republican senators wanted to delay the party's leadership elections, set for the morning of 10 November, so candidates could explain their plans after the midterm losses. I could not confirm the outcome of that election or any floor action today, which is a federal holiday. Senate Democrats will hold their own leadership elections in early December, with Chuck Schumer lining up support for majority leader, and at least 17 senators are leaving at the end of the term. The debt limit and Iran war funding are among the lame-duck questions.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is closed today for the Veterans Day legal holiday and has no argument scheduled. This week it heard Nielsen v. Watanabe and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission on 9 November and Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards on 10 November, and it issued an order list on 9 November along with a per curiam in Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church (26A560). Commentators expect election-law emergency applications to reach the Court as the post-midterm period unfolds, and the interim docket remains active on immigration disputes. The next argument session begins 30 November with Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia.

White House.

The president's posted public schedule for today centers on Veterans Day. It lists a wreath-laying ceremony with the First Lady at Arlington National Cemetery at 11:00 a.m., Veterans Day remarks at 11:30 a.m., and further remarks at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool at 2:00 p.m., with a return to the White House around 3:00 p.m. The schedule page I found is a third-party tracker with inconsistent location labels, so treat times as indicative. Separately, Trump has been defending Hegseth ahead of the House impeachment push, saying he is "guilty of two things, basically winning two wars."

What's Coming Up.

Tomorrow, 12 November, about 50 leaders meet at the European Political Community summit in Dublin, with Zelensky expected, and an informal EU27 gathering follows on 13 November where Chancellor Merz wants Ukraine financing and burden-sharing on the agenda. The Security Council meets on the Middle East on 13 November and should vote on the Abyei mission before it expires on 15 November. In Washington, the Hegseth impeachment vote, the Senate Republican leadership fight and the December 11 funding deadline will drive the lame-duck calendar, and the Supreme Court is out until its 30 November session. ASEAN-related leaders' meetings in Manila are slated for roughly 15 to 18 November, and the Commission chairs a Trans-Caspian corridor dialogue on 17 November.

International Politics Analysis.

The center of gravity today is Abu Dhabi, where Ukrainian, Russian and US delegations are holding a first round that a German official called a "kick-off." Europeans are fighting for a seat: Italy and Poland want a wider role, and Germany says any deal must be part of a comprehensive settlement with NATO consensus and European participation in security decisions. The talks follow a G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris, where Ukraine's foreign minister said discussions covered defense and energy support, sanctions, frozen Russian assets and Europe's role. Reports also mention a possible large prisoner exchange after the talks. On the battlefield, Ukraine reported Russian strikes on an aid distribution point that killed 18, and Washington's posture is uncertain after Republicans were routed in the midterms, even as Trump approved a 2.1 dollarsbillion sale of 500 Patriot missiles.

The Middle East remains a second pressure point. Congress is still wrestling with funding for the US war with Iran, Security Council members are watching disruption to shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandab, and in October China and Russia vetoed a US-backed sanctions panel on Iranian nuclear facilities, triggering a General Assembly debate. Sudan's war is spilling into the Abyei border region, with drone attacks on a market there prompting UN calls for civilian protection. The week's pattern of vetoes, on Iran and on Western Sahara, underlines the Security Council's paralysis and feeds the ongoing push for reform.

The wider diplomatic calendar is crowded and fractured. Secretary of State Rubio chaired G20 foreign ministers in Atlanta on 30 October, urging cooperation despite rifts over Ukraine and the Middle East, and a leaders' round tied to ASEAN in Manila is due mid-month. Inside the EU, Hungary is now represented by Prime Minister Peter Magyar at European Council meetings, a notable shift in the bloc's internal politics, and Brussels is simultaneously managing enlargement talks, Frontex expansion and the 2028 to 2034 budget. Semafor's autumn coverage described the system as fracturing in the face of American unpredictability, which is a fair summary of the backdrop to this week's meetings.
