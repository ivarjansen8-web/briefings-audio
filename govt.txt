Here is your government briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Lame-duck Congress faces December. 11 funding cliff as UN renews Abyei and CAR missions.

Sunday, November 15, 2026.

United Nations.

The Security Council's most recent substantive business came at the end of the week. It adopted resolution 2838 (2026) extending the UN Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA) and its support for the Joint Border Verification and Monitoring Mechanism until 15 November 2027, by 13 votes in favour with China and Russia abstaining over the linkage of future renewals to political benchmarks. It also renewed the UN mission in the Central African Republic (MINUSCA) until 15 November 2027, in a France-drafted resolution reported as unanimous. Both missions' mandates had been due to expire today. Elsewhere, the General Assembly met in October to examine a Security Council veto cast by China and Russia over a US-backed sanctions panel on Iran's nuclear facilities, and the Council-reform and Secretary-General selection debates continue. As it is a Sunday, I found no formal Council or Assembly meetings scheduled for today.

European Union.

Today is a quiet Sunday across the Commission, Parliament and Council, and I found no votes or formal meetings scheduled. Recent background includes the Commission's 2027 work programme (COM(2026) 710), which reached the Council on 4 November. The EU-Canada summit in Montreal on 29 to 30 October launched an "Alliance for the Future", though Germany pushed to strip the term "associate membership" that Commission President von der Leyen favours, leaving a visible split between Berlin and Brussels. The Council's website also lists an EU-UK Summit, with media registration closing on 13 November, but the sources I found did not give its date. Reporting on the Abu Dhabi talks notes that the EU has no seat at the Russia-US-Ukraine negotiations, with European officials meeting the Ukrainian delegation on the sidelines.

US House of Representatives.

The House is not expected to hold votes today. It returned on 9 November for a five-week lame-duck session after the midterms, in which Democrats won at least 255 seats, with Hakeem Jeffries expected to become Speaker on 3 January. The main task is funding that expires on 11 December, with a short-term continuing resolution the likeliest path. The National Defense Authorization Act is also pending and entangled in disputes over the Iran war. Republican Rep. Thomas Massie has introduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth that the House is likely to vote on, and Republicans are contesting leadership posts, including Steve Scalise against Jim Jordan for minority leader. Democrats hold their own leadership elections the week after Republicans.

US Senate.

The Senate is not in legislative session today. Its published schedule has it convening at 3:00 p.m. Monday, 16 November, with two roll-call votes at 5:30 p.m., the first on confirming Daniel Desmond Domenico to the Tenth Circuit. Last week's floor record shows a 73 to 25 cloture vote on 9 November (roll call 257), a 62 to 21 vote on a motion to proceed on 10 November (roll call 258), and a series of military nominations confirmed by voice vote. Senate Democrats, who could reach as many as 56 seats in the next Congress, are weighing whether to use the 11 December funding deadline as leverage to force an end to the Iran war.

US Supreme Court.

The Court has no sitting or order list today. On 9 November it granted the Trump administration's emergency request in Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church (26A560) to resume border wall construction in the Big Bend region of Texas, with Justice Jackson noting she would deny it. The same day it denied review in more than 200 cases and asked the solicitor general for the government's views in a terrorism-financing case against pharmaceutical companies. It heard Nielsen v. Watanabe (Bivens liability) and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission on 9 November and Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards on 10 November. On 13 November it agreed to hear a case on the administration's suspension of asylum. The November session is over, and the next arguments begin 30 November.

White House.

The president's public schedule for today shows only "Executive Time" at the White House at 8:00 a.m., closed to the press, and he is spending the weekend in Washington. Saturday was similar. On Friday he held four closed policy meetings in the Oval Office, after which Markwayne Mullin and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy briefed reporters on an FAA ground stop. He is due to travel to the Philippines for the ASEAN meetings, arriving 17 November, then to China for APEC.

What's Coming Up.

Tomorrow the Senate returns at 3:00 p.m. with the Domenico confirmation vote at 5:30 p.m. Trump arrives in the Philippines on 17 November and attends the ASEAN-US meeting on 18 November before heading to the APEC summit in China. The Supreme Court resumes arguments on 30 November. The pivotal date is 11 December, when government funding expires, with the NDAA, a possible Hegseth impeachment vote, Iran-war funding fights and a January debt-ceiling deadline all converging before Democrats take control on 3 January. Senate Democratic leadership elections are slated for early December, and the ICC trial of former Philippine president Duterte is reported to begin 30 November.

International Politics Analysis.

The Ukraine track is the clearest diplomatic movement of the week. US-mediated talks between Russia, Ukraine and the US in Abu Dhabi on 10 to 11 November, the first since February, ended with an agreement in principle on a major prisoner exchange but no breakthrough. The agenda shifted toward post-war security arrangements for Ukraine and Europe, which Moscow's negotiator framed as the central issue, while strikes continued during the talks. Analysts at CFR argue the war is likely to continue unless Washington moves off its quasi-neutral stance, and the exclusion of the EU from the table is a recurring grievance in Brussels. Those talks only resumed after earlier peace efforts stalled when Trump launched the war against Iran.

The Iran war remains the dominant background fact in Washington. It is unpopular at home, Trump has acknowledged it contributed to his party's midterm losses, and it now shapes the NDAA and funding negotiations. Reporting, whose date I could not confirm, describes the US and Iran working with mediators on a 14-point memorandum to frame a monthlong period of talks to end the war, and a third round of Israel-Lebanon peace talks was reported as possible soon. At the UN, China and Russia's October veto of a US-backed Iran sanctions panel shows how split the Security Council remains.

The week ahead is crowded with summitry. The 49th ASEAN Summit opens today in the Philippines, followed by East Asia Summit sessions on 16 to 17 November and Trump's arrival on 17 November. Trump is then due at APEC in China, and Xi Jinping is expected at the G20 in Miami in December, after a spring Trump visit to Beijing. Elsewhere, Paraguay is in political turmoil after President Peña resigned on 5 November under pressure over a bank collapse, was impeached 78 to 0 and removed by the Senate on 6 November, and is under house arrest. A UNODC survey found Afghan opium poppy cultivation up 330 percent year-on-year in 2026.
