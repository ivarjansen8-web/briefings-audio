United Nations.

The Security Council, under Latvia's November presidency, met Monday on Ukraine, hearing that 328 civilians were killed and 2,105 injured in September, an 85 percent rise on a year earlier, and that October strikes on Kyiv's three main power stations left 600,000 people without electricity. Council members stayed split on responsibility for the war, with Ukraine and Poland demanding an unconditional ceasefire while the US and China urged both sides to use the opening for negotiations; the US credited a Melania Trump-facilitated humanitarian pause in Oleshky that allowed about 800 civilians to be evacuated. Today the Council is due to hear a briefing on South Sudan (UNMISS). Later this month it must renew the UNISFA (Abyei) and MINUSCA mandates before their 15 November expiry, along with Yemen sanctions and the Somalia Al-Shabaab sanctions regime, and it is continuing straw polls on the next Secretary-General. Latvia's signature event will be a high-level open debate on emerging technologies and security.

European Union.

The EU week opened Monday with the Eurogroup and ECOFIN in Brussels, where ministers took up the digital euro, defence financing and tobacco taxation. Today (Tuesday) the 2027 EU budget enters conciliation between Parliament and Council. Parliament holds a two-day Brussels mini-plenary starting Wednesday, while Council working parties continue on carbon pricing, cloud and AI infrastructure, electricity costs, border management, medical devices and 2027 fishing opportunities. US House of Representatives.

The House is back for a lame-duck session after Democrats won the chamber in the November 3 midterms; Republicans lose control on January 3. The headline fight is Rep. Thomas Massie's push this week to force a vote on articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over his handling of the Iran war, with some Republicans, including Rep. James Comer, reportedly voicing reservations about Hegseth. Massie, who lost his primary to a Trump-aligned challenger, also wants another Epstein-related vote. Leadership would rather avoid both, and government funding expires December 11. I did not find a confirmed floor schedule or vote counts for today.

US Senate.

Democrats also flipped the Senate, though two Senate races (reportedly Mississippi and Alaska) remained too close to call as of Monday; Democratic Sen.-elect Adam Hamilton won an upset in Kansas. In the lame duck, Senate Republicans are racing to confirm Trump administration officials and judicial nominees before losing the majority, and must deal with the annual National Defense Authorization Act, which could become a vehicle for fights over the Iran war and Hegseth. A bipartisan permitting bill is also pending, and at least 17 senators are leaving, raising attendance questions. No specific roll-call votes for today were confirmed in my search.

US Supreme Court.

Today the Court hears argument in Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards, LLC (No. 25 to 966); Wednesday, November 11, is a legal holiday. On Monday the Court granted the administration's emergency application to lift a district judge's order halting border-barrier construction in the Big Bend region of West Texas, including Big Bend National Park, with Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson the only publicly noted dissenter. The Court said the 1996 waiver statute likely precludes judicial review of the challengers' claims and that the government's interest outweighs the alleged aesthetic, recreational and environmental harm. Monday's arguments were Nielsen v. Watanabe and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission.

White House.

I could not find a published schedule for the president today. Yesterday Trump met Qatar's prime minister, then made a surprise appearance in the briefing room to say further military action against Iran "won't be necessary" for now and that the war is "militarily, effectively over," while adding that US forces remain "locked and loaded." He has appointed Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff, and has questioned the midterm results and demanded Justice Department probes of Democrats who sent threatening letters. Attorney General Todd Blanche warned Democrats planning oversight investigations to be "worried." The White House is so far standing by Hegseth, with Trump calling his performance "very good."

What's Coming Up.

US-backed trilateral talks between Russia and Ukraine are slated for November 10 to 11 in Abu Dhabi. The EU Parliament plenary opens Wednesday in Brussels, and the European Political Community summit and an EU leaders' discussion on AI follow in Dublin at the end of the week. At the UN, the UNISFA and MINUSCA renewals and the Yemen sanctions renewal are due by November 15. In Washington, expect Massie's attempt to force a Hegseth impeachment vote this week, and the December 11 funding deadline and NDAA frame the rest of the lame duck. The Supreme Court is off Wednesday for Veterans Day.

International Politics Analysis.

The dominant story is the US-Iran war, now in its ninth month after the US-Israeli strikes of February 28 that killed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. After the midterms, which Vice President Vance and Trump both linked partly to public opposition to the war, diplomacy has accelerated: Qatar is circulating a framework document that both sides are amending, and Trump says momentum points to an end "fairly soon." The gaps remain large. Iran demands seven preconditions, including an end to the war on all fronts and lifting of the maritime blockade, plus control over Hormuz, while Washington wants Iran's enriched uranium diluted or removed and Secretary of State Rubio called Iran's demands "outrageous" on Sunday. Meanwhile pressure continues: Task Force Iron Gate was set up on November 7 to choke Iranian smuggling routes, ships in Hormuz are still being attacked, and the Houthis took the Bab al-Mandeb Strait in September and have struck Saudi targets, including a missile hit near Aramco's Riyadh offices. Hardliners in Tehran may resist any deal, and Gulf states are divided on how much Iran should gain.

On Ukraine, Russian strikes on the power grid and civilians are intensifying ahead of winter, while a humanitarian ceasefire in Oleshky has given some hope for the Abu Dhabi talks on November 10 to 11. The Security Council debate showed the familiar divide, with Poland and Ukraine insisting aggression must not be rewarded and the US and China pushing for a negotiated settlement. Attacks on Black Sea shipping also threaten grain exports.

Elsewhere, G7 foreign ministers met in Paris this weekend, COP31 is under way in Antalya, Turkey, and the Security Council's secretary-general selection straw polls are continuing, a reminder that leadership succession at the UN is moving in parallel. Domestically in the US, the Democratic takeover of Congress in January will constrain the administration's war powers and set up the oversight confrontations already being previewed between Blanche and Rep. Robert Garcia, which will shape how freely Trump can pursue either escalation or a deal with Iran.
