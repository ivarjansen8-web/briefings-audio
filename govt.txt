Wednesday, November 18, 2026: Daily Government Briefing.

United Nations.

Secretary-General António Guterres spent the start of the week in Manila. At his final ASEAN-UN Summit on Monday he restated UN support for the bloc's Five-Point Consensus on Myanmar and pressed for Security Council reform. By video, he told the Seventh Review Conference of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons in Geneva that states should negotiate a legally binding instrument on autonomous weapons, with "clear prohibitions and restrictions." In New York, the race to succeed him is deadlocked: Guterres leaves office in under seven weeks, eight closed-door Security Council straw polls since July 30 have produced no consensus candidate, and the late entries of the UAE's Lana Nusseibeh and Türkiye's Feridun Sinirlioğlu have made the field more crowded. On Tuesday the Council, under Latvia's presidency this month, issued a press statement condemning terrorist attacks in Mali. The General Assembly's Fourth Committee finished its debate on Israeli practices and settlement expansion. These follow last week's 13 to 0 to 2 renewal of the Houthi sanctions regime, with China and Russia abstaining, and a Bahrain-requested Council meeting on Gulf maritime security.

European Union.

Monday's Foreign Affairs Council, chaired by Kaja Kallas, backed moving ahead with an EU-Syria Association Agreement as part of a major upgrade in ties with Damascus. Ministers also called for tighter visa policy toward Russians, held a first discussion on Israel's election result, and met counterparts from Central Asia, the South Caucasus, Moldova, Türkiye and Ukraine on Black Sea security and connectivity. Defence ministers followed on Tuesday, the same day the 37th Ramstein-format meeting took place at NATO headquarters. Kallas cited the EU treaties to argue that she should have a seat at the Ukraine peace table, although leaders have given the EU's places to aides of Costa and von der Leyen. The Commission issued preliminary findings that Temu is failing to counter the sale of illegal products on its platform, and Commissioner Dombrovskis presented the Autumn 2026 Economic Forecast. The College meets today, and the Circular Economy Act is expected. Von der Leyen is also addressing an EU Enlargement Forum. In Parliament, Roberta Metsola hosted an event marking 20 years of Bulgarian and Romanian membership, and an MEP delegation is at COP31 in Antalya.

US House of Representatives.

The lame-duck House returned on Tuesday and passed suspension bills, including H.R. 8195, the Responsible Cormorant Management and Control Act. The Rules Committee then met on the week's two main items. The House meets at noon today, with votes expected around 1:30 p.m. on a rule (sponsored by Rep. Houchin) for H.R. 5267, the American Franchise Act (Hern), which would narrow the joint-employer standard for franchisors, and H.R. 3495, the Direct Seller and Real Estate Agent Harmonization Act (Kiley), which would classify those workers as independent contractors under the FLSA. Leadership is also shifting for the next Congress. House Democrats, who won the majority, meet today to formally nominate Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker in a recorded vote. House Republicans have made Jim Jordan their top leader and are electing steering representatives today. The House leaves after Friday and returns November 30.

US Senate.

The Senate resumed consideration of H.R. 2347, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, working post-cloture on Capito substitute amendment #6883. A 2:15 p.m. confirmation vote was scheduled on Tuesday for Anna St. John to the Fifth Circuit; cloture on her nomination was invoked 49 to 26 last week, and the final tally had not been published at the time of writing. In floor speeches, Majority Leader Thune talked about the Congressional Review Act and permitting, and Minority Leader Schumer criticized the president's Asia trip and the White House renovations. Committee work included Judiciary hearings, among them one on Roblox and children's online safety, as well as Armed Services, Rules, Intelligence and Foreign Relations sessions. Lame-duck pressure is building around government funding and the FY2027 NDAA. Iran war funding is the main sticking point, and Durbin and Hawley are trying to attach online child-safety provisions through a manager's amendment.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is between argument sittings. Its November 16 order list denied review in dozens of cases, including a University of California scholars' challenge to research-funding cuts, a challenge to California's diversity requirements for continuing medical education, and a Second Amendment challenge to Maryland's "sensitive places" gun restrictions. It also asked the Solicitor General for the government's views on three terrorism-victim suits against the Taliban. On Friday it granted review in Mullin v. Refugee and Immigrant Center for Education and Legal Services, on whether the administration can suspend asylum access at the southern border, and in a Robinhood case. At the next private conference the justices will consider petitions on transgender servicemembers, E. Jean Carroll's jury award against the president, and acting U.S. attorneys. Separately, Fed Governor Lisa Cook said she will not attend a White House hearing on whether to fire her, citing bias. Cook previously won at the Court in Trump v. Cook.

White House.

President Trump arrived in Manila late Tuesday and held his first Quad leaders' meeting, with India's Modi, Japan's Takaichi and Australia's Albanese. The summit produced initiatives on AI, maritime surveillance and critical minerals, and Trump announced a Quad Business Leaders Forum for 2027. He then signed a U.S.-Thai trade deal and attended the ASEAN-U.S. Special Summit and dinner, where he praised the Marcos family. This morning Manila time he holds bilateral talks with President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. before flying to Shenzhen for the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting (November. 18 to 19). Back home, Trump nominated a new Army chief of staff, setting up a confirmation fight. The Washington Post reports more White House grounds renovations, and Nvidia's chip-export turmoil has exposed a rift inside the administration. Stephen Miller has been chief of staff since November 9.

What's Coming Up.

The main event is tomorrow's (November. 19) first-ever trilateral summit of Xi Jinping, Trump and Vladimir Putin, set for 2:30 p.m. local time at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen. U.S. officials say Trump will push to bring China and Russia into a new nuclear arms-control framework but "do not expect to get any kind of agreement." Xi and Putin are meeting today ahead of it. On Capitol Hill, the House plans franchise and worker-classification votes before recessing Friday, and the Senate continues to work on H.R. 2347 and nominations, with funding and the NDAA unresolved before the December deadlines. The Supreme Court's December argument session opens November 30. In Europe, EU leaders meet November 26 to 27 to discuss 2027 Ukraine funding and a 22nd sanctions package, and Ukraine's backers are due to meet in Berlin as the "Coalition of the Willing." Gadi Eisenkot has until December 6 to form an Israeli government.

International Politics Analysis.

After a roughly two-month lull, the Iran war is heating up again. Iran fired drones at U.S. military targets in Bahrain on November 13, 15 and 17; Tuesday's attack targeted a radar at Sheikh Isa Air Base, and Bahraini air defences intercepted the drones. The U.S. responded with "self-defense" strikes on launchers on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, Kuwait intercepted drones of its own, and a tanker in the Strait was reportedly hit a second time. Analysts read Tehran's escalation as a calculation that breaking the U.S. naval blockade by force may cost less than living with it. Iran has set seven conditions for a formal ceasefire, including lifting the blockade and sanctions. Araghchi held talks in Muscat without a breakthrough, and Qatar warned that the attacks "undermine diplomatic efforts." Vice President Vance says the war will not be allowed to run two years, but there are reports of U.S.-Israeli planning for strikes on Iranian economic infrastructure, and the conflict increasingly resembles a "frozen war."

Ukraine diplomacy has a new format. On November 16, negotiators from France, Germany, the UK, Italy and Poland, together with the Commission, the European Council and NATO, joined U.S. envoys Witkoff and Kushner and Ukraine's team for the first time, after U.S.-Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi the week before. The Kremlin has not accepted a European role: aide Ushakov called the idea "extremely dangerous," and Peskov said Moscow wants peace lasting "years, even centuries" rather than a temporary truce. Fighting has not slowed. Russian ballistic missiles hit two Kyiv bridges, Ukrainian drones struck the Kerch Bridge two days running, and Zelenskyy cited North Korean missile supplies to Russia. Trump says Putin is "backing down a bit," and Shenzhen will give the two leaders direct contact, though China has reportedly kept Ukraine off the formal trilateral agenda.

In Asia, the ASEAN week showed the competition for influence in the region. Trump skipped the East Asia Summit and arrived as the 49th ASEAN Summit was ending. Chinese Premier Li Qiang used the gathering to urge ASEAN to "jointly oppose foreign interference," a line aimed at Washington. The revived Quad and the Shenzhen meetings put Beijing at the centre of great-power diplomacy without obliging it to give ground on any crisis. In Israel, the October 27 election left no bloc with a majority: Netanyahu's camp has 53 seats, Eisenkot's opposition 48 and the Arab parties 19. Eisenkot, who holds the mandate, refuses to serve under Netanyahu, so a unity government looks unlikely and another election is possible. In the U.S., the midterm results mean Democratic control of both chambers from January 3, which limits how far the administration's agenda can go and pushes allies and adversaries to hedge on the next two years of American policy.
