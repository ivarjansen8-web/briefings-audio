Here is your government briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Post-midterm shake-up: Trump reshuffles staff as a Trump–Xi–Putin summit looms.

Note: today is a Sunday, so most institutions are not in session. Search coverage of the UN, EU and Congress floor activity was thin for today specifically; those sections say so and lean on the most recent verifiable reporting.

United Nations.

No Security Council or General Assembly meetings of note surfaced for this Sunday. The most recent institutional context is the September high-level week of the 81st General Assembly, where Secretary-General António Guterres closed his speech by acknowledging his forthcoming departure, and the race to succeed him is under way. Al Jazeera reported in September that experts see the UN facing a crisis as major powers act unilaterally. A Security Council session in July on Gaza and the West Bank noted mounting regional tensions amid the continuing US–Iran war, and the EU's October summit conclusions voiced concern over the lack of progress implementing Security Council Resolution 2803 on Gaza.

European Union (Commission, Parliament, Council)

Brussels is quiet on a Sunday. The week just ended saw MEP committees vote on revised CO2 emission performance standards for new cars and vans, per EU Business's weekly agenda. Earlier, on October 21, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen told the Parliament the EU will propose a new instrument letting member states "immediately" limit single-market access when surges of cheap imports threaten European industry. The European Council of October 15 to 16 reaffirmed support for Ukraine and expressed grave concern over Gaza and the West Bank. The Commission has also publicly questioned Moscow's willingness to end the war, and Europe is watching with alarm a proposed US–China–Russia summit that would exclude Ukraine and the EU.

US House of Representatives.

The House is not in session today; the story is the midterm result. Democrats have won control of Congress, and the House is expected to flip, though NPR's tracker still listed some uncalled races as of Saturday night. Democratic leaders told NPR they are "clear-eyed" about a mandate centered on the economy when the new Congress convenes in January. Meanwhile Republicans are weighing one last partisan reconciliation bill during the lame duck, with discussion of Iran war funding and a debt limit increase, though Politico reported in October that lawmakers doubt their big lame-duck ambitions will pan out.

US Senate.

The Senate is also out today. NPR's tracker as of Saturday evening showed Democrats with 52 seats called (plus 2 independents) against 44 for Republicans, a net Democratic gain of 7 against the 51 needed for a majority, with a few races, including Alaska (Sullivan versus Peltola) and Mississippi (Hyde-Smith versus Colom), still uncalled per CBS. Reuters reports that Senate records show Trump moving to nominate a Texas Supreme Court justice who lost her election Tuesday to a lifetime federal judgeship, a sign the lame-duck session will be used to push confirmations before Democrats take over.

US Supreme Court.

No opinions or orders today. Justice Sonia Sotomayor made news on November 7, saying she hopes the Court will reverse some of the "crazy things" it has done, including its 2024 presidential immunity ruling, and describing the US as in a "rough patch" for the rule of law. On election eve the justices took only hours to reject a Trump bid to interfere in the election, per the New York Times. The Washington Post reports GOP losses will raise pressure on conservative justices to retire during the lame duck, probably Trump's last chance to get a nominee confirmed by a Republican Senate. CNN also analyzed the Court's pro-religion trend, with the five other conservatives appearing ready to side with Catholic challengers in a pending church-state case.

White House.

President Trump spent the weekend in West Palm Beach, Florida. On Saturday he posted that he had offered the White House press secretary job to UFC CEO Dana White; CNN's source says White declined, and an aide called it a joke. This follows Trump's announcement that chief of staff Susie Wiles is out and Stephen Miller will replace her. A clip from his "60 Minutes" interview, airing Sunday, has Trump saying he could go six months without insulting Democrats if it meant getting a deal done. He also met a Russian envoy this week, saying a Russia–Ukraine deal is near.

What's Coming Up.

The key date is the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18 to 19, where a Trump–Xi–Putin meeting is being discussed; China has confirmed talks but nothing is final. Russia agreed to resume US-mediated talks with Ukraine after November 10, possibly in Abu Dhabi, per Putin aide Yuri Ushakov. In Washington, Congress returns for the lame duck with open questions over a possible reconciliation bill, remaining uncalled races, and Trump's judicial nominees. Watch also for retirement signals from Supreme Court justices and for the White House staffing changes taking effect under Stephen Miller.

International Politics Analysis.

The dominant story is the proposed trilateral summit between Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin on the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen. Putin floated it in October as a way to settle Ukraine on terms favorable to Moscow without Volodymyr Zelenskyy at the table; Kyiv's foreign minister warned it would only encourage Putin, and the Commission questioned Moscow's sincerity. China has confirmed discussions, but German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier reported that Xi said the Ukraine war is a European conflict where Europeans must be at the table, and Beijing has rejected joining trilateral nuclear arms talks. Analysts expect Beijing to keep any outcome vague, while Moscow gains standing just by being there.

The midterm result colors all of it. Democrats winning Congress weakens Trump's leverage abroad; Brookings' Robert Kagan told CNBC that Russia and China may feel the next two years are the time to move if Trump looks to be on his way out. Al Jazeera notes a Democratic Congress could also begin sending money to Kyiv, limiting Trump's room to trade concessions. Trump has meanwhile waived new sanctions on Russia, struck a diesel export arrangement with Putin, and reportedly pressed Ukraine to halt strikes on Russian oil infrastructure, straining ties with Kyiv. He has also held back a roughly 14 dollarsbillion Taiwan arms package as a "bargaining chip" with Beijing.

The Middle East remains unresolved. The US–Israeli war on Iran, begun in late February, continues to drive fuel prices and politics; the Wall Street Journal recently reported US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, and Iran's security chief met Putin this week to deliver a message from Khamenei. In Gaza, EU leaders warn of a worsening humanitarian crisis as winter nears, and the EU has criticized the postponement of Palestinian elections. Together these threads show a Europe sidelined on both Ukraine and the Middle East while Washington, Moscow and Beijing negotiate directly.
