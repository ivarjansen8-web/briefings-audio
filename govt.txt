Here is your government briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Dublin summits open, Iran blockade talks drag on, Senate permitting bill waits until Monday.

Thursday, November 12, 2026.

United Nations.

The Security Council will meet on Friday (November 13) on Persian Gulf tensions and maritime threats at Bahrain's request. Bahrain wants to address the risk of renewed fighting and implementation of the March resolution 2817, which condemned Iranian attacks on Gulf states and threats to navigation. Separately, Denmark and other European countries are pushing for an emergency Council meeting on Israel's E1 settlement plan, while Israel is working with the US and Greece to block it, according to the Times of Israel's November 11 liveblog. In the General Assembly, members recently debated a Council veto cast by China and Russia against a US-backed sanctions panel of experts on Iranian nuclear facilities. I found no recorded Council votes or Assembly plenary actions for today itself, and COP31 is under way in Antalya, Türkiye (November 9 to 20).

European Union.

The ninth European Political Community summit is being held in Dublin today, co-chaired by Council President António Costa and Taoiseach Micheál Martin, with roughly 50 leaders attending, a closing press conference at 17:45 and a dinner at the National Gallery. Tomorrow an informal meeting of the 27 heads of state or government, devoted entirely to artificial intelligence, follows, with Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton and Mistral AI CEO Arthur Mensch addressing leaders. In Brussels, the Permanent Representatives Committee has been preparing the Dublin meeting, the Commission's 2026 enlargement package, the November 20 EU-UK summit and the Council's side of a possible conciliation meeting with Parliament on the 2027 budget. I found no European Parliament plenary vote or specific Commission decision reported for today.

US House of Representatives.

The House returned November 9 for a five-week lame-duck session after Democrats won the majority in the November 3 midterms. Speaker Mike Johnson will not seek the Republican leadership post in the next Congress, and House Republicans elected Jim Jordan minority leader on November 10; Tom Emmer kept his whip job by 103 to 76 over Rep. August Pfluger. The agenda is dominated by the December 11 funding deadline, Rep. Thomas Massie's privileged resolution to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war (which leaders postponed from September), and Massie's discharge petition on the Epstein Transparency Act II, which needs two more Democratic signatures. I could not confirm specific floor votes scheduled for today.

US Senate.

The Senate is working on the bipartisan permitting overhaul, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (S. 5653), which Trump supports. It advanced on Monday 73 to 25 on the motion to proceed (51 Republicans, 21 Democrats, one independent; Sen. Mike Rounds the lone Republican no) and again Tuesday 62 to 21 in a further procedural vote. Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse said Wednesday that no more votes are expected until next week, when senators take up amendments, including a push led by Sen. Adam Schiff to strike National Historic Preservation Act provisions that tribes oppose, and final passage. Reuters reported the Monday tally as 75 to 23, a discrepancy I could not resolve. Majority Leader John Thune says more nominees will be confirmed before year-end.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is in its November argument sitting; the week of November 9 included Nielsen v. Watanabe and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission. On Monday it issued a four-page unsigned order letting the administration resume border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region, staying a district court injunction while the government seeks review; the justices said the government is "exceedingly likely to prevail" and wrote that "there is no such thing as a 'constitutional' major-questions claim." The order does not resolve the underlying suit over Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin's environmental waivers. I found no reported rulings or orders specific to today.

White House.

I could not find a published public schedule for President Trump today. The Wall Street Journal reports he has authorized Vice President JD Vance to negotiate with Iran, with a possible meeting with Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei under discussion. Axios reports Trump plans to govern more aggressively on executive power in his final two years, with chief of staff Stephen Miller now running the West Wing.

What's Coming Up.

Tomorrow brings the Security Council's Gulf meeting and the Dublin informal EU summit on AI. Senators return Monday (November 16) for amendment votes and final passage on the permitting bill, which then needs House action. Trump is due in Manila for the ASEAN summit on November 16 and then Shenzhen for APEC (reported as November 17 to 19 or 18 to 19), where he may meet Xi Jinping; the EU-UK summit is November 20 in Brussels. At home, the December 11 funding deadline, the Hegseth impeachment resolution and the Epstein discharge petition will shape the House lame duck. Turkish intelligence has claimed a Vance-Rezaei meeting in Qatar "next week", which Iran denies.

International Politics Analysis.

The US-Iran standoff is the dominant strand. Trump says the war is militarily over but keeps the blockade, and now a land-route task force, as leverage; Iran says Hormuz stays shut until the US accepts seven conditions, including lifting the blockade and sanctions, an end to fighting on all fronts (notably Israel's operations in Lebanon) and unfrozen assets. Mediators have floated pulling US carriers back and easing the blockade in exchange for Iran letting 25 tankers through, per the WSJ, but positions remain far apart: Washington wants nuclear limits written in from the start, while Tehran's Rezaei and Speaker Ghalibaf frame the ball as in America's court. Tanker attacks and mine strikes continue, Gulf states are reported to prefer the blockade stay for now, and Trump's own advisers are reportedly split between Rubio and Miller (hold the blockade) and Vance (direct talks).

On Ukraine, US-mediated trilateral talks between Ukraine, Russia and the US were scheduled for Abu Dhabi on November 10 to 11, the first full round since the Iran war interrupted them in February, with agenda items including post-war security arrangements, a possible energy and maritime ceasefire, prisoner exchanges and returning Ukrainian children. I could not find a reliable account of this round's outcome. Germany's Merz said Russia has not yet engaged seriously, and Russia kept striking Ukrainian cities, including a strike on an aid hub that Ukraine says killed 18 to 31 people. A possible Trump-Xi-Putin meeting around APEC in Shenzhen is being discussed, which Zelensky sees as benefiting Moscow most.

The wider backdrop is a US political reset after the Democratic midterm sweep, which weakens Trump's domestic hand while he leans on executive action abroad, and a fragmenting multilateral order. The UN is split over Security Council vetoes and reform, European leaders are using the Dublin summits to talk AI sovereignty and competitiveness while preparing the enlargement package and an EU-UK summit, and Asia's summit season opens with Xi expected to skip ASEAN in Manila (Premier Li Qiang attending) before APEC. COP31 in Antalya, which runs to November 20, is a further test of cooperation without full US participation.
