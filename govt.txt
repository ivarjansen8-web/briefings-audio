Quiet Weekend in Washington and Brussels as Post-Midterm Power Shifts Take Shape.

United Nations.

The Security Council's main action came Friday, when it adopted a resolution extending the UN Interim Security Force for Abyei (UNISFA) through November 15, 2027, with Washington's explanation of vote tying continued support to benchmarks such as the full withdrawal of Rapid Support Forces and South Sudanese troops from Abyei and the deployment of the Abyei Police Service. Latvia holds the November presidency, and the Council's programme of work calls for continued straw polls on the next Secretary-General. The eighth straw poll was held November 5, with results not officially released; reporting before that put Costa Rica's Rebeca Grynspan ahead of Argentina's Rafael Grossi and Guyana's Carolyn Rodrigues Birkett, with Türkiye's Feridun Sinirlioğlu added to the ballot. A candidate needs at least nine "encouraged" votes and no "discouraged" vote from a permanent member, and Guterres' term ends at the end of this year. No Council or General Assembly meetings appear to be scheduled for today.

European Union.

It is a weekend, and I found no Commission, Parliament or Council decisions dated today. The most recent substantive item is the Commission's 2027 work programme, transmitted to the Council on November 4 as COM(2026) 710, with annexes listing files such as the ETS review, network charges and a Public Procurement Act. Parliament held a short Brussels mini-plenary on November 11 to 12 and returns to Strasbourg on November 23 to 26. Council and Commission attention is turning to the EU-UK summit in Brussels on November 20, where Costa and von der Leyen will host UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham. Separately, European officials were kept out of the trilateral Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi this week, holding parallel consultations instead.

US House of Representatives.

No floor votes are indicated for today. The week's big moment was Tuesday, November 10, when the House voted 211 to 204 to refer Rep. Thomas Massie's eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing them; 11 Democrats missed the vote. The articles centered on the conduct of the Iran war and strikes in Yemen, Venezuela and the Caribbean. The same day the House adopted, 215 to 209, the rule for H.R. 1013 on 403(b) plans, and House Republicans chose Jim Jordan as their leader for the new Congress. Jeffries is expected to become Speaker in January, and Democrats could revive the Hegseth effort then.

US Senate.

The Senate has no recorded floor business today. On Tuesday, Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune as their leader by acclamation after hours of closed-door talks, even as some members had urged delaying the vote to discuss the party's midterm losses; he will lead a minority in the next Congress. Earlier in the week the floor handled a cloture vote on the motion to proceed to H.R. 2347 (Vote 257, November 9) and routine voice-vote confirmations of Navy nominations and simple resolutions. Government funding runs through December 4 under the stopgap passed this summer, so appropriations are the next big test.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is not sitting today. On Monday, November 9, it heard arguments in Nielsen v. Watanabe and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission, released an order list, and ruled on the emergency docket in Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church (26A560), a per curiam decision that lets border wall construction continue. Tuesday's argument was Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards, and Wednesday was a legal holiday. SCOTUSblog's latest relist roundup flags petitions including the GEO Group private-detention dispute. The next argument session begins November 30 with Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia.

White House.

President Trump's public schedule for today shows only Executive Time at the White House at 8:00 a.m., closed press, with an in-town pool call at 8:30 a.m.; the weekend guidance has him at the White House through Sunday. On Friday he held four closed-press policy meetings in the Oval Office, and on Wednesday he and the First Lady visited Arlington National Cemetery for Veterans Day. He delivered public remarks on November 9. He stood by Hegseth after the House vote, and criticized Democrats over the impeachment effort.

What's Coming Up.

The most concrete date is the EU-UK summit in Brussels on November 20, where food and carbon-market deals, defence cooperation, a youth visa and Erasmus+ participation are on the table. The European Parliament reconvenes in Strasbourg November 23 to 26, and the Supreme Court's next argument session opens November 30. In Congress, the funding stopgap expires December 4, and both chambers will spend the lame-duck weeks before Democrats take the House in January. At the UN, more Secretary-General straw polls are expected this month, while US envoys were reported to be preparing to meet Putin after the Abu Dhabi round. A large Russia-Ukraine prisoner exchange agreed in principle there still has to be carried out.

International Politics Analysis.

The central diplomatic story is the Russia-Ukraine track. The first trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia talks since February ended Wednesday in Abu Dhabi with only an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange and no progress on a ceasefire or territory. The talks centered on post-war security arrangements for Ukraine and Europe, and Russia's chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky dismissed European officials who held side meetings, reportedly calling for a Yalta-style settlement. Zelensky, on a state visit to Copenhagen, said he wants Europeans at the table, as did Secretary of State Rubio. The talks resumed after months of stalling that followed the US-Israeli war on Iran, which began in February and whose spillover, including disruption in the Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab, still occupies the UN Security Council.

The Iran war is also reshaping American politics and alliances. The Democratic midterm sweep and the Hegseth impeachment fight both turned partly on the conduct of that war, and commentary this week suggested the electoral shift is already changing the politics of US aid to Israel. At the UN, China and Russia vetoed a US-backed sanctions panel on Iranian nuclear facilities in October, and the Council remains split over the Secretary-General selection, where two permanent members signalled opposition to the front-runner in an earlier straw poll.

Elsewhere, Paraguay is in turmoil after a major bank collapse: President Santiago Peña resigned on November 5, was impeached 78 to 0 by the lower house and removed by the Senate on November 6, and is reportedly under house arrest. In Europe, Britain's new Prime Minister Andy Burnham is using the November 20 summit to pursue a trade and defence reset with Brussels. Looking ahead, a Xi Jinping visit for the G20 summit in Miami in December is the next scheduled US-China touchpoint, following Trump's May state visit to Beijing.
