Here is your government briefing for Monday, 9 November.

Democrats prepare to take Congress as Trump reshuffles the White House and heads to China.

United Nations.

The UN's main story is the race to replace Secretary-General António Guterres, whose term ends in December. The contest has been jolted by last-minute entrants and deep divisions among Security Council members, and Costa Rica's Rebeca Grynspan extended her lead in the latest straw poll. Türkiye's candidate was pressed on Cyprus and Greece in a candidate dialogue. Guterres himself has warned that the Council is "practically paralyzed" by US and Russian vetoes. The UN peacekeeping chief also warned of a "security vacuum" as Blue Helmets withdraw from Lebanon and Western Sahara, with the Council yet to agree any follow-on arrangement. Guterres called COP31 "a moment of truth" on climate. I found no specific Security Council or General Assembly vote for today.

European Union.

The Council's Economic and Financial Affairs meeting convenes today, with ministers invited to reach political agreement on the tobacco taxation package, take decisions under the Stability and Growth Pact, and discuss simplification. The Eurogroup also meets today. On Friday the Council agreed negotiating positions to postpone EU methane-regulation monitoring rules by one year and to simplify energy and tyre labelling. In Parliament, the Environment Committee followed the Industry and Transport Committee in voting to relax the combustion-engine phase-out. The Commission has proposed a new Frontex Regulation, with a roughly 30,000-strong agency to steer EU deportations, and has announced new crisis-preparedness steps.

US House of Representatives.

The House is in its post-election lame-duck period after Democrats won control in the November 3 midterms; the new majority is seated January 3. Republicans had held a 218 to 214 edge entering the vote. Democrats say they will use their majority for aggressive investigations and for lawmaking on healthcare and tariffs, but not impeachment. The immediate deadline is December 11, when government funding expires. A senior GOP aide expects a stopgap continuing resolution into late March or mid-April, and the annual defense authorization bill is still pending. I could not confirm today's floor schedule or any votes.

US Senate.

Democrats also won the Senate, which Republicans held 53 to 47 going into the election, with a majority that takes effect in January. The outgoing Republican majority faces a crowded lame-duck agenda. The funding deadline is December 11, and Democrats say the Appropriations Committee is well behind schedule. Republicans are reportedly eyeing one last reconciliation bill, and the defense authorization bill is unfinished. Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats will not allow Trump to put "a right-winger" on the Supreme Court. The Senate chamber schedule for today was not retrievable.

US Supreme Court.

The Court issues orders from Friday's conference at 9:30 a.m. ET today. Last week it heard four arguments, including St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, where it appeared poised to side with Catholic preschools in Colorado. It also quickly denied, with no noted dissents, a Trump administration request to intervene in a California ballot-counting dispute. A fully briefed administration request to resume border wall construction in Texas's Big Bend region could be decided at any time. Arguments in Nielsen v. Watanabe and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission are set for today. Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards follows tomorrow. The Justice Department is reportedly pursuing a criminal grand jury investigation into the Dobbs leak. After the midterms, speculation about Justices Alito and Thomas retiring has grown, though the Wall Street Journal called a lame-duck retirement "exceedingly unlikely."

White House.

Trump returned to Washington from Mar-a-Lago Sunday night and is reshuffling his team after the midterm losses. Deputy chief of staff departures are being reported under a shake-up involving Stephen Miller, who is reported to be becoming chief of staff. Trump said he may make a "few changes" in his cabinet and that his immigration crackdown "might be too tough." He floated UFC chief Dana White as press secretary over the weekend, then said he was kidding, and White is due at the White House today for other reasons. Trump also told reporters he expects to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18 to 19 and said he is "very close" to a Ukraine deal. A 60 Minutes interview airs tonight. The Justice Department is taking a combative stance toward Democratic investigations.

What's Coming Up.

The EU's budget conciliation committee meets tomorrow to try to settle the 2027 annual budget. Foreign ministers meet November 16 to 17, an informal leaders' meeting is November 13, and the EU-UK summit is November 20. At the Supreme Court, Sun Valley Orchards is argued tomorrow, and the Texas border wall request could be decided any day. In Washington, the December 11 funding deadline and the defense bill dominate the lame duck, while any retirement announcement by a justice would trigger a fight over a rapid confirmation. Trump travels to Shenzhen for APEC on November 18 to 19.

International Politics Analysis.

The Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz dominate. The US has carried out limited strikes on Iranian air defenses on Qeshm Island, and two oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines. Iran says no unauthorised vessels transited overnight after navigation aids were destroyed. Trump says renewed strikes are "highly unlikely" if talks advance, while Secretary of State Rubio says a diplomatic agreement may not be possible. Washington is also pressing Iraq to close all but two border crossings with Iran. G7 foreign ministers meet in Paris today and tomorrow, partly on how Europe can join US efforts to end the Ukraine war and how to urge Washington not to resume military action against Iran.

On Ukraine, Trump said he is "very close" to a deal that would leave a "little bit different shape Ukraine", without giving specifics. The Wall Street Journal reported that Putin wants a trilateral Trump-Xi-Putin meeting to include ending the Ukraine war, which would put Kyiv and European capitals on edge. Politico reports NATO countries face a roughly 7 eurosbillion shortfall in military aid funding for Ukraine by year-end. Beijing has extended its suspension of port fees on US-linked ships, a sign that US-China trade tensions are being managed ahead of the Shenzhen summit.

Elsewhere, Israel's president has tasked Gadi Eisenkot with forming a new government, and Trump is pressing Netanyahu to press ahead with the Gaza plan. Türkiye is positioning itself as mediator across the Russia-Ukraine, Gaza and Armenia-Azerbaijan files. The US midterm result also changes the calculus for foreign capitals, since a Democratic Congress from January will constrain Trump's tariffs and war powers, and Trump's APEC diplomacy may be partly aimed at securing deals before that happens.
