Daily Government Briefing — Tuesday, 17 November 2026.

United Nations.

Secretary-General António Guterres is in Manila for the ASEAN summit cycle. On Monday he co-chaired what he called his "final ASEAN-UN Summit as Secretary-General" (he leaves office at year's end), pressing for an end to the Myanmar crisis, attention to the nearly 1.2 million Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh, and reform of the Security Council and the international financial institutions. At his press conference he called Iran's human rights violations "unacceptable" and said "the time of fossil fuel is coming to an end." Separately, in a video message to delegates from more than 120 member states meeting in Geneva, he called for a legally binding instrument with "clear prohibitions and restrictions" on autonomous weapons. In New York, the Security Council under Latvia's November presidency holds a private meeting on the AU Support and Stabilization Mission in Somalia (AUSSOM) today. This follows last week's renewals of MINUSCA in the Central African Republic (Resolution 2836, with a reduced troop ceiling), the Yemen sanctions regime (Resolution 2837, panel expanded to six members with maritime expertise) and UNISFA in Abyei (Resolution 2838, to 15 November 2027).

European Union (Commission, Parliament, Council)

Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis presents the Commission's Autumn 2026 Economic Forecast today. Last week he said growth this year had beaten expectations, but warned that high deficits and rising debt in some heavily indebted states "call for very prudent fiscal policies," and that energy prices remain high and volatile. Monday's Foreign Affairs Council, chaired by Kaja Kallas with Ukraine's Andrii Sybiha attending, made several decisions.

113 new Russia-related listings and sanctions on 30 individuals and two entities over disinformation and cyber activity.

Approval of the largest-ever Ukraine Facility payment, 8 eurosbillion.

A message from ministers that talks with Moscow on Europe's security architecture would be premature before a ceasefire.

Kallas set out Europe's demands of Russia: respect for Ukraine's borders, accountability for war crimes, reparations and the return of deported children. Ireland's Helen McEntee, whose country holds the presidency, said a new sanctions package will be discussed later this week. Also on Monday, Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands unveiled a pilot "return hub" in Rwanda, the first outside the EU. It would hold about 100 rejected asylum seekers and aims to open in 2027. The Commission welcomed it; Spain's Pedro Sánchez said it had no "future or humanity." In Parliament, EPP and Renew tabled compromise amendments on the Emissions Trading System overhaul without S&D support, ahead of Thursday's industry committee vote.

US House of Representatives.

The House returns today for the second week of the lame-duck session. It meets at noon, with votes postponed until 6:30 p.m. on suspension bills including H.R. 7954, the Don Young Doug LaMalfa Indian Buffalo Management Act. Later in the week, Majority Leader Steve Scalise's floor lookout features worker-classification bills for direct sellers and real estate agents, and Rep. Kevin Hern's H.R. 5267, the American Franchise Act, which sets a federal joint-employer standard for franchises. The bigger question is Rep. Thomas Massie's discharge petition for the Epstein Files Transparency Act II. It reached the 218 signatures needed last Wednesday, so a floor vote can be forced without the Speaker's agreement. Last Tuesday the House voted 211 to 204 to refer Massie's Hegseth impeachment articles to the Judiciary Committee, effectively shelving them. Government funding runs out on 11 December.

US Senate.

On Monday the Senate confirmed Chief Judge Daniel Domenico of Colorado to the Tenth Circuit by a party-line 51 to 46 vote, succeeding Judge Timothy Tymkovich. Anna St. John's Fifth Circuit nomination has already cleared cloture and can come up for confirmation at any time. Cloture has also been filed on four district court nominees, and the chamber is moving on federal permitting reform for energy and infrastructure projects. Majority Leader John Thune has made judges a top priority before Democrats take control on 3 January. Twenty-three nominees remain pending, and Sens. Thom Tillis and John Cornyn are blocking some of them over home-state consultation; the White House says it expects all of them confirmed this year. The Judiciary Committee meets at 9 a.m. today.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is between argument sittings after finishing its November session. Monday's order list mostly turned away high-profile petitions. In Khatibi v. Yip, it declined to hear a California ophthalmologist's First Amendment challenge to the state's implicit-bias requirement for continuing medical education courses. Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Alito, dissented, writing that "First Amendment protections do not give way simply because a State heavily regulates speech," and Justice Gorsuch would also have granted review. The Court also denied Novotny v. Moore, a challenge to Maryland's restrictions on carrying firearms, with a statement from Justice Thomas. Maryland's separate cross-petition (Moore v. Kipke) remains pending. Gun-rights groups counted 15 of 17 Second Amendment petitions denied. The December sitting opens on 30 November with Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia.

White House (President's schedule)

President Trump is in Asia on his first foreign trip since the midterms, travelling with Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Chief of Staff Stephen Miller. Today in Manila he hosts the first in-person Quad leaders' meeting (US, Japan, Australia, India) in two years, on the sidelines of the ASEAN summit. He is also expected to sign a trade deal with Thailand and to meet Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. before flying to Shenzhen on Wednesday for APEC. Before leaving Washington on Monday he clashed with reporters over his White House renovations during the FAA's nationwide ground stop: he said he took no responsibility, defended the projects and called NBC's Yamiche Alcindor "pathetic." The administration has asked Congress for 50 dollarsbillion to replace aging air-traffic infrastructure. He also said he "would not mind" if Jensen Huang returned as Nvidia's CEO, and hinted he "may meet" Kim Jong Un in China.

International Politics Analysis.

This week's diplomacy centres on Asia. A weakened Trump, whose party lost both chambers of Congress on 3 November in results widely tied to the Iran war's economic toll, goes from the ASEAN/East Asia Summit in Manila (16 to 18 November) to APEC in Shenzhen. There the White House has set up the first-ever three-way meeting of US, Chinese and Russian leaders. China's foreign ministry says the meeting has "groundbreaking significance," and the agenda covers nuclear disarmament, the Iran war and, Trump says, Ukraine. The Quad's revival in Manila, with India's Narendra Modi and Japan's Sanae Takaichi both attending, gives Washington a counterweight in the same week it courts Beijing and Moscow. Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelenskyy has publicly worried that the three leaders could use Shenzhen to redraw spheres of influence.

The Iran war is the most dangerous variable. The USS Roosevelt became the third US carrier in the region on Monday, and Iran claimed a drone attack on Bahrain, ending a lull in strikes. Air defences were reportedly activated in Isfahan. Trump has alternated between calling the war "effectively over" and threatening large-scale strikes, and US officials say 38 bridges are marked as targets if fighting resumes in earnest. Mediation is running in parallel: Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said in Islamabad that the Economic Cooperation Organization backed Tehran's "two-day" Hormuz plan, and Pakistan and Qatar remain the main mediators. Iran's demands still include lifting the US naval blockade and sanctions, releasing frozen funds and a ceasefire on all fronts, including Lebanon. Tehran has also ruled out UN inspections of damaged nuclear sites until the war ends.

On Ukraine, the 10 to 11 November Abu Dhabi talks, the first trilateral round since February, produced a large prisoner exchange but no movement on territory or security guarantees. Moscow still demands Ukraine's withdrawal from Donbas and a renunciation of NATO membership. Its chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, appeared to call for a Yalta-style settlement, and Washington's push to fold a new European security architecture into the talks has unsettled EU capitals. Fighting has escalated: Russian strikes hit two major Kyiv bridges and power infrastructure, and the Crimean Bridge was attacked overnight. Europe is responding by insisting on a seat at the table, a format agreed at the 13 November Dublin informal summit, while adding sanctions and money for Kyiv. Elsewhere, COP31 continues in Antalya after Guterres told leaders that fossil fuels "must go the way of the dinosaurs," and Spain's Sánchez hosted Brazil's Lula in Madrid on Monday.
