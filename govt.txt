EU leaders meet in Dublin on AI; Hegseth impeachment shelved; Ukraine talks stall.

Friday, November 13, 2026.

United Nations.

I found no reporting on a specific Security Council or General Assembly action dated today. The most relevant pending item is two peacekeeping mandates that expire on 15 November 2026: the Abyei force (UNISFA) and the Central African Republic mission (MINUSCA). Both were last renewed to that date, so Council action on them is due by this weekend. In October the Council failed to renew a US-backed sanctions panel of experts on Iran's nuclear programme after a China-Russia veto, and General Assembly President Khalilur Rahman convened a debate on the veto. The Assembly also continues negotiations on Security Council reform after its 28 July decision.

European Union.

The 27 EU leaders are meeting informally in Dublin today for the first leaders' meeting devoted entirely to artificial intelligence. European Council President António Costa invited Mistral AI chief Arthur Mensch and Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton as guests, and the meeting is expected to end by about 16:00. It adopts no formal conclusions and cannot change the AI Act, which would need a Commission proposal and agreement between Parliament and Council. It follows yesterday's European Political Community summit in Dublin, the largest gathering of national leaders Ireland has hosted. Ukraine dominated that agenda, including a roughly 70 eurosbillion hole in Kyiv's 2027 budget and European anger at being excluded from the Abu Dhabi talks. I found no reporting on a Parliament plenary vote or a Commission decision dated today.

US House of Representatives.

The week's defining vote came Tuesday, when the House voted 211 to 204 to refer Rep. Thomas Massie's articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee. That effectively ends the effort for this Congress, which expires in January. All Democrats present voted to proceed and all Republicans but Massie voted to refer. Eleven Democrats and seven Republicans did not vote. Earlier in the week, on Monday, the House passed the Nuclear REFUEL Act under suspension of the rules. Republicans still control the chamber during the lame-duck session even though Democrats won the midterms. I could not confirm today's floor schedule.

US Senate.

The Senate opened its lame-duck session on Monday, November 9, with a 73 to 25 cloture vote (Record Vote 257) on a motion to proceed. Reporting identifies the first item as the bipartisan energy permitting package, which needs 60 votes on final passage. Backers worry the Democratic midterm sweep could complicate the lame-duck path. Republican leaders also want judicial confirmations, a farm bill and the defense authorization bill finished before January. I found no confirmed record of floor votes today.

US Supreme Court.

I found no opinions or orders dated today. On Monday the Court issued an unsigned order allowing the administration to resume border wall construction in the Big Bend region of Texas, pausing a district judge's injunction. The same day it added two cases to next term's argument docket and heard two arguments, including Nielsen v. Watanabe and Hoffmann v. WBI Energy Transmission. The justices are in their November sitting, and the interim docket remains active.

What's Coming Up.

The UNISFA and MINUSCA mandates expire on November 15, so Council votes should come in the next two days. In Brussels, EU leaders face an EU-UK summit on November 20, a General Affairs Council on November 19 that includes the annual rule-of-law dialogue, and budget conciliation talks with Parliament on the 2027 EU budget. In Washington, the Senate permitting package has to clear a 60-vote final-passage threshold, and the stopgap funding measure runs out in mid-December, with one report citing December 11. The ICC trial of former Philippine president Rodrigo Duterte is reported to begin November 30.

International Politics Analysis.

The Russia-Ukraine track restarted this week, but with no sign of a breakthrough. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner brought the two sides together in Abu Dhabi on November 10 to 11, the first such talks in nine months. Russia's side was led by Putin aide Vladimir Medinsky, and Ukraine's by Rustem Umerov. An unnamed US official said both sides showed some flexibility on territory, but the core disputes over territory and security guarantees remain. Moscow objected to European participation, though it said EU representatives could join at a later stage. Kushner said a lasting settlement would require direct talks between Russia and its European partners. Russian strikes on Ukrainian power infrastructure continued throughout. That left Europeans gathering in Dublin worried about both Kyiv's funding gap and the US posture after the midterms.

The Iran war is the other open front. It began with US-Israeli attacks in late February, and ceasefires in April and June both unravelled. Qatari-mediated talks ended around November 2 without a breakthrough, although Tehran said contacts continue and a draft proposal was circulating. President Trump had repeatedly suggested the US could strike Iran again after the November 3 midterms, while Iranian officials say their forces are better prepared than ever. The Strait of Hormuz disruption keeps energy markets on edge, and it is the backdrop to the House's Hegseth impeachment fight over the war.

Elsewhere, the midterm result is reshaping calculations abroad: Democrats won both chambers, which Europeans, Kyiv and Tehran are all weighing. In Latin America, Paraguay's president reportedly resigned on November 5 amid a major bank collapse, then was impeached by the lower house 78 to 0 and removed by the Senate on November 6. China is also taking a more active role in Gulf diplomacy.
