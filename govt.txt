Saturday, 28 November 2026 — Daily Government Briefing.

United Nations.

The race to succeed António Guterres is still stuck as the Security Council heads into its tenth straw poll, set for Monday, 30 November at 3 pm EST. In the ninth, colour-coded poll on 20 November, Costa Rica's Rebeca Grynspan kept a narrow lead with 10 "encourage" and 4 "discourage" votes. Two of those discourage votes came from permanent members, reported to be Russia and the United States. That was down from 12 encourage votes on 5 November. The IAEA's Rafael Grossi drew 8 encourage votes but 3 P5 discourages, the UAE's Lana Nusseibeh got 7, and every one of the eight candidates received at least two P5 discourage votes. A tenth poll would tie the 1996 record for the most in a single selection. With Guterres's term ending 31 December, analysts warn the Assembly may have little time to act. Separately, Guterres condemned this week's deadly drone attack in Kuwait.

European Union.

A special European Council in Brussels (26 to 27 November) broke up early on the 27th with no deal on the 2028 to 2034 budget (MFF), and leaders cancelled the planned second day. Council President António Costa's compromise of about 1.582 eurostrillion cuts roughly 204 billion euros (about 10%) from the Commission's proposal, which satisfied neither the frugal states nor the net beneficiaries. Costa said there was no agreement on the budget's size, how it is split between priorities, or new own resources. On Ukraine, 24 member states back the Commission's proposed 45 billion euros loan for 2027, with Bulgaria joining only the civilian part. Slovakia, Hungary and Czechia are holding out, and Chancellor Merz is tying Germany's backing for the MFF to all 27 sharing the cost. Leaders did put forward Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde at the ECB. In Strasbourg, Parliament approved the 2027 annual budget on 25 November, setting aside 650 million euros to service the Ukraine Support Loan. It also adopted its negotiating mandates for Horizon Europe FP10 (458 to 190 to 14) and the European Competitiveness Fund (386 to 252 to 23), formally opposed Costa's compromise, and on 26 November held its final vote on a deal giving fossil-fuel importers until May 2030 on methane reporting.

US House of Representatives.

The House is out for the Thanksgiving recess and returns next week with only about a dozen session days left before Democrats take the majority on 3 January. Just before the break, 14 House Republicans missed a procedural vote, forcing leaders to pull a rule from the floor. Speaker Mike Johnson called attendance "a great frustration" and publicly urged members to show up. The top priority is a stopgap or full-year funding before the 11 December deadline. Democratic leaders Hakeem Jeffries and Chuck Schumer are reported to want Iran-war language in any bipartisan deal, while Appropriations ranking member Rosa DeLauro has argued that "shutting the government down doesn't stop the war." The NDAA, which the House passed 216 to 212 in July, permitting reform and a farm bill are also still open.

US Senate.

The Senate held a pro forma session at noon on Friday, 27 November, with no business, and returns on Monday, 30 November at 3:00 pm. At about 5:30 pm it will vote to confirm Kyle Reardon as a District Judge for the District of Alaska. Cloture has already been invoked on four more district-court nominees: Daniel Ballou (E.D. Ky.), Robert Long (M.D. Fla.), Peter Mansfield (E.D. La.) and Jesus Osete (W.D. Mo.). Republicans are pushing to confirm as many judges as possible before losing control of the calendar. Before the break, they confirmed Richard Bennett (S.D. Tex.) 49 to 43 and invoked cloture on Courtney Coker (N.D. Tex.) on party-line votes. Majority Leader John Thune is under fire from the Senate Conservatives Fund and others for the 10-day recess. Funding needs 60 votes, and the Senate has never taken up its version of the NDAA.

US Supreme Court.

The Court has nothing scheduled this weekend; its December argument session opens Monday. It hears Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia and Wassily v. Blanche on Monday, then Jouppi v. Alaska on 1 December. On 2 December it hears the consolidated Second Amendment cases Viramontes v. Cook County and Grant v. Higgins. On recent emergency matters, on 9 November the Court issued a per curiam stay (Justice Jackson would have denied it) in Mullin v. Friends of the Ruidosa Church, which lets border-barrier construction in Texas's Big Bend sector resume. On 19 November the administration withdrew its application over protected status for Venezuelan nationals. The 16 November order list turned away DEI-related disputes and a Second Amendment challenge to gun bans in "sensitive places." Petitions on transgender servicemembers, E. Jean Carroll's jury award and acting U.S. attorneys are pending at conference.

White House.

President Trump is spending a six-day Thanksgiving stay at Mar-a-Lago, having flown down on 24 November after pardoning the turkeys "Freedom" and "Feathers." I found no public events on his schedule for today. On Thanksgiving Day he played golf in West Palm Beach, then he and First Lady Melania Trump called deployed service members. On that call he told troops victory over Iran is "at hand" and played down Chinese and Russian support for Tehran. The family then hosted dinner in the Mar-a-Lago ballroom. Earlier in the week he called Iran's negotiators "professional tap dancers" and repeated that he would give Tehran until the new year to agree a deal. He also publicly backed the Iraqi prime minister's crackdown on oil smuggling.

What's Coming Up.

Monday, 30 November is the busiest day ahead. The Security Council holds its tenth Secretary-General straw poll, the Senate returns for the Reardon confirmation vote, and the Supreme Court opens its December sitting. That sitting runs through 9 December and includes RNC v. Mi Familia Vota (8 December) and DHS v. D.V.D. (9 December). The main deadline is 11 December, when U.S. government funding, flood insurance and the cyber threat-sharing law all expire. A shutdown would start on Saturday 12 December, and the fight over Iran-war funding makes a deal uncertain. EU finance ministers also meet on 11 December. EU leaders try again on the MFF and the 45 billion euros Ukraine loan at the 17 to 18 December European Council, where they are also due to make a final decision on the ECB presidency. Several U.S. health provisions and Guterres's term both lapse on 31 December.

International Politics Analysis.

The U.S.–Iran war, which began on 28 February, is still the main driver of global politics, and the Gulf looks more dangerous this week. CENTCOM said it disabled a commercial vessel that tried to breach the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports and that it was allegedly carrying precision munitions. Iran vowed to break the blockade and said it fired warning shots to turn a U.S. F-16 away near the Strait of Hormuz. The Revolutionary Guards reported that another tanker hit a mine near the strait. A drone strike in Kuwait killed two Italian citizens. Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, says the drone was Iranian-made, but Tehran denies it was involved, and Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador over "continued Iranian attacks." The conflict is also spreading into Saudi Arabia's security: Houthi drones aimed at Riyadh were intercepted, and Pakistan and Turkey pledged to back Riyadh. South Korea, meanwhile, told Tehran it has no plans to join the war.

In Europe, the war in Ukraine and the question of who pays for it are now tied together. President Zelensky said crews were restoring power in six regions after Russia fired ballistic missiles and more than 100 drones, and warned of another major strike on the grid. The U.S.-brokered trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, whose third round opened on 10 November, have not stopped the fighting. That leaves Kyiv dependent on the EU's stalled 45 billion euros loan to cover about two-thirds of its estimated 70 billion euros financing gap for 2027. The Central European holdouts, Slovakia, Hungary and Czechia, now amount to a test of whether the EU can act on Ukraine at all. Greek Prime Minister Mitsotakis warned that elections in the first half of 2027 will make any compromise harder after December.

In East Asia, the China–Japan rift that began with Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's November 2025 remarks about a Taiwan contingency is worse a year on. At the APEC meetings in Shenzhen on 18 to 19 November, Xi Jinping shook hands with Takaichi but held no bilateral talks. Beijing now says Tokyo must "correct its wrong words" on Taiwan. On 26 November each side accused the other's coast-guard ships of intruding near the Senkaku/Diaoyu islands, and China's export curbs on rare earths and dual-use goods to Japan remain in place. Beyond the Gulf, Lebanon's Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said his government will not let the country's future depend on Iran's wishes. Australia imposed counterterrorism-financing sanctions on two people it says carried out antisemitic arson attacks on Iran's behalf. Both show the Iran conflict reaching well past its battlefield.
