Daily Government Briefing — Friday, 20 November 2026.

United Nations.

The Security Council is scheduled to hold its ninth straw poll at 12:00 EST today to choose the next Secretary-General. It was pushed back from Thursday. The ballots are colour-coded, so they will show whether any "discourage" vote came from one of the five veto-holding members. After eight rounds, Costa Rica's Rebeca Grynspan is still the frontrunner. However, no candidate has yet cleared the bar of nine votes with no P5 veto. No new candidates have entered since the 5 November poll. CELAC repeated its demand that the post go to a Latin American or Caribbean national. Guterres's term ends on 31 December, and the Charter does not say what happens if no one has been chosen by then. Officials are drafting contingency plans for a leaderless UN, which has never happened before. Separately, the Trump–Xi–Putin declaration issued in Shenzhen on Thursday called for "reform" of the UN. It gave no details.

European Union.

Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen host UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham in Brussels today for the second EU–UK summit. Three agreements are on the table: an agrifood deal to cut red tape, a link between the UK and EU emissions-trading schemes, and a youth experience scheme letting under-30s live in each other's territory for up to three years. EU member states approved the package late Thursday, but the UK side still has to give final sign-off. The Commission has proposed a new loan of up to 45 eurosbillion to cover about two-thirds of Ukraine's 2027 financing needs: up to 25 eurosbillion for military aid and 20 eurosbillion in budget support tied to reforms. Member states still have to approve it. On Thursday the General Affairs Council took the Commission's 2027 work programme. The Council also gave final approval to a law simplifying chemical-labelling rules. Separately, Parliament and Council negotiators agreed provisionally to delay methane monitoring rules for fossil-fuel importers by two years, to January 2029. Parliament has no plenary this week.

US House of Representatives.

The House is out today. Republican leaders cancelled Friday votes and sent members home early for Thanksgiving. On Wednesday they had also dropped a procedural rule vote, which would have set up the Direct Seller and Real Estate Agent Harmonization Act (H.R. 3495) and the American Franchise Act (H.R. 5267), because too few Republicans were in town to pass a party-line vote. That is a recurring problem now that the GOP has lost its majority. The House's last vote of the week came Thursday morning: it passed the Preventive Health Savings Act (H.R. 4464) 401 to 0. The chamber returns on 30 November. Still unresolved are the 11 December government-funding deadline and three House-passed bills sitting in the Senate: the NDAA, the farm bill and a 95 dollarsbillion reconciliation package.

US Senate.

On Thursday night the Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA) 62 to 33. It is the most significant permitting overhaul in years. It rewrites parts of NEPA, the Clean Water Act, the Endangered Species Act and the National Historic Preservation Act, and speeds up electricity transmission projects. The authors are Sens. Capito, Whitehouse, Lee and Heinrich. More Democrats voted against it than for it, Senate Minority Leader Schumer among them, and none of the late changes senators had negotiated made it in. The bill goes to the House. A spokesperson for House Minority Leader Jeffries has said the bill "falls short" and that he won't "rubber-stamp" it. Earlier Thursday, the Senate voted 48 to 51 to reject H.Con.Res. 93, the House-passed war powers resolution directing the president to withdraw US forces from hostilities with Iran. It was the first war powers vote in either chamber since the midterms. Majority Leader Thune blamed the GOP's loss of nine Senate seats on the Iran war, gas prices and inflation. The Senate is scheduled to be off for Thanksgiving from 23 to 27 November.

US Supreme Court.

The Court is between argument sessions and has no private conference for about two and a half weeks. Most of the action is on the emergency docket. On Wednesday the administration filed an emergency application to keep Temporary Protected Status for Venezuelans terminated. It withdrew the application on Thursday after the Ninth Circuit extended its own stay, so TPS for Venezuelans stays ended while the case continues. Also on Thursday, the administration brought its fight over the White House ballroom back to the justices. When the Court next meets in conference, the petitions it will consider include the ban on transgender servicemembers (Talbott), E. Jean Carroll's jury award against Trump, and Trump's appointments of acting US attorneys. Last week it agreed to hear the administration's suspension of asylum at the southern border.

White House.

President Trump arrived back at Joint Base Andrews this morning from his three-day Asia trip. He has scheduled a news conference at the White House at noon ET to discuss the trip and new trade deals with Thailand, the Philippines and others, which he signed in Manila. The main event of the trip was Thursday's first-ever trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen. It lasted about 90 minutes and ended with a four-point "peace declaration." In it, the three leaders reaffirmed that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and pledged a dialogue on nuclear risk and non-proliferation. They also called for UN reform, more energy supply and limits on the ICC. The declaration set no arms-control framework or timetable and did not mention Ukraine or Iran. Trump rated the meeting "a 12" out of 10, and Senator Schumer called it a "historical embarrassment." On Iran, Trump told Fox News he would "finish it off — soon," while also signalling there was no rush to decide.

What's Coming Up.

The result of today's Security Council straw poll will be the first real sign of whether the UN can name a new Secretary-General before 31 December. In Brussels, the EU–UK summit should end with a joint declaration later today. Next week is busy in the EU.

Parliament's Strasbourg plenary runs 23 to 26 November, with votes on Tuesday 24 November.

The Agriculture Council and the Cohesion General Affairs Council meet on 23 November.

The EU–Western Balkans summit is on 25 November.

A Euro Summit and a special European Council meet 26 to 27 November. The new 45 eurosbillion Ukraine loan is likely to be a central topic.

Washington is quiet over Thanksgiving. Both chambers return on 30 November, and only about three weeks remain before the 11 December funding deadline. A stopgap looks more likely than full-year bills, and the House now has to decide what to do with the Senate's permitting bill. The Supreme Court's December argument session also opens on 30 November with Crowther v. Board of Regents of the University System of Georgia. Looking further ahead, the US hosts the G20 in Miami on 14 to 15 December.

International Politics Analysis.

This week's diplomacy has moved on two tracks that pull against each other. In Shenzhen, Trump, Xi and Putin produced a mostly symbolic declaration. Its nuclear language echoes Reagan and Gorbachev, but it contains no arms-control machinery, and it lines Washington up with Beijing and Moscow on curbing the ICC and "reforming" the UN. Analysts quoted by the South China Morning Post largely saw the summit as a win for Xi, who hosted and set the terms, while noting relief in Brussels and Tokyo that Trump made no major concessions. The meeting came at a weak moment for Trump at home: his party lost both chambers of Congress in the midterms, and US and global opinion on the Iran war is negative. Before Shenzhen, the Quad met in Manila on the sidelines of ASEAN for the first time in Trump's second term. That was a modest signal to Asian allies worried about Chinese pressure.

Europe, meanwhile, is trying to get a seat at the table on Ukraine. On Thursday, nearly 40 delegations met in Berlin for a Coalition of the Willing summit hosted by Chancellor Merz. Zelenskyy, Macron, Burnham, NATO's Rutte and US envoys Witkoff and Kushner attended, and Trump called in from Air Force One. According to Kushner, Russia has accepted a multilateral format that includes Europe and is "comfortable" with Article 5-style security guarantees for Ukraine. Zelenskyy said the next round of talks would be four-way. Putin's own wording was more cautious: he invited the Europeans to Moscow first to explain what they would bring. With the Commission's new 45 eurosbillion loan and next week's European Council, the EU is trying to pair a negotiating role with money. Burnham's summit in Brussels today, the UK's main post-Brexit reset so far, also strengthens European defence cooperation.

The Middle East remains the most dangerous theatre. The US–Israel war with Iran has started up again after a period that analysts called a "frozen conflict." The latest incidents include.

three commercial vessels reported attacked around the Strait of Hormuz since Monday.

Kuwait fending off drones as Iran and the US trade strikes on Qeshm island and in Bahrain.

a Houthi missile damaging a tower in Riyadh.

Israel is pushing for new strikes, Iranian lawmakers are warning Gulf states that host US bases, and the IEA says the war could permanently reshape global energy markets. On the humanitarian side, the WHO on 18 November kept the Ebola outbreak in the DRC classified as a public health emergency of international concern. It is the largest outbreak in the country's history, with more than 11,300 confirmed cases and about 5,500 deaths, and armed groups attacking health workers are slowing the response. Upcoming elections include Cabo Verde's presidential runoff on 29 November and votes in The Gambia (5 December) and Guinea-Bissau (6 December).
