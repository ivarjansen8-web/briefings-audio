Daily Government Briefing — Wednesday, 25 November 2026.

United Nations.

Latvia's month as Security Council president peaked this week with two high-level sessions chaired by President Edgars Rinkēvičs. On Monday the Council held a briefing on Ukraine's energy infrastructure and Black Sea food routes, where Russia ruled out any new Black Sea grain deal, calling it "a scheme of Ukrainian food for Western weapons" and accusing Kyiv of attacks on more than 200 commercial vessels in 2026. On Tuesday the Council held a high-level open debate, "Conflict resolution in an era of rapid technological change," with briefings from Secretary-General António Guterres, ICRC President Mirjana Spoljaric Egger, IPI's Jenna Russo and Microsoft's Frederick Humphries; speakers called for human control and legal oversight of AI in warfare. The debate came a week after a push by about 100 states in Geneva to negotiate a binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapons fell short. Today is the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, which opens the 16 Days of Activism campaign.

European Union (Commission, Parliament, Council)

Brussels hosts the EU–Western Balkans summit today. European Council President António Costa and Commission President Ursula von der Leyen will review reform progress and the Growth Plan for the Western Balkans with the region's six leaders, though it was still unclear late last week who would represent Kosovo. In Strasbourg, Parliament's 23 to 26 November plenary voted Tuesday on the 2027 EU budget joint text after a debate with Commissioner Piotr Serafin, and President Roberta Metsola is due to sign the budget into law today. Tuesday also brought a debate on restoring rule of law in Hungary after its April elections (the resolution vote comes in December), the Commission's European Semester autumn package, and debates on Gaza and the Türkiye–Libya maritime memorandum. Today's agenda covers a key debate on protecting the international order and securing peace for Ukraine, a debate on unlocking the 2028 to 2034 MFF talks, a vote on draft amending budget No 3/2026, and the plenary vote on the EU–Switzerland package of agreements.

US House of Representatives.

The House is in its Thanksgiving recess. It adjourned Friday despite Speaker Mike Johnson urging members to keep working through the lame duck, and returns on 30 November. After that it is scheduled to sit for only 12 more days, through 17 December, before Republicans hand the majority to Democrats, who won both chambers in the midterms. The main pending business is a stopgap or full-year spending bill before current funding runs out on 11 December. On 23 November the White House sent Congress an "anomalies" request asking that any stopgap fund NASA's Artemis program and the Golden Dome missile-defense initiative. Earlier in the lame duck, the House blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, 211 to 204.

US Senate.

The Senate is in a state work period for Thanksgiving (23 to 27 November) and holds no votes this week. Before the break, the floor worked on the bipartisan permitting overhaul and a run of Trump judicial nominees. Behind the scenes, Sen. Chris Van Hollen's challenge to Minority Leader Chuck Schumer is building toward Democratic leadership elections on 1 December. Senate Republicans have paused their push for 350 dollarsbillion in extra Pentagon funding through reconciliation, citing uncertain votes. Senate Democrats are finalizing language that would bar offensive operations against Iran without congressional authorization, which they may offer in exchange for munitions money in the year-end funding deal.

US Supreme Court.

The Court has no arguments this week, and the building closes Thursday and Friday for Thanksgiving. On Tuesday afternoon the justices deferred a decision on Cook County, Illinois's request to dismiss the AR-15 ban challenge as moot. The county filed the request after lead plaintiff Cutberto Viramontes was dropped from the case, which now proceeds as Khaya v. Cook County ahead of the 2 December argument on assault-weapon bans. Citing Acheson Hotels v. Laufer, the order suggests the justices may take up mootness after hearing argument. Commentators are also focused on this month's grant in Mullin v. RAICES, which reviews the administration's day-one proclamation suspending asylum at the southern border.

White House.

President Trump pardoned the National Thanksgiving turkeys, Freedom and Feathers, on the South Lawn Tuesday. He then flew with the First Lady to Mar-a-Lago, where he is scheduled to stay through Sunday. Today's main item is a closed-press meeting at Mar-a-Lago with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, around 4 p.m. ET, followed by Rutte's talks with senior administration officials. The meeting is expected to center on Ukraine ceasefire diplomacy, allied unease over Trump's outreach to Putin, and recent Russian security incidents in Poland and Estonia. In a New York Post interview, Trump said the Iran war is "funded" through January and warned Democrats against halting it. The rest of his holiday schedule is mostly closed policy meetings and "Executive Time," plus a Thanksgiving call to service members.

What's Coming Up.

EU leaders meet tomorrow and Friday (26 to 27 November) at a Special European Council that opens with a Euro Summit. Costa will table his MFF 2028 to 2034 proposal, and leaders will try to agree on the Commission's plan for up to 45 eurosbillion in extra support for Ukraine in 2027, against open opposition from Czech PM Andrej Babiš and Hungary to funding Ukraine through the EU budget. Spain votes in a general election on Sunday 29 November. Congress returns Monday 30 November with about ten legislative days before the 11 December shutdown deadline, and Senate Democrats elect their leaders on 1 December. The Supreme Court's December sitting opens on 30 November, with the AR-15 cases argued on 2 December, the same day NATO foreign ministers meet in Brussels. Estonia's road-border closure with Russia also takes effect on 1 December.

International Politics Analysis.

The Iran war is again the most volatile front. Iran's IRGC Navy says two oil tankers hit naval mines while using an "unauthorised route" through the Strait of Hormuz, after UKMTO reported three vessels struck by projectiles on Monday. Saudi Arabia says it has intercepted 209 aerial threats from the Iran-backed Houthis in two months, and the Houthis are threatening Jeddah and other Red Sea ports. In Washington, NBC reports officials are debating strikes on senior Iranian leaders. Trump, meanwhile, has said Israel's goal of regime change is "probably doable" but that he is "not rushing" and could wait until 2027. In Tehran, an adviser to Speaker Ghalibaf called the US naval blockade an "act of war" that could legitimately be broken by force. Israel's Defense Minister Israel Katz alleges a chemical-weapons program, which Tehran denies. Qatar is urging both sides back to negotiations. In Israel, Prime Minister-elect Gadi Eisenkot is assembling a coalition, which Avigdor Liberman expects by the end of next week.

On Europe's eastern flank, Russia's Foreign Ministry summoned Estonia's chargé d'affaires and warned that Tallinn's "provocative" policies put it "on the verge of triggering open war." Estonia is closing its road crossings with Russia for at least six months from 1 December, after attempted arson attacks on defense firms that it attributes to the GRU. Tallinn has rejected Moscow's claim that it lets Ukrainian drones use its airspace. Rutte says NATO is monitoring Estonia and Poland, where a recent Russian missile incident has raised tensions. On Ukraine diplomacy, Zelensky told NATO parliamentarians in Berlin he wants a four-way meeting of Ukraine, Europe, the US and Russia "as soon as possible." Merz and Macron are insisting Europe have a seat at the table after a series of US-led formats that left it out.

The wider backdrop is the 19 November Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral at APEC in Shenzhen. It produced a joint declaration on preventing nuclear war, oil production and UN reform, and Putin has been invited to the G20 in Miami. Supporters call the format a breakthrough in great-power diplomacy. Critics warn it rewards Moscow and sidelines Europe and the UN. The EU, for its part, is hedging through trade, including the EU–Indonesia agreement signed on 21 November that removes 98.5% of tariffs. Spain's election on Sunday is the next test of Europe's electoral cycle: final polls put Feijóo's PP narrowly ahead of Sánchez's PSOE (40dB: 31.8% versus 30.9%), with PP and Vox together projected at roughly 190 to 210 of 350 seats. The COP31 climate talks in Antalya continue against a backdrop of energy-driven inflation.
