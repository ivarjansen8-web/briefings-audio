Here is your Israel briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Eisenkot opens unity talks with Likud as Netanyahu presses Trump on Iran and Gaza ceasefire strains.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 22 November 2026, mostly 18 to 21 November. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Gadi Eisenkot has invited Netanyahu's Likud to coalition talks starting Sunday 22 November, with a 6 December deadline on his mandate. He says he will put a minority "national emergency" government to a Knesset vote if a broad government fails, so the next two weeks decide whether Israel gets a unity government, a minority cabinet or another election.

Netanyahu is pressing Trump for new joint strikes on Iran, while Trump says he is "not in a hurry" and may wait until after the new year. Channel 13 quotes Netanyahu telling ministers an operation is "unavoidable", and a Channel 12 source says the chances of action are growing.

The IDF crossed Gaza's "yellow line" with tanks in central Gaza, the first major ground raid since the ceasefire, and said on 19 November it had ended it. Hamas called it a "flagrant violation"; the Board of Peace separately warned the deal could collapse.

The Washington Post reports Netanyahu asked Mossad chief Roman Gofman to prepare a covert Iran operation without telling the cabinet or the IDF. An Israeli security official called the claim "false".

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister after the 27 October election, in which his bloc won 53 seats to Eisenkot's four-party alliance on 48. Arab-led parties took a record 19, so neither side has the 61 needed.

Likud has set conditions for a unity government: Netanyahu serving a full four-year term and Eisenkot halting cooperation with Arab parties. Eisenkot has ruled out a rotation deal, so the gap looks wide.

Eisenkot is also courting the Haredi parties, reportedly prioritising them over Arab parties. UTJ denies that talks have advanced significantly and Shas remains loyal to Netanyahu, per Times of Israel.

Hasidic leaders reportedly discussed requiring all 48 Eisenkot-bloc MKs to back a bill exempting Haredi men from service. This is a single-report claim (unconfirmed) and runs against the bloc's public line.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The anti-Netanyahu bloc won the Knesset speakership, with Matan Kahana named speaker and Liberman chairing the Defense Committee. This is an early parliamentary setback for Netanyahu and shows the bloc can hold together on procedure.

Liberman's committee began work on draft-enforcement legislation, saying a fair law must satisfy Chief of Staff Zamir, the High Court and UTJ's Rabbi Lando. The three-way test makes a quick bill unlikely.

Pre-election polls showed Eisenkot's Yashar narrowly ahead of Likud, with neither bloc at 61, and the result matched that deadlock. No post-election poll dated within 72 hours was found.

Eisenkot says he will not drag out talks, and a failed mandate on 6 December would pass the question back to President Herzog.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Netanyahu's defence withdrew its objection on 18 November, so prosecutors may cross-examine former AG Avichai Mandelblit. The High Court closed the related petition without settling the wider legal question.

President Herzog has invited talks toward an agreed resolution before deciding on Netanyahu's pardon request; prosecutors accepted, and Netanyahu had not responded. A pardon route could reshape the coalition arithmetic.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara remains in post after the High Court's 7 to 0 annulment of her dismissal in December 2025. Ministers then urged defiance, and no new development was found in the last 72 hours.

5. Gaza.

Gaza health authorities say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, while the IDF says five Israeli soldiers have died. Figures are attributed to each side and are not independently verified.

About 6,000 Palestinians reportedly left areas hit by the central Gaza raid, which the IDF said targeted a Hamas tunnel. Israel presents the raid as counter-terror, and Hamas as a ceasefire breach.

A Board of Peace report to the UN says both Israel and Hamas missed ceasefire commitments, and the International Stabilisation Force is still not deployed. It faults Hamas for armed activity and taxing goods, and Israel for not enabling a Palestinian police force; 17 dollarsbillion is pledged for recovery.

A Board of Peace adviser warned the body could "throw the keys back to the UN" if Israel keeps blocking preparations. This is an adviser's account, and it is framed against Israel.

OCHA says 38 deaths were reported 10 to 16 November, and a storm flooded shelters affecting about 46,000 households. Over 150,000 tents are reportedly waiting outside Gaza.

Hostage issues: no reporting on hostages was found in the window (Quiet).

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians, one seriously, in Atara north of Ramallah on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA. The IDF said it cleared structures at two illegal outposts linked to the violence.

Palestinian reports describe continuing IDF raids and settler attacks across the West Bank, including on olive harvesters near Jenin. These come from Palestinian sources and outlets sympathetic to them, and none was dated 21 November.

A mosque in Ed-Dahi was set on fire on 19 November, with "Tefillin revenge" graffiti, and the Shin Bet is probing a nationalist motive. Arab lawmakers blamed incitement by Netanyahu's camp.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF struck a Hezbollah missile site in Janta in the Bekaa Valley, saying members were trying to restore it. Israel says this breaches the ceasefire; Lebanon says Israeli operations continue in occupied and unoccupied areas.

Israeli troops still hold a self-declared "security zone" about 10 km deep in south Lebanon, and Israel says Hezbollah must disarm before any withdrawal. Hezbollah rejects disarmament.

A French plan for a 2,000 to 5,000-strong European force to replace UNIFIL is gaining momentum, and Israel's UN envoy Danon rejects any alternative force. Lebanon's government supports the plan.

The Houthis fired missiles at Riyadh on 21 November, and the Saudi-led coalition says it downed three. The front is Saudi-Houthi, with Israel not directly involved in this item.

Iran's foreign minister says keeping enriched uranium in the country is a red line, though a "fair solution" is possible, and a Qatari delegation visited Tehran. Iran calls Israeli chemical-weapons allegations "fraudulent".

Al Jazeera analysis says Israel's presence in southern Syria will likely continue under any next prime minister. US-mediated Israel-Syria talks have been paused or slow in recent coverage.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The IDF says it is short about 12,000 soldiers, including 7,500 in combat roles. The gap is the core of the draft fight.

Haaretz reports Haredi enlistment has reached 4,298 after a year of sanctions. It also argues the anti-Netanyahu bloc will not help Haredim avoid the draft.

The 2026 budget passed at NIS 850.6 billion, with NIS 143 billion for defence, after the draft bill was set aside. It also added over NIS 1 billion for Haredi institutions.

A senior IDF officer told Channel 13 the IDF and CENTCOM have "shared and approved plans" for Iran and that "the question is the timing." This is a single anonymous source (unconfirmed).

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

The Washington Post says Netanyahu asked Gofman, who succeeded David Barnea in June, to prepare a covert Iran operation before the election. It says Chief of Staff Zamir opposed a pre-election strike and learned of the plan; an Israeli security official denies it.

Channel 12 reported that a pre-election Iran plan was shelved after the US was accidentally informed. Anonymous-source reporting (unconfirmed).

Israeli officials reportedly passed Washington intelligence alleging Iranian chemical-weapons work, which Tehran denies. The claim is unverified and the IAEA says it has no access to key Iranian sites.

The Shin Bet is probing the Ed-Dahi mosque arson as possibly nationalist. No arrests were reported.

OSINT: nothing notable found in the window (Quiet).

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Rubio has floated a third path of sustained economic pressure on Iran instead of strikes or a deal, and US officials are reportedly sceptical of Netanyahu's push. This rests on Channel 12 reporting.

Trump told Channel 12's Barak Ravid he was "not in a hurry" on Iran, after a China summit.

France's UNIFIL-replacement push puts Paris at odds with Jerusalem, and Italy reportedly declines to send troops. The US is weighing an alternative without new units.

ICC warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant remain in force on their second anniversary, and no new ICC or ICJ ruling was found.

Egypt, Jordan, Turkey and the Gulf: nothing specific found (Quiet), apart from the Saudi-Houthi exchange above.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel hit a 2026 low against the dollar, with Ynet reporting a 4.5% weekly drop on post-election uncertainty. Globes calls it the weakest in nearly a year.

Israel's CPI fell to 1.1%, a five-year low.

The Bank of Israel rate is 3.25%, with a Monetary Committee decision reportedly due 23 November. Low inflation fuels cut speculation, while a weak shekel argues for caution.

Hapoalim projects the shekel at NIS 3/$ if Eisenkot forms a government, and warns that fresh elections would weigh on investment. This is a bank forecast, not a market outcome.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Haredi protesters rallied against the draft as leaders weigh a deal with Eisenkot's bloc.

A driver rammed residents in Kafr Qasim on 19 November, wounding eight, hours before the Ed-Dahi arson.

A man was killed in a criminal shooting in Reineh on 21 November. Violence in Arab towns remains a recurring issue.

Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas blamed government incitement for the mosque attack, and centrist and left opposition figures condemned it.
