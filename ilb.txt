Here is your Israel briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

Kahana elected Knesset speaker 66 to 52 as PMO vote-buying affair probe widens; Board of Peace warns Gaza truce may collapse.

Compiled from sources dated 15 to 18 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Knesset elected Yashar's Matan Kahana as speaker, 66 to 52, on 17 November, ousting Likud's Ofir Katz as the Netanyahu bloc's candidate, after a six-hour Likud-led filibuster. The win needed Arab-party votes and is the first real test of the Eisenkot bloc's working majority; Likud and allies called it "an eternal disgrace."

Channel 12's Uvda reported that Netanyahu's chief of staff Ido Norden passed police a vote-buying complaint against Ra'am activists the day before the 27 October election, leading to three arrests of Bedouin men hours before polls opened. Police and the Attorney General are reportedly examining whether the PMO orchestrated the affair to delegitimise the vote; Netanyahu's office calls the report false.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council on 17 November that the Gaza ceasefire faces collapse without action. It says Israel has blocked the technocratic civil administration from starting work in Gaza.

Mossad chief Roman Gofman visited Washington on 13 November while Netanyahu is reported to be pressing Trump to resume strikes on Iran. Trump has not restarted large-scale combat and Rubio says there is "no need" to.

The UK sanctioned eight Israeli construction firms and the activist group Tzav 9 on 17 November. Foreign Minister Gideon Saar called it a hostile act and promised countermeasures.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu's government is a caretaker administration with limited powers until a new coalition is formed. Eisenkot's four-party bloc won 48 seats and Netanyahu's right-wing/religious bloc 53, so neither reaches 61.

President Herzog gave Eisenkot the first mandate, with six weeks to form a government. If both men fail, a new election would follow early next year.

Eisenkot has said Netanyahu cannot remain prime minister amid the alleged vote meddling, and his bloc reportedly rules out a unity cabinet with Likud. The ruling-out comes from Press TV citing Kan, a state-aligned outlet relaying a Kan report (unconfirmed).

Eisenkot's route to 61 runs through Arab parties: the Joint List (11 seats) will not vote for a government but will not topple it, while Ra'am (about 8) is under pressure to label Hamas a terrorist organisation. The Hamas condition is aimed partly at Eisenkot's own Jewish-Israeli voters.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The election was held on 27 October; no bloc has a majority and the speaker vote on 17 November is the first parliamentary test. The 66 to 52 margin shows the Eisenkot bloc plus Arab parties can outvote Likud on procedure.

The Eisenkot bloc submitted bills on its first Knesset day to create an October 7 state commission of inquiry and nix the judicial overhaul, but reportedly put the inquiry on the back burner. Bereaved families object; Yisrael Beytenu's Liberman pledged on 14 November to set up a commission.

Exit polls on election night diverged by channel (Channel 12 had Likud 24, Yashar 22, B'Yachad 12, Joint List 11, Ra'am 7). Use official results rather than exit polls for seat counts.

No new post-election seat poll turned up in this search. Quiet on polling.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara and the State Attorney are examining how the election-fraud claims moved from the PMO to police. Times of Israel also reports she told police to halt their internal probe after a Yashar MK flagged a conflict of interest; accounts differ on who runs which inquiry.

Police Commissioner Danny Levy reportedly told closed meetings the PMO "may have fooled us," and ordered an internal review of election-day police conduct. The raids were reportedly carried out without proper warrants.

Former Shin Bet director Yoram Cohen wrote to the AG demanding a criminal investigation; ex-PMO employee who gave police the tip denies a conspiracy and says he got the information from a Joint List MK. Netanyahu's office calls the Channel 12 report "another false report wrapped in dramatic music."

Netanyahu appeared at the Jerusalem Magistrate's Court on 17 November for an evidentiary hearing in his civil lawsuit against Yigal Carmon and others. Reported via a Times of Israel homepage caption only (unconfirmed).

Background: the High Court annulled the government's dismissal of the Attorney General in December 2025. Justice Minister Levin said then that "no judicial order can force cooperation" with her.

5. Gaza.

The Board of Peace report warns that "a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely," and says Israel's restrictions on supplies and equipment, plus funding gaps, hamper shelter and winterisation. I found no official Israeli reply. Herzog separately said Hamas disarmament is essential for the next phase.

Gaza's Health Ministry says at least 1,650 Palestinians have been killed and 5,975 injured since the truce began. The figure is the ministry's, as relayed by Anadolu-linked anews, which also reports an Israeli strike and house demolitions in central Gaza on 17 November.

An Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis reportedly killed six and wounded about 20; the IDF said it targeted Hamas militants operating from the site. Casualty figures come from Gaza-side reports via AFP/CNN and are unverified (unconfirmed).

A nine-month-old died of hypothermia in Khan Younis, the first cold-related death of the winter per the Health Ministry, which also says Israel is cutting the number of patients allowed out for treatment.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers killed a 72-year-old Palestinian woman and wounded 10 others in the central West Bank, per WAFA; witnesses told Reuters settlers shot outside her home. Details of the location differ across reports and I did not confirm the IDF's account.

Masked settlers attacked olive harvesters near Sa'ir, injuring at least five, per WAFA; the IDF said such attacks undermine stability. ACLED says 2026 is on track to be the most violent year for settler violence since its monitoring began.

The UK sanctions target firms building in settlements and Tzav 9; the PA foreign ministry welcomed them, and Peace Now said Netanyahu and Smotrich are driving Israel toward a heavy diplomatic and economic price. Saar vows to strengthen settlements in response.

IDF raids and arrests continued on 17 November per WAFA; specifics were not available.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF struck a suspected Hezbollah arms shipment near the Syria-Lebanon border overnight on 15 to 16 November, a rare deep strike after months of restraint at US request. Lebanese state media reported no casualties; Hezbollah acknowledged the explosives were meant for "resistance."

Israel also destroyed Hezbollah tunnels in the south and a drone hit near Mayfadoun; Middle East Monitor claims Israel abducted two Lebanese brothers (unconfirmed, partisan outlet). The US-sponsored Israel-Lebanon framework calling for Hezbollah disarmament and phased Israeli withdrawal remains stalled.

Iran says UN inspectors will not enter bombed nuclear sites until the war ends; the US struck an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island, and a tanker reportedly hit a mine near Hormuz. The US-Israeli war on Iran began 28 February; core disputes over the nuclear programme, Hormuz and sanctions are unresolved.

The Houthis are attacking Saudi targets, and Syria denies sending troops to Yemen. No Houthi attack on Israel was found in this window.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The IDF is operating in Lebanon, Gaza and the West Bank concurrently while the government is a caretaker. The Haredi draft law remains unresolved; officials earlier said the coalition's bill was unlikely to pass the High Court.

Campaign proposals on the draft (Bennett's shift of NIS 30bn from draft dodgers; Winter's 25% Haredi exemption cap) now feed coalition talks. Dated about five weeks ago, so treat as background.

Quiet on new procurement or budget decisions in the last 72 hours; earlier reporting had the IDF seeking NIS 140bn and the Finance Ministry offering NIS 112bn, and the Bank of Israel warned the draft bill would not ease the reserve burden. Undated in search results.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad Director Roman Gofman held talks in Washington on 13 November, with CIA Director Ratcliffe and reportedly Witkoff in Miami, per Axios. Netanyahu denied a reported heated call with Trump; the Financial Times says he contacts Trump daily about Iran.

N12 reported, citing 10 former senior officials, that Gofman's tenure has put Mossad in turmoil, with the deputy director and several division heads removed. The sourcing is former officials (unconfirmed).

A disputed Channel 12 report alleged Netanyahu and Gofman considered an operation against Iran shortly before the election, shelved after Washington learned of it (unconfirmed).

Critics accuse Netanyahu of installing loyalists at Mossad and Shin Bet (David Zini heads the Shin Bet); the PMO-police affair now raises questions about Shin Bet and police independence.

OSINT: no notable open-source finding surfaced this window. Quiet.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

UK sanctions are the first against Israeli entities since the election; officials stress trade inside the Green Line continues. Saar's promised countermeasures had not been specified.

Washington is the key audience on Iran: Rubio says the US has "no need" to restart combat, while Trump says "we don't need a deal" and Tehran holds to its demands. The gap with Netanyahu's push is the central strain.

The Board of Peace, mandated by the Security Council through 2027, is now the main channel on Gaza. Ron Dermer reportedly says Israel should work with it; Israel's record on the civil administration is the board's main complaint.

Legal front: the Trump administration sanctioned the ICC in October; Turkey continues a legal campaign against Israeli officials. No new ICC or ICJ ruling found in the window.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The dollar closed at NIS 3.199 on 13 November, about 2.6% weaker for the shekel than on 30 October. The Bank of Israel rate stays at 3.25%.

Bank Hapoalim's strategist expects the shekel near NIS 3/$ if Eisenkot forms a government. The Bank of Israel might resume dollar purchases if the shekel appreciates sharply below that.

The TA-125 was about 3,869 on 17 November, roughly flat (a CFD quote, not the exchange itself).

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Arab parties are kingmakers for the first time in years; the Joint List and Ra'am both recommended Eisenkot. The debate over Ra'am's conditions (Hamas designation, national service) will shape Arab-Jewish politics.

Netanyahu has repeatedly claimed without proof that Arab parties committed foreign-backed fraud; the Uvda report suggests the vote-buying claim itself may have been seeded. Times of Israel headlines say Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of fuelling a "violent and racist" campaign against Arabs (unconfirmed in full text).

Haredi-draft politics: Haredi parties are now outside the likely government if Eisenkot succeeds, and Forward argues he must choose between Arab and Haredi partners.

US-Iran talks and any Trump decision on renewed strikes; further Israeli strikes on Hezbollah arms routes.
