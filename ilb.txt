Here is your Israel briefing for Tuesday, 24 November.

Netanyahu pushes Iran strike as Eisenkot unity talks stall; settler attacks and Gaza ceasefire strain.

Compiled from sources dated 18 to 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Netanyahu is pushing for renewed US-Israeli strikes on Iran, citing its chemical weapons programme, while unity talks with Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party stall. Haaretz reports that chemical weapons are now his main argument, and that security experts see the 440 kg of 60%-enriched uranium as the bigger threat. Critics, including some US officials reportedly, question the urgency and suspect a political motive.

Likud and Yashar teams met for about four hours on 22 November without a deal and agreed to more talks; a third round followed on the evening of 23 November. The sticking points are who leads the government (Netanyahu reportedly insists on going first in any rotation, Eisenkot opposes rotation) and whether Itamar Ben-Gvir joins. The outcome of the 23 November session was not found.

The US naval blockade of Iran continues, and Iran says it will use "all means" to end it. CNN reports Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz at some of the highest levels since the war began, which raises the stakes of any Israeli move.

Settlers attacked several Palestinian villages on 22 November, and a Palestinian shepherd was reportedly shot dead by a settler near Bethlehem on 23 November. Eisenkot called the attacks "Jewish terrorism"; Defense Minister Katz says police, not the IDF, should handle them. The shepherd's death is from Palestinian outlet PIC (unconfirmed).

2. Government and coalition.

Eisenkot has until 6 December to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension. If unity talks fail, Yashar reportedly plans to seek a Knesset confidence vote for a minority government before the mandate expires.

Netanyahu reportedly met Ben-Gvir and Smotrich before the talks resumed. It shows him keeping his far-right partners close while negotiating with the centre.

Naftali Bennett's party backs a unity government under Eisenkot, and he and Eisenkot reportedly split on giving Likud a deadline. Democrats chair Yair Golan urged Eisenkot to end the Likud talks.

Avigdor Liberman rejects any government including Netanyahu and calls an independent Iran strike without the US "manifestly illegal". He says it would be politically motivated, which signals how the Iran file now shapes the coalition fight.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Final results from the 27 October election gave Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats (Likud 27), the opposition 48 and Arab-led parties 19. The pro-Netanyahu camp is eight short of 61; the opposition plus Arab parties total 67.

Likud claimed the election was "stolen" without presenting evidence; the Central Elections Committee said there is no basis for claims of systematic compromise. The foreign-interference allegation was referred to the Shin Bet, and the committee certified the results on 4 November.

A Knesset committee began work on conscription-enforcement legislation, with Liberman as temporary chair. He says any draft law must win the approval of Chief of Staff Zamir, the High Court and UTJ's Rabbi Lando.

No new poll dated 22 to 24 November was found. (unconfirmed gap)

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The Netanyahu trial panel ordered Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara to appear; she declined the 18 November date, and a fresh appearance is reportedly due 25 November. The judges have reportedly hinted that the bribery charge in Case 4000 may not hold.

Former AG Avichai Mandelblit testified on 3 November, and prosecutors were cleared to cross-examine him. Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana then urged President Herzog to pardon Netanyahu, a reading that goes beyond what the court has established.

Netanyahu's pardon request, filed in November 2025, remains undecided. Herzog has pushed for a plea deal first.

A Haaretz review found none of the Supreme Court's 39 law clerks is Arab, despite rulings on equal representation.

5. Gaza.

The October 2025 ceasefire is under strain. On 18 November the Board of Peace accused both Israel and Hamas of failing their commitments, warning the truce is in danger of collapse.

The IDF launched its first ground operation beyond the Yellow Line since the ceasefire, near Nuseirat and Bureij on 18 to 19 November, and ordered civilians out. The IDF said it targeted a tunnel; Hamas-linked authorities said about 6,000 people were displaced and called it a flagrant violation.

An Israeli strike killed an UNRWA worker near a well in Jabalia on 23 November, per UNRWA and medics. Three others were wounded.

A strike on a vehicle in Gaza City on 21 November killed three, per the Gaza Health Ministry. The IDF said it targeted Islamic Jihad militants.

The Board of Peace says reconstruction is stalled; Israel has not signed a status-of-forces agreement for the International Stabilization Force and demands Hamas disarm before any withdrawal. An adviser warned on 20 November the Board could hand Gaza back to the UN.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers burned vehicles and vandalised Kafr Malik, Deir Abu Mash'al and Deir al-Sudan on 22 November. Al Jazeera cites monitoring that attacks rose from 647 in September to 1,369 in October.

Police arrested seven teens at an illegal outpost used as a launchpad, and Foreign Minister Sa'ar condemned the rampage. Israeli police said they would enter villages under IDF escort to collect evidence.

IDF sources told Haaretz that settler violence spikes during outpost evacuations.

Palestinian outlets report raids, arrests and demolitions across the West Bank, and a Palestinian killed in a raid on 20 November. PIC is Hamas-aligned, so treat figures with caution.

Mahmoud Abbas cancelled the 28 November legislative elections, resetting presidential and legislative votes for 11 September 2027. Sa'ar called Abbas a "corrupt dictator".

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Israel struck Hezbollah targets in the Bekaa on 19 November, the first time in months, and a weapons shipment near the Syria border on 16 November. Haaretz said the latter broke a period of restraint kept at US request.

Lebanese media reported strikes on Kfar Tebnit on 20 November and artillery on southern villages. Hezbollah refuses to disarm and the Israel-Lebanon framework is stalled.

The IDF says it demolished a Hezbollah tunnel in southern Lebanon on 15 November.

Iran's Foreign Ministry called the chemical weapons allegations "fraudulent" on 19 November.

Houthi fire is focused on Saudi Arabia and Yemen; no launch at Israel was found for 22 to 23 November.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The Haredi draft remains unresolved, with yeshiva students jailed for ignoring orders. Haredi protests on 22 November blocked Jerusalem's light rail, and police dispersed a demonstration on Bar Ilan Street.

Reports say Yashar and UTJ are making progress on a draft framework, and Liberman has reportedly softened his stance. This is the main coalition-bargaining chip. Times of Israel, 22 November (unconfirmed)

The 2026 budget passed in March after the coalition set the draft-exemption bill aside to fund the Iran war.

Ynet reports the IDF regards Iran chemical weapons intelligence as the most worrying short-term issue but sees no clear urgency.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad chief David Barnea (per a recent Jerusalem Post report) and Shin Bet chief David Zini visited the UAE during the Iran war to coordinate.

A senior official warned that Netanyahu "will stop at nothing", and Zini reportedly plans to replace the head of the Shin Bet's Israel Division. Israel Hayom ran it as an opinion column. Israel Hayom, 20 November (unconfirmed)

Reports conflict on who presented the Iran chemical intelligence in Washington. One summary names Mossad chief "Roman Gofman" and another source names Barnea, so the identity is unconfirmed. Haaretz, 23 November (unconfirmed)

Ex-IDF chief Herzi Halevi said on 1 November that 7 October was preventable but there was "absolutely no internal betrayal".

No notable open-source findings were found for the last 72 hours. Quiet.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Herzog said on 23 November that the peace treaties with Egypt and Jordan are the base of Israel's regional position and that King Abdullah's concerns deserve attention.

The US is discussing military training for Syrian forces with about 15 allies, including Saudi Arabia, building on the Damascus Coordination Center in Jordan.

The US ambassador-nominee to Saudi Arabia said normalisation with Israel is "part of the negotiation process".

Qatar is mediating between the US and Iran on lifting the blockade.

The ICC arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant stand; the ICC's deputy prosecutor said in September that no steps to withdraw them were taken. No new ICC or ICJ developments found for this week.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The Bank of Israel held its rate at 3.25% on 23 November, citing uncertainty. It was the last decision of 2026.

The shekel has reportedly weakened about 8% since 21 October, to roughly 3.35 per dollar. A weekly report put the representative rate at 3.3260 on 20 November. Israel Investment Report (unconfirmed)

The TA-35 closed 20 November at 3,861.51, down 4.37% on the week.

Iran-war escalation fears and political deadlock are the main stated drags. No 23 November TASE close was found.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Haredi protests over the jailing of yeshiva students disrupted Jerusalem on 22 November. Police declared a Bar Ilan Street demonstration unlawful after reports of blocked roads and damaged infrastructure.

Arab parties won 19 seats and reportedly discussed possible cooperation with UTJ. Likud's "stolen election" claim targeted Arab-party voting. Times of Israel, 12 November (unconfirmed)

Haaretz found no Arab law clerks at the Supreme Court.
