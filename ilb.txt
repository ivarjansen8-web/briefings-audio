Here is your Israel briefing for Monday, 16 November.

Netanyahu faces election-day arrests scandal as Eisenkot races to form a government; Iran strike weighed.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 16 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Eisenkot says Netanyahu "cannot remain prime minister for even one more minute" after a Channel 12 report that Netanyahu's chief of staff steered police to three Ra'am activists, who were arrested on election day (27 October). The report says the tip was baseless and that Shin Bet chief David Zini also asked to wiretap five Arab MKs (the attorney general refused); Likud calls the charge "outrageous incitement" and Netanyahu's office denies wrongdoing.

Neither bloc has 61 seats, and Herzog has given Gadi Eisenkot until 6 December to form a government. Netanyahu leads a caretaker government and is pushing a "unity" cabinet that Eisenkot calls a "bluff".

The US and Israel are reportedly weighing new strikes on Iran, including its economic infrastructure. The report comes via the NYT and an Iran International live blog; the Wall Street Journal says Iran still holds 1,500 to 2,500 missiles.

Israel killed senior Hamas commander Samir Abu Younis in Khan Younis on 15 November, which shows how far the October 2025 ceasefire has eroded.

A trial-court summons for the attorney general is due 18 November. The Netanyahu trial judges ordered Gali Baharav-Miara to appear; her office had said she would not.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu's government stays in office as a caretaker after losing its majority on 27 October. Netanyahu has said he is open to excluding Itamar Ben Gvir from a unity government.

Yashar cancelled scheduled talks with Likud on 10 November after Netanyahu's "divisive speech". Eisenkot rules out serving under Netanyahu but is open to a deal with Likud without him.

Eisenkot's envisaged 61-seat coalition is his bloc's 48 seats plus 13 from United Torah Judaism (7) and Shas (6). Haredi leaders are fielding several overtures.

Netanyahu called on 14 November for Haredi-inclusive unity talks "without preconditions". Eisenkot called it "another spin".

Avigdor Liberman set coalition conditions on 6 November that leave room for a Haredi draft compromise.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The 27 October election produced deadlock. Final pre-election polls had Maariv at 54 seats for the "change" bloc against 48 for Netanyahu's, and Channel 12 at Yashar 23, Likud 21 and Bennett's list 15. The actual seat counts were not in what I found, apart from Yashar's bloc at 48.

Opposition parties are moving to reverse the judicial overhaul as part of coalition talks.

The Knesset legal adviser reportedly says a Haredi draft law cannot pass before a new government is sworn in. Shas fears any deal will not hold.

Polling since the election: Quiet. I found no November polls.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The Netanyahu trial panel ordered AG Baharav-Miara to appear on 18 November over management of the proceedings. She had declined, and the judges cited "added value" in her presence.

The dispute is over investigation approvals. The panel earlier recommended that prosecutors drop the bribery charge, and there was no response.

The AG reportedly refused Zini's request to wiretap five Arab MKs on election day (unconfirmed, a Channel 12 report relayed by others). Eisenkot wants a commission of inquiry into the PM's office and the security agencies.

Haaretz reports the election-fraud tip's source was a former Netanyahu employee.

5. Gaza.

Israel killed Rafah brigade commander Samir Abu Younis in al-Mawasi on 15 November, per Hamas's confirmation. CNN says it came two months after Israel killed his predecessor.

The Gaza Health Ministry's count of Palestinians killed since the 11 October 2025 ceasefire stands at about 1,643 to 1,647, with about 5,969 injured. These are ministry figures (Al Jazeera and ABNA differ slightly).

The Board of Peace is sidelined. The National Committee has not entered Gaza and no international stabilisation force has deployed. Talks with 17 countries on a civilian police force were reported on Friday.

The Board blamed Hamas for an Israeli strike on Nasser hospital, which drew criticism.

Humanitarian: aid enters only via Kerem Shalom while Rafah is open for limited movement of people. Between 3 and 9 November, 119 patients were evacuated, and agencies are preparing for winter storms.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Dozens of masked settlers attacked olive harvesters near Sa'ir on 14 November, with about five injured per reports. The IDF says it sent troops and Border Police.

Five Palestinians were wounded in Atara on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA. The IDF says it evacuated two illegal outposts.

IDF sources say settler violence spikes during outpost removals.

European backlash over settler violence is growing.

Background: Smotrich has called for a Gaza-style war in the West Bank and the dismantling of the PA. The security cabinet approved land and civil-control measures that Palestinian officials say end Oslo.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF says it demolished a 35-metre Hezbollah tunnel at Majdal Zoun in southern Lebanon. It is part of a campaign that began 2 March 2026.

US UN ambassador Waltz is in Beirut for talks on Hezbollah. Lebanese forces raided a Hezbollah-area depot in Beirut on 13 November.

Hezbollah chief Naim Qassem told Lebanese critics on 11 November to let Israel attack.

On Iran, the security cabinet reportedly ordered more preparation for a possible strike (Kan, via Anadolu). Eisenkot, backed by 67 lawmakers, urges deferral until a new government.

The IRGC claims two more tankers hit mines in Hormuz. This is an Iranian state-media claim relayed by Iran International.

The Houthis are escalating against Saudi Arabia. They struck Aramco-linked sites on 12 November, a claim Critical Threats could not verify, and Syria is sending troops to help Riyadh.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir is reportedly the only security chief opposing an immediate Iran strike. Channel 12 says Netanyahu, Mossad chief Roman Gofman and Zini favour one.

Trump said on election day that US military aid (about 4 billion dollars a year) will stop at the end of 2026. Reports say talks continue on a payment framework running to 2033.

Netanyahu's office hailed a shift "from aid to partnership". The current MOU runs to 2028 and Herzog called the news "a bolt from the blue".

The IDF's winter recruitment cycle opened with about 230 Haredi men expected to report. Protests and clashes followed.

Ex-chief of staff Halevi said 7 October was preventable but there was "no internal betrayal".

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad chief Roman Gofman briefed CIA director Ratcliffe and Witkoff on Iran and will update the security cabinet. Netanyahu reportedly did not inform Eisenkot of the trip.

Shin Bet chief Zini is under fire over the reported wiretap request and election-day arrests (unconfirmed, Channel 12). Haaretz carried a critical piece on 4 November.

Reports split the services: Gofman and Zini are portrayed as aligned with Netanyahu on Iran, and the IDF as the brake (Channel 12, unconfirmed).

Open-source: Critical Threats' 12 November Iran Update covers Houthi-Saudi strikes and Iranian Hormuz activity. These are open-source and unverified.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Trump's aid announcement is straining ties. Huckabee and the Heritage Foundation back it, and Israeli opposition figures call it a security blow.

Senate hearings on Trump's Saudi and Egypt ambassador nominees linked Israel normalisation to a Saudi nuclear deal. Several Gulf posts remain unfilled.

Trump may ask Erdogan to help Saudi Arabia against the Houthis, and Ankara is noncommittal. Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey signed a defence pact in August.

Analysts say Trump may push for an Iran deal after his midterm setback.

ICC/ICJ: Quiet. The warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant stand and I found no new rulings.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The TA-35 rose 3.09% to 3,941 on 6 November, its best day since March, but fell 2.9% on the week.

The shekel has weakened about 4.5% since the election, to around NIS 3.15 to 3.17 per dollar.

The Bank of Israel held its rate at 3.25% in October after three cuts. It projects 5.6% growth in 2026 and 7.5% in 2027, and it cites political and currency risk.

The next rate decision is scheduled for 23 November (the BoI's own date; one other source says 26 November).

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Herzog urged the next government to "intensify the fight" on Arab-community crime after a grenade attack killed Zidan, the 200th Arab victim this year (Herzog's count). The 2025 total was a record 252.

The election-day arrests of Ra'am activists put Arab political rights in focus.

Haredi draft: NIS 147,000 was reportedly handed to 15 draft evaders at a Bnei Brak ceremony. Herzog called for "joint responsibility" for service.

Haaretz reports the anti-Netanyahu bloc will not help the Haredim avoid the draft.

What to watch.

18 November: Attorney General Baharav-Miara is due before the Netanyahu trial panel, and she may again decline.

This week: the security cabinet's next Iran discussion, after Gofman's briefing, and any strike decision before a new government.

23 November: Bank of Israel rate decision (some sources say 26 November).

Ongoing: Yashar-Likud and Haredi talks, with Eisenkot's deadline of 6 December (a 14-day extension is possible).

Pending: demands for a commission of inquiry or police probe into the election-day arrests.

Year-end: the US aid cutoff and talks on a replacement framework.
