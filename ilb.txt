Here is your Israel briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Eisenkot's coalition talks stall after Netanyahu speech; Iran strike pressure; Gaza flooded.

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 11 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The new Knesset was sworn in on 10 November with no government in place, and Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister. The 27 October election cost his bloc its majority but gave Eisenkot's camp no clear one either, so the coalition arithmetic now drives everything else.

Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) cancelled scheduled coalition talks with Likud after Netanyahu's Knesset speech calling for a unity government and attacking Arab MKs. Eisenkot called the speech "delusional, divisive, hate-filled"; the Joint List called it "inciting and racist".

Netanyahu told the Knesset that Iran is working to renew its nuclear threat and that Israel will face pressure to accept it. Financial Times and Axios reports, relayed by Iran International and Gulf News, say he is pressing Trump to resume strikes, which Gulf leaders and Erdogan reportedly oppose (unconfirmed, secondhand).

Gaza's first winter storm flooded displacement sites while the Board of Peace called Israel's aid response "insufficient". OCHA says more than 6,000 family shelters were destroyed and 150,000 tents wait outside Gaza; Israel blames Hamas.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu leads a caretaker government while Likud's own allies are split. United Torah Judaism (7 seats) declined to recommend any candidate over the Haredi draft dispute, leaving Netanyahu with only 46 recommendations against Eisenkot's 67.

Haaretz reports Netanyahu plans to nominate Gideon Sa'ar as Interior Minister (unconfirmed, single-source). It signals Netanyahu is still making appointments as a caretaker.

Defence Minister Israel Katz said the mission against Iran is "not finished" and that renewed attacks are possible. His line aligns with Netanyahu's, against the US preference for diplomacy.

Rubio said Israel is "rich enough" to buy its own weapons and could get off US aid by year's end if it wishes. The Times of Israel frames this as Trump wanting a swift end to military aid.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

President Herzog gave Eisenkot the mandate on 7 to 8 November after 67 recommendations (48 opposition, 19 from Ra'am and the Joint List). Recommendations do not commit the Arab parties to joining; Ra'am's Mansour Abbas backs Hamas disarmament, one of Eisenkot's four conditions.

Eisenkot has 28 days, with a possible extension, to form a government; if he fails Herzog could turn to Netanyahu. Sources differ on the extension length (one says 14 days, others say up to six weeks in total); a failure of both could trigger new elections.

Seat math: Likud holds 27 seats and Yashar 21, per Al Jazeera. A Yashar-Together-Yisrael Beiteinu-Likud unity government would total 67 but requires Eisenkot to drop the pledge to remove Netanyahu.

Lieberman will not back any government including Netanyahu and also rules out a minority government relying on Ra'am and the Joint List. That leaves Eisenkot squeezed between right and Arab parties; Israel Hayom says "Lieberman blinked first" but Eisenkot's real test lies ahead.

Haaretz says the anti-Netanyahu bloc will not help Haredim dodge the draft, making conscription the crux of any Haredi deal. Right-wing protesters also warned of "civil war" if Arab-party support enables a government (Times of Israel).

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The High Court dismissed a Lavi petition against Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara over her handling of the probe into Arab MKs and aides. The panel ruled Lavi had not exhausted remedies with the AG first; the date is roughly 9 to 10 November (unconfirmed).

Netanyahu's pardon request to Herzog is still pending, with no deadline. Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana urged a pardon in October after ex-AG Mandelblit's testimony; Ohana's characterisation goes beyond what the court has established, and a Justice Ministry paper called a pardon mid-trial "highly problematic".

A July 2026 law freezing enforcement against some yeshiva students was suspended by the Supreme Court a day after passing. Its present status and a reported 30 November expiry could not be confirmed.

The High Court annulled the government's dismissal of the AG 7 to 0 in December 2025 (background). Justice Minister Levin called the ruling "theater of the absurd" and ministers urged defiance; nothing new in the last 72 hours.

5. Gaza.

Israeli attacks killed at least seven people in Gaza on 10 November, including a father and his 13-year-old son, a year into the ceasefire. Territory health officials say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the truce, and Israel says four soldiers have died.

Negotiators have not agreed terms for Hamas disarmament or Israeli withdrawal; Israeli troops control about two-thirds of the Strip. Israel says Hamas is rearming; Hamas says it keeps to the ceasefire and accuses Israel of undermining it.

The Board of Peace's July roadmap for Hamas disarmament has not produced a deployed stabilisation force. Al Jazeera's one-year review says Hamas accepts handing weapons to the technocratic committee but rejects the "disarmament" clause; a revised UN draft tying withdrawal to benchmarks was reported on 10 November.

About 1.4 million people (65 percent of the population) live in tents, and OCHA says stocks inside Gaza are virtually exhausted. COGAT says 91,000+ tents and 8,200 trucks have entered; OCHA attributes the gap to Israeli restrictions plus funding shortfalls, and the two figures are not directly comparable.

Eight countries including Egypt, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UK urged Israel to ease dual-use restrictions on Gaza. The statement date is unclear.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Israeli forces killed a 14-year-old boy in Burqa on 10 November and wounded two other teenagers, per WAFA. The IDF account was not in the sources found.

Settlers torched excavators, vehicles and equipment in the Beita industrial zone near Nablus; the owner put losses above NIS 3.3 million. WAFA also reported attacks on farmers at Beit al-Roush near Hebron.

OCHA recorded 457 settler attacks with casualties or damage in October, the most in any month since 2006. Al Jazeera's weekly describes the West Bank as "on fire".

Around 30 armed settlers reportedly took over eight homes near Jericho, displacing about 70 Palestinians (unconfirmed, date unclear).

E1 tenders (August) and Smotrich's stated aim to "bury" a Palestinian state remain the backdrop; Chatham House calls it accelerating de facto annexation. No new E1 step found in the last 72 hours.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Israeli strikes hit Qantara and Kfar Tebnit in south Lebanon on 9 November, inside the IDF buffer zone. Violence is lower since the June US-brokered framework, but Israel ties withdrawal to Hezbollah disarmament and Hezbollah ties disarmament talks to withdrawal.

A 5 November Israeli strike killed three girls and their grandmother near Aynata, causing outrage in Lebanon. Lebanese reports describe residents stocking up over fears of escalation.

The IDF is preparing for a surprise Iranian attack while the US pursues a deal. Reported terms: partial easing of the US blockade for reopening Hormuz and nuclear inspections; Iran's Rezaei denies talks exist and demands seven conditions.

The IDF assesses Iran could rebuild a weapons capability in one to two years; Iran says it stays in the NPT while hardliners float a bomb.

The Houthis are firing at Saudi Arabia (Riyadh, Abqaiq) rather than Israel in the sources found. A Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted missiles on 9 to 10 November; casualty claims are unverified.

Syria: Sharaa said in July a security deal was about 90 percent agreed before Israel pulled back. Nothing new in November.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The IDF opened its winter recruitment cycle for Haredim on 2 November, targeting about 4,800 recruits for the year. Haaretz cites 4,298 Haredi enlistees after a year of sanctions.

Haredi conscription is now the central bargaining issue in coalition talks. The 2026 budget passed in March by shelving the draft law and adding over NIS 1 billion for yeshivas.

Israeli defence officials are on high alert for a US offensive or Iranian retaliation against bases hosting US personnel.

A Republican congressman moved on 9 November to impeach US Defence Secretary Hegseth over the Iran war. It matters for Israel because US war politics shape any resumption of strikes.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

An IDF soldier was indicted for spying for Iran for about a year, paid in crypto, allegedly photographing interceptors and US refuelling planes. A military court extended his detention to 12 November.

The Shin Bet's January report said 25 people were indicted for Iran spying in 2025 and 120 incidents were thwarted. Haaretz describes the new recruits as "young, broke and mostly clueless".

No state commission of inquiry into 7 October exists; former Shin Bet figures keep demanding one. Ronen Bar warned without it Israel is "condemning itself to the next October 7"; no new development in the last 72 hours.

Open-source: nothing notable surfaced in the last 72 hours. Quiet on Mossad and Unit 8200 leadership news.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Trump says the Iran war is "effectively" over after talks with Qatar's prime minister; Qatar and Pakistan mediate. Qatar says progress is being made but a deal is not certain.

Saudi, UAE, Qatari and Turkish leaders are reportedly urging Trump to allow diplomacy more time. Netanyahu's preference for strikes puts him at odds with them (unconfirmed, secondhand).

French presidential hopeful Mélenchon pledged to enforce the ICC warrant against Netanyahu if elected in 2027. A campaign pledge, not a court action; no new ICC or ICJ rulings found.

Egypt and Jordan bilateral news with Israel: Quiet in the sources found.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The TA-35 rose 1.84% to 4,013.50 on 9 November.

The representative rate was NIS 3.171 per dollar on 9 November, down 0.72%. Investing.com shows about 3.156 on 10 November (single quote source).

The Bank of Israel policy rate stands at 3.25%.

Rubio's remark on ending US military aid and the Hormuz closure are the main risk factors.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Arab-sector murders hit a record 252 in 2025, and police have solved only 12% of homicides this year, per the Abraham Initiatives watchdog. Critics blame Ben-Gvir's tenure; protests continue.

Netanyahu's attack on Arab MKs and the "civil war" warnings from right-wing protesters sharpen tension over any Arab-party role.

Ben-Gvir accused the police chief of ordering a violent response to Haredi protesters in October. Details were thin.

Coming days: whether Trump authorises renewed Iran strikes or a Hormuz deal; Netanyahu's reported lobbying versus Gulf pushback.

30 November: reported expiry of the Haredi yeshiva-student enforcement freeze, pending Supreme Court review (unconfirmed).

Gaza: winter storms, tent entry decisions, and the UN Security Council draft on withdrawal benchmarks.
