Here is your Israel briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

Netanyahu pushes Iran strike as Eisenkot hunts for a majority; IDF tanks cross Gaza yellow line.

Compiled from sources dated 17 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Netanyahu is pressing Washington for a 4 to 7 day air campaign against Iran, while Trump says he is "not rushing" and will wait until January. Opposition MK Avigdor Liberman, newly chair of the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee, says any strike now would be politically motivated, and US officials cited by Channel 12 reportedly share that suspicion.

Eisenkot holds the mandate to form a government but has no workable majority. Herzog tasked him on 8 November after 67 MKs recommended him, yet some partners reject reliance on Arab parties, and Netanyahu keeps demanding a "unity government".

Israeli tanks crossed Gaza's "yellow line" on 18 November, the first ground operation since the October 2025 truce. The IDF said it was destroying a Hamas tunnel near Nuseirat and Bureij and withdrew on 19 November; Hamas called it a flagrant violation.

The new Knesset began unwinding Netanyahu's judicial and media overhauls on 18 November. Preliminary votes advanced repeals touching judicial appointments, the Attorney General's authority and broadcasting.

Houthi missiles keep targeting Riyadh. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three ballistic missiles on 21 November, part of an escalating Houthi-Saudi exchange.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu, who lost his Knesset majority in the 27 October election, repeats that a unity coalition is needed because "Israel's fate is at stake". He argues the Iran threat cannot be handled by a minority government reliant on Arab parties; critics call this inflation of the threat for political gain.

Eisenkot has rejected joining a Netanyahu-led coalition and calls for a "national emergency government". His 28-day window from 8 November runs to about 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension, though AP says up to six weeks.

Ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism has left the door open to cooperating with Eisenkot, with signs the Haredi parties may leave Netanyahu. Both Arab lists recommended Eisenkot but made no commitment to join.

Netanyahu's allies are under scrutiny over election-day arrests of Ra'am activists. Channel 12 and Ynet reported the arrests followed a call from the PM's chief of staff Ido Norden to police brass; the PMO denies wrongdoing.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Matan Kahana (Yashar) was elected Knesset speaker 66 to 52 on 17 November. It shows the Eisenkot bloc controls the floor even without a government.

A Knesset panel waived the waiting period so repeal bills could advance. One report puts the media-overhaul repeal at 63 to 49 in a preliminary vote; sources differ on how many repeals cleared. Justice Minister Levin called the effort "hysterical" and "shameful".

Likud bills advanced unopposed after the Eisenkot bloc and Arab parties boycotted the votes. Repeal bills still need three more readings.

The election itself ended without a clear Netanyahu win: pre-election poll averages had his bloc near 50 seats against 54 for Eisenkot's, and Channel 12's exit poll projected Netanyahu's bloc falling from 68 to 52.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The Netanyahu trial judges ordered AG Gali Baharav-Miara to appear in the Jerusalem District Court on trial management; she declined personal participation in proposed talks. She says it conflicts with prosecution procedures but stays open to direct settlement talks; the defence says the court can summon her regardless. The hearing date is unclear in the coverage I found.

Netanyahu's pardon request, filed last November, remains unresolved. Earlier reports said the AG was expected to find it fails legal requirements.

The AG and State Attorney reportedly asked the police commissioner to shelve an internal review of the election-day arrests. Police and the AG are reportedly examining whether the intelligence was planted; no charges have resulted from the vote-buying probe.

The election commissioner and Shin Bet say no evidence of vote-buying large enough to affect results. Netanyahu and Likud have framed the arrests as an attempt to steal the election.

5. Gaza.

Israeli tanks and bulldozers operated along Salah al-Din Road for over 12 hours on 18 November before the IDF said it had ended the operation. Gaza's Hamas-run media office said about 6,000 people fled (Hamas-run government figure).

Gaza's Health Ministry says 1,658 Palestinians have been killed and 6,000 wounded since the ceasefire began; the IDF says five of its soldiers have died in that time. Figures are from each side; I found no independent count.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council on 17 November the ceasefire risks collapse. It cites the stalled stabilisation force, withdrawals and reconstruction, and will present to the Council at the end of November.

Defence Minister Katz said on 18 November Israel will keep security zones in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria indefinitely. He conditions withdrawal on Hamas being dismantled and Hezbollah disarmed, and warns of more territory seized after any attack.

Humanitarian: OCHA reports trucks offloaded fell about 42% week on week and that Israel suspended shipments from Egypt from 1 November. Heavy rain and flooding hit over 26,000 households, with 150,000+ tents held outside Gaza.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

A settlement security coordinator shot dead an unarmed Palestinian, named by WAFA as Firas Fares Samad, 36, in the central West Bank; the IDF is reviewing. Palestinian and Israeli accounts rest on WAFA and Times of Israel respectively.

Settlers attacked in several areas on 20 November, including Teqoa, where a couple was assaulted and pepper-sprayed. Reported via WAFA-sourced Palestinian outlets.

A settler assault on 17 November killed a Palestinian woman and wounded 10. Al Jazeera calls it a pogrom.

OCHA figures cited by PAL Info recorded 40 settler attacks across 30 communities from 10 to 16 November, and the same outlet counted 54 IDF raids on 20 November (single-source count).

A mosque in Dahi was set alight overnight 18 to 19 November and vandalised with pro-settler graffiti; police are investigating.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IAF struck a former Hezbollah missile production and storage site at Janta in the Beqaa on 19 November, reportedly with no casualties. The IDF says operatives had returned to the site; Hezbollah has not commented.

Lebanon's NNA reported five airstrikes in the east and Israeli drones exploding on a roof in Nabatieh al-Fawqa. Fighting has eased since June under a US-Iran understanding and a US-sponsored Israel-Lebanon framework, per reports.

Israel and the US are reportedly weighing an Iran attack in coming days (CBS, via Times of Israel), while Trump says he will wait until January. Netanyahu pushes a 4 to 7 day campaign; the signals conflict.

Iran's Foreign Ministry called chemical weapons allegations "fraudulent" on 19 November.

Houthi missiles hit a Riyadh financial-district building (material damage, no injuries) on 19 November, and the coalition struck Hodeidah. The Houthis threaten Jeddah and Red Sea ports.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

A Haredi draft bill based on the outgoing government's version is to be advanced, per Times of Israel's 20 November liveblog, but Liberman says no outline exists and any law needs the Chief of Staff's approval.

Haredi protesters clashed with police in Beit Shemesh overnight 19 to 20 November after a false rumour of a draft-evader arrest. Police used stun grenades and one officer was lightly hurt; Eisenkot condemned it.

Ultra-Orthodox men also blocked a road near Petah Tikva over yeshiva-student imprisonments.

Background: Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir raised "10 red flags" on manpower, and the 2026 budget passed without a draft law, adding over NIS 1bn for Haredi institutions.

Katz says the IDF is ready to seize more territory if fronts erupt.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Shin Bet chief David Zini faces criticism over the election-day probe and reportedly sought wiretaps on five Arab MKs; the AG reportedly refused (unconfirmed). A Jerusalem Post opinion piece questions his loyalty; Shin Bet and the election chief say no evidence changed results.

Shin Bet is reportedly probing suspected Qatari and Turkish funding to boost Arab turnout. A government-leaning framing; results so far show no effect on the outcome.

Mossad chief Roman Gofman ordered a fresh internal probe of the agency's October 7 and wartime conduct (June report), and the Mossad chief was reportedly sparring with aides over a PM-backed Iran strike plan (October, unconfirmed).

Open-source (unverified): satellite imagery analysed by the Institute for Science and International Security shows renewed activity at Minzadehei, an Iranian site Israel struck in March. The IAEA reportedly lacks access at Pickaxe Mountain and Isfahan.

Open-source (unverified, single outlet): Misbar reports new excavation and tunnel work about 13 km southeast of Dimona.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Trump is weighing new Iran action but says he will decide after the start of 2027; a US official close to him reportedly saw Netanyahu's push as politically motivated.

The ICJ found Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya on 19 November; experts say it bears on South Africa's case against Israel, and fears grow that Washington will target the ICJ.

ICC arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant stand, and an appeal on jurisdiction is pending. No new step found this week.

Turkey's Fidan said on 17 November that the Turkey-Saudi-Pakistan defence pact will invite others once institutional arrangements are set (FDD brief). Egypt reached an IMF staff-level deal for about 2.3 billion dollars.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The Bank of Israel decides on rates on Monday 23 November, with the rate at 3.25%. October inflation fell to 1.1%, the lowest since April 2021, strengthening the case for a cut, though an earlier market showed a hold favoured.

The dollar traded at about 3.237 shekels on 18 November, roughly 4% above its 10 November level. The shekel hit a 2026 low.

Tel Aviv stocks fell up to 3.5% as election results came in on 27 to 28 October.

Weekly market summaries show recovery after the election (single newsletter).

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Arab parties Ra'am and the Joint List recommended Eisenkot, but some potential partners refuse to depend on them. This is the central tension in coalition talks.

The arrests of Ra'am activists on election morning remain contested. Opposition and Arab leaders see political targeting; Likud claims vote-buying.

Mosque arson in Dahi with pro-settler graffiti is the latest such attack inside Israel.

Haredi unrest over the draft continues in Beit Shemesh and near Petah Tikva.

What to watch.

Further Knesset readings of the judicial and media repeal bills, and any Haredi draft bill.

End of November: the Board of Peace presents to the UN Security Council on Gaza.

Iran: any US-Israeli decision; Trump signals January, Netanyahu wants sooner.

AG court appearance in the Netanyahu trial (date unconfirmed) and any pardon ruling.
