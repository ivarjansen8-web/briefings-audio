Here is your Israel briefing for Friday, 13 November.

Eisenkot gets coalition mandate as caretaker Netanyahu readies Iran strike; Nasser Hospital hit.

Compiled from sources dated 10 to 13 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Eisenkot has a 28-day mandate to form a government, and Netanyahu's caretaker cabinet is meanwhile preparing for a possible new strike on Iran. President Herzog tasked Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot after 67 MKs endorsed him against 46 for Netanyahu, so any war decision now collides with the coalition question.

Defense Minister Katz said on 12 November that the caretaker government needs no approval "from the opposition or any legal authority" to order strikes. He was answering Eisenkot's 11 November letter urging restraint until a new government is sworn in.

An Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis early on 12 November killed six people, including a surgeon, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. The IDF says it targeted a Hamas brigade commander and offered no public evidence; Hamas denies using the hospital.

The judges in Netanyahu's corruption trial ordered Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara to appear in court, over her objection. It is an unusual step that sharpens the government-AG conflict during the transition.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu remains interim prime minister and is pushing a national unity government that includes Likud. The blocs' arithmetic makes that an uphill route while the president's mandate sits with Eisenkot.

Avigdor Liberman warned that a caretaker government is not authorised to take decisions that could drag Israel into war with Iran. Liberman is a key Eisenkot-bloc partner, so his stance signals the bloc will contest security decisions publicly.

Eisenkot initially complained he was being denied security briefings and asked the attorney general to intervene; a joint statement later said he would receive one. Bloomberg reports he fears strikes on Iran could be launched on false pretenses (Bloomberg characterisation of his concerns).

Katz's office says Netanyahu, Katz and the chief of staff "see eye to eye" on Iran, while Channel 13 reports the IDF told Eisenkot there is no immediate urgency to strike (unconfirmed, single-source). The two accounts point to a gap between political and military framing.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The 27 October election gave Arab parties pivotal weight: the Joint List holds 11 seats and Ra'am 8, with UTJ at 7 and Shas at 6. The central election committee certified the results on 4 November after finding no evidence of fraud.

Ra'am and Joint List leaders are reportedly in regular contact with UTJ chairman Yaakov Asher while negotiating with Eisenkot's bloc (Kan, reported by JPost). Mansour Abbas reportedly frames it as making UTJ a "kingmaker" on a lenient draft arrangement.

Backlash against any Arab-party role is growing: Yashar MK Elyasaf Peretz's mother said he would resign if Ra'am or the Joint List joined, and a rabbinic din rodef ruling drew a distancing statement from Chabad. The episode shows how fraught the arithmetic is inside the winning camp.

Eisenkot and Liberman reportedly agreed on a Knesset speaker candidate and green-lit outreach to Haredi parties. Whether the bloc can reach 61 without either Arab or Haredi parties remains open.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The Jerusalem District Court ordered the attorney general to attend a special hearing on how the Netanyahu trial proceeds; the prosecution said she would not come. The date is unclear in reports (roughly the week of 16 to 20 November).

Netanyahu's lawyer Amit Hadad argued she should be summoned even if unwilling. The dispute grew out of testimony by former AG Avichai Mandelblit on investigative approvals.

Eisenkot has asked the attorney general to guarantee his access to security briefings. It makes her a referee between caretaker prerogatives and the incoming coalition.

5. Gaza.

The Nasser Hospital strike hit the surgical building, killing six including a surgeon and a 13-year-old, with 27 wounded per Hamas-run Health Ministry figures. The hospital says the facility is out of service; the IDF names the target as a Khan Younis brigade commander but sources differ on his name and fate.

Hamas called the strike a war crime and blamed the Board of Peace; the Board urged both sides to keep to the plan. The Board's roadmap links Hamas disarmament to Israeli withdrawal, and no stabilisation force has been reported deployed.

Heavy rain since 6 November has flooded displacement sites across Gaza, and the UN relief chief urged immediate entry of winter supplies. OCHA also reported gunfire damaging tents at a Rafah displacement site on 4 November.

Katz said no legal approval is needed for assassinations in Gaza. That signals targeted killings continue under the caretaker government despite the ceasefire framework.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians, one seriously, in Atara north of Ramallah on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA as cited by Haaretz. The Palestinian Red Crescent separately reported a 67-year-old beaten.

IDF and Border Police dismantled four outposts on 11 November, and IDF sources say removals trigger violent settler pushback. Two security vehicles were damaged, the IDF said.

Palestinian officials say Israeli forces killed a 14-year-old in Burqa; the IDF says troops identified suspects about to throw stones at a highway. WAFA adds that soldiers detained the boy's father and delayed an ambulance (Palestinian account, unconfirmed).

The UN counted over 2,060 settler attacks causing casualties or damage from January to 12 October, and ACLED calls 2026 the most violent year it has recorded.

The PA legislative election first set for 28 November appears to have been postponed or cancelled (unconfirmed: Washington Institute and MECO, with differing accounts). Hamas was to be barred from running.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Israeli strikes and shelling hit southern Lebanese towns on 12 November, per Lebanon's state news agency. Lebanon's Health Ministry counts 4,390 dead and 12,429 wounded since 2 March, as of 5 November.

Hezbollah's Naim Qassem said the group will not disarm while Israel occupies parts of the south. He also welcomed US midterm setbacks for Republicans.

A Lebanese official says no more Israel-Lebanon talks are expected until Israel has a new government, and the Tampa round produced little (single-source).

Kan reports the security cabinet told the IDF to raise readiness for a possible new attack on Iran, while Katz says the mission is "not complete". Trump says the US "doesn't need a deal" with Iran; Iran says it will not release enriched material.

The Houthis claim drone strikes on a Saudi pumping station in Yanbu and the East-West pipeline; Saudi forces say they downed three cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh. The Houthi focus has shifted toward Saudi Arabia.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Haredi protesters blocked roads near Beit Shemesh and the Jerusalem light rail after the military detained yeshiva students who had not enlisted. The arrests are a bargaining chip in coalition talks.

Eisenkot says his bloc will "not surrender" on universal service; reported Haredi asks include a one-year (possibly 18-month) transition with no sanctions or arrests (unconfirmed). Haaretz judges a broad exemption unlikely to survive the High Court.

Israel has no 2027 budget; the 1 November submission deadline passed and the Bank of Israel assumes an interim budget. Defence spending for 2027 is undetermined and was NIS 144bn in 2026 after wartime top-ups.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

An IDF soldier was indicted in late October for allegedly spying for Iran for cryptocurrency; his military-court detention was extended to 12 November. Shin Bet said it thwarted about 120 Iranian espionage incidents in 2025.

Earlier reporting says the Mossad and Shin Bet chiefs secretly visited the UAE during the Iran war; I found no confirmation of current Mossad leadership (unconfirmed).

Open-source: a BBC investigation of satellite imagery found construction at two major missile sites and 12 other Iranian bases (reported 6 November). Analysts at MAIAR tie it to Garmdareh and Shahroud; labelled open-source and unverified.

Open-source: the IRGC unveiled an underground "missile city" on 10 November; location undisclosed and the claim is Iranian state messaging.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

The European Commission will review a citizens' initiative with over 1.3 million signatures seeking suspension of the EU-Israel Association Agreement (single-source via a Palestinian outlet). Acceptance obliges no action.

Saudi Arabia is pressing the EU to designate the Houthis a terrorist group; Riyadh's normalisation with Israel is reported off the table under Netanyahu.

Turkey excluded Israel from the COP31 climate summit in Antalya (9 to 20 November), citing Gaza.

Syria and Saudi Arabia signed a defence cooperation agreement on 4 November. US-mediated Israel-Syria de-escalation talks were held in Jordan in August.

ICC/ICJ: Quiet, nothing new in the last 72 hours. The ICJ genocide case remains pending.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel fell about 2.2% against the dollar in the week to 6 November, to roughly 3.185 per dollar. Analysts cite rate gaps, delayed US cuts and Iran tensions.

The Bank of Israel rate is 3.25%, with its next decision on 23 November.

The TA-125 dropped 2.06% to about 4,059 on one session in early November (Substack summary, unconfirmed).

Without a 2027 budget, Calcalist estimates a possible NIS 80bn gap between funds and commitments under a continuing budget.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Organised crime in Arab communities is a core demand of both Ra'am and the Joint List in coalition talks, with housing and demolition halts also on the table.

Haredi leaders are fielding overtures from several sides; UTJ says it will not join any coalition without its conscription and funding demands, and Shas denies it will join an Eisenkot government.

Netanyahu has warned against an "Arab-Jewish coalition", framing the choice as a threat to Jewish identity of the state (his campaign framing).
