Here is your Israel briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Herzog hands Eisenkot the mandate over Netanyahu as Iran strike plans and Gaza stall loom.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

President Herzog formally gave Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form Israel's next government on 8 November, passing over Benjamin Netanyahu. Eisenkot had 67 MK recommendations against 46 for Netanyahu, but 19 of the 67 are Arab-party MKs he has said he will not sit with, so the arithmetic remains hard.

The 27 October election left both camps short of 61: Eisenkot's four-party alliance won 48 seats, Netanyahu's right-wing/religious bloc 53, and the Arab parties 19. Likud is the largest single party with 27.

Trump reportedly pressed Netanyahu in a post-election call to move on the stalled Gaza peace plan (unconfirmed, Axios via a secondary report). Rubio, Witkoff and Kushner were said to be on the call.

US-Iran talks via Qatar and Pakistan continue while the US and Israel have reportedly drawn up plans to resume strikes. The Hormuz closure is feeding oil prices and the Israeli markets.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu released a video proposing a unity government but said he would not abandon his bloc, and warned Eisenkot would rely on Arab parties he called supporters of terrorism. Eisenkot's Yashar called the speech "hysterical" and said it would join only if Netanyahu cut ties with "extremists".

Eisenkot vowed an "emergency government" using "unconventional steps" and signalled Likud and "its friends" could join, though the offer did not name Netanyahu. Al Jazeera framed him as open to Netanyahu joining; the Times of Israel reads it more narrowly, so the wording is contested.

Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit, Smotrich's Religious Zionists and Zehut recommended Netanyahu, while UTJ declined to recommend anyone. UTJ's abstention helps Eisenkot, and Degel HaTorah has broken with Netanyahu over the failed draft-exemption law.

Netanyahu remains in office while Eisenkot negotiates; if Eisenkot fails, Herzog may give Netanyahu or another MK a chance, and failing that Israel faces elections in early spring. Netanyahu's camp keeps claiming the vote was "stolen", a claim the results do not support.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Eisenkot has 28 days to form a government, extendable by 14 (Al Jazeera and PBS cite six weeks; Axios says 28 days). The 67 recommendations are not a governing coalition, because they include 19 Arab-party seats.

Avigdor Liberman (Yisrael Beytenu) wants a 61-seat government with the Haredi parties or new elections, and is reportedly talking separately to UTJ and Shas. He said flexibility on the draft does not mean his red lines are erased and denied sabotaging Eisenkot.

Haaretz's exit-poll coverage said a majority of voters backed the opposition while Likud emerged largest. Pre-election polls had Eisenkot's bloc ahead at 52 to 56, so the result was tighter for him than forecast.

Eisenkot and Liberman were expected to meet on Monday 9 November.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Netanyahu's corruption trial continues with defence witnesses, and the judges are weighing bringing AG Gali Baharav-Miara into a closed-door discussion over how investigations were authorised. This followed defence challenges during ex-AG Mandelblit's testimony, and no decision has been made.

The court ruled 2 to 1 in October against widening defence questioning of Mandelblit into investigative steps. Netanyahu finished his own testimony in June after 98 hearings.

Netanyahu's pardon request to Herzog is stalled: the AG says she is open to plea talks only without preconditions and with an admission of guilt. Heritage Minister Amichay Eliyahu demanded she hand over pardon materials. This is background from May-June, not new.

Shin Bet chief David Zini is in a dispute with the AG and the election committee over a probe of alleged foreign election funding (see section 9).

5. Gaza.

Gaza's Health Ministry says at least 1,610 Palestinians have been killed and 5,819 injured by Israeli fire since the ceasefire began on 10 October 2025; five more were wounded on 8 November. Figures are the ministry's, via Anadolu, and are not independently verified.

Israel closed Rafah to travellers, aid and commercial trucks on 3 November, citing smuggling and Hamas interference. Kerem Shalom is unaffected; Gaza's government media office (Hamas-run) says only about 33% of agreed trucks have entered since the ceasefire.

Phase two is stalled: Hamas agreed a disarmament document in July but says it will not proceed while Israel keeps striking and has not withdrawn. No International Stabilization Force has deployed, though Channel 12 reported preparations in August.

The Board of Peace has spent months on an implementation plan for a technocratic Palestinian government and the ISF, refined with Israel's government and military.

All hostages, including the last remains (Ran Gvili), were returned by late January 2026.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Security forces detained 18 settlers who attacked troops during the evacuation of the illegal outpost Givat Ora Yisrael near Rehelim, around 2 November. The IDF said dozens of settlers took part in the violence.

Palestinian outlets report IDF raids and arrests overnight into 8 November (unconfirmed; Hamas-aligned PIC).

TRT World reports an IDF raid that wounded a Palestinian child and settler attacks on olive harvesters near Jenin (unconfirmed; dates unclear). Olive-season settler violence is a recurring pattern.

Annexation: no new November announcement found. Smotrich's 2025 plan to take control of about 82% of the West Bank remains the reference point, and Crisis Group describes creeping sovereignty in practice.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Israeli strikes in Lebanon resumed on a heavier scale: a drone strike killed a motorcyclist on 4 November, the first such death since September. The IDF said it hit two suspects watching troops; Lebanon says the war has killed over 4,300.

A 5 November strike on a car near Aynata reportedly killed three girls and their grandmother (unconfirmed, single aggregator summary). On 6 November Israeli forces bombed al-Tayri in Bint Jbeil.

A US-brokered Israel-Lebanon framework signed 26 June underpins talks; Aoun says negotiation beats war, but Hezbollah rejects direct talks.

Iran: Qatari mediators were back in Tehran on 2 November, and Tehran offered to reopen Hormuz for US concessions. Araghchi says no direct talks are held; Trump says he is in no hurry to resume strikes.

Houthis: missiles aimed at Riyadh were intercepted on 8 November, with shrapnel hitting an Aramco tower; no Israel-targeted launch found this week. The IDF reportedly advised against striking the Houthis.

Syria: Sharaa said in July that a security deal was about 90% agreed before Israel pulled back; no November movement found.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Haredi winter recruitment opened on 2 November, with a 2026 to 27 target of about 4,800 recruits. Only about a quarter of the roughly 4,000 current Haredi recruits enter combat units, per Ynet analysis.

The IDF said it has not proactively enforced enlistment orders against Haredi draft dodgers lately, despite High Court orders; the AG puts evaders at over 67,000. These items are weeks old.

The IDF reportedly needs about 12,000 more soldiers, and the draft is now a coalition bargaining chip. Liberman's flexibility is a notable shift.

The defence establishment is seeking a 2027 budget starting at NIS 117 billion, and Israel could run all of 2027 on a continuing budget. That would leave a gap of about NIS 80 billion.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Shin Bet chief David Zini is reportedly refusing to pass evidence of alleged foreign election influence to police, against election committee directives. Haaretz is the source and parts of its reports are paywalled.

Channel 12 reported Zini likened the alleged flow of foreign money into the election to October 7 and the Yom Kippur War, and that three suspected funding channels are under examination (unconfirmed). The probe is reported to target Arab-party leaders, which critics call political.

Zini is pressing AG Baharav-Miara for more investigative powers, and Haaretz reports internal Shin Bet criticism.

Open-source: no notable OSINT or satellite findings surfaced for this window. The Mossad, Aman and Unit 8200 items found were Quiet.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Trump reportedly asked Netanyahu to accelerate the 20-point Gaza plan (Hamas disarmament, demilitarisation, technocratic government). Washington wants to reactivate it now that both countries' elections are over (unconfirmed, single-source).

Trump again claimed on 6 November that Israel would not exist had he not stopped Iran's nuclear weapon, and spoke with Qatar's emir, who urged restraint.

The ICC warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant stand, and Turkey issued its own warrants in November 2025. No new ICC, ICJ, EU or Egypt development turned up for this window: Quiet.

Saudi normalisation: no 2026 reporting found.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel hit a seven-month low against the dollar around 3 November and has fallen about 4.5% since the election. Globes blames political and geopolitical uncertainty and weak Wall Street.

The TA-125 closed the week to 30 October at 3,936, down 2.45%.

The Bank of Israel held its rate on 21 October, noting a 3.1% shekel fall and Brent up from about 72 dollarsto 110 dollarssince July.

Budget: a possible continuing budget through 2027 adds fiscal uncertainty.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

The Joint List (11 seats) and Ra'am (8) hit a historic high of 19 seats and both recommended Eisenkot. NPR reports the Joint List will not formally partner with him, and Netanyahu is campaigning against any reliance on them.

Ra'am ran openly for a coalition seat, with a Jewish former police official in second place.

The Shin Bet probe of Arab party leaders is a flashpoint for Arab citizens (see section 9).

Haredi society: the draft fight now shapes coalition choices, with Degel HaTorah and UTJ moving away from Netanyahu.
