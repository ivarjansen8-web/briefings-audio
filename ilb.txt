Here is your Israel briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

Eisenkot races 6 December deadline as Netanyahu eyes rotation; Israel and US weigh new Iran strike; IDF tanks re-enter Hamas-held Gaza.

Compiled from sources dated 25 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Gadi Eisenkot has until 6 December to form a government and accuses Likud of stalling talks to force new elections. His camp holds roughly 36 seats with Yisrael Beytenu and the Democrats, far short of 61, so the outcome hinges on Bennett's Beyachad (12 seats) and on whether Netanyahu blocks a deal.

Netanyahu is reportedly considering offering to serve as second prime minister in a rotation with Eisenkot. This is a Jerusalem Post report citing sources, and Eisenkot's camp is sceptical; Channel 12 reportedly says Likud wants a full Netanyahu term with no rotation (unconfirmed).

Israel and the US are reportedly weighing a new assault on Iran while Israel's military is in a heightened defensive posture. CBS cites sources; Israel Katz has alleged Iranian chemical-weapons work, which Iran denies.

IDF tanks entered Hamas-controlled central Gaza for the first time since the October 2025 ceasefire, targeting a tunnel network. Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya called continued Israeli strikes "unacceptable" and said a response is being weighed.

The Knesset advanced a bill for a state commission of inquiry into 7 October by 65 to 50 in a preliminary reading on 25 November. Netanyahu opposes it as politically biased; three more readings are needed.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister after the inconclusive October election. He said he sees no direct and immediate Iranian threat requiring action before a new government forms.

Eisenkot's Yashar signed a coalition deal with Yair Golan's Democrats on 27 November, after an earlier deal with Lieberman's Israel Beytenu. He aims to finalise bloc agreements and cabinet-post distribution by the weekend, though Beyachad has not yet signed.

Bennett warned Eisenkot not to rush into a minority government and favours a unity government. He reportedly believes a further 14-day extension is needed (Ynet, i24NEWS reporting).

Herzog can extend Eisenkot's mandate by up to 14 days, to 20 December, and then hand the task to another MK for 28 days. That raises the odds of a drawn-out process or another election.

Netanyahu met Ben-Gvir and Smotrich amid the coalition deadlock. The far-right partners are central to any Likud-led alternative.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The election left neither bloc with a majority; Eisenkot and presumed partners won about 48 seats by one report, short of 61 in the 120-seat Knesset. Netanyahu's party alleged the vote was "stolen", a claim reported by PBS.

A Jerusalem Post live blog tracks the race between Eisenkot and Netanyahu to build a government. It frames the next days as decisive for whether a government is sworn in before the deadline.

A bill on prime-ministerial term limits also advanced in a preliminary reading. It passed alongside the October. 7 inquiry bill.

No fresh post-election polls found for 25 to 27 November. Pre-election polls had Eisenkot's Yashar narrowly ahead of Likud but with neither bloc at 61.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Times of Israel reports that judges in Netanyahu's corruption trial have suggested dropping most charges, and Netanyahu demands the trial end (unconfirmed, single outlet and not independently verified here). Its analysis says a conclusion may be further away, not closer.

Netanyahu requested a presidential pardon in November 2025; Herzog declined for now and urged plea-deal talks. Speaker Ohana's later letter urging a pardon was criticised as lacking legal basis.

The High Court annulled the government's firing of Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara as unlawful in December 2025. The dispute with the government continues over police appointments and the Haaretz boycott.

No new High Court rulings on the AG found in the last 72 hours. Treat the above as background.

5. Gaza.

IDF tanks concluded a first incursion into Hamas-held central Gaza since the ceasefire; the IDF said the target was a tunnel network. This is the IDF's account.

Israel has killed three senior Hamas officials in three days, per the Times of Israel, and the US is urging Netanyahu to proceed with the ceasefire plan. CNN reports no sign Netanyahu is ready to make Gaza concessions.

The IDF said a Gaza City strike killed two Hamas operatives working as guards at a Doctors Without Borders clinic. MSF condemned the strike, saying its clinic was hit directly.

Gaza's Health Ministry reported 29 deaths for 17 to 23 November, and OCHA put post-ceasefire fatalities at 1,650 as of 18 November. OCHA also says aid operations face bureaucratic impediments and access restrictions.

Board of Peace adviser Josh Gruenbaum blames political elements in Netanyahu's government for the stalled deployment of the International Stabilisation Force. Israel has not signed the Status of Forces Agreement; Netanyahu's office says it fully backs Trump's 20-point plan.

Netanyahu reportedly told Jared Kushner he would let in more aid and back moving ahead on disarming Hamas. The Board of Peace said it has heard similar assurances before.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Palestinian media (WAFA) report settlers burned cars in several Ramallah-area villages, including seven in Kafr Malik. These are Palestinian-sourced claims.

Settlers demolished a Palestinian home in Jalud; the IDF called it "a serious criminal act involving deliberate property damage." An unusually direct public IDF rebuke of settlers.

A Palestinian man was reportedly killed by settlers east of Bethlehem (WAFA, via SANA) and another reportedly shot near Ramallah. Both are single-source Palestinian reports (unconfirmed).

WAFA figures cited by Al Jazeera count 3,133 attacks in October, 1,764 by Israeli forces and 1,369 by settlers. Haaretz reports the IDF shooting at olive harvesters as settlers attacked villages.

Arab and Islamic foreign ministers welcomed UK and Australian sanctions over settlement activity on 19 November.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF said its air force struck dozens of Hezbollah rockets and launchers at a Radwan Force training camp in eastern Lebanon on 26 November. No injuries were reported (IDF statement).

President Aoun told Hezbollah to disarm, rejected an Israeli verification role and would accept an international mechanism. Implementation of the June framework has stalled, and Macron warned Aoun on 23 November of rising escalation risk.

Iran threatened air and sea action to break the US blockade and ruled out reopening Hormuz. A drone strike on a Kuwaiti site killed two Italian contractors; the GCC blamed Iran, which denied it.

Israel is bracing for Iran-backed proxy plots, including kidnappings and car bombs, per an Israeli broadcaster citing a senior security official.

ISW/CTP-cited assessments say Iran is developing new ballistic-missile variants. A think-tank report with satellite imagery also reportedly shows a fortified underground complex between two nuclear sites (unconfirmed).

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir reportedly prefers coordinating any renewed Iran strike with Washington, while Mossad chief Gofman backed an independent strike. The split shows the security chiefs divided as a caretaker government faces a decision.

Liberman, now Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee chair, said a Haredi draft law must pass the IDF chief, the High Court and the rabbis. He added there is "no plan at the moment".

Mandatory service is set to fall to 30 months from January 2027, and the IDF says it urgently needs 12,000 recruits. Zamir has pressed for a draft law and a reserve law.

Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons and left open preemptive strikes. Iran denies this.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad chief Roman Gofman reportedly backed an independent strike on Iran, citing alleged chemical-weapons work. He was reportedly in Washington for talks with the CIA, and Netanyahu reportedly did not brief Eisenkot (Times of Israel).

Critics argue Gofman and Shin Bet chief David Zini, both outsiders, are loyal to Netanyahu; the High Court upheld both appointments 2 to 1 over the AG's objections. An op-ed urges the next government to end their tenures.

Prosecutors indicted Wissam Awad on 15 November for passing information to Iranian agents, allegedly from ideology and not money. The case came from a joint Shin Bet-police probe.

A 69-year-old Holon man was arrested on 16 November for allegedly distributing leaflets for an Iran-linked operative; a court released him under restrictions. An IDF soldier was also indicted around 29 October for allegedly photographing interceptors and US refuelling aircraft.

Open-source (unverified): a Washington think tank published Vantor satellite imagery of a fortified underground complex between two Iranian nuclear sites. Treat as OSINT analysis not independently confirmed.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

The Trump administration is pressing Netanyahu to advance the Gaza ceasefire plan, and the Board of Peace says it is "encouraged" after talks with him. UN Security Council Resolution 2803 authorises the Board and ISF to 31 December 2027.

Egypt and Jordan agreed on 24 November to build international pressure on Israel, possibly including sanctions. Jordan's foreign minister Safadi said Amman will not fall into "the trap set by Israeli extremists".

A JNS analysis says Israel's peace with Egypt and Jordan is under strain as Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Pakistan deepen defence ties. JNS is a pro-Israel outlet and this is analysis, not news.

Nineteen Arab and Islamic states welcomed UK and Australian settlement sanctions. No ICC or ICJ development found in the last 72 hours.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The Bank of Israel held its rate at 3.25% on 23 November, citing political uncertainty and a weaker shekel. The shekel fell 7.6% against the dollar between 21 October and 20 November.

Annual inflation was 1.1% in October, the lowest since April 2021, yet the bank did not cut. It expects inflation above 2% next year.

The representative rate stood at about NIS 3.343 per dollar on 23 November.

The TA-35 was about 3,965 on 18 November; no later level found.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Searches found only earlier-2026 coverage of Arab-community crime protests; 2025 saw a record 252 murders in Arab communities per the Abraham Initiatives.

What to watch.

6 December: Eisenkot's mandate to form a government expires unless Herzog grants the 14-day extension to 20 December.

Weekend of 28 to 29 November: Eisenkot aims to finalise cabinet-post deals in his bloc; watch whether Bennett's Beyachad signs.

Any Netanyahu rotation offer: a formal rotation proposal to Eisenkot would be the biggest swing in coalition talks.

Gaza: Hamas's promised response to Israeli strikes and whether Israel signs the ISF Status of Forces Agreement.

Iran: possible US-Israeli strike decision and Iranian moves against the US blockade.

Knesset: next committee steps on the October. 7 state commission bill (three readings remain).
