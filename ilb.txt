Here is your Israel briefing for Tuesday, 10 November.

Herzog taps Eisenkot over Netanyahu; Gaza floods and Board of Peace rebuke; US-Iran talks amid Qeshm strikes.

Compiled from sources dated 6 to 10 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

President Herzog has tasked Gadi Eisenkot, not Benjamin Netanyahu, with forming the next government. Eisenkot received 67 recommendations to Netanyahu's 46, but his bloc holds only 48 of 120 seats, so a minority government resting on outside Arab-party votes is the likeliest route.

Netanyahu renewed his call for a unity government without Arab parties on 9 November. He calls those parties "Hamas supporters", while Eisenkot's bloc rejects serving under him; the framing is Likud's own.

Gaza storms flooded tens of thousands of tents and the US-backed Board of Peace publicly criticised both Israel and Hamas. Israel says it delivered 91,000 tents; Hamas blames Israel.

US "defensive" strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island continue while Qatar-mediated talks hang in the balance. Trump says renewed major strikes are unlikely if talks progress, and Defence Minister Katz says "the mission is not yet complete".

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu leads a caretaker government with limited powers until a coalition is sworn in. This constrains major appointments and policy shifts during the transition.

Netanyahu-aligned sources floated a unity plan with Eisenkot as rotating prime minister, excluding Haredi parties (unconfirmed). The change bloc categorically rejects Eisenkot joining a Netanyahu-led government.

Likud accused Arab parties of "stealing" the election amid record Arab turnout. Opposition figures see the push as an attempt to undermine the result.

Defence Minister Israel Katz said Iran-related "mission is not yet complete" at a memorial ceremony. It signals the outgoing government's hawkish line during coalition talks.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Eisenkot has 28 days to form a government, extendable by up to 14. His bloc has 48 seats versus 53 for Netanyahu's, leaving both short of 61.

Eisenkot's bloc agreed on a Knesset Speaker candidate from his Yashar party on 9 November, after meeting Avigdor Lieberman. The speakership is the first test of whether the bloc holds.

Eisenkot reportedly signalled readiness for a minority government, and all Arab-majority parties recommended him to the president. Haaretz's Anshel Pfeffer describes a plan to form quickly and recruit partners later; Eisenkot has ruled out Arab parties in cabinet.

The Israel Democracy Institute's Assaf Shapira warns a 48-seat government would be exceptionally fragile. IDI's Yohanan Plesner flags splits over the draft law and the Arab parties' role.

Haredi parties' hopes of avoiding the draft under the anti-Netanyahu bloc are described as unfounded. The draft is the decisive issue in any Haredi-inclusive coalition.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said she knows of no evidence "even close" to affecting the election result. She was responding to the Shin Bet foreign-interference probe, which Central Elections Committee chair Justice Noam Sohlberg ordered.

She called the Police Investigation Department law a "poison capsule" for law enforcement on 9 November. The High Court has an interim order suspending provisions on appointing the PID director.

Netanyahu trial judges are weighing summoning the Attorney General over how investigations were approved. No formal decision has been issued.

The High Court froze the broadcasting law pending answers on legislative defects. It adds to the pattern of court-government confrontation.

Netanyahu's pardon request to Herzog remains unresolved, with no deadline. Trump has publicly attacked Herzog for not granting it, and the Justice Ministry's pardons department called it highly problematic.

5. Gaza.

Storms flooded tens of thousands of tents; Hamas claims about 70,000 and Gaza authorities report at least five dead. Civil Defense said it answered 218 of 2,166 distress calls; these are Hamas-run bodies' figures.

The IDF/COGAT says 91,000+ tents and 8,200+ trucks of winter supplies have entered Gaza. The Board of Peace says Israeli steps are "insufficient" and aid is stuck at the Egyptian border.

Israel says no reconstruction begins until Hamas disarms, and has barred some equipment. The Board of Peace also faulted Hamas's refusal to disarm, so phase two of the Trump plan remains stalled.

Nearly 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, per Al Jazeera, citing Gaza figures. Israel describes its strikes as responses to ceasefire violations.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

OCHA counted 377 olive-harvest-related settler attacks between 1 September and 2 November, its highest on record. European diplomats were attacked during raids.

The IDF says it arrested 18 settlers during an illegal outpost evacuation, after which Palestinians torched an Israeli vehicle. Unusual enforcement action, taken against a backdrop of settler hostility to it.

Settlers reportedly torched equipment at an industrial zone in Beita on 9 November (unconfirmed). The account is a single Substack relaying WAFA and other field reports.

WAFA, via Middle East Eye, reports three Palestinians beaten near Salfit and demolition notices near al-Eizariya on 8 November. Hundreds of settlers also entered Hebron's Old City.

Quiet on the Palestinian Authority: no fresh reporting found. Earlier reporting put withheld clearance revenues at about 4.4 dollarsbillion.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Israel is reportedly pressing Lebanon, via US mediators, to stop Hezbollah joining a renewed Iran war. It asked Beirut to set up roadblocks where Hezbollah operatives live.

Israeli aircraft reportedly hit at least five southern Lebanese villages on 9 November, injuring residents, according to local authorities. Katz warned Hezbollah of "extremely severe blows" if it joins an Iranian attack.

Reuters-sourced Lebanese security officials say Hezbollah spent three months rearming with Iranian cash. A June agreement ended main fighting but clashes continue.

Iran: US struck Qeshm again on 6 to 8 November and Tehran threatens to "rain down hell" if the US escalates. Reuters says Iran's proposal trades Hormuz reopening for an end to bombing and sanctions relief, with nuclear talks later.

Yemen: the Houthis are fighting Saudi Arabia, and Israel's military reportedly advised against entering that conflict. Coalition forces intercepted missiles aimed at Riyadh on 9 to 10 November, and Syria's troop role is disputed.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Haredi draft: Quiet on legislation, but Haaretz says the draft is the crux of coalition talks. Earlier, Bank of Israel argued the draft bill could not ease the reserve burden.

Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir declared the October 7 probes concluded (about five weeks ago) and called 2027 the year for whole-war lessons. He had urged an external state inquiry, straining ties with Katz.

Katz had held up senior appointments, citing alleged involvement in October 7 failures. A caretaker government may complicate any resolution.

Quiet: no new procurement or budget news found in the last 72 hours.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Haaretz reports the Shin Bet has not produced evidence for chief David Zini's foreign-interference claims. Officials say investigators found hundreds of thousands of shekels from the PA but no sign of vote-buying.

Zini sought to question Arab leaders including Ahmed Tibi, Mansour Abbas, Sami Abu Shehadeh and Ayman Odeh. The Attorney General reportedly agreed there was reasonable suspicion to question Odeh as a suspect.

The Mossad is assisting the probe, with focus on Qatar and possibly Turkey; Qatar categorically denies interference. The Shin Bet chief reportedly compared the alleged interference to October 7 (single-outlet, unconfirmed).

Open-source: no notable OSINT findings located in this window. Quiet.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

US: a Republican congressman, Thomas Massie, filed eight impeachment articles against Defence Secretary Hegseth over the Iran war. Republicans blame the war for midterm losses; Trump backs Hegseth.

Trump reportedly blocked an Israeli multi-day Iran offensive earlier this month, per Time and PBS-cited reporting. Washington's leash on Israeli action matters for the transition period.

Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Pakistan and Turkey criticised Israel and urged a two-state solution in a late-October Istanbul meeting. Analysts see a Saudi-led axis forming outside the UAE.

ICJ/ICC: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Colombia earlier withdrew from the South Africa v. Israel intervention.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The TA-125 closed at 3,898.91 on 9 November, up 1.97%, after a 2.86% fall the prior Friday to 3,823.63. Tel Aviv Stock Exchange data.

The shekel fell about 2.2% in the week to 6 November, with the dollar at 3.1853 on Friday. Ynet reported a 4.5% weekly drop in a post-election week, the weakest since March.

The Bank of Israel policy rate remains 3.25%. Political uncertainty is the main driver of the shekel.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Eleven people were arrested in a mass brawl in Lod on 8 November after a far-right "sovereignty march" through an Arab neighbourhood. Marchers, supporters of Ben Gvir, said they were showing Arabs "who's the landlord".

Police summoned both organisers, one barred from Lod for 15 days; Mayor Yair Revivo condemned "extremist" outsiders. Haaretz says he contacted the Shin Bet chief.

Demonstrations urged Eisenkot's bloc to cooperate with Arab parties, and +972 describes the election as a moment of Palestinian unity. The debate over Arab parties' role is central to coalition-building.
