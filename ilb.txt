Here is your Israel briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

Tanks cross Gaza's Yellow Line, Knesset majority moves to repeal Netanyahu's overhauls, Iran strike push.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 19 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Israeli tanks crossed the Yellow Line into Hamas-held central Gaza near Nuseirat on 18 November, the first ground operation past the ceasefire boundary since the October 2025 truce. The IDF said it was removing a tunnel threat to troops, while Hamas called it a "flagrant ceasefire violation"; IDF sources told the Jerusalem Post there is no plan for an expansive invasion.

The new Knesset majority elected Yashar's Matan Kahana speaker 66 to 52 on 17 November, after a six-hour filibuster by the Netanyahu bloc, then advanced repeal of the judicial and media overhauls. It is the first concrete sign that the Eisenkot-led bloc controls the floor while Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister.

Netanyahu is reportedly pressing Washington for a four- to seven-day air campaign on Iran, while the US and Iran trade strikes around the Gulf. The NYT, relayed by the Times of Israel, says Trump is weighing another extensive round; a senior Israeli official disputed that Netanyahu pressured Trump (reports rely on anonymous sources).

Channel 12 reported that Netanyahu's office was behind a complaint that led to the arrest of three Ra'am activists on election morning; the PMO denies it. The opposition calls it an attempted "coup", and the affair now involves the Attorney General and the police.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council that both Israel and Hamas are failing their ceasefire commitments, warning a truce that "delivers nothing visible" cannot last.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu and Naftali Bennett met for about two hours in the Knesset on 18 November amid talk of a unity government; Eisenkot's office said it had not been told. Netanyahu insists on staying prime minister in any unity deal, which Eisenkot rejects.

Herzog gave Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government on 8 November, starting a 28-day clock that runs to about 6 December. Eisenkot talks of an "emergency" coalition and a possible minority government.

Eisenkot's arithmetic is hard: his bloc has 48 seats, and 67 only if the 19 Arab-party MKs are counted. He has ruled out Arab-party participation, and Avigdor Liberman says he would leave a coalition that depends on them, so Haredi parties or Likud become the alternatives.

Opposition figures demand the removal of Shin Bet chief David Zini and Mossad chief Roman Gofman over the alleged election interference and the Iran lobbying (unconfirmed, KAN via TRT World).

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The Knesset gave preliminary approval on 18 November to bills repealing the judicial-selection overhaul and the law weakening the Attorney General, 63 to 47 per the Times of Israel. Reports differ on which bill carried which tally, and each needs three more readings.

A preliminary vote of 63 to 49 backed repealing the media overhaul law; Communications Minister Shlomo Karhi opposes it.

Official results from the 27 October election: Likud 27 seats, Yashar 21, the pro-Netanyahu bloc 53, the anti-Netanyahu bloc 48 and the Arab parties 19. The Knesset was sworn in on 10 November.

No new polls were found dated within 72 hours. Quiet on polling.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara ordered police to suspend an internal review of Election Day arrests, and said she would hold a discussion on information passed from the PMO to police. Israel Hayom framed it as blocking the probe; critics of the AG will read it as overreach, and her office says it is examining whether intelligence was planted.

Channel 12 reported that Shin Bet chief Zini sought to wiretap five Arab lawmakers on Election Day and the AG refused (unconfirmed, single-source).

The Netanyahu trial is in its late stages: the AG is due to appear before the panel on 25 November, and prosecutors were cleared to cross-examine former AG Avichai Mandelblit.

Netanyahu's pardon request is still pending with Herzog, who is reportedly seeking a plea-style settlement first. Knesset Speaker Ohana (until his replacement) urged a pardon.

5. Gaza.

The Gaza crossing on 18 November was the first IDF ground push into Hamas-controlled territory since October 2025; residents near Nuseirat were told to evacuate. The IDF also said it killed two Hamas operatives it said were rebuilding capabilities; it said it was unaware of an incident in Deir al-Balah where residents reported deaths.

Gaza's Health Ministry counts 1,602 killed since the 11 October 2025 ceasefire and 74,618 since October 2023 (figures as of early November); the IDF says five Israeli soldiers have died in Gaza since the truce.

The Board of Peace says the delays leave the planned new Palestinian administration and the international stabilisation force stuck.

The Health Ministry said on 17 November that Israel is limiting the number of patients allowed out for treatment.

Hostages: no new developments found. Remains of some deceased hostages were reported still in Gaza in earlier coverage; no figure was verified this week.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers killed a 72-year-old Palestinian woman, Fadda Fheidat, and wounded 10 people in an attack in the central West Bank on 17 November, per WAFA and the Palestinian Health Ministry. Al Jazeera calls it a "pogrom"; no IDF account was found.

OCHA recorded 52 settler attacks with casualties or damage across 38 communities from 3 to 9 November, against a weekly average of 53 in 2026. ACLED says 2026 is on track to be the worst year for settler violence it has recorded.

IDF sources say settler violence spikes when outposts are removed, and link it to the post-election political climate.

Palestinian Authority: Abbas postponed the 28 November legislative elections to 11 September 2027, citing "Israeli measures".

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The US and Iran are trading strikes: US jets reportedly hit an IRGC missile site on Qeshm Island, and Iran claimed drone attacks on Ali al-Salem base in Kuwait, which Kuwait said it intercepted. Bahrain also reported intercepting Iranian attacks; CENTCOM says no US personnel were hurt.

Israel struck a Hezbollah-bound arms shipment in eastern Lebanon on 16 November, a rare deep strike after restraint at US request. Hezbollah acknowledged the explosives were its own; an air strike was also reported near Qantara on 18 November.

A Saudi-led coalition struck Houthi sites on 18 November; the Houthis say 17 were killed and fired missiles toward Riyadh. No Houthi strike on Israel was found this week.

Syria: Israeli fire was reported in Quneitra on 6 November; the security-deal talks remain frozen.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The Washington Post reports Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir resisted Netanyahu's push for new Iran strikes before the 27 October election, fearing it would disrupt the vote (anonymous sources).

The IDF is reported short about 12,000 soldiers, 7,500 of them in combat roles, which keeps the Haredi draft at the centre of coalition talks.

The Bank of Israel earlier judged the draft bill too weak on penalties to ease the reserve burden, and officials doubt it would survive the High Court.

Procurement and budget: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad chief Roman Gofman reportedly urged renewed joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran in Washington last week; Eisenkot says he was not told (single-source, anonymous).

Critics say the Mossad and Shin Bet heads, Gofman and Zini, are Netanyahu loyalists appointed from outside their agencies. Critics also say the election-day affair shows politicisation.

Open-source, unverified: BBC Verify, using October satellite imagery, reported new tunnel entrances and excavation at IRGC missile bases at Garmdareh and Shahroud.

Open-source: CTP-ISW sees no sign Iran has successfully re-mined the Strait of Hormuz and treats IRGC mine claims as information operations.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Gulf leaders reportedly asked Trump not to attack Iran, fearing retaliation, and Saudi Arabia leads the push for diplomacy. Israel and Arab states are competing for Trump's ear before an Iran decision.

Trump has sent mixed signals, saying new strikes are unlikely while calling the military "locked and loaded".

A senior US commander was reported visiting Israel as the Gaza operation began (unconfirmed, state-linked outlet).

ICC/ICJ and the EU: nothing new found in the last 72 hours. Quiet on those files.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel is at its weakest against the dollar in nearly a year, per Globes, around 3.25 per dollar. Trading Economics shows a drop of about 4.9% over a month, though its page metadata looks stale.

Israel's five largest banks earned NIS 8.1 billion in Q3, down about 6%, on lower rates and the surtax on excess profits.

The Bank of Israel policy rate stands at 3.25%.

No TA-35 close was found for 18 November.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of a "racist" campaign against Arab citizens, who won a record 19 seats; Netanyahu has alleged fraud by Arab parties, which election authorities rejected.

Arab party leaders asked the AG to investigate the PMO's alleged role in the Ra'am activist arrests.

Haredi draft politics remain unresolved and will shape any Eisenkot coalition.

What to watch.

Through 6 December: Eisenkot's 28-day mandate to form a government expires; watch for a Likud or Haredi deal, or a request for an extension.

Coming days: the repeal bills on judicial selection, the AG's powers and the media law move to committee and further readings.

25 November: Attorney General Baharav-Miara is due before the Netanyahu trial panel.

Any day: a Trump decision on renewed Iran strikes, and further Iran and Gulf exchanges.

Gaza: whether the IDF operation near Nuseirat widens, and the Board of Peace follow-up at the UN Security Council.

Pending: Herzog's decision on Netanyahu's pardon request; the AG's discussion on PMO-police information transfer.
