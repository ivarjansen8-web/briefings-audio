Here is your Israel briefing for Monday, 9 November.

Herzog tasks Eisenkot with forming government; Shin Bet-AG rift over election probe; Katz: Iran 'mission not complete'

Compiled from sources dated 6 to 9 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

President Herzog tasked ex-IDF chief Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar) with forming the next government on 8 November, passing over Netanyahu. Eisenkot had 67 recommendations to Netanyahu's 46 after the 27 October election and has 28 days, extendable by 14.

Netanyahu answered with a video calling for a "national unity government" under his leadership and warning against a government "with terrorist supporters". This is a reference to the Arab parties that backed Eisenkot; Eisenkot called the video hysterical.

Shin Bet chief David Zini and Attorney General Baharav-Miara are in an open rift over a foreign-funding "election interference" probe targeting Arab party leaders. The AG found no reasonable suspicion to open criminal cases against several of them; critics say the agency is being politicised.

Defence Minister Katz said on 8 November that the "mission is still not complete" on Iran, while US-Iran nuclear exchanges continue. He also ruled out withdrawals from Gaza, Lebanon and Syria.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister while Eisenkot builds a coalition. If Eisenkot fails, Israel could face another election.

Eisenkot says he wants a "national emergency government" built on his 48-seat bloc and is open to Netanyahu's allies, including Smotrich, joining. The Arab parties (19 seats) endorsed him but, per the Joint List MK quoted by Haaretz, are not demanding cabinet seats.

United Torah Judaism declined to endorse Eisenkot but signalled it could join an Eisenkot-led coalition. This is the main crack in Netanyahu's bloc.

Likud attacked the elections chief for saying the results were valid despite the interference probe. The committee ratified the results regardless.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The 27 October election gave the Eisenkot bloc 48 seats and the Arab parties 19. Netanyahu's bloc fell short of 61 even with Likud as the largest party, per the outlets above.

Pre-election averages had Yashar about two seats ahead of Likud, with neither bloc at 61. Final Maariv numbers had the change bloc at 54 and Netanyahu's at 48; these are pre-vote polls, not results.

Avigdor Liberman defended his flexibility on a Haredi draft compromise while vowing to keep core draft principles. The draft is a key bargaining chip for any coalition.

No post-election polls were found in the last 72 hours (Quiet on polling).

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The AG denied Zini's request to interrogate senior Arab lawmakers, in a rare public statement accusing him of undermining the rule of law. Haaretz reports Zini withheld evidence from police against the election committee's directives.

Two election-interference probes involving Arab-led parties are reportedly falling apart (unconfirmed, TV report). Likud's claim that the AG blocked the probe was called false by Times of Israel.

The AG told the High Court that 67,230 Haredi men are draft dodgers and asked it to weigh further sanctions. The IDF has admitted limited proactive enforcement in recent months.

Netanyahu's pardon request, filed November 2025, remains undecided; Herzog has urged plea-deal efforts first. Speaker Ohana's October appeal to Herzog was criticised as lacking a legal basis.

5. Gaza.

Gaza's Health Ministry says 1,599 Palestinians have been killed and 5,774 wounded since the 11 October 2025 ceasefire (3 November bulletin). Reuters' count was at least 1,580; the IDF says its strikes target militants.

The Health Ministry's cumulative toll stands at 74,618 killed. This is a Hamas-run ministry figure.

Trump reportedly pressed Netanyahu by phone to advance the Gaza plan now the election is over (Axios-sourced, unconfirmed). Disarmament versus Israeli withdrawal remains the core dispute; the Board of Peace has drawn up a 2.45 dollarsbillion six-month recovery programme.

Humanitarian pressure ahead of winter: UNRWA says nearly 2 million people need shelter help, and Israel says the UK is rallying states behind a statement on loosening Gaza restrictions.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers beat three Palestinians near Salfit, one with a broken jaw, and attacked olive harvesters in Sinjil on 8 November (WAFA, via Middle East Eye). OCHA counted 377 harvest-related settler attacks from 1 September to 2 November, a record.

Israel ordered 12 foreign activists detained in Kafr Ni'ma deported. Arab News cites WAFA.

IDF sources say settler violence spikes when illegal outposts are removed.

Troops opened fire on a Molotov suspect; Palestinians say he was 13.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

On 8 November Israeli reservists reported finding Hezbollah weapons caches in Mays al-Jabal, southern Lebanon (IDF claim). Israel has been at war with Hezbollah since 2 March 2026, linked to the wider Iran war.

Katz warned Hezbollah against joining Iran in any attack and ruled out withdrawing from Lebanon.

US forces struck air defences on Qeshm Island on 6 November, which Trump called "minor strikes"; Washington is reviewing Iran's 5 November nuclear proposal. Per a live-blog compilation; the Iran war began 28 February.

Yemen's government accuses the IRGC and Hezbollah of sending 21 operatives to aid the Houthis (Iran denies); the Houthis claim strikes on Saudi targets. Saudi confirmation is absent for the Dammam claim.

Syria's foreign minister said at the Manama Dialogue that Israel has carried out over 1,000 attacks on Syria and demanded withdrawal to pre-December 2024 lines. Talks have been largely frozen since February.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The IDF is seeking a 2027 budget starting around NIS 117 billion; an August report said the Finance Ministry might add NIS 20 billion (expected, not confirmed). Zamir has warned of readiness strain.

Haredi combat enlistment rose about 30% on the first day of the current cycle (about 260 recruits).

The IDF personnel chief says the army can absorb any number of Haredi recruits with advance notice.

The Supreme Court struck down the latest Haredi draft law on 3 September, per Jerusalem Post analysis.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Shin Bet chief Zini reportedly compared alleged foreign election funding to October 7 and the Yom Kippur War, and says it may involve thousands of people. Reports are secondhand.

Haaretz reports Zini removed dissenting officials and installed an ally to lead the probe (paywalled; unconfirmed).

Former Shin Bet chief Ami Ayalon warned the probe risks eroding public trust; other former senior officials said they were "appalled". Ben-Gvir urged arresting all Arab party leaders.

Mossad, Aman and open-source findings: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

A Saudi source told Kan on 6 November that normalisation is "not on the agenda" while Netanyahu's government is in power. Riyadh also demands an "irreversible pathway" to Palestinian statehood.

On 25 October Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Egypt and Pakistan called Israel "the fundamental source of instability" in the region. Trump has urged these states to join the Abraham Accords.

Trump again claimed Israel "wouldn't exist" had he not stopped Iran's nuclear weapon.

ICC: the Netanyahu warrant stands; a Turkish court's in-absentia case adjourned to 30 March 2027, and a French candidate pledged to enforce the warrant. A Smotrich warrant request is unconfirmed.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel weakened about 2.2% against the dollar last week, with Globes citing 3.154 per dollar, the weakest since late March (secondary report).

The Bank of Israel held its rate at 3.25% on 21 October; the next decision is 23 November. Some analysts say further weakness could pause cuts.

The TA-35 closed 30 October at 4,058.95, down 1.58% on the week; no 6 November figure found.

The budget dispute risks leaving Israel without an approved 2027 state budget until summer, per Ynet.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Eleven people were arrested in a mass brawl in Lod on 8 November after a far-right "sovereignty march" through an Arab neighbourhood. Marchers, supporters of Ben-Gvir, cited the Arab parties' 19 seats; police denied reports of a stabbing.

Lod's mayor blamed outside extremists and local yeshiva teenagers for the escalation.

Netanyahu's framing of an Arab-backed government as a threat to Israel's survival is shaping the climate. Times of Israel commentary says the warnings serve his bid to stay in power.

What to watch.

Eisenkot's coalition clock: 28 days from 8 November (about 6 December), extendable by 14; watch UTJ, Liberman and Haredi draft terms.

23 November: Bank of Israel rate decision, with the shekel at multi-month lows.

Trump-Netanyahu Gaza plan: any announced timetable for Hamas disarmament, the technocratic administration and the stabilisation force.

AG versus Shin Bet: whether the joint police/State Attorney task force receives Zini's evidence, and whether any Arab MK is questioned.

US-Iran talks: the US response to Iran's 5 November proposal and further strikes.

30 March 2027: Turkish in-absentia hearing (distant, for reference); Herzog's pardon decision remains open.
