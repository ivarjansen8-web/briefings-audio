Here is your Israel briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Eisenkot bloc seizes Knesset as Netanyahu courts Haredim and presses Trump on Iran.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Caretaker PM Netanyahu used a Saturday video to urge Gadi Eisenkot to open "unity government" talks without preconditions, including Haredi parties. Neither bloc holds a majority after the 27 October vote (Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats, Eisenkot's four-party bloc 48), and Eisenkot, who holds the mandate, has ruled out sitting under Netanyahu.

US, Iranian and Qatari negotiators reportedly fear Netanyahu could torpedo talks to end the Iran war by authorising a new strike. Axios reported on 13 November that Mossad's director was in Washington as Netanyahu presses Trump to resume fighting, while Trump has so far held back (anonymous sourcing).

The Eisenkot bloc won a 63 to 50 Knesset vote on Wednesday, its first show of parliamentary control. It plans to replace Speaker Amir Ohana with Yashar's Matan Kahana and to use committees to repeal judicial-overhaul laws, with a vote expected Monday.

The Netanyahu trial judges ordered Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara to appear on 18 November. Prosecutors said she would not attend; a judicial source told Kan this is "a very thick hint" to the prosecution.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister until a new government is sworn in. Cabinet decisions therefore carry a caretaker constraint, which the Attorney General has used in disputes over appointments and major security moves.

Netanyahu told Haredi parties they cannot trust Eisenkot's promises. He is reportedly buying time, but analysts say he lacks an alternative coalition. Coverage here is from the Jerusalem Post, which leans centre-right.

Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich urged Haredi parties to reject reported talks with Eisenkot's bloc. Shas says it still backs Netanyahu and is talking to no one.

Opposition leader Eisenkot asked the Attorney General to require Netanyahu to give him security briefings. A two-hour briefing took place Thursday; sources differed on what Iran intelligence was shown, and Eisenkot's camp said it did not change its view that Netanyahu must go (per Channel 12).

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The 27 October election produced deadlock: Netanyahu's right-wing and religious bloc won 53 seats, Eisenkot's bloc 48, and Arab parties the rest. Pre-election polls had shown Yashar ahead (24 seats to Likud's 21), and Arab parties hit a record in some surveys.

Eisenkot reportedly prioritises a coalition with Haredim over Arab parties, following Liberman's push for a majority without Arab-led lists. The Change bloc is divided on this. UTJ is quietly talking to the bloc; Ra'am could abstain.

UTJ chairman Yaakov Asher said his party will not back replacing Speaker Ohana before a government forms. This could cap the bloc's Knesset-control plan.

Liberman is set to chair the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee from 16 November and pledges a state commission of inquiry into 7 October (ANI report, unconfirmed).

No post-election poll dated within 72 hours turned up. Treat seat projections as pre-election data.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The trial panel ordered the Attorney General to court on 18 November at 9:30 a.m. to discuss moving the Netanyahu trial forward. Prosecutors argued her attendance is not the right procedure but said she and State Attorney Amit Aisman remain open to direct talks toward a resolution.

The Attorney General has yet to answer the judges' recommendation to withdraw a bribery charge in one case. This is the most concrete plea-deal-adjacent signal in the trial.

Herzog has withheld a decision on Netanyahu's pardon request, pushing plea talks instead (position as of April; no newer update found). Trump has publicly pressed for a pardon.

The Eisenkot bloc aims to use Knesset committees to undo the 2023 to 25 judicial overhaul. Haaretz frames this as undoing a "judicial coup"; the right will call it a power grab by a bloc without a government.

5. Gaza.

The IDF said it struck a senior Hamas military commander on 14 to 15 November; Hamas sources told Reuters Samir Abu Younis was killed in Khan Younis. The IDF separately confirmed a Rafah Brigade commander was killed overnight.

Gaza health authorities say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli operations since the ceasefire began. This is a Hamas-run ministry figure and not independently verified.

A group of 53 countries led by Britain, Qatar and Jordan urged Israel to open all crossings for unrestricted aid on 12 November. UN aid chief Tom Fletcher said waiting for a new Israeli government is no reason to delay aid.

The Board of Peace called Israel's winter flood response "insufficient"; COGAT says 91,000 tents and 8,200 winter-supply trucks have entered. The EU High Representative repeated the call on 14 November.

Israel has reportedly authorised about 250 foreign troops for Gaza, but no stabilisation force appears deployed (unconfirmed). Hamas disarmament remains the sticking point, with envoy Mladenov saying the ceasefire hinges on it.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Dozens of masked settlers attacked Palestinian olive harvesters in the southern West Bank on Saturday; WAFA says at least five were hurt. The IDF says troops fired in the air at two vehicles that fled to an outpost, and condemned the attackers.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians, one seriously, in Atara north of Ramallah on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA. The IDF cleared structures at two illegal outposts.

IDF sources told Haaretz that settler violence spikes during outpost removals and with post-election tension.

Israeli forces raided towns including Burqa, Ya'bad and Beit Dajan on 13 to 14 November, arresting former prisoners. The source is Palestinian (PIC), so treat details as partisan.

European criticism of settler violence is mounting.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

US intelligence believes Iran may still hold 1,500 to 2,500 missiles; Israeli officials say over 1,500 ballistic missiles (WSJ via Times of Israel). The estimate is shaping US deliberations on attacking; Trump reportedly may hold off absent new provocations.

The IRGC claimed it hit a US radar at a Bahrain base; Iran says it downed a US MQ-1 over Qeshm. Both are Iranian claims, unverified. A tanker reportedly hit a mine in Hormuz.

Channel 13 reported the IDF told Eisenkot there was no immediate urgency to strike Iran; a Turkish outlet headline claimed Israel ordered the military to prepare an attack (unconfirmed).

Israeli strikes continue in southern Lebanon (Kfar Tebnit, Qantara, Mansouri) while the Lebanese army raided a Hezbollah weapons depot in Beirut's southern suburbs on 13 November. A 26 June US-Israel-Lebanon framework ties withdrawal to Hezbollah disarmament.

A Saudi-Houthi front is active; Houthis claim strikes on Saudi-led forces and the coalition says it downed three cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh. Damascus denied sending troops to Yemen.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The 2026 defence budget was set at NIS 112 billion against the IDF's 140 billion ask, with reserve duty assumed at about 40,000 on average. The new government will face pressure to revisit it.

The Haredi draft exemption bill was shelved to pass the budget; UTJ now wants a one-year, possibly 18-month, transitional arrangement from an Eisenkot-led coalition. Eisenkot vows not to fold on "service for all".

The IDF reportedly assessed no urgency for an Iran strike, a point of friction with Netanyahu's push. (Channel 13 via Times of Israel; unconfirmed.)

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad's director was in Washington on 13 November amid Netanyahu's push to resume the Iran war (Axios, anonymous sources). The visit shows the intelligence chiefs as a channel in the Netanyahu-Trump dispute.

Ex-IDF chief Herzi Halevi said on 1 November that 7 October was a "preventable failure" with "absolutely no internal betrayal." No state commission of inquiry exists yet; Netanyahu resists the court-appointed model.

Sources differed on the Iran intelligence shown to Eisenkot. Dispute over what the opposition leader may see is itself a government-versus-security-establishment flashpoint.

Open-source note: searches found no new satellite or ISW/Critical Threats findings dated in the window. Earlier imagery showed Iran repairing missile-base tunnel entrances (March 2026); treat as background only.

Quiet: no new espionage indictments or leadership changes found in the last 72 hours.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

The European Commission will examine a citizens' initiative with about 1.1 million signatures calling to suspend the EU-Israel Association Agreement; it has six months to respond. A European Parliament hearing is expected.

Trump reportedly sides with Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE in keeping the blockade and diplomacy track on Iran, rejecting Netanyahu's calls for fresh strikes. Araghchi says a Hormuz reopening proposal is under discussion in Doha, with no direct US talks.

Israel's next prime minister faces pressure from the US, Europe and Iran, with settlements a key European issue.

Netanyahu said he spoke with Syria's al-Sharaa in October, the first disclosed call; Syria talks remain stalled over Israel's southern Syria presence. Nothing newer found.

Quiet: no fresh Egypt, Jordan, Turkey, ICC or ICJ developments found in the last 72 hours.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel fell about 4.5% in a week of post-election uncertainty before steadying near NIS 3.16 to 3.19 per dollar on 6 to 11 November. The dollar was NIS 3.172 on 11 November (Globes table).

Tel Aviv stocks slumped after the election, with the TA-90 down up to 3.5% on one day. Figures come from different days.

The Bank of Israel rate is 3.25%; analysts warn a weak shekel could stall cuts or bring a hike. The next decision date was not found.

A prolonged coalition process threatens a 2027 budget timeline (analyst concern, not an official forecast).

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

A grenade attack in Kafr Manda killed a 51-year-old woman, the 200th Arab victim of violence this year; three suspects were arrested. Herzog called the number "chilling" and urged the next government to intensify the fight.

Police placed snipers on rooftops in Rahat to curb gun violence.

Haredi parties are fielding overtures from both blocs, with draft terms the core demand.

Arab-party role in a coalition is contested inside the Change bloc.
