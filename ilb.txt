Here is your Israel briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Eisenkot freezes Likud talks after Netanyahu speech; AG-Shin Bet election probe clash; Trump ends US aid, claims Iran 'win'

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 12 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar! cancelled a scheduled coalition negotiation with Likud on 10 November after Netanyahu's furious Knesset speech, despite holding a 67-MK recommendation mandate. Netanyahu told the new Knesset that Arab MKs want Israel's destruction, which Eisenkot's camp called divisive; the deadlock now turns on whether Likud returns to the table.

The 26th Knesset was sworn in on 10 November, while Netanyahu's government stays on as a caretaker until a coalition forms. President Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government; Eisenkot received 67 recommendations against 46 for Netanyahu.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said on 8 November she knows of no basis that could influence the election result, amid her open clash with Shin Bet chief David Zini over a "foreign interference" probe. Police reportedly see little evidence to support the probe.

Trump said on 11 November that the US has "won" the Iran war but wants "total victory", and that 18 US service members have died. Reporting says Iran refuses to ship out enriched uranium and talks via Qatar are slow; Israel's role in the talks is not described.

Trump announced on 3 November that roughly 4 billion dollars in annual US military aid to Israel will end by the end of 2026; on 11 November Israel's PMO called the wind-down a "joint step" with Trump. The "gradual" framing differs from Trump's year-end cutoff, and Congress appropriates the money.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu leads a caretaker government and is pressing for a "national unity" government. The day after his Knesset speech he said unity remains possible, while Yashar! says it will not sit with him.

Sources close to Netanyahu reportedly signalled Likud would not insist on including Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit in a unity deal, but Likud denies it (unconfirmed). The report would be a concession aimed at Eisenkot's camp, which says Likud members could join without Netanyahu.

Finance Minister Smotrich urged Netanyahu and Eisenkot to reach an agreement on forming a government. It is a rare public push from a coalition hardliner, whose own bloc fell short of 61.

Eisenkot warned Netanyahu in a letter not to launch military or security operations absent an immediate threat, arguing the caretaker government no longer enjoys Knesset confidence. It reflects opposition fears of a pre-handover escalation.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The 27 October election left no bloc with 61: CNBC reports Likud and hard-right allies won 53 seats and the Eisenkot-led opposition 48, with Arab-majority parties holding the remainder. Opposition parties plus Arab parties total 67, but Eisenkot, Bennett and Liberman have rejected relying on them.

Arab parties won an unprecedented 19 seats, which makes them the arithmetic kingmaker. Netanyahu's speech is read by analysts as an effort to block Eisenkot from leaning on them (partisan commentary, so treat with caution).

The Central Elections Committee ratified the results despite the Shin Bet-AG dispute. Likud had claimed the election was "stolen".

An Israel Democracy Institute flash survey found the largest share of Israelis prefer new elections if no coalition forms. It signals public fatigue and raises the odds of a fresh vote if the mandate lapses.

IDI's Ofer Kenig notes the new Knesset has a record number of women and non-Jewish members.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Zini asked to question four senior Arab MKs as suspects; the AG denied the request, saying questioning needs an evidentiary basis. Likud accuses her of obstruction; Arab parties call the probe politicised; Eisenkot warned of a "constitutional crisis".

Police reportedly found no basis for election-financing or security offences in the foreign-interference material so far. The AG will decide whether to continue, narrow or close the probe.

Herzog spoke with both Zini and Baharav-Miara, who met at Shin Bet headquarters and agreed to lower the public temperature. The truce is fragile, as Haaretz reported on 11 November that Zini approved far-reaching investigative measures.

Netanyahu's pardon request remains unresolved: Herzog has pushed a plea deal first, and the Justice Ministry's pardons department found no legal basis without an admission of guilt. No November decision was found in the search results.

5. Gaza.

The Board of Peace on 10 November called Israel's response to flooding in Gaza's tent camps "insufficient" and also demanded Hamas disarm. Reports put flooded tents in the tens of thousands; the Board said delays slowed handover to the Palestinian technocratic committee.

The IDF says more than 91,000 tents have entered Gaza; UN OCHA says over 150,000 tents are outside, ready to enter. Israel's PMO says reconstruction starts only after Hamas disarms; Hamas blames Israeli restrictions.

The ceasefire framework is stalled: Trump urged Netanyahu by phone to advance his 20-point plan now the election is over. The PMO is calling on mediators to demand immediate Hamas disarmament across Gaza.

Israeli strikes continue: Palestinian media reported one killed in a strike on a tent near a school in eastern Gaza City on 10 November (IDF had not commented), and the IDF said an earlier Gaza City strike killed Hamas intelligence operations chief Jamal Abu Saeed. The IDF frames strikes as responses to ceasefire violations.

All hostage remains have been returned; the last, Ran Gvili, was recovered on 26 January 2026. The "Yellow Line" persists, and the army chief reportedly treats it as a permanent security boundary.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

A 14-year-old, Moataz Daghlos, was killed by IDF fire during a raid in Burqa near Nablus on 9 to 10 November, according to the Palestinian Health Ministry. Two others were wounded; the IDF account was not in the sources found.

Democracy Now reports more than 50 settlers attacked a village south of Nablus and burned vehicles (unconfirmed, single outlet). It fits a pattern of rising settler violence.

Haaretz reports, citing IDF sources, that settler violence spikes during illegal outpost removals; the IDF said it arrested 18 settlers during an evacuation near Salfit.

Haaretz sources say the IDF Central Command chief knew of settlers' plans to enter Palestinian homes near Jericho and told troops to wait, contradicting the army's account (unconfirmed, anonymous sourcing).

Quiet on the Palestinian Authority and annexation: no November 2026 development found.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Iran war diplomacy continues: Qatari mediators were back in Tehran in early November; a senior Iranian official told Reuters the sides are far from a deal. Iran reportedly threatens US bases in Europe if used against it.

Iran stands firm on its nuclear stockpile and the terms for reopening Hormuz, per one outlet; WSJ reportedly says Vance weighed direct talks in Doha (unconfirmed, no date).

Lebanon: a truce in force since 20 June is being tested; AP reported the deadliest day since then, with 11 killed in southern Lebanon (the date was not shown in the search result). Israel says it answers Hezbollah attacks; direct Israel-Lebanon talks are under way.

The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two Houthi drones toward Makkah and the south on 11 November.

Syria and Iraq: nothing significant found for the last 72 hours.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The 2026 defence budget was set at NIS 112bn, but a later top-up of about NIS 40bn to NIS 184bn was reported, along with Zamir's complaint of "empty coffers" (article date not confirmed).

The Defense Ministry reportedly owes about NIS 15.5bn to Israel's three largest defence manufacturers.

Haredi draft: legislation is stalled. The Supreme Court tied housing and childcare benefits to draft compliance in April, and Eisenkot has vowed not to support a draft-evasion law. No November vote found.

Haaretz reports the IDF seized only 4 weapons in a 10-day Hebron raid.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Shin Bet chief Zini removed senior officials who disagreed with him from the election-interference probe and installed an ally, Haaretz reports.

Senior law-enforcement officials told Haaretz the Shin Bet has so far produced no evidence of foreign interference. The AG's office says the Shin Bet did not hand materials to the joint team.

Tel Aviv protesters on 7 November called for the incoming government to fire Zini.

Ex-IDF chief Halevi said on 1 November that October 7 was preventable but there was "absolutely no internal betrayal".

Mossad chief David Barnea and Zini reportedly visited the UAE during the Iran war (WSJ, May); Mossad leadership status in November was not confirmed.

No notable OSINT findings located in this window.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Rubio said on 11 November that Trump wants the Foreign Military Financing portion to end "as soon as possible"; the PMO says it is moving "from aid to partnership". An analyst at the Atlantic Council doubts aid will stop by year-end.

Eisenkot pledged on 4 November to renew the bipartisan US-Israel alliance after the US midterms.

A Republican US lawmaker moved on 9 November to impeach Defense Secretary Hegseth over the Iran war.

Turkey: an Istanbul court opened a trial in absentia against Netanyahu and 34 others over the flotilla interception, adjourned to 30 March 2027. Israel is reportedly preparing an ICC case against Erdogan.

ICC: no steps to withdraw the Netanyahu warrant, per the deputy prosecutor; French candidate Mélenchon pledged to enforce it.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The dollar traded at about NIS 3.17 on 11 November (Globes intraday quote).

The Bank of Israel holds its rate at 3.25% after three cuts.

The TA-35 closed 6 November at 3,941.14, down 2.9% on the week; no later close found.

Moody's kept Israel at Baa1 stable, raised 2026 growth to 5% and 2027 to 6%, and forecasts a 5% deficit in 2026; it flags judicial independence and foreign relations as risks.

The end of US military aid adds fiscal pressure on top of defence top-ups.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Eleven people were arrested in a mass brawl in Lod on 8 November after a far-right "sovereignty march" through an Arab neighbourhood. Police blame two activists tied to Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit and summoned them.

The Lod mayor blamed outside extremists; an activist cited the Arab parties' 19 seats as a reason for the march.

Netanyahu's claim that Arab MKs want Israel's destruction drew sharp criticism and heightens Jewish-Arab tension.
