Here is your Israel briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

Netanyahu aide tied to election-day arrests as Eisenkot races for a coalition and Israel readies for Iran.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 17 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Channel 12 reported on 15 November that Netanyahu's chief of staff Ido Norden fed police a tip that led to election-day arrests of Ra'am activists. The PMO calls the report false and says Norden only passed on information as any citizen would; police opened an internal probe and the Attorney General will discuss how PMO information reached police. The opposition calls it an attempted "coup" (unconfirmed: single-broadcaster report, contested).

Israel's security cabinet has reportedly ordered the IDF to step up preparations for a renewed strike on Iran, while Trump is holding off. Mossad chief Roman Gofman briefed US officials including CIA director Ratcliffe and Witkoff, and a third US carrier has reached the region; no strike decision is reported.

President Herzog tasked Gadi Eisenkot with forming a government after 67 MKs recommended him against 46 for Netanyahu. Neither bloc has 61 seats, so the outcome hinges on Haredi parties, Arab parties and Naftali Bennett.

The IDF struck an arms shipment near Janta on the Syria-Lebanon border on 16 November, its first declared deep strike in the Beqaa in about three months. It matters because Israel fears Hezbollah could join a renewed Iran war.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu publicly urged Eisenkot to form a unity government, but the first Likud-Yashar meeting (75 minutes, Herzliya, 15 November) ended without agreement. Bennett sent adviser Shalom Shlomo as an indirect envoy.

Yashar had cancelled scheduled negotiations with Likud on 10 November over Netanyahu's "divisive speech". Talks resumed within days, which shows both sides keeping the unity option open.

Lapid, Liberman and Golan have told Eisenkot they will not sit in a government headed by Netanyahu. That limits Eisenkot's room to accept a rotation or unity formula with Likud (Ynet report).

Netanyahu hosted Czech PM Babiš on 16 November and warned that "Europe is a target" of Iran. He is also reported to be pressing Trump to resume strikes, which the opposition links to his political survival (opposition framing).

Eisenkot called on 16 November for an inquiry and said Netanyahu cannot remain PM given the alleged vote meddling. A senior figure in his bloc also argued Shin Bet chief Zini and Mossad chief Gofman should be fired.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Certified results (4 November) gave Netanyahu's bloc 53 seats, Eisenkot's Zionist bloc 48 and the Arab parties 19; Likud has 27, Yashar 21 and Shas fell to six. No bloc reaches 61, so the Arab lists and Haredim are kingmakers.

Eisenkot has 28 days from the 8 November mandate, running to roughly early December. The 25th Knesset's term formally ended 11 November.

Eisenkot reportedly asked Ra'am to recognise Hamas as a terrorist organisation as a condition for support, putting Ra'am's leader under pressure. Both Ra'am and the Joint List recommended him to Herzog without demanding cabinet seats.

Haredi sources told Haaretz on 16 November of "significant progress" with the Eisenkot bloc, but UTJ's demands collide with his "service for all" pledge. Reported asks include a one-year freeze on compulsory service, no arrests of draft evaders and sign-off from all 48 bloc MKs (Channel 12 via Jordan News, unconfirmed).

Post-election polling: Quiet; no new poll dated in the last 72 hours was found. Pre-election polls had Yashar competitive with Likud.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara will discuss how PMO information reached police on election day, and police opened an internal probe. Courts had refused to extend custody for the Ra'am suspects, saying arrests lacked valid warrants; all were released.

Netanyahu's trial continued on 16 November with defence witness Topaz Luk, his communications adviser, testifying on the 2015 campaign and Walla. The defence is now calling other witnesses after his own testimony ended in June.

The court postponed the Attorney General's ordered appearance in the trial to Wednesday 25 November. She had earlier declined a judges' proposal to appear for a discussion.

Netanyahu's pardon request to Herzog remains unresolved; Herzog proposed talks between the parties first. The Knesset Speaker has urged a pardon (partisan framing).

The High Court dismissed a Lavi petition against the Attorney General over alleged limits on the probe of Arab MKs and aides, on procedural grounds only. It did not rule on whether she imposed restrictions.

5. Gaza.

An Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis on 12 November killed six, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old, per the Gaza Health Ministry. The IDF said it targeted a Hamas brigade commander and is reviewing reports on medical staff; Hamas denies militant use of the hospital.

The Board of Peace blamed Hamas and said the strike "underscores" the need for the peace plan. Critics note it avoided assigning responsibility to Israel.

Health authorities say 1,622 people have been killed by Israeli fire since the October 2025 truce. Israel says Hamas violates the truce, and Netanyahu vows no withdrawal until Hamas is "genuinely" disarmed.

Forensic Architecture and Drop Site News say Israel has built 13 new outposts in Gaza since the truce (advocacy-group analysis, unverified). A US coordination centre run by CENTCOM is reportedly planned.

Hostages: Quiet; nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers uprooted dozens of olive trees near al-Dhahiriya (Hebron) and raided Osarin, Talfit and the Yarza Bedouin community on 16 November, per Palestinian Information Center. The outlet is PA-aligned and the claims are mostly unconfirmed by the IDF.

Settlers attacked farmers near Bethlehem and a house in Beita on 15 November, and vehicles at al-Mughayyir. One Palestinian was reported injured.

Five Palestinians were wounded in a settler attack on Atara on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA. It is part of a steady pattern of attacks.

Settlers reportedly injured a 67-year-old Palestinian man in a village attack. Middle East Eye separately reported a 14-year-old killed by Israeli forces (unconfirmed, date unclear).

A UN commission report released before the election urged the ICC to examine West Bank crimes. Israel rejects such findings. Consortium News (critical outlet).

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF says the Janta strike hit a Hezbollah-bound shipment of probable Iranian origin and that Israel stays committed to the ceasefire. Defence officials reportedly approved it amid fears Hezbollah joins any new Iran round.

The IDF said on 15 November it demolished a Hezbollah tunnel in southern Lebanon, and Lebanese media report troops detained two brothers near Shebaa on 16 November. Israel still occupies parts of the south; the ceasefire dates from 16 April.

US-mediated Israel-Lebanon talks reportedly ended without a breakthrough, and the 26 June framework has stalled. Washington is reportedly pushing a force to replace UNIFIL.

Iran's security chief warned every base used in an attack, "from West Asia to Europe", would be a target. Iran also reports mines hitting tankers in Hormuz and a drone attack on a US base in Bahrain (IRGC claims, unverified).

Houthis accused Saudi Arabia of strikes in Yemen (3 to 5 dead) and fired missiles at Riyadh; no Houthi fire at Israel was found for 16 November. Houthis reportedly advanced to about 62 miles from Aden (Reuters).

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The security cabinet ordered the IDF to intensify readiness for a possible Iran strike (Kan, about 11 November). Eisenkot urged no deployment without a direct, immediate threat before a new government.

Ynet reports Israel can act alone on Iran but prefers coordination with Washington, and the caretaker government weighs renewed war while the US stays reluctant.

The Haredi draft is now a coalition bargaining chip: UTJ demands an arrest freeze and exemptions. The Attorney General earlier told the High Court the government was failing to comply with enforcement directives.

The 2026 budget passed in March with NIS 143 billion for defence. No new procurement or budget news was found in the last 72 hours.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Channel 12 reported Shin Bet chief David Zini asked on election day to wiretap five Arab MKs in a foreign-interference probe (unconfirmed, single outlet). Zini faces criticism over the Arab-vote fraud narrative.

Mossad chief Roman Gofman has fired about a quarter of senior management, including the deputy director. Haaretz says he replaced a top official with a close associate (Haaretz, 4 November).

Gofman met CIA director Ratcliffe and Witkoff in the US to deliver intelligence on Iran, then briefed the cabinet.

Open-source, unverified: satellite imagery analysed by the Institute for Science and International Security shows activity at Minzadehei, an Iranian site Israel struck in March. A BBC investigation using MAIAR analysis found construction at two missile sites and 12 bases.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defence pact in Istanbul on 13 November; the text is unpublished. The joint statement criticised Israel's actions in Gaza; no Israeli official reaction was found.

Trump reportedly declined to authorise an Iran strike around 8 to 9 November and says he sees no reason to restart the war. Reports of tense Netanyahu-Trump calls were denied by Israel.

A UN commission report asked the ICC prosecutor to consider genocide in Gaza and West Bank warrants. No new ICC or ICJ action was found in November; the 2024 Netanyahu/Gallant warrants stand and Israel disputes jurisdiction.

Czech PM Babiš visited Jerusalem on 16 November.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The TA-125 fell about 0.3% to roughly 3,910 on 16 November (Trading Economics; figure approximate).

The shekel ended last week slightly weaker near NIS 3.20/$; the Bank of Israel rate is 3.25% (Substack weekly report).

Bank Hapoalim sees the shekel near NIS 3/$ if Eisenkot forms a government. It shows markets pricing a political change as well as Iran risk.

Analysts debate how much of the post-election selloff was Israel-specific (Substack commentary).

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Three Ra'am activists were arrested in the south on 27 October, election day, without valid warrants; courts released them. Police now examine whether PMO information was planted (contested).

Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen called the episode a "despicable blood libel" and an attempted coup, and demanded a criminal probe, an inquiry commission and suspension of involved officials. Ahmad Tibi also urged a criminal investigation.

The former PMO employee behind the tip denies a conspiracy and says he got the information from a Joint List MK. The claim is unverified and disputed.

Haredi protesters rallied against the draft as leaders weigh a deal with Eisenkot's bloc.
