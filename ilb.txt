Here is your Israel briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

Israel kills Hamas security chief as Kushner warns of 'pariah' status; unity talks stall before Eisenkot deadline.

Compiled from sources dated 20 to 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Israel killed Naim Abu Nahal, head of Hamas's internal security apparatus, in a Gaza City strike on Tuesday 24 November. Israel and Hamas both confirm the killing, which comes weeks after Israel captured his predecessor and tests the fragile October 2025 ceasefire; Hamas called it an assassination and a "war crime".

Jared Kushner warned Israel is heading toward "global-pariah status" over Gaza, and Netanyahu phoned him the next day. Kushner says Netanyahu's opposition has stalled the US-backed reconstruction plan, while Netanyahu insists Hamas must disarm first; the call is reported only via an unnamed Israeli official (unconfirmed).

Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar held a fourth round of unity-government talks with no breakthrough; Eisenkot's mandate to form a government expires 6 December. Sticking points include the judicial overhaul, an October 7 inquiry, Ben Gvir's role and who leads; Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister after the 27 October election.

Defence Minister Katz asked the military censor to block a Kan investigation into IDF use of human shields in Gaza, due to air Wednesday night. The request has drawn criticism as political pressure on public media; Haaretz separately reported the IDF is probing 16 such cases.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu is reportedly failing to arrange talks with United Torah Judaism MKs, as the Haredi parties weigh a deal with the Eisenkot bloc. If UTJ moves, Netanyahu's route back to a 61-seat majority narrows.

Netanyahu is said to be open to excluding Ben Gvir from a unity government, though Likud also demands Netanyahu stay sole PM and Otzma Yehudit be included (reports conflict). Both positions come from leaks, and no deal text has been published (unconfirmed).

Eisenkot says he will bring a government under his leadership to a Knesset vote if broad talks fail. He is counting on the Democrats and UAL, with the Joint List abstaining, and treats Bennett and Liberman as wild cards.

Bennett and Eisenkot reportedly disagree on whether to give Likud a deadline; Democrats leader Yair Golan urged Eisenkot to end the "deceitful" talks. The split shows how fragile the anti-Netanyahu bloc is.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The Eisenkot bloc won preliminary votes 63 to 47 on bills repealing parts of the judicial and media overhauls. The bills must still clear committee and three readings; the bloc says it will boycott legislation advanced by Netanyahu's side.

A Likud bill barring MKs who refuse to recognise Hamas as a terror group advanced 48 to 0 as the Eisenkot bloc boycotted. It targets Arab parties and is likely to face High Court scrutiny.

The Central Elections Committee certified the 27 October results after finding no evidence of fraud. Netanyahu has since argued against a coalition with Arab parties.

No new post-election poll surfaced; the last pre-election surveys had Yashar ahead of Likud (Channel 12: 23 versus 20 seats). These are pre-election figures and not a guide to current standing.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Supreme Court President Yitzhak Amit called settler violence a "desecration of God's name" and a threat to the Zionist vision, in a ruling on a petition from the village of Burqa. The panel ordered the state to detail steps to end the violence.

Police and the Attorney General are reportedly probing whether the Prime Minister's Office orchestrated an Arab vote-buying scandal to delegitimise the election. Channel 12 traced the election-day tip to a former Netanyahu aide; the PMO denies this (unconfirmed).

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara publicly rebuked Shin Bet chief Zini for seeking to question four Arab politicians while giving prosecutors evidence on only one. The clash is part of the wider fight over her status; the High Court has frozen moves against her while petitions are pending.

The High Court suspended the Broadcasting Law and gave the government and Knesset until 7 January 2027 to respond. The Attorney General had sought the freeze; the Eisenkot bloc is separately moving to repeal the law.

5. Gaza.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council on 17 November that the ceasefire could collapse without action, and blamed Israel's blocking of a technocratic administration for delaying the stabilisation force. The report is the clearest sign of US-linked bodies pressing Israel; critics such as World Israel News say the board neglects Hamas rearming.

Board adviser Josh Gruenbaum warned on 21 November the board could "throw the keys back to the UN" if Israel keeps blocking efforts. He says Netanyahu has not signed the stabilisation-force terms in over six months.

The Board of Peace says Hamas and other factions agreed to a disarmament roadmap; Hamas leaders publicly reaffirm armed struggle. The two claims are in tension and none is independently verified.

Gaza health officials count more than 1,600 Palestinians killed since the October 2025 ceasefire (UN: over 1,400 earlier in November). A strike on Nasser Hospital on 12 November killed six including a surgeon; the IDF said it targeted a Hamas commander.

Aid is reportedly about half the agreed 600 trucks a day for most of 2026. Figures come from press accounts, not a single official tally.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers torched at least a dozen cars and vandalised homes across the West Bank overnight on 21 to 22 November. Bennett called it "Jewish terrorism"; seven teenagers were arrested at an outpost police describe as a launchpad for attacks.

OCHA reportedly recorded 428 settler attacks causing injury or damage between 1 September and 16 November. The count underscores the scale during the olive harvest, when Haaretz reported troops firing at harvesters.

Palestinian media (Palinfo, Hamas-aligned) report intensified IDF raids on 20 November, with one Palestinian killed. The source is partisan and the claim is single-source (unconfirmed).

The IDF said it arrested 18 settlers while evacuating an illegal outpost on 2 November. Enforcement remains sporadic against a background of continuing attacks.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Katz said on 24 November Iran is developing chemical weapons and that Israel and the US have plans for renewed strikes. Iran denies this and calls the allegations "fraudulent"; US officials earlier doubted Israeli intelligence on the issue, and no independent verification has been published.

The IDF said it struck a Hezbollah missile-production and storage site at Janta in the Bekaa around 19 to 20 November. No casualties were reported; hostilities have fallen since June under a US-sponsored framework, though Israel holds a roughly 10 km "security zone".

The IDF said on 15 November it demolished a Hezbollah tunnel in southern Lebanon. Hezbollah rejects the disarmament framework.

Houthis are attacking Saudi Arabia, with a cruise missile intercepted on 19 November, and Saudi Arabia and Syria signed a defence cooperation agreement on 5 November. Damascus denies reports of sending troops to Yemen.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Eisenkot's draft plan reportedly would scrap enlistment quotas and let full-time yeshiva students keep studying without sanctions; Yashar and UTJ deny an agreed framework. Whether the High Court would accept it is unclear.

UTJ MKs filed bills to defer service for yeshiva students until 26 and to suspend enforcement against them for a year. They are a bargaining chip in coalition talks.

Haredi protesters blocked roads and the light rail after the military detained yeshiva students who failed to enlist, around 11 November. Tension over arrests continues.

Middle East Eye's press review reports IDF suicides at their highest level since 2010. The source summarises Israeli press and the underlying figures are unverified here (unconfirmed).

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Shin Bet chief David Zini reportedly told Eisenkot two days after the vote he had intelligence since 2018 that "Arabs are stealing elections". The Shin Bet says it acted on the instructions of election committee chair Justice Sohlberg; the election czar said the vote-buying probe found no evidence that could affect the result. Details come from Channel 12/13 leaks.

Zini reportedly sought wiretaps on five Arab MKs and held a secret meeting with Eisenkot about the probe. Critics like Yair Golan say he is serving Netanyahu politically; the Shin Bet denies that reports on targets and methods came from it (unconfirmed).

Mossad chief Roman Gofman was reportedly in Washington for Iran talks with CIA director Ratcliffe, and Netanyahu did not brief Eisenkot (unconfirmed). It shows the caretaker government managing the Iran file alone.

Open-source (unverified): Al Jazeera's Open Source Unit and Forensic Architecture report Israeli outposts inside Gaza, and Bellingcat found 46 of 54 towns inside the IDF's Lebanon line heavily damaged. Al Jazeera cites a Gaza media office claim that the yellow markers moved about 300 m on 20 November, which is unconfirmed.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Kushner's "global pariah" warning is the sharpest recent US public criticism of Netanyahu's Gaza stance. It reflects frustration over the reconstruction plan and the stabilisation force.

Israel pressed the US and Gulf states with intelligence on Iranian chemical weapons in early November, but officials in Washington doubted its credibility. The dispute matters for coordination on renewed strikes.

Iranian officials reportedly talked with Qatari mediators in Doha while Washington pressed for limits on Iran's nuclear programme. Single-source report (unconfirmed).

The ICC rejected Israel's bid to cancel the Netanyahu and Gallant warrants, and the prosecutor Karim Khan has been suspended by Britain's legal regulator over misconduct allegations. The warrants stand, and Hungary does not consider itself bound. Date of these items is not confirmed from the results.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel hit its weakest level against the dollar in nearly a year, reaching about 3.345 on 24 November per Globes data. Globes attributes much of the slide to the indecisive election result and the coalition delay.

The TA-35 closed at 3,861.51 on 20 November, down 4.37% on the week, with the shekel about 4% weaker. The figures come from a newsletter and should be treated as indicative.

The Bank of Israel reportedly kept its rate at 3.25% amid the shekel drop. Its research department earlier forecast one further 0.25-point cut within a year; the decision comes from a Times of Israel summary (unconfirmed).

Inflation fell to 1.1%, a five-year low, according to a market-analysis note. Weak shekel may revive price pressure; Bank Hapoalim estimates a 0.06% boost to November's index.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

A Haredi group was convicted over a June riot at a Supreme Court justice's home and faces up to two years. It shows the legal system pushing back on pressure campaigns; details were thin in the available summary (unconfirmed).

Homicides in Arab society reached 201 in 2026, the fourth straight year above 200. Ynet reports three killings in under a day, including a young man shot in Reineh; critics blame police neglect.

The Zini vote-fraud affair, with arrests of Arab activists on election day, has raised concern about the delegitimisation of Arab voters. Likud sought to block certification citing fraud; none has been found.

A man was charged with stabbing and choking his ex-wife to death in front of their six-year-old son. The case adds to concern over domestic violence.

What to watch.
