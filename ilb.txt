Here is your Israel briefing for Monday, 23 November.

Netanyahu-Eisenkot unity talks collapse as Gaza ceasefire warning and West Bank settler arson dominate.

Compiled from sources dated 20 to 23 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar ended coalition talks on Sunday 22 November without a deal. The sides stayed split over who leads the next government and whether Itamar Ben-Gvir sits in it, per Channel 12 as relayed by Al Jazeera and Yahoo.

Eisenkot's mandate to form a government runs to 6 December, with a possible 14-day extension. If unity talks fail, Yashar plans to seek a Knesset confidence vote for a minority government.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council the Gaza ceasefire could collapse without full implementation. It said a ceasefire delivering nothing visible cannot be sustained and blamed Israeli delays on the stabilization force and police training.

Settlers burned at least 12 vehicles in Ramallah-area villages on Sunday, per WAFA. The IDF condemned the violence as undermining order and diverting forces.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu reportedly insists on the premiership first, and Eisenkot refuses both to serve under him and to accept a rotation. Bennett had floated an Eisenkot-then-Netanyahu rotation. Lieberman and Bennett have ruled out any government with Netanyahu or his bloc.

Likud set conditions for a unity government, including an end to "incitement" against Netanyahu, while the talks met in a Herzliya hotel. The conditions reflect Netanyahu's push to reframe the post-election fight as one over legitimacy.

Netanyahu reportedly drew up ten demands for a unity deal, and reports say no state commission on October 7 would be created under one (a Likud official disputed that it was discussed). The inquiry is a core sticking point. World Israel News (unconfirmed)

A Saudi-linked source said normalization with Israel cannot happen while Netanyahu remains in office. It raises the diplomatic cost of the coalition deadlock. JPost, November 2026 (unconfirmed)

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Certified results from the 27 October election give Likud 27 seats and Yashar 21. Bennett's list won 12, the Joint List 11, and the Democrats and Ra'am 8 each; the Central Elections Committee certified the count on 4 November.

Neither bloc has 61: Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 seats and the opposition 48, with a record 19 for Arab parties. Any non-Netanyahu government would need Arab-party support or abstentions.

Liberman became head of the temporary Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee on a vote of 8 to 0. He said a universal draft law will not pass and that he seeks a "fairest" law acceptable to the IDF and High Court.

Eisenkot's bloc is advancing a bill for a state commission of inquiry into October 7 amid the unity talks.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

Quiet on fresh rulings in the last 72 hours; the pending fights are the police-oversight and Haredi-arrest-freeze laws. The Knesset passed the arrest-freeze law on 14 July and the Supreme Court blocked it a day later pending petitions.

Attorney General Baharav-Miara asked the High Court in July to block a law placing the police-misconduct unit under the Justice Minister, calling it a "democratic breakdown". The case remains a live test for the next government. Haaretz, 28 July (background, not new)

The AG ordered an investigation into a Channel 12 report that Netanyahu's staff colluded with police and the Shin Bet to file false reports against Ra'am members. The allegation is one report and is disputed territory. i24NEWS (unconfirmed)

Likud weighed refusing to recognize the election result before certification on 4 November. Likud and Shas members abstained in the committee vote.

5. Gaza.

An Israeli strike on a vehicle in Gaza City killed three on 21 November, per the Gaza Health Ministry. The IDF said it targeted Islamic Jihad militants; witnesses said the vehicle was distributing food.

At least 1,600 Palestinians and five Israeli soldiers have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, per Palestinian and Israeli tallies. The truce halted major fighting but not strikes or Hamas disarmament.

The Board of Peace says Israel must authorize the stabilization force, new police and the technocratic administration, and that it is not demanding withdrawals. An adviser, Josh Gruenbaum, warned the file could return to the UN.

Haaretz reports the mechanism meant to prevent ceasefire violations is faltering, with Israel striking without notifying the parties (anonymously sourced).

The Board urged more winter supplies into Gaza; Al Jazeera asks where the 18 billion dollars pledged to the Board has gone. Board officials say funds cannot be spent until troops or police enter.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers set fire to 12 vehicles in Kafr Malik, Deir Abu Mashaal and Deir al-Sudan on 22 November, per WAFA. Attacks also hit al-Tuwani; the IDF said the violence diverts troops from core duties.

A Beit El settlement security coordinator shot dead an unarmed Palestinian, identified by WAFA as Hamza Masoud Salim Abdo, 42, on Friday. Separately, masked settlers ambushed police.

Haaretz reports the IDF shot at Palestinian olive harvesters as settlers attacked villages. The harvest season keeps friction high.

The IDF has warned that Jewish nationalist violence undermines counterterrorism operations.

The Palestinian Authority remains in fiscal crisis, with Smotrich withholding clearance revenues and the World Bank warning of "fiscal collapse". No new November transfer news found. Ynet (background)

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF struck a Hezbollah missile-production and storage site in Janta, Bekaa Valley, on 19 to 20 November, with no casualties reported. It said Hezbollah members were trying to restore the site.

Israeli forces still hold a self-declared "security zone" about 10 km deep in south Lebanon; the US-brokered 26 June framework is stalled with no talks since August. Aoun ties resumption to formation of an Israeli government.

UNIFIL's mandate ends this year and a US envoy says talks on a post-UNIFIL plan continue. France and the US are working on a UN resolution.

ISW/CTP saw reports of more Hezbollah fighters and weapons moving south but no clear signs of imminent attack on Israel.

The US-Iran war is described as a frozen conflict, with the US blockade reimposed on 14 July and Hormuz attacks continuing. Houthis fired missiles at Saudi Arabia, not Israel, in the latest reports.

Defense Minister Katz said the "mission is not complete" on Iran, claiming Tehran is trying to renew nuclear efforts. The date of the remark is uncertain (about two weeks old).

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The IDF says it is short about 12,000 soldiers, including 7,500 in combat roles. That is the manpower case for Haredi conscription.

Haredi parties are reportedly eyeing Liberman's 2018 draft law as talks over joining an Eisenkot government progress (unconfirmed).

A US-Israel long-term defense pact to replace the expiring memorandum is reportedly under negotiation. Ynet (background)

Procurement and budget: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Haaretz reports the Shin Bet has not produced evidence of foreign election interference, despite Zini's claims. The AG said she had seen nothing that would have affected the result; Qatar denies the allegation.

Zini removed critics from the probe and appointed an ally to lead it, per Haaretz, and dozens have been questioned. The Mossad was asked to assist.

Open-source: BBC Verify, citing October satellite imagery, reported Iranian tunnel-entrance construction at the Garmdareh and Shahroud missile bases. Treat as open-source and unverified by Israeli officials; the US pressed imagery firms to restrict coverage.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

The Board of Peace's UN report puts pressure on Netanyahu's government and the US-backed Gaza track. A JPost piece says the Board denies demanding Israeli withdrawal before disarmament.

Sharaa has tied any Syria deal to Israeli pullback to 1974 lines; Israel demands a demilitarized south and Katz says troops will stay. No new talks found this week. Times of Israel (background)

Netanyahu has lobbied Trump against F-35 sales to Turkey. Trump rejected outside advice on sales. CNN, 28 July (background)

ICC arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant remain in place; no new ICC or ICJ ruling found.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The Bank of Israel holds its rate at 3.25% and its next decision is listed for 23 November (one calendar says 26 November). Its research department projects 3% in a year.

The bank raised its 2026 growth forecast to 5.6% when it held rates earlier, citing calmer geopolitics and a weaker shekel.

No reliable Tel Aviv market or shekel-rate data found for the last 72 hours. Quiet: nothing significant confirmed.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Three Arab men were shot dead in under 24 hours, and the Abraham Initiatives counted the man killed in Reineh on 21 November as the 210th Arab homicide victim of 2026. Haaretz's count differs (201 on 20 November).

Last year was the deadliest on record with 252 Arab victims, and the 2026 toll is on pace to approach it.

President Herzog urged the next government to intensify the fight against violent crime in the Arab community.
