Here is your Israel briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Mossad chief in Washington as Netanyahu pushes Iran strike; Eisenkot races to build coalition; AG ordered to Netanyahu trial.

Compiled from sources dated 11 to 14 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Mossad director Roman Gofman was in Washington on 13 November meeting CIA director John Ratcliffe, with a Miami stop to see envoy Steve Witkoff expected. Axios says Netanyahu is pressing Trump to resume the Iran war, and Trump has said he could use force "right now" if allies or ships are threatened; the details rest on anonymous sources and Mossad, CIA and the White House declined comment (unconfirmed).

Gadi Eisenkot has 28 days from 8 November to form a government, with 48 seats in his four-party alliance against 53 for Netanyahu's bloc and a record 19 for Arab-led parties. Herzog says mutual vetoes mean the 67 to 46 recommendation tally settles nothing, and failure would mean a new election within 90 days with Netanyahu as caretaker.

An Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis overnight 11 to 12 November killed six people, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old, per Gaza's Health Ministry. The IDF says it targeted Khan Younis Brigade commander Mohammed Abu Ismail and had warned about military activity at the site; the hospital director denies any warning.

Judges in Netanyahu's corruption trial ordered Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara to appear on 18 November at 9:30. Her office objected, and the order is highly unusual.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu, now heading what Haaretz calls a caretaker government, plans to name Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar interior minister, a post vacant since Shas's Moshe Arbel resigned in July 2025. The cabinet was expected to approve it on 11 November; I could not confirm the vote.

Netanyahu is calling for a "national unity" government and warns that his rivals would rely on "Hamas supporters." This is campaign-style framing from the Likud side aimed at Eisenkot's reliance on Arab parties.

Netanyahu's office says Israel will move off US military aid "gradually," contradicting Trump's claim that it ends after 2026. Trump said on 3 November that roughly 4 billion dollars a year would stop; the PMO calls it a shift "from aid to partnership."

Israel barred French far-left figures Jean-Luc Mélenchon and Mathilde Panot, citing "anti-Israel incitement." Paris criticised the move.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Eisenkot is reportedly prioritising a coalition with the Haredi parties over reliance on Arab parties. Channel 12 reports the shift follows Avigdor Liberman's objections; UTJ is quietly talking while Shas calls reports it will join "complete fake news."

Ahmad Tibi showed text messages on Channel 12 that he says came from Netanyahu associate Natan Eshel seeking Joint List help to block Yashar moves in the Knesset. Tibi, who offers outside support for an Eisenkot government but no ministers, says the party would back replacing Speaker Amir Ohana and a state commission on October 7; the messages are unverified (unconfirmed).

UTJ chair Yakov Asher says his party will not back removing Ohana before a new government exists. Yashar had invited Shas and UTJ to join the effort.

Election-night Channel 12 exit poll (27 October) gave Likud 24, Yashar 22, B'Yachad 12, Joint List 11 and Ra'am 7; no new polls surfaced in the window. Treat the exit poll as superseded by final results.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The Netanyahu trial panel summoned the attorney general over how proceedings and probe approvals are managed; prosecutors told the court she would not attend a discussion. She is due on 18 November, and a clash between the bench and her office is likely.

The High Court dismissed a Lavi petition against the AG over alleged limits on investigating Arab MKs in an election-fraud probe. The justices said Lavi had not exhausted channels with her office first; the exact date is unclear (about 9 to 10 November).

Background: the High Court voided the government's dismissal of Baharav-Miara 7 to 0 in December 2025, and some ministers urged defiance. She remains in office.

5. Gaza.

Talks on Hamas disarmament and Israeli withdrawal remain stalled, with heavy rain worsening conditions for displaced people in tents, DW reports. Israeli strikes continue under the truce.

A running tally cited by Wikipedia puts post-ceasefire deaths at least 1,400 Palestinians and 5 Israeli soldiers as of 20 October. This is a compiled figure, not an official one (unconfirmed).

Nasser Hospital is out of service and surgeries are suspended after the 12 November strike. France 24 also reports a 9-month-old baby froze to death.

The framework remains UN Security Council Resolution 2803: a Board of Peace and stabilization force, with a July announcement of a Hamas disarmament deal. I found no November reporting on deployment.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians, one seriously, in Atara north of Ramallah on 12 November, per Yesh Din and WAFA. The military said it cleared structures at two illegal outposts linked to violence.

Israeli forces raided towns on 13 November, arrested a groom at a wedding near Ramallah and shot a man in the thigh in Jaba, per Palestinian outlets. The wording comes from Hamas-aligned Palestinian Information Center, so treat the framing as partisan.

OCHA recorded 52 settler attacks causing casualties or damage across 38 communities from 3 to 9 November. In Masafer Yatta, settlers have besieged one home for 40 days.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

Trump said he would count it a defeat if Iran's regime is not toppled by the end of his term and warned of a strike with "no hesitation." Critical Threats says Iran claims mines hit ships on the US-backed Hormuz route, while sources conflict on whether talks are live.

Iran warned of wider retaliation as Israel reportedly readies an attack, and the security cabinet reportedly met on 11 November (Kan). Reported via secondary summaries (unconfirmed).

The Lebanese army said it seized a "large quantity of weapons" in a raid on a Hezbollah stronghold in Beirut. Israeli strikes in south Lebanon (Qantara, Kfar Tebnit) continued on 12 November.

The Houthis are fighting mainly Yemeni government forces and Saudi targets; I found no Houthi launches at Israel in November. The claims of attacks on Saudi Arabia are unverified.

Syria: Sharaa said in September talks were about 90% done before Israel pulled back; nothing newer found.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The Haredi draft is the central coalition sticking point, with 4,298 Haredi enlistees after a year of sanctions, per Haaretz. The anti-Netanyahu bloc will not help Haredim "dodge the draft."

Agudat Yisrael advisers want all 48 Eisenkot-bloc MKs to sign a pledge for an exemption law with an override clause; Eisenkot vows not to fold on service for all.

The commander of the US Navy's 5th Fleet visited Israel to discuss naval coordination.

Aid phase-out raises procurement questions: one analyst expects missile defence and counter-drone support to be hard to move to foreign military sales. Trump says arms sales continue.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Mossad's Gofman is in Washington on Iran (see Top stories). Reported through Axios and Israeli outlets citing anonymous sources (unconfirmed).

No official commission of inquiry into October 7 exists, per an October IRSEM study; Aman and Shin Bet published internal reviews in 2025. Shin Bet chief David Zini says "additional issues" remain.

A Yedioth Ahronoth report relayed by Anadolu says Netanyahu received warnings before 7 October 2023 and convened no meeting (unconfirmed, single-source).

Open-source: Critical Threats' 12 November assessment says Iran aims to split the US and Israel from Arab states. This is analyst judgment, not verified fact.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said he won't be cowed by Iranian death threats.

Secretary of State Rubio attacked the ICC on 13 November, and a report says the Netherlands is working with the US on limiting the court's jurisdiction over non-member nationals. These are proposals, not adopted measures, and the ICC warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant stand.

Foreign Policy argues Netanyahu lost his bet on Trump after the US midterms, and Biden's memoir says Netanyahu made empty promises on Gaza civilian harm. Both are commentary and memoir claims.

Turkey and Qatar reportedly signed a defence pact in Istanbul. Single aggregator report (unconfirmed).

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

USD/ILS rose 0.43% to about 3.21 on 13 November and the shekel is down 3.55% over a month, per Trading Economics. A Globes item gave 3.167 on an earlier session, so levels differ by source.

The TA-35 rose 3.09% to 3,941.14 in a Friday session but ended that week down 2.9%, per Globes; the date of the session is uncertain. An analysis discusses a post-election selloff.

The aid phase-out and political uncertainty are the main macro risks; no Bank of Israel decision surfaced.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Police deployed rooftop snipers in the Bedouin city of Rahat on 13 November after clashes among six families killed two young people in a month. Nearly 200 Arabs have been killed in crime this year; a security source calls the move temporary and says arrests work better.

Record Arab-party representation (19 seats) makes Arab votes decisive; Netanyahu calls reliance on them "national suicide." Critics call that rhetoric delegitimising.

Haredi leaders are fielding overtures from both camps, with schooling funding and draft status the demands.

Speaker vote: Any motion to replace Amir Ohana, depending on UTJ and Joint List.
