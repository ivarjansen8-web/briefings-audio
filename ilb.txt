Here is your Israel briefing for Friday, 20 November.

Netanyahu pushes Iran war as Eisenkot races to form government; Israeli tanks cross Gaza Yellow Line.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 19 November 2026 (mostly 17 to 19 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Netanyahu has reportedly told aides a renewed war with Iran is "unavoidable", while Trump says he is "not rushing". Channel 13 (via Times of Israel) says Netanyahu is pressing Washington to join a four-to-seven-day air campaign, and Trump is reportedly weighing options after a US-Israeli claim that Tehran is advancing chemical or biological weapons; Iran's Foreign Ministry calls the claim "fraudulent". Times of Israel, 19 November (unconfirmed, single-channel report)

Netanyahu reportedly asked Mossad chief Roman Gofman to plan a covert Iran operation without informing the IDF, and Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir pushed back on strike plans. If accurate, it shows a deepening rift between the PM's office and the military leadership. Times of Israel, 19 November (unconfirmed)

Netanyahu lost his majority in the 27 October election; Gadi Eisenkot holds the mandate to form a government. Haaretz reports he plans to put a government to a Knesset vote before the mandate expires even if talks fail.

Israeli tanks and bulldozers pushed past the Gaza "Yellow Line" into central Gaza, the largest such incursion since the October 2025 truce. The IDF calls it a preventive operation against tunnels; Palestinians report thousands displaced.

A mosque in the Arab village of Ed-Dahi near Afula was firebombed overnight on 18 to 19 November with pro-settler graffiti. Police treat it as arson and the Shin Bet is helping investigate a possible nationalist motive.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu's caretaker position is weakening as the new Knesset moves against his legacy. Justice Minister Yariv Levin called the repeal effort "hysterical" and "shameful"; the practical effect is that the outgoing government has little legislative power left.

A Likud tribunal is probing alleged fraud in a Central Committee vote that gave Netanyahu eight reserved slate slots. The tribunal found Netanyahu was recorded as voting though he did not take part; the result could be annulled.

Eisenkot's bloc is demanding the removal of Shin Bet chief David Zini and Mossad chief Gofman over alleged PMO election-day interference, calling it an "attempted coup". This is an opposition framing, reported via Kan; the claims are allegations.

Bennett and Netanyahu met for a "security briefing" amid unity-government talk. Eisenkot-bloc sources have said the chance of a unity government with Likud is near zero.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

The opposition bloc won 67 seats (including 19 Arab-party seats) against 53 for Netanyahu's bloc. Even so, a stable majority depends on the Haredi parties or on outside Arab-party support.

Avigdor Liberman rejects a minority government backed by Arab parties and wants the Haredim inside the coalition. Shas (six seats) is the key piece; United Torah Judaism told Herzog it would endorse neither candidate.

The Knesset elected Eisenkot ally Matan Kahana speaker over Netanyahu's pick. It signals the new majority controls the floor.

Preliminary readings passed on 18 November to repeal the judicial-selection overhaul (67 to 47 per one report, 63 to 47 per another) and the media law (63 to 49). Both return to committee.

Polls: Quiet. No new polls since the election; the final pre-vote Channel 12 poll had Yashar first.

4. Judiciary, legal system and the Attorney General.

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara's appearance before the Netanyahu trial judges moved from 18 to 25 November. The panel wants to discuss case management and has not summoned her as a witness.

The defence dropped its objection to prosecutors cross-examining former AG Avichai Mandelblit. It largely moots the prosecution's High Court petition, and the defence has asked to cancel the 22 November hearing.

Mandelblit is due to be cross-examined in the corruption trial. His testimony on the original indictment decisions is central to the defence's attacks on the case.

A former PMO employee who told police about election-day vote-buying denies a conspiracy, saying he got the information from a Joint List MK. The affair is feeding the dispute over the Shin Bet's election-day activity.

5. Gaza.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council the ceasefire could collapse without immediate action. Its report ties progress to deploying the International Stabilization Force, Israeli compliance and Hamas disarmament.

Per OCHA (cited by Al Jazeera), Israeli-held territory has grown from 58% to 65% of Gaza and about 1.4 million people live in makeshift shelters. Aid groups call the US plan a failure ("displacement by design").

An Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis killed six, including a surgeon, per Gaza's Health Ministry; the IDF says it targeted Hamas commander Mohammed Abu Ismail. The Ministry also counts over 1,600 killed since the truce.

Defence Minister Israel Katz says Israel is ready to take more Gaza territory and fold buffer zones into its perimeter if fighting widens.

Hostages: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers killed a 72-year-old Palestinian woman and wounded about 10 in Ein Duyuk al-Tahta near Jericho on 16 to 17 November, per WAFA and rights groups. Al Jazeera calls it a pogrom; a WAFA photographer was reportedly beaten by soldiers.

Settlers wounded five Palestinians in Atara on 12 November; the military says it evacuated two illegal outposts.

Settlers reportedly injured a 67-year-old man in a village attack.

Palestinian Authority: Quiet: nothing significant found.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF struck a former Hezbollah missile site near Janta in the Bekaa on 19 November, saying it hit entrances to prevent restoration. It is the second Bekaa strike that week after Monday's strike on a Syria-origin weapons shipment, the first in the east in months.

Haaretz reports Israel ended a long period of restraint at US request. Strikes and shelling also continued in the south, where Israeli forces hold a roughly 10 km "security zone".

Houthis hit a building in Riyadh's financial district and attacked Saudi cities for a second night; Iran says two more tankers hit mines in Hormuz. Regional war risk remains high.

Syria: Quiet: nothing significant on Israel-Syria in the last 72 hours.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

Zamir reiterated that the IDF needs more combat soldiers amid talks on Haredi exemptions. The IDF says mandatory service drops to 30 months in January 2027, which it calls an "unreasonable burden" on reservists.

Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee chair Liberman denied a Channel 12 report that the committee's legal adviser opposes his draft framework. He says any law needs Zamir's and the High Court's approval and plans to summon security chiefs.

Yashar proposes a reset for about 90,000 Haredi draft evaders.

Budget: Israel has no 2027 budget; the Bank of Israel assumes an interim one. The Finance Ministry is preparing a two-year plan and the 2026 deficit is forecast at 4.4% of GDP.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Gofman went to Washington with intelligence on an alleged Iranian chemical-weapons programme, per NYT and Axios; some US officials see it as "agenda driven". The trip was reportedly undisclosed to the Eisenkot bloc during unity talks.

Channel 12 reports Zini sought to wiretap five Arab MKs on election day over suspected foreign interference. Opposition figures call it political; (unconfirmed).

Liberman will summon Zamir, Gofman, Zini and the Police Commissioner.

October 7 inquiry: still no state commission; Liberman pledges one.

Open-source (unverified): analysts report new construction at Iranian missile sites such as Garmdareh and Shahroud, and CSIS reports a surge at Pickaxe Mountain. I found no open-source confirmation of a chemical programme.

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

Trump is at the APEC summit in China and reportedly pulled back from Iran strikes several times in recent weeks.

A European Citizens' Initiative with 1.1 million signatures to suspend the EU-Israel Association Agreement was accepted for examination; the Commission must reply by 12 May 2027. Acceptance does not suspend anything.

Egypt is discussing de-escalation with Gulf counterparts and holding naval drills with Turkey.

ICC/ICJ: Quiet: nothing significant found.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The shekel hit its weakest against the dollar in nearly a year around 18 November, per Globes (Vantage cites a 7-month low). Inflation fell to 1.1% in October.

The Bank of Israel holds the rate at 3.25% ahead of its final 2026 decision on 23 November. A weaker shekel complicates a cut.

The Finance Ministry floats freezing tax brackets and ending a fuel subsidy, worth about NIS 10 billion.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas called the Ed-Dahi arson a "hate crime and act of terror" and blamed Netanyahu's incitement; Lapid also condemned it.

Ultra-Orthodox Jews visited the mosque to show solidarity.

Police say the fire is under arson investigation, with graffiti reading "tefillin revenge"; one report links it to an Afula incident last month. No suspects have been named.

What to watch.

22 November: High Court hearing on the prosecution's Mandelblit petition (the defence wants it cancelled).

23 November: Bank of Israel final 2026 rate decision.

25 November: Baharav-Miara appears before the Netanyahu trial judges.

Early December: Eisenkot's 28-day mandate (from 7 November) runs out; watch for a Knesset vote on his government and the Shas/UTJ decision.

Coming days: Further Knesset readings of the judicial and media repeal bills; Liberman's committee hearings with Zamir, Gofman and Zini.

Ongoing: Trump's Iran decision (reportedly after the new year) and the Gaza Yellow Line.
