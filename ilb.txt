Here is your Israel briefing for Friday, 27 November.

Netanyahu trial may collapse as Eisenkot coalition nears; Gaza strikes test ceasefire.

Compiled from sources dated 24 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. Many Haaretz items are paywalled, so only headlines and openings were visible.

1. Top stories.

Netanyahu's corruption trial may be collapsing: the presiding judges recommended the Attorney General drop the bribery charge in Case 4000, saying a conviction is unlikely. Netanyahu declared the trial "fallen apart", while Baharav-Miara opposed dropping charges without an "appropriate arrangement" or a ruling; no charge has been withdrawn.

The Eisenkot-led opposition is closing in on a government: Yair Golan's Democrats signed a coalition agreement with Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar, with Golan set to replace Ben-Gvir as police minister. The Times of Israel reports four ministries for the Democrats, and a target of forming a government by about 6 December (reported, not confirmed).

Israel killed Hamas's deputy education minister Khaled Abu Nada in a third straight night of strikes, with at least nine dead, per Gaza officials. Hamas says the killings violate the ceasefire while Washington presses Netanyahu to implement the Gaza plan.

The Knesset advanced an October 7 state commission of inquiry bill 65 to 50 in a preliminary reading. Netanyahu opposes it; the bill would also cover Qatargate, and three more readings are needed.

2. Government and coalition.

Netanyahu is a caretaker prime minister after the 27 October election, and Likud-Yashar unity talks ended without a deal on 22 November. Disputes reportedly centred on who would lead and whether Ben-Gvir would sit in the government.

Bennett has reportedly held direct talks with Ron Dermer about a unity government (unconfirmed). It signals Netanyahu's camp is still exploring ways to stay in power.

Netanyahu is reported to be trying to lure opposition MKs to keep power. The Arab-sourced report is single-source and the outlet is critical of Netanyahu. The New Arab, November 2026 (unconfirmed)

Defence Minister Katz alleged Iran is building chemical weapons; Haaretz cites Israeli experts saying he is exaggerating the threat. Opposition figures fear escalation could be used to cling to power.

3. Knesset, elections and polls.

Neither bloc holds 61 seats on its own: Netanyahu's camp has 53 and Arab parties 19, according to a summary of the results. This leaves the Eisenkot coalition dependent on the Democrats and a wider arrangement.

The Knesset also advanced a bill on prime ministerial term limits alongside the October 7 inquiry bill. Both passed preliminary readings this week.

Yashar and United Torah Judaism reportedly agreed on key principles of a Haredi draft law. Haredim are also reported to be weighing an Eisenkot-led coalition, a risk to the religious-right bloc.

Polling is now moot for seat counts; no fresh poll dated in the last 72 hours was found. Pre-election surveys had Yashar leading Likud narrowly.

4. Judiciary, the legal system and the Attorney General.

The Netanyahu trial judges summoned Attorney General Baharav-Miara, who attended the 25 November hearing. She said she and the prosecution would study the recommendation.

Prosecutors petitioned the High Court over a 2 November ruling limiting them to re-examination of Mandelblit. No decision was reported.

Haaretz commentary warns a High Court ruling on Jewish terror risks "a dangerous illusion of action". This is an opinion piece.

The Shin Bet released an Arab Israeli held for 10 days over alleged foreign election interference. Few details are public.

5. Gaza.

Israeli strikes killed at least nine overnight on 25 to 26 November, including Hamas's deputy education minister; Netanyahu claimed the killing of a Hamas minister. Casualty figures come from Gaza officials and medics.

Israeli troops control roughly two-thirds of Gaza, and at least 1,600 Palestinians and 5 Israeli soldiers have died since the October 2025 ceasefire, per respective Palestinian and Israeli tallies. Hamas chief Khalil al-Hayya said the group cannot withstand continued attacks.

Kushner pressed Netanyahu to fulfil Gaza plan commitments, and Netanyahu reportedly agreed to let in more aid and back Hamas disarmament. The call was described as "positive and constructive" by two sources; Netanyahu cited his caretaker status.

The Board of Peace says Hamas and other factions agreed to a disarmament roadmap, but its adviser Josh Gruenbaum blames "political elements" in Netanyahu's government for stalling. Neither the technocratic NCAG nor the stabilization force has entered Gaza. Israel disputes the Board's account; the pro-Israel JNS argues the Board blames Israel while Hamas rearms.

Aid groups say about 1.4 million Palestinians live in makeshift shelters and reconstruction has not started despite roughly 18 billion dollars pledged. The Board warned of "another winter in tents and rubble".

6. West Bank and East Jerusalem.

Settlers used a front loader to demolish a Palestinian home in Jalud overnight on 25 to 26 November, which the IDF called "a serious criminal act". Haaretz reports the damage came after the High Court ordered homes restored.

UN figures cited by Arab News put settler attacks at about 53 per week in 2026; Yesh Din says 93.6% of investigations close without indictment.

Eisenkot called the 22 November settler attacks "Jewish terrorism" and blamed Ben-Gvir and Smotrich. Netanyahu has previously called such violence the work of "juvenile delinquents".

WAFA reports IDF raids in Biddu and demolitions in Masafer Yatta on 25 November. WAFA is Palestinian Authority-run.

7. Northern front and regional threats.

The IDF says it struck dozens of Hezbollah rockets and launchers at a Radwan Force camp in eastern Lebanon on 26 November. No casualty information was given.

Israel and Lebanon will hold an eighth round of talks before year's end as the IDF withdrawal plan stalls.

Trump said the US could hit Iran's Pickaxe Mountain site if strikes resume, and reports say the US and Israel are weighing a new assault. Qatar and Pakistan are mediating; Iran refuses IAEA access. The unitedagainstnucleariran source is advocacy-aligned.

Houthis claim attacks on Saudi Ras Tanura and Riyadh on 25 November, escalating the Saudi-Houthi fight. Damascus denies reports it will send troops to Yemen.

8. IDF and defence establishment.

The IDF warns a budget freeze is pushing readiness "to the edge", with a reported NIS 40bn gap and fears of no approved state budget until summer 2027. Zamir was quoted saying the coffers are empty. The report's date is unclear.

The AG told the High Court 67,230 Haredi men are draft dodgers and urged more sanctions. The report is about five weeks old.

Haredi enlistment to combat units rose 30% in the 2026 cycle, and the IDF admitted no proactive enforcement against dodgers in recent months. These are older reports.

Yashar and UTJ's reported draft principles are the first sign of a deal that could reshape conscription. Details were not published.

9. Intelligence and OSINT.

Quiet on leadership news: searches found no new Mossad, Shin Bet or Aman appointments in the last 72 hours. Earlier reports name Shin Bet head David Zini, and a Mossad leadership change in 2026.

Open-source: satellite imagery reportedly shows fast construction of a new underground facility near Isfahan (Behjatabad), purpose unclear (open-source, unverified). The Institute for Science and International Security says an outer security perimeter is being installed.

Open-source: ISW/Critical Threats and BBC Verify report excavation at Iranian missile bases including Garmdareh and Shahroud (open-source, 6 November).

Bellingcat found EU Sentinel imagery over Gaza and parts of the West Bank is delayed by 24 hours. It limits independent monitoring.

Report: Israel has known for over a decade about Iran's chemical weapons programme (unconfirmed, single outlet).

10. Diplomacy and foreign relations.

The Board of Peace and Kushner are pressing Israel to drop its "wartime mindset", and Kushner says Hamas disarmament proceeds regardless of who governs.

Egypt and Jordan agreed to push for international pressure on Israel, possibly sanctions. Jordan's Safadi made the remark on 24 November.

A bloc of Türkiye, Egypt, Indonesia, Jordan, Pakistan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE keeps issuing joint statements against Israeli West Bank policy.

ICJ and ICC: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

11. Economy, markets and the shekel.

The TA-35 rose 2.34% at Thursday's close, led by real estate, tech and communications stocks. The dollar/shekel pair was down 1.11%, implying a stronger shekel; the date on the report is slightly uncertain.

The defence budget standoff is the main fiscal story: the opposition rejected an emergency military funding bill.

No Bank of Israel decision was found in the window. Quiet.

12. Society, religion and Arab citizens of Israel.

A man was shot dead in Qalansawe, bringing the Arab community's death toll from violence to 207 this year (Haaretz count). Police believe the killing was likely criminal.

Haredi parties are negotiating draft terms with Liberman and Eisenkot, with a right-wing official warning of the religious-right bloc's collapse.

Haaretz's Eisenkot column argues the Democrats' mission is to "de-Ben-Gvirize" Israel. It is opinion from a left-leaning outlet.
