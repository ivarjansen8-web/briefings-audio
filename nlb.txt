Here is your Netherlands briefing for Tuesday, 24 November.

Jetten cabinet limps on after tax crisis as regional transport strikes and 13 PFAS sites flagged.

Compiled from sources dated 17 to 24 November 2026 (core items 21 to 24 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Regional public transport is on a two-day national strike, 23 to 24 November. FNV and CNV walked out after members rejected the employers' final offer on the OV collective agreement (cao) by 95% and 93%; NS trains and Amsterdam's GVB and The Hague's HTM keep running, but about four in five regional trips were cancelled on Monday, per CNV. More strike days follow on 3 to 4, 14 to 15 and 18 December.

The Jetten minority cabinet survived its worst crisis, but trust is fragile. After D66 and CDA struck a tax deal with PRO and Volt, VVD leader Yesilgoz-Zegerius called the minority cabinet a "failed experiment"; roughly seven hours of talks kept the VVD in. The question now is whether a three-party minority can govern at all.

Thirteen of 57 PFAS "attention locations" pose an unacceptable risk to people. Responsibility is spread across 12 provinces and dozens of municipalities, and NOS found the list itself contains errors.

Foreign Minister Berendsen is in India pressing the Insiya abduction case. The girl was taken from Amsterdam in 2016; Delhi's block on her mother's accounts triggered an unusual diplomatic push.

Cold snap: the KNMI warned of fog and icy roads as the first moderate frost arrives, with forecasts of the coldest November in 30 years.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

D66, VVD and CDA say trust is restored and will continue as a minority cabinet. The crisis began when the VVD voted against the tax plan after D66 and CDA dealt with the left; an extra ministerraad followed.

Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Raad van State, has a month to organise parliamentary support for the cabinet. He is mainly an adviser and no fourth party (JA21 had been floated) is joining.

Jetten admitted he was "too optimistic" about how easily support could be arranged and now favours working with a "blok van partijen". This is a shift from case-by-case majorities toward fixed blocs.

Jetten receives Lithuanian president Nausėda with the King on Wednesday 25 November.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 and the box 3 novelle on 12 November, 76 to 74. The narrow margin shows how little room a minority has.

The nine VVD senators said on 17 November they will back the amended Belastingplan in the Eerste Kamer. That appears to secure a majority; the Senate debates on 7 to 8 December with a vote reported for 15 December (dates differ between sources).

Eerste Kamer committees hold technical briefings today (24 November) on the Belastingplan and on the sequencing of the nitrogen (stikstof) package.

A motie requiring a debate on a new formation after any cabinet collapse was adopted on 10 November. A similar motion by Bikker (ChristenUnie), Flach (SGP) and Van den Brink (CDA) also drew majority support.

A motion to cap vague ministerial responsibilities passed on 17 November: the Kamer backed a motie for a possible ministry of Digital Affairs. Supporters included PRO, SP, D66, VVD and others.

4. Parties and polls.

Ipsos I&O (fieldwork 17 to 18 November) has PRO largest at 28 seats and VVD down from 15 to 13 (not statistically significant). The poll fits a pattern of left-of-centre strength under a weak cabinet.

Peilingwijzer (20 November, as quoted by an aggregator) shows PRO 28, PVV 20, JA21 18, D66 15, VVD 14, CDA 14. Figures come from a secondary listing and should be checked against the official page. (unconfirmed)

Maurice de Hond (20 November) put the PVV at 22 and the CDA at 13. Peil.nl via Peilingwijzer listings (unconfirmed)

Ipsos I&O reports cabinet satisfaction at a new low. The release points to the VVD voters' unhappiness as a factor in the coalition rift.

Volt's leader warned the coalition against VVD "blackmail" over the tax deal.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen met his Indian counterpart Jaishankar on 23 November and raised Insiya's case and the Dutch extradition request for her father. He called the case "legally extremely complex"; India does not treat a child's stay with one parent as abduction. No concrete result yet.

The Jetten cabinet's Gaza policy is essentially unchanged from the Schoof cabinet, per The Rights Forum. The cabinet has acted against settlement products and wants the EU-Israel Association Agreement reassessed; the source is an advocacy group, so treat the framing with care.

Former officials and diplomats have called for tougher measures against Israel, and a Woo-based report says ministers set aside legal advice on joining the genocide case. (unconfirmed)

Middle East: no new Dutch decisions found for 22 to 24 November. Earlier in 2026 Berendsen pushed de-escalation over Iran and Lebanon; see the foreign ministers' agenda below.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence Minister Dilan Yesilgoz-Zegerius (VVD), with state secretary Boswijk (CDA), runs a Defensienota aiming to legally anchor 3.5% of GDP for defence by 2035.

The Kamer debated the EU Foreign Affairs/Defence Council of 17 November.

Ukraine support continues: a further 250 million euros for drones (1 billion euros total) and extra 20mm ammunition for F-16s, announced earlier this autumn. No new announcement found for 22 to 24 November.

Quiet: nothing else significant in the last 72 hours.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Netherlands hosts an international round table on the future of the asylum system, 24 to 26 November. Participants and agenda were not found.

Minister of Asylum and Migration Bart van den Brink (CDA) has called Ter Apel "untenable" and asked municipalities for extra places. The first 2,400 were due by early November; the Red Cross emergency shelter is reported full.

From 30 November part of Stayokay Haarlem hosts 68 asylum seekers until 1 March 2027.

A cabinet letter of 16 November tightens the approach to disruptive asylum seekers: 6 million euros a year extra, faster procedures and removals.

The minister and COA asked Amsterdam to keep its emergency asylum shelter open longer.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

House prices were 2.9% higher in October than a year earlier (CBS), and sales fell almost 7%. With inflation at 3.7%, homes are slightly cheaper in real terms; new regional data come on 22 December.

Inflation hit 3.7% in October, the 2026 high, driven by energy and fuel up 14.4%. Tax-bracket indexation of 1.55% in the Belastingplan lags it, so fixed-income households lose purchasing power.

Box 3 stays on the forfait system for 2027 (notional return on other assets 6.37%); the new two-rate system (35.5%/38.5%) is planned from 2029.

Dutch gas use is down up to 10% while electricity use climbs sharply.

9. Justice, courts and security.

The PFAS list is under legal and administrative scrutiny: 13 sites with unacceptable human risk, 10 more with ecological risk, 20 unassessed. NOS says nearly half were listed prematurely.

The healthcare inspectorate (IGJ) put the Erasmus MC intensive care under heightened supervision; Diederik Gommers stepped down as department head. The board owes an improvement plan within six weeks; the report is due in December.

A Rotterdam court rules on 2 December in a case tied to the "Preventie met Gezag" approach to young offenders.

Quiet: no major court rulings found for 22 to 24 November.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Mayor Halsema recorded a video in Mumbai for Insiya, circumventing an Indian internet block. Delhi blocked 622 URLs and accounts; Halsema's India trade mission ran 15 to 20 November.

GVB, HTM and NS run normally during the regional strike; RET buses in Rotterdam do not.

An escalator accident at Bijlmer ArenA station injured several people (unconfirmed).

The Amsterdam council backs exploring a memorial for the resistance paper Het Parool (date unclear).

11. Society and culture.

Moderate frost arrived, with fog and icy roads; this may be the coldest November in 30 years.

Rents for newly let Rotterdam homes average roughly 23 euros-28 per square metre per month, per DutchReview. (unconfirmed)

FNV and CNV say bus drivers are striking against erosion of their own cao.

Quiet: nothing else significant in the last 72 hours.
