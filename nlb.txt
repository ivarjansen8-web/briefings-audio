Here is your Netherlands briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

Jetten cabinet survives four-day crisis, Buma called in; Rwanda return hub deal; prisoners freed early.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 17 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten cabinet survived a four-day crisis on Monday evening, with D66, VVD and CDA announcing they will carry on as a minority coalition. The row began when D66 and CDA struck a deal with opposition parties PRO and Volt to amend the unpopular tax plan, and VVD leader Dilan Yeşilgöz had called the arrangement a "failed experiment" days earlier.

The coalition asked Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Raad van State, to help broker working agreements with the opposition on the budgets. Sources say he acts as adviser rather than deal-maker and has about a month.

Five EU states, including the Netherlands, reached an in-principle deal with Rwanda on a "return hub" for rejected asylum seekers. It is the most concrete step yet in outsourcing returns.

Prisoners with sentences up to two years can be released up to 28 days early because of a cell shortage. State secretary Claudia van Bruggen (J&V) called it "a bitter message" for victims.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The coalition wants to continue under the banner "herstel van vertrouwen" (restoring trust), after crisis talks at the Catshuis and a Hilversum estate. Yeşilgöz had said earlier that the cabinet "could not go on like this" but that nobody wants new elections.

An extra ministerraad (cabinet meeting) was set for Tuesday morning to settle course and cooperation with the Kamer. Details were due to go to cabinet members that day.

Finance Minister Eelco Heinen's position looks increasingly untenable, according to Nederlands Dagblad. He must execute a tax plan he does not back, and economist Bas Jacobs blames his box 3 course for the crisis.

The coalition had no contact yet with JA21, BBB and SGP, the right-leaning parties that could widen its base. ND's scenarios range from a continuing minority cabinet to JA21 joining or new elections.

Municipal bodies including the VNG urged the cabinet to carry on. They fear a standstill on housing and asylum files.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer adopted the Belastingplan 2027 package by 76 votes to 74, with the VVD voting against. The PRO/Volt amendment (a wealth levy, with income tax for workers cut by about 1.5 eurosbillion) was the trigger of the crisis.

The Eerste Kamer lacks a majority for the plan: supporters hold about 34 seats and need at least four more. Treatment is pencilled in for 7 to 8 December with a vote on 15 December, and the Senate discusses procedure on 17 November.

PRO leader Jesse Klaver demanded an emergency debate with Jetten, the deputy PMs and Heinen. BNR expects it on the agenda Wednesday, conflicting with Tuesday elsewhere.

A PVV motion of no confidence reportedly failed, 34 for and 116 against (unconfirmed).

4. Parties and polls.

PRO is far ahead in the polls, at 32 seats in De Hond's 13 November poll. PVV follows on 21, JA21 on 16, D66 and VVD on 14, CDA on 12 and FvD on 11.

The last Peilingwijzer I could find (24 October, so older than 72 hours) had PRO at 26.8 seats and the three coalition parties at roughly 44 combined. That is far from the 76-seat majority line.

JA21 leader Joost Eerdmans blamed a "coalition without cement" and accused Jetten of lacking leadership. JA21 and BBB said they were disappointed that D66 and CDA dealt with the left instead of them. Wilders also blamed Jetten.

VVD under Yeşilgöz is the coalition's wild card: she called the experiment "failed" yet stayed in. Her party voted against the budget it governs by.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen told the Kamer on 11 November that the cabinet pushes a broad package of pressure measures on Israel, mainly in Europe. He rejected claims it does too little and cited 37 eurosmillion spent on medical care for sick and injured Gaza children.

Opposition parties PvdD and DENK say Gaza policy is too soft, and The Rights Forum argues it is unchanged since the Schoof cabinet. DENK wants a stop on buying weapons from Israel.

EU foreign ministers agreed on 16 November to advance a modernised EU-Syria Association Agreement. Berendsen's Kamerbrief reportedly backs closer ties but stresses careful decision-making; critics note it omits earlier Dutch demands for a suspension clause (unconfirmed, single outlet).

Berendsen's push to drop national vetoes in EU foreign policy (unanimiteit to QMV) is confirmed cabinet policy. He also calls the Commission's EU budget proposal an unacceptable rise in Dutch contributions.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence Minister Dilan Yeşilgöz-Zegerius announced Dutch participation in a Coalition of the Willing exercise, preparing the Multinational Force Ukraine. The force is meant to reinforce Ukraine's army after the war.

Yeşilgöz said in July that the Netherlands is "at its limit" on direct military aid, having spent 9.1 eurosbillion. 11.6 eurosbillion more is reserved. This is background, not from the last 72 hours.

Quiet: no new NATO or procurement decisions found in the last 72 hours. The cabinet crisis risks delaying the defence budget towards the 3.5% NATO norm.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Netherlands, Germany, Austria, Denmark and Greece agreed in principle with Rwanda on a "terugkeerhub" for rejected asylum seekers. Only single men from African countries qualify initially, for at most dozens of people from 2027; a declaration with Rwanda is to be signed early next year.

About 40 organisations warn the plan risks serious human-rights violations. Costs are unclear and EU funding is uncertain.

A Kamerbrief of 16 November sets a tougher approach to nuisance-causing asylum seekers. COA and Westerwolde agreed to move people with little chance of a permit away from Ter Apel.

Ter Apel is still under strain with controlled access since 20 May, and Minister Bart van den Brink asked municipalities for extra places. Amsterdam was asked to keep 230 emergency places open longer.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Belastingplan 2027 carries billions in measures that start on 1 January 2027, so Senate failure would cost heavily. The plan also extends box 3 wealth-gains tax.

House prices of existing homes were 3.4% higher year on year in Q3 (CBS/Kadaster, 22 October), down from 5.4% in January. Prices fell in 13 municipalities. This is older than 72 hours but the latest data found.

Rabobank expects eurozone-style HICP inflation to reach 3.0% this year and 3.9% next (unconfirmed, single source). Lending norms loosen slightly on 1 January 2027.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Men serving up to two years can leave up to 28 days early from late November, and women get a first-ever 14-day eindverlof. Violent and sex offenders are excluded; the cause is cell shortage from renovations, which leaves arrestees in police stations.

Police recorded 14,176 "nepagent" (fake-officer) incidents through October, above all of 2025 (about 13,000). 783 suspects were arrested, the youngest 13, and police expect at least 16,000 by year-end.

Quiet: no major court rulings found for 17 November. A Stadskanaal child-abuse case has a procedural hearing.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Amsterdam removed 180 elms after an eight-year Wageningen study on soil for large city trees. Results will guide planting where cooling is most needed.

Amsterdam struggled to hire a gemeentesecretaris, with the 170,000 eurossalary deterring some candidates. Woo documents prompted the Parool report.

Major works on Surinameplein began 16 November, with diversions lasting almost a year.

Sinterklaas arrived in Amsterdam on 15 November (88th edition), reportedly by police boat after a bridge stopped his ship.

11. Society and culture.

Germany beat the Netherlands 5 to 0 in Berlin in the Nations League on 16 November, Oranje's heaviest defeat in 67 years. The Dutch still reached the quarter-finals as group runners-up, after a 7 to 0 win over Greece.

Netherlands-wide Sinterklaas intochten ran on 14 to 15 November, starting in Grave.
