Here is your Netherlands briefing for Monday, 23 November.

Jetten cabinet survives tax crisis as Buma is tapped; OM hacked Tata Steel; transport strikes.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 23 November 2026 (search was thin for the last 48 hours). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten cabinet is limping on after a four-day crisis over the 2027 tax plan (Belastingplan). D66, CDA and VVD agreed on 17 November to continue as a minority cabinet with 66 of 150 seats, but the opposition doubts the repair is more than cosmetic.

Prosecutors (OM) covertly hacked Tata Steel's network and wiretapped phones in an environmental investigation. This was disclosed at a first pro-forma hearing (regiezitting) in Amsterdam on 20 November, and the defence wants access to all material because it may include privileged communications.

Regional public transport (streekvervoer) strikes hit the country on 23 and 24 November. FNV and CNV are fighting over the sector cao after 95% of voting FNV members rejected the employers' final offer.

The COP31 climate summit in Antalya ended without a deal. The Dutch climate minister called the result "disappointing" and said the Netherlands and EU would not backtrack.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The cabinet brought in former CDA leader Sybrand Buma to improve cooperation with the opposition, particularly on financial matters. Buma, who heads the Raad van State, is meant to find a sturdier financial footing for a cabinet without a majority.

VVD leader Dilan Yeşilgöz had called the minority cabinet a "failed experiment" before the parties declared "restored trust". The VVD was angered after D66 and CDA struck a tax deal with PRO and Volt on 11 November and the amended plan passed on 12 November by a single vote.

Premier Rob Jetten promised "less bickering" and structural agreements on a financial framework for budgets and taxes, plus more for SMEs. The unresolved question is whether the cabinet leans left or right on money; Jetten did not say which bloc it prefers.

The opposition is sceptical about Buma's mission. PRO leader Klaver called his help unnecessary and PVV leader Wilders said the premier should do this work himself.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer rejected a windfall-profits tax motion (motie) from Dijk and Teunissen on 10 November, 49 for and 101 against. It shows where the cabinet's broad majority sits on left-wing fiscal demands.

The Eerste Kamer is aiming for a plenary debate on the box 3 novelle on 14 or 15 December, with a vote around then. The Tweede Kamer approved the novelle on 12 November, and senators can submit written questions until 24 November.

The Eerste Kamer finance committee holds a technical briefing with ministry officials on 24 November (9.30 to 11.00). It is a procedural step, not a vote.

Opposition scepticism about the coalition surfaced in the 17 November debate. ChristenUnie MP Bikker said she saw zero trust in the coalition.

4. Parties and polls.

PRO is the largest party in the latest Ipsos I&O poll (fieldwork 17 to 18 November) at about 28 seats. The figure comes from a Wikipedia poll table, so treat it as approximate (unconfirmed).

A NOS piece on the Peilingwijzer showed the coalition tens of seats down a year after the election, with PRO at 25 to 31 seats. The article predates the crisis, so the November damage is not yet measured.

The VVD's public break with its coalition partners raises the question of its poll standing. I found no post-crisis Peilingwijzer figures (unconfirmed).

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Dutch-Israeli relations are at a low after the Netherlands banned imports from Israeli settlements. Israel's foreign minister Sa'ar expelled Dutch representatives from the US-backed Gaza coordination centre in Kiryat Gat in response.

Foreign minister Tom Berendsen declined to rule out talks on curbing the powers of the International Criminal Court (ICC) and promised MPs a letter. This matters for Dutch credibility as host of The Hague's courts.

The cabinet takes no extra measures against Israel and does not use the term genocide. Jetten says pressure works mainly at EU level, where there is no majority yet.

The "Red Line" (Rode Lijn) march in Amsterdam on 8 November drew tens of thousands (organisers: about 200,000). Jetten stayed away, calling attendance "not fitting" for a premier.

Israel's ambassador was summoned for a reprimand after criticising Dutch ministers over the Ben Gvir and Smotrich sanctions. I could not date the summons precisely (unconfirmed).

6. Defence and NATO.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The only recent item found is a 16 November report that a US proposal tying Ukraine's NATO path to Donbas concessions faces major obstacles; no Dutch defence news surfaced.

7. Asylum and migration.

Asylum minister Bart van den Brink still considers Syria safe despite a Raad van State instruction to re-examine. The IND temporarily suspended rejections of pending Syrian applications while policy is reviewed.

The Netherlands and four EU states are preparing a deportation centre in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers. The IND itself concluded Rwanda cannot be classed as a safe third country.

A rejected Uzbek family left church sanctuary in Kampen after two years, having filed a new application. They may stay during the procedure.

Under the EU migration pact the Netherlands must take in asylum seekers next year or pay to opt out.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

House-price growth has slowed to 2.9% year on year in September 2026, down from 12.0% in November 2024. It is now roughly in line with 2026 inflation of about 3.3%.

Maximum rent increases for 2027 are estimated at 3.6% (social), 4% (free sector) and 5.3% (mid-market). Figures rest on estimates for October and November (unconfirmed).

The 2027 tax plan fight leaves the budget framework open. Buma's task includes a financial framework for budgets and taxes.

9. Justice, courts and security.

The OM suspects Tata Steel IJmuiden of five offences linked to coke-plant pollution, with ten incidents charged. A decision on prosecuting individual managers is due in Q1 2027.

Defence lawyer January Leliveld wants full disclosure of what the OM collected via hacking and taps. Possible legal privilege (verschoningsrecht) is the issue.

A murder suspect was attacked by the victim's relatives during an Amsterdam court hearing. Prosecutors seek 12 years plus compulsory psychiatric treatment (tbs).

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Regional bus and train services are disrupted nationwide on 23 to 24 November, but GVB and city buses in Amsterdam, Rotterdam and The Hague run. NS trains run as normal.

The Tata Steel case sits in the Amsterdam court. It is a Randstad-region environmental test case.

Dutch weather service KNMI warned of fog and icy roads on 22 November. Expect slow commutes (unconfirmed).

11. Society and culture.

Weekly regional transport strikes may continue until Christmas. Further dates are 3 to 4, 14 to 15 and 18 December.

A Dutch OnlyFans model returned home after 11 days in US ICE detention.
