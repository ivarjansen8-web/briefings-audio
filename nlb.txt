Here is your Netherlands briefing for Tuesday, 10 November.

Jetten skips COP31 to save tax plan as box 3 majority nears; 200,000 march on Gaza.

Compiled from sources dated 6 to 10 November 2026 (some context items older, flagged). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Prime Minister Rob Jetten has cancelled his trip to the COP31 climate summit in Antalya to lobby for the Belastingplan 2027 (tax plan). The plan still lacks a Tweede Kamer majority and Eerste Kamer support is also uncertain, with the Kamer voting on Thursday 12 November.

A Kamer majority now looks within reach for the box 3 novelle (amendment bill on taxing investment returns). D66, VVD, CDA and JA21 are in favour, with SGP and BBB also supportive, but the Eerste Kamer is not yet secured.

Roughly 200,000 people joined the fourth "Rode Lijn" (Red Line) march in Amsterdam on 8 November, demanding tougher measures against Israel. Organisers want a wider trade ban and an arms-sales stop.

The cabinet named five more national housing sites (Assen, Best, Walcheren, Roosendaal, Zuidplas) for about 25,000 homes. That brings the total to 31 sites and more than 400,000 homes by 2040.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The minority cabinet of D66, VVD and CDA (in office since 23 February 2026) is about 10 seats short of a majority. Every big file therefore depends on opposition support.

Minister of Finance Eelco Heinen (VVD) did not attend the first box 3 debate, sending staatssecretaris Eelco Eerenberg instead. A majority of the Financiën committee wants Heinen to appear himself. Dagelijkse Standaard (single partisan source, unconfirmed)

Commentators describe the coalition as fragile, with PVV leader Geert Wilders saying the cabinet could fall at any time. Analysis pieces, not reporting of a concrete crisis.

Under the novelle the current box 3 allowance stays in 2027 and 2028, and the new system is pushed to 1 January 2029. Opposition parties allege a funding gap of over EUR 17 billion.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Belastingplan 2027 debate continues today, 10 November, with votes (stemmingen) on Thursday 12 November.

The Raad van State reportedly called the box 3 novelle hastily drawn and difficult to implement. The bill must pass both chambers before 31 December 2026 for a 2028 start, though the Eerste Kamer majority is not yet there. De Belegger; Dagelijkse Standaard (unconfirmed)

On 3 November the Kamer voted on motions from several budget debates, including one telling the health inspectorate (IGJ) to stay out of certain work.

The Eerste Kamer's Financiën committee already questioned Heinen on 5 October about funding and the legislative route.

4. Parties and polls.

The latest Ipsos I&O seat poll found in this search (14 October, so older than 72 hours) puts PRO at 28 seats, about ten ahead of the runner-up.

D66 and VVD are being punished by voters while JA21 and PRO gain. No new Peilingwijzer or I&O poll dated 9 to 10 November turned up.

A Maurice de Hond poll reportedly puts the three coalition parties at 44 seats combined. Indepen (unconfirmed, low-quality source)

JA21's support for the box 3 novelle shows an opposition party propping up the cabinet. It gives the minority government a route past the PVV and the left.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Jetten said before the march that "Nederland steekt wel degelijk de nek uit," defending the cabinet's Israel policy. He did not join the march, citing his role as premier.

The cabinet is not taking additional measures against Israel beyond the September import ban on settlement products. Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen rejects claims of doing too little.

The Rights Forum argues Gaza policy under Jetten is unchanged from the Schoof cabinet, with no recall of the ambassador from Tel Aviv. The CAVV (international law advisory committee) reportedly warns that international law is no longer the default basis of Dutch foreign policy.

AVROTROS reports US-Russia-Ukraine talks were due to start 10 November with Berendsen involved, while Russia does not want Europe at the table. AVROTROS (unconfirmed, single source)

6. Defence and NATO.

Dutch F-35s and several hundred troops are taking part in a Multinational Force Ukraine exercise in Poland until 22 November. It is led by the UK and France and prepares a possible post-war mission, which the Tweede Kamer would still have to approve.

Defence Minister Dilan Yeşilgöz-Zegerius's Defensienota 2026 commits to legally anchoring defence spending at 3.5% of GDP by 2035. Rijksoverheid (June context, not new)

Quiet: no new NATO decisions found in the last 72 hours.

7. Asylum and migration.

COA reception figures for 2 November show 83,474 people entitled to reception, with 34,126 in emergency reception.

The PVV pressed Minister Bart van den Brink on 3 November about serious incidents around COA locations. A report counts 173 serious incidents January-September 2026. Tweede Kamer; Moordplekken (secondary source)

The cabinet keeps the Spreidingswet (dispersal act) but says it is not working, leaving a shortfall in reception places. Eerste Kamer (background, May)

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Flash CBS figures put October inflation at 3.7%, up from 3.4% in September. That squeezes the 1.3% purchasing-power gain budgeted for 2026. Vastgoedjournaal (verify against CBS)

Water charges are set to rise about EUR 35 to EUR 535 per household in 2027.

The five new housing sites get no extra national money for accessibility. Critics say only Best fully meets the assessment framework; the Nota Ruimte follows later in November.

House price growth is cooling, around 2.9 to 3.4% year on year, roughly level with inflation.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Four explosions hit Vlaardingen, Rotterdam-Pendrecht and Schiedam within about twelve hours, after a house was shot at the night before. No deaths; police treat the incidents as separate for now.

A silent march was held for a 19-year-old killed in the Rotterdam area.

A 60-year-old Rotterdammer is suspected of manslaughter or murder in the 2 November shooting at Evenaar that killed a 36-year-old. Two other suspects were released.

Rotterdam court acquitted a 37-year-old of the fatal Schammenkamp explosion (27 October).

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Dozens of hospitality leaders oppose a higher Amsterdam tourist tax (toeristenbelasting), writing to the city.

Rising homelessness in Amsterdam is among AT5's most-read stories.

Amsterdam hosted the 8 November Red Line march from the Museumplein.

Zuidplas (near Rotterdam) gets about 6,000 homes in the new village of Cortelande.

11. Society and culture.

The national Sinterklaas arrival (intocht) is Saturday 14 November in Grave, North Brabant, 12:00 on NPO 3. The Sinterklaasjournaal started 9 November.

NS plans extra trains in 2027, including restoring a direct Groningen-Rotterdam link.

Hindu communities marked Diwali, and APG announced job cuts.

What to watch.

Tue 10 November: Tweede Kamer continues the Belastingplan 2027 and box 3 debate; watch whether Heinen appears.

Thu 12 November: Tweede Kamer votes on the Belastingplan and novelle; Eerste Kamer majority still open.

11 to 12 November: COP31 leaders' segment in Antalya, without Jetten.

Sat 14 November: Sinterklaas intocht in Grave; Amsterdam's follows around 15 November (sources differ).

Later in November: Nota Ruimte (national spatial strategy) presented.

Until 22 November: MNF-Ukraine exercise in Poland; outcome of Ukraine talks for Dutch role.
