Here is your Netherlands briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

VVD turmoil over Yeşilgöz's double role, ICC talks with US, record-cold November.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 28 November 2026 (mostly 25 to 27 November; search coverage of the last 72 hours was thin). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Dilan Yeşilgöz faces open VVD unrest over holding both party leadership and the Defence portfolio (deputy PM). Former VVD chair Christianne van der Wal said many members doubt the dual role works, especially in a minority cabinet; she addresses the party congress today in 's-Hertogenbosch. NOS, 27 November ·.

PM Rob Jetten says he is not afraid the VVD turmoil will destabilise his D66-VVD-CDA minority cabinet. He argues his own combination of premiership and D66 leadership is workable.

The cabinet survived a four-day crisis over the 2027 tax plan in mid-November with a "restoration of trust" and no fourth party or snap election. The aftershocks now drive VVD internal politics.

Record cold: November is on course to be the coldest since 1998 after record-low nights on 27 November (Twente -8.1°C).

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The ministerraad approved a bill from Interior Minister Pieter Heerma on replacing political office-holders during pregnancy, childbirth or illness, sending it to the Raad van State for advice. It is a routine but notable parental-leave-for-politicians measure.

Sources say the crisis was about the VVD voting against the cabinet's own 2027 tax plan; PRO leader Jesse Klaver called that "totally irresponsible." Options of continuing without the VVD or adding a fourth party were dropped.

Raad van State vice-president Sybrand Buma was reportedly asked to help mend the coalition, a role criticised in the Tweede Kamer (unconfirmed). It raises separation-of-powers questions.

A Maurice de Hond poll reportedly found a majority wants new elections if the VVD leaves the cabinet (unconfirmed).

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer held budget (begroting) debates on 26 November, including Agriculture, Nature and Food Quality, followed by a legislative debate; motions were filed by Van der Plas, Grinwis, Podt and others. Vote results were not available in my sources.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 on 12 November; it is now before the Eerste Kamer.

The box 3 novelle (37.053), already passed by the Tweede Kamer, was open for written input in the Eerste Kamer on 24 November; a vote is expected around 15 December.

MPs in the foreign-affairs budget debate voiced strong concern about Dutch-US talks on the ICC (see section 5).

4. Parties and polls.

Maurice de Hond (20 November): PRO 31, PVV 22, JA21 18, D66 14, CDA 13, VVD 12, FvD 10. The three coalition parties together sit well below a majority.

Ipsos I&O (14 October, latest found): PRO largest with 28 seats, coalition parties 43 combined, JA21 18. Dated; a newer I&O poll was not found.

VVD provincial officials reportedly fear a poor result in the provincial elections in March. That fuels the leadership debate.

VVD branches are split: Groningen's Willemijn Brilstra favours separating the roles; Limburg and Gelderland members see no problem. Vice-chair Annemarie Jorritsma took no position.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) declined to deny that the Netherlands is in talks with Washington on curbing the ICC's powers, saying the cabinet "cannot accept a gutted Strafhof." D66, PRO, DENK, SP and PvdD fear the court will be weakened. Middle East Eye, 12 November ·.

ICC member states (Assembly of States Parties) meet in The Hague from 30 November to 7 December, relocated from New York over US sanctions and visa restrictions. New US sanctions take effect in March; the ICC banks with ABN AMRO.

On Israel/Gaza, Berendsen told the Kamer on 11 November that pressure is multi-track: a declaration of intervention at the ICJ (South Africa v. Israel) and a national import ban on settlement goods. The cabinet announced no new measures after the 8 November Rode Lijn demonstration. Tweede Kamer verslag, 11 November ·.

The Netherlands will back an EU suspension of the trade part of the Israel association agreement only with a clear majority of member states.

The Rights Forum argues Gaza policy is unchanged since the Schoof cabinet: ambassador summoned, none recalled, no visa tightening. An advocacy view.

Klaas Knot reportedly lost the ECB presidency race to Spain's Hernández de Cos (unconfirmed, snippet only).

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence Minister Yeşilgöz spoke to Leiden students about deterring Russia on 26 November, while state secretary Derk Boswijk (CDA) visited Sweden and Norway on NATO's northern flank and procurement.

The Defensienota 2026 sets a goal to anchor defence spending at 3.5% of GDP by 2035 in law. It prioritises readiness for a large-scale NATO conflict.

The Kamer is scheduled to debate European defence cooperation, following the 17 November EU Foreign/Defence Council; costs and dependency are key questions.

The Netherlands announced a 1.2 billion euros, three-year non-military support package for Ukraine in late October.

7. Asylum and migration.

Asylum and Migration Minister Bart van den Brink (CDA) expects 96,000 reception places needed by early 2028 and holds to the Spreidingswet. First applications Jan-October are about 6% up year on year. Dagelijkse Standaard, November ·.

He reportedly told the Kamer he is willing to use coercion on municipalities and foresees a shortfall of 4,500+ beds by early 2027 (unconfirmed, opinion outlet).

A ministerial letter says the effects of the EU Migration Pact on IND processing times, courts and returns remain uncertain.

Amsterdam is considering extending the azc at the Willinklaan.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Inflation rose to 3.7% in October (CBS), driven by energy, especially gas. The November flash estimate is due 1 December.

House prices were 2.9% higher in October year on year while sales fell almost 7%.

Rents rose 4.4% on average in 2026, below the previous two years; the 2027 maximum rent increase depends on Dec-November inflation.

DutchNews headlines include data centres' growing share of electricity use and the EU's widest gender pay gap.

9. Justice, courts and security.

NL Times reported Yeşilgöz defending parliamentary debate on legalising hard drugs, but the dating is unclear (unconfirmed).

The Rotterdam court rejected a liability claim against the police by a man shot six times at age 19, finding the officer could fire to avert mortal danger.

Police reportedly seized hundreds of Amsterdam taxis in a money-laundering investigation (unconfirmed, single source).

NOS reported that Jetten said Russia is likely behind a strike near an embassy (earlier in November). Details were not retrievable.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Nearly the entire Amsterdam council (except JA21 and FvD) asked Tweede Kamer MPs to stop calling for Mayor Femke Halsema's dismissal, after a PVV motion.

The Amsterdam Light Festival has started, with the stadhuis on the route for the first time.

The council adopted the 2027 budget on 12 November; Halsema led a trade mission to India around the Sinterklaas arrival.

11. Society and culture.

The cold snap set date records at 27 stations on 27 November.

Quiet: nothing else significant found in the last 72 hours.
