Here is your Netherlands briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

Buma starts coalition-repair job as Jetten cabinet limps on; court orders binding climate targets; EU weighs 45 billion euros for Ukraine.

Compiled from sources dated 20 to 25 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Hague Court of Appeal ruled on 24 November that the Netherlands must keep drafting binding climate targets while the State's appeal runs. The court refused the State a suspension in the Bonaire climate case (Greenpeace and Bonaire residents), set a deadline of 28 July 2027, but agreed aviation and shipping can be left out for now; it is a rebuke for a cabinet already weakened by crisis.

Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Raad van State, began a one-month advisory job on Monday 23 November to help the minority cabinet work with the opposition. He is meant to advise on an orderly budget process rather than strike deals himself, which leaves the left-or-right choice for coalition partners unresolved.

Regional public transport staff struck for a second day on 24 November after members of FNV and CNV rejected the employers' final offer. More strike days are announced for 3 to 4, 14 to 15 and 18 December; services restarted on 25 November.

A poll on 24 November shows the three coalition parties at only 43 seats combined. Pro (Progressief Nederland) leads with 29, far short of any majority for either bloc.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The Jetten cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA) is continuing as a three-party minority government after four days of crisis talks. The crisis began when the Kamer passed the tax plan against the VVD's wishes, after D66 and CDA struck a deal with Pro and Volt; no fourth party is joining for now.

The coalition agreed structural rules on the financial framework for budgets and taxes, plus procedural ground rules. Jetten promised "less bickering"; the substance remains thin.

Opposition reaction to Buma is sceptical. Klaver (Pro) says you do not need Buma to make choices, Wilders accuses Jetten of outsourcing his job, and Volt's Dassen asks if Buma is being "put in front of a cart".

Voters back the restart but not the coalition: an EenVandaag panel found trust remains low, with confidence in the premier down from 34% to 27% since late October. It signals little political capital for tough budget choices.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer held a voting block (stemmingen) on 24 November, including motions on an A2 language requirement for labour migrants and on tightening the "Z-route". Individual outcomes were not found in the sources.

The Kamer also held a legislative debate on the foreign trade and development cooperation budget (Begroting Buitenlandse Handel en Ontwikkelingssamenwerking) on 24 November.

The Eerste Kamer finance committee held a technical briefing on the Belastingplan 2027 on 24 November. The coalition and its supporters hold 34 of 75 Senate seats, but the VVD senators said on 17 November they will back the amended plan, so a majority now looks likely; debate is planned for 7 December and the vote for 15 December.

4. Parties and polls.

EenVandaag/Verian (24 November): Pro 29, JA21 16, D66 15, VVD 14, CDA 14, FvD 11, 50PLUS 7, ChristenUnie 4, Volt 3. The coalition has lost 27 seats versus election day, and the crisis changed little.

Peil.nl (20 November): Pro 31, PVV 22, JA21 18. Pro is far ahead, while the PVV and JA21 compete for the right. Peil.nl via Wikipedia summary search (unconfirmed)

The NOS Peilingwijzer puts Pro at 25 to 31 seats and D66 at 13 to 17. NOS notes seat polls between elections have little predictive value.

Klaver reportedly refuses to cooperate if the cabinet makes separate deals with JA21, while Eerdmans (JA21) says any government must deal with him or Klaver. The coalition's dilemma between left and right support is the structural issue Buma is meant to ease. RD (unconfirmed)

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

An extra European Council on 26 to 27 November will discuss Ukraine financing and the EU's 2028 to 2034 multi-annual budget (MFK). The Commission proposes lending Ukraine a further 45 eurosbillion for 2027, on top of the earlier 90 eurosbillion for 2026 to 27.

Jetten calls it "unthinkable" that an EU member state opts out of the Ukraine loan, while Slovakia and the Czech Republic refuse to share joint debt. Germany wants all members to take part and threatens not to approve the MFK otherwise.

Middle East: no new development in the last 72 hours was found. Context: Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen pushed ahead with a ban on trade with Israeli settlements (due September), and Israel expelled two Dutch officials from a Gaza support centre in retaliation.

A 2026 court order concerning 48 Palestinians with Dutch residence permits in Gaza, which Berendsen reportedly did not act on, remains a pressure point for the minister. The Rights Forum (unconfirmed)

Winter support for Ukraine continues: 500 eurosmillion in air defence and drones, plus 1.2 eurosbillion in non-military aid over three years.

6. Defence and NATO.

Dilan Yeşilgöz-Zegerius (VVD), Minister of Defence since 23 February 2026, gave a speech on 11 November at the European Defence Innovation Forum. She remains the key VVD figure in the cabinet during the crisis.

Defensie signed contracts with more than ten companies on 19 November, and societal resilience (weerbaarheid) was the theme on 23 November.

The coalition agreement aims to anchor defence spending at 3.5% of GDP by 2035 in law; the 2026 total equals about 2.02% on the NATO measure. The tax-plan fight shows how hard financing this will be.

The Kamer debated the 17 November EU defence ministers' council (RBZ Defensie).

7. Asylum and migration.

Asylum Minister Bart van den Brink (CDA) called violence against Christians in azc's (asielzoekerscentra) unacceptable during the budget debate. He embraced a ChristenUnie/SGP initiative and floated registering religious violence separately.

COA reception is at 102% capacity, and PRO MP Westerveld demands that municipalities be forced to host asylum seekers under the Spreidingswet. Dagelijkse Standaard (unconfirmed)

The minister says a residence permit cannot be revoked for repeated nuisance without a final conviction for a serious crime. NieuwRechts (unconfirmed)

More "Meedoenbalies" are opening in azc's to help newcomers find work.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

The Belastingplan 2027 (tax package) passed the Tweede Kamer on 13 November and awaits the Eerste Kamer on 15 December. The cost of failure would be billions in the budget.

CBS and Kadaster reported on 23 November that house prices keep rising but more slowly, with prices falling in 13 municipalities. One summary cites +2.9% in October, but its dating is inconsistent. CBS; Hypotheker.nl (unconfirmed)

DutchNews headlines refer to inflation at 3.7% and a coalition under strain over a tax deal. The page dating was unreliable. DutchNews, Economy (unconfirmed)

The OV collective labour agreement (cao) conflict affects staff at Arriva, EBS, Keolis, Qbuzz and Transdev.

9. Justice, courts and security.

The Court of Appeal's Bonaire climate ruling is the main legal event of the week.

CBS and WODC report that about 1.3 million residents experienced domestic violence this year.

Police are investigating a body found in a freezer on a street in Utrecht's Pijlsweerd on 24 November. No cause of death has been given.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours from Amsterdam city politics found in the searches. Regional strikes and fog/ice affecting flights are the main Randstad items.

11. Society and culture.

The first snowflakes of the season were seen in Eindhoven, with fog and ice affecting flights.

The domestic violence statistics and the OV strike dominate social debate.
