Here is your Netherlands briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

Jetten cabinet limps on after tax-plan crisis; Russian drone hits Dutch embassy in Kyiv; Tata Steel trial opens.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

A Russian drone struck behind the Dutch embassy in Kyiv on 19 November, shattering windows; no staff were hurt and the embassy stays open. NOS reports the damage was probably caused by an intercepted kamikaze drone and that the embassy was likely not the target.

Minister Berendsen summoned the Russian ambassador, and PM Jetten called the incident "an escalation" and said the Netherlands will not be intimidated. Moscow reportedly said Dutch diplomats had better leave Kyiv, which NOS says puts the strike in a different light.

The criminal trial of Tata Steel for "intentional pollution" opened in Amsterdam on 20 November; prosecutors say harmful substances were released deliberately 598 times between 2018 and mid-2025. The OM (Openbaar Ministerie, public prosecution) says its evidence includes wiretaps and a hack of the company network; a decision on charging executives is still pending.

The Jetten cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA) survived its worst crisis so far but remains fragile. After the Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 against the VVD's wishes, the three parties agreed to continue as a minority cabinet.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

D66, VVD and CDA decided to carry on as a minority cabinet (minderheidskabinet) after roughly seven hours of crisis talks, but the underlying disputes are unresolved. Together they hold only 66 of 150 seats.

VVD leader and deputy PM Yeşilgöz called the minority experiment "failed" and said the cabinet "cannot continue like this". She demanded a correction in content and political balance; the coalition was set to hold "fundamental talks" over the weekend.

Jetten informed the Kamer on 17 November that the coalition continues in the same composition. Raad van State vice-president Buma was brought in to help mend relations, and Jetten did not rule out amending coalition agreements.

The cabinet will defend both the Belastingplan 2027 and the separate box 3 bill in the Eerste Kamer, despite advising against the amendment that shifted about EUR 1 billion from labour to capital.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer passed the amended Belastingplan 2027 by 76 to 74 on 12 November, with D66, CDA, Pro and Volt backing a Pro/Volt amendment and the VVD voting against. This is the vote that triggered the coalition crisis.

The Kamer rejected PVV MP Marjolein Faber's motion asking the government to propose Amsterdam mayor Halsema's dismissal. The council of Amsterdam had urged MPs to stop such requests; the vote tally was not confirmed. NieuwRechts, 17 November; AT5 (unconfirmed)

On 17 November the Eerste Kamer held the Algemene Financiële Beschouwingen and voted on motions. The Finance committee aims for the plenary vote on the box 3 novelle on 14 or 15 December.

Motions on the 2027 Asylum and Migration budget were filed on 20 November, including a call for a full asylum stop (asielstop) and measures to cut inflow. Budget votes follow later in December.

4. Parties and polls.

Maurice de Hond's 20 November seat poll keeps Pro (Progressief Nederland) largest at 31 seats, with PVV on 22 and JA21 on 18. The numbers come from a single report. NieuwRechts, 20 November (unconfirmed)

The previous De Hond poll (13 November) gave Pro 32, PVV 21, JA21 16 and VVD 14. The coalition parties together were around 38 seats.

The AllePeilingen average of 13 November put Pro at 28.7, PVV at 19.7 and JA21 at 16.6 seats. The last NOS Peilingwijzer I could find dates from late October.

Pro leader Klaver said he does not need Buma's help to mediate in the coalition. Pro is positioning as the Kamer's majority-builder on tax policy.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) is the lead on the Middle East file; the Netherlands has banned trade in goods from illegal settlements since 22 September 2026. He wants an EU-wide import ban but acknowledges member states are divided.

The Eerste Kamer BDO committee takes up the settlement sanctions decree (Tijdelijk sanctiebesluit onrechtmatige nederzettingen) on 24 November. Raad van State and OM have flagged enforcement difficulties.

The cabinet condemns Israeli calls to annex Lebanese territory and has pledged extra support to the Lebanese armed forces. I found no new Gaza-specific item in the last 72 hours.

EU foreign-affairs chief Kallas also lashed out at Russia after the Kyiv embassy strike.

6. Defence and NATO.

Thousands of pupils and students walked out of class at 13:00 on 20 November in a national protest against conscription and rising defence spending. Organisers (Stop the War Fever) say 16 Dutch municipalities took part, within an action in about 25 countries.

NRC reported on 19 November that employees of Dutch drone maker Destinus tried in Ukraine to shoot down Russian Shahed drones, and possibly took part in attack-drone launches. Russian diplomat Polyansky demanded explanations from The Hague on 20 November; Destinus says Ukrainian forces choose targets. NOS, 19 November; TASS, 20 November (unconfirmed on the Crimean Bridge claim)

The Netherlands is estimated by NATO to spend about 2.58% of GDP on defence in 2026, while the Dutch budget figure is 2.02%. The gap reflects different accounting; the new NATO target is 3.5% by 2035.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Netherlands, Rwanda, Denmark, Germany, Greece and Austria presented a return hub (terugkeerhub) in Rwanda on 16 November in Vienna. It targets rejected asylum seekers who cannot return home, initially single men, with first transfers possible in spring or summer 2027.

The IND has itself concluded that Rwanda cannot be classified as a safe third country, a likely point of legal and parliamentary contention.

The opposition says the 2027 asylum budget funds only 25,000 reception places against a forecast of 130,000, and that about 91,000 people await an IND decision. The budget debate ran around 19 to 20 November.

First asylum applications fell 13% in Q3 under the EU Migration Pact, but shelters remain overcrowded.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

CBS reports 18,100 new homes were completed in Q3 2026, about 3,200 more than a year earlier. Permits fell to 16,600 after a Q2 peak.

With 59,000 homes added in three quarters, the cabinet's 100,000 target looks out of reach. RD says 41,000 would be needed in Q4.

The Belastingplan 2027 includes a temporary cut in the box 2 top rate from 31 to 29 percent, and the new box 3 system is delayed.

Stricter rules for buy-now-pay-later providers such as Klarna and Riverty are expected from around 20 November. IamExpat (unconfirmed)

9. Justice, courts and security.

The Tata Steel trial at the Amsterdam court centres on deliberate emissions, breached environmental rules and poor maintenance. The OM says the executives' case will conclude in early 2027.

Prosecutors are seeking 17 years for a suspect in the death of an NS worker poisoned by MDMA.

An investigation reported a WhatsApp group in which men shared 13,000 "creep shots" of women.

The Kamer rejected, 42 to 108, a Faber motion to dismiss the police leadership (korpsleiding).

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Nearly the whole Amsterdam council, except JA21 and FvD, signed a joint statement urging MPs to stop calling for Mayor Halsema's dismissal. They argue she acted within the law.

Halsema is leading a trade mission to India and will miss the Sinterklaas arrival in Amsterdam.

JA21 councillor Rijpkema filed questions over a Halsema meeting on "Jewish life in Amsterdam" whose guests were reportedly mainly groups involved in the Middle East conflict.

11. Society and culture.

Airbnb hosts face stricter house-rule limits from 20 November.

The youth walkout shows conscription is becoming a mobilising issue for under-25s. Jonge Democraten chair Guy Kortes called the activists' link between defence spending and young people's future unjustified.

The IDFA documentary "The Mayor: Halsema and Amsterdam" is part of this year's festival.

What to watch.

Weekend of 21 to 22 November: coalition "fundamental talks" on revising agreements between D66, VVD and CDA.

24 November: Eerste Kamer BDO committee on the settlement sanctions decree.

Coming days: possible Kamervragen on Destinus and Russian reaction, and on the embassy strike.

Later in November/early December: Tweede Kamer votes on motions from the Asylum and Migration budget.

14 to 15 December: target for the Eerste Kamer vote on the box 3 novelle.
