Here is your Netherlands briefing for Friday, 20 November.

Jetten cabinet survives crisis as Dutch missiles hit Crimean Bridge and EUR 1.6bn defence push lands.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 20 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

A Dutch arms maker, Destinus, says seven of its Ruta cruise missiles hit the Crimean Bridge on 17 November. Zelensky had already named the Ruta missiles, and the Russian embassy now asks whether the Netherlands is "joining the war", which makes this a diplomatic problem for The Hague as well as a corporate story.

The Jetten cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA) survived its worst crisis so far, but the opposition calls the fix empty. The coalition agreed to continue on 16 November after more than nine hours at the Catshuis; the opposition doubts that anything has changed.

The government unveiled a EUR 1.6 billion defence-industry programme on 19 November. It aims to grow the sector 40% and cut reliance on foreign suppliers.

Regional bus and train staff will strike on 23 and 24 November. FNV and CNV members rejected the employers' final offer by 95% and 93%.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

D66, VVD and CDA said on 16 November that trust is restored and the minority cabinet continues. The crisis began when D66 and CDA struck a deal on the Belastingplan 2027 (tax plan) with opposition parties PRO and Volt on 11 November without the VVD.

The cabinet brought in former CDA leader Sybrand Buma, now vice-president of the Raad van State, to improve relations with the opposition. Jesse Klaver said the cabinet does not need Buma to make choices; Jetten refused to send a further letter on Buma's brief.

Jetten admitted he had been too optimistic about broad majorities and must now choose between left and right blocs. The choice has again been postponed, which keeps the minority cabinet fragile.

Dilan Yesilgoz (VVD) demanded thorough changes as the price for staying in. The substance of the deal has barely been disclosed.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 by 76 to 74. The narrow vote shows how little room a minority cabinet has.

The Eerste Kamer looks likely to pass the tax plan after VVD support, but the final vote is still to come. Nextens calls a majority "likely", not certain.

A majority in the Tweede Kamer backed a motie (motion) on 10 November requiring a debate on a new formation after any cabinet fall. The motion came from Bikker (ChristenUnie), Flach (SGP) and Van den Brink (CDA); the presidium must now propose a change to the Reglement van Orde.

The Eerste Kamer adopted the alcohol-meter bill (rijden onder invloed) on 17 November. Only FVD and PvdD voted against.

The Eerste Kamer committee wants to vote on the box 3 novelle (werkelijk rendement) on 15 December.

4. Parties and polls.

The latest De Hond poll (13 November) gives PRO 32 seats, PVV 21, JA21 16, VVD 14, D66 14, CDA 12 and FvD 11. The coalition parties are far below their 2025 results.

The NOS Peilingwijzer of 24 October put PRO at 26.8%, ahead of PVV (18.9%), JA21 (16.2%) and VVD (15.9%). D66 was at 13 to 17 seats.

Ipsos I&O's mid-October poll had PRO 28, PVV 18, JA21 18, VVD 15 and D66 15. I found no newer I&O poll.

Geert Wilders says Jetten is being sidelined. PvdD's Teunissen called the decision "fundamentally delayed".

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

About 200,000 people joined the fourth "Red Line" rally in Amsterdam on 8 November, by organisers' count. The Rights Forum, Amnesty and others demand an end to Israeli arms purchases and sanctions.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) says the cabinet wants a European majority to review the EU-Israel association agreement critically. On 6 November the cabinet also noted that other countries are following the Dutch ban on settlement products.

During the 11 November budget debate Berendsen said the Netherlands will not accept the situation in Gaza. MPs called for a halt to arms trade with Israel.

The Rights Forum argues that Gaza policy under Jetten is unchanged since the Schoof cabinet. This is an advocacy group's view.

On 16 November the foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and the UK urged Israel to improve humanitarian access to Gaza now. Israeli military figures show only about half of agreed aid trucks enter.

6. Defence and NATO.

The NPDII programme sets aside EUR 1.6 billion for 2026 to 2029, presented at the NEDS fair in Ahoy on 19 November. It targets sector turnover of EUR 15 billion by 2030, with priorities in air defence, drones, data-driven command and sustainment.

A EUR 200 million fund with Invest-NL, half paid by Defence, will finance scale-ups. Financing does not imply purchases, which follow separate procurement rules.

Destinus employees reportedly tested weapons at the front in Ukraine (NRC, as cited). Two days after the bridge strike a Russian drone crashed near the Dutch embassy in Kyiv (unconfirmed; single source).

BNR says Yesilgoz stayed silent on Destinus the same day (unconfirmed). A partisan outlet makes the same claim.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Tweede Kamer debated the Asiel en Migratie budget for 2027 on 18 and 19 November with minister Bart van den Brink (CDA). I found no reports of the outcome.

Written answers to first-round questions were published on 18 November.

Key passages of the landsadvocaat (state lawyer) advice on former minister Faber's asylum noodrecht (emergency law) remain redacted. Trouw obtained the documents via a Woo request; the ministry says the plans could return.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Rents rose 4.4% on average on 1 July 2026, with free-sector homes up 4.5%. Amsterdam rose 4.3% and Rotterdam 4.7%.

An analysis of 16 November expects inflation around 3.3% and house prices rising at a healthier pace.

Data centres used 5% of Dutch electricity last year, according to CBS (via DutchNews headline).

Regional transport strikes on 23 to 24 November exclude Amsterdam, Rotterdam and The Hague; NS trains run. More action is reported for December.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Police say they foiled potential violent acts linked to a September Revolut data breach, with arrests in The Hague and Amsterdam. Details were withheld.

Two Dutch suspects, aged 17 and 20, are in custody in Germany after a foiled bomb plot in Frankfurt.

A murder suspect was attacked by the victim's relatives during an Amsterdam court hearing on 17 November.

The OM (prosecution) is appealing the acquittal in the Bonaire murder case.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Amsterdam holds an information evening on the Lelylaan station redevelopment (19 November).

The city plans more checks and support on energy saving for businesses and non-profits.

The city is stepping up action against rising drug nuisance (overlast).

Court case: Amsterdam court convicted a man for an attack on bystanders at a pro-Palestinian protest (3 November). Background for the tension around demonstrations.

11. Society and culture.

Amsterdam Art Week runs 18 to 22 November and IDFA runs 12 to 22 November.

An Amsterdam court upheld the dismissal of a McDonald's worker for exaggerating illness; no severance.

The weather turns drier and colder, with night frost, lows of about -1 by Monday.
