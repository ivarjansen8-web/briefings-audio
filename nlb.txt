Here is your Netherlands briefing for Friday, 27 November.

US ASML bill rebuffed, Knot loses ECB race, VVD doubts Yeşilgöz's double role.

Compiled from sources dated 23 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Netherlands drew a line against a US bill that would restrict ASML's sales to China, with minister Sjoerdsma saying The Hague will not accept "foreign dictates" over export policy. The MATCH Act would push allies to align chip-equipment export controls with US rules, and Sjoerdsma warned ASML could be shut out of China entirely.

Klaas Knot lost the race to lead the ECB; eurozone leaders backed Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos after the Netherlands reportedly dropped its support for Knot. Formal steps remain (European Parliament and ECB consultation), with Lagarde due to leave on 7 February 2027.

VVD unrest over Dilan Yeşilgöz's double role as Defence Minister and party leader went public. Former party chair Christianne van der Wal said "many members" have doubts, and provincial board members floated an "elegant" exit in which she keeps the ministry but gives up the leadership.

Police placed administrative seizures on more than 600 Amsterdam taxis in a money-laundering investigation.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Raad van State, is touring the parties to advise the minority cabinet on working with the opposition, and has invited all 17 fractievoorzitters (parliamentary group leaders), including the PVV. Jetten asked for advice "if possible within a month" and says the cabinet will take the financial decisions itself afterwards.

The opposition remains sceptical: PRO leader Klaver says Buma's help is not needed, and Wilders says Jetten is passing difficult work to someone else. The choice between a left or right majority for the budget has again been postponed.

D66, VVD and CDA hold just 66 of 150 seats in the Tweede Kamer and are 16 short in the Eerste Kamer. This arithmetic is why every vote needs ad hoc partners.

The VVD debate over Yeşilgöz (see section 1) adds a second source of instability to the coalition; she was due to speak at a party congress on Saturday. No decision had been reported at the time of writing.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer wants 10 eurosmillion in winter emergency aid for Gaza, and the cabinet has no objection. The motie (motion) by Denk's Van Baarle is backed by D66 and CDA, so passage is near-certain; funds come from Sjoerdsma's humanitarian budget.

Two ASML motions were tabled in the MATCH Act debate: JA21's Hoogeveen asks the US Congress not to adopt the bill, and D66's Bamenga asks the cabinet to press the European Commission for a joint public rejection. Nearly every party signed the first; only the PVV's De Roon did not. A formal vote result was not confirmed.

A Kamer majority wants action against the shooting of cats, mainly in Friesland, but minister Van Essen will only ask provinces to limit it.

The Kamer also wants an ADHD drug trialled against crack addiction.

Eerste Kamer: the Box 3 expansion (37.053) passed the Tweede Kamer on 12 November, and a committee majority wants to vote on it on 15 December.

4. Parties and polls.

The Peilingwijzer (25 November) shows the VVD with the biggest shift of the last two months, down 2.4 points to 8.0 to 10.0% (12 to 16 seats). PRO leads at 17.1 to 19.6% (27 to 31 seats), PVV 11.6 to 14.0% (18 to 22) and D66 8.8 to 11.1% (13 to 17).

A De Hond poll of 20 November reportedly gave the PVV 22 seats, D66 14 and VVD 12 (unconfirmed, secondary report).

The VVD's slide and its leadership debate are linked: the party leader holds a cabinet post while polling at a low.

Ipsos I&O reports record-low satisfaction with the cabinet in its latest seat poll (date not verified).

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Israeli Defence Minister Katz is threatening a new Gaza offensive unless Hamas disarms, saying Israel controls 70% of Gaza. NOS reports his plan involves moving about a million Palestinians further south.

The 10 eurosmillion Gaza winter package (section 3) shows a broad Kamer majority for more humanitarian aid, even as critics say Gaza policy under Jetten is largely unchanged from Schoof. The Rights Forum is an advocacy source.

The cabinet says it was among the first to implement sanctions on settlement products and wants a critical review of the EU-Israel Association Agreement.

Sjoerdsma is working with Japan, Germany and South Korea against the MATCH Act and wants it on the agenda of EU trade ministers.

Foreign minister Berendsen says Ukraine support must continue through the coming hard winter and that the cabinet encourages US-Ukraine-Russia talks.

6. Defence and NATO.

The Netherlands formally joined OCCAR, the European organisation for joint armaments programmes, handing its papers to France.

Chief of Defence Onno Eichelsheim is a candidate to chair the EU Military Committee.

Defence minister Yeşilgöz's dual role (section 1) now raises the question of who will run Defence if she gives up or loses her party post.

The 2026 budget reportedly meets the NATO 2% norm, with a continued contribution to the battlegroup in Lithuania.

7. Asylum and migration.

Asylum minister Van den Brink (CDA) rejected a full asylum stop, a Dutch "ICE" and an end to compulsory municipal reception under the Spreidingswet (dispersal law) at the end of the asylum budget debate.

He placed 11 more municipalities under supervision for taking in too few asylum seekers, so about one in five is now on the list.

The asylum budget asks for billions more but sets no target for lower inflow, according to NieuwRechts (partisan source).

The Eerste Kamer page for the Asylum and Migration Pact implementation law (36.871) lists a 16 November ministerial progress letter.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Existing homes were 2.9% more expensive in October than a year earlier, at an average of 508,793, euroswhile sales fell 6.7% to 20,376. The CBS figures from 23 November show that price growth is slowing sharply.

October inflation was 3.7% per the CBS, so house prices fell slightly in real terms.

ASML and the ECB outcome (section 1) are the main economic stories of the week.

Dutch businesses' use of AI more than doubled in a year, especially among SMEs.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Police seized 6,470 kg of illegal fireworks in Rotterdam and arrested two people.

A suspect is in custody for the fatal stabbing of a 34-year-old mother in Houten.

Police made a breakthrough in five long-unsolved sex-crime cases with new DNA kinship investigation.

The Amsterdam court imposed tbs (hospital order) on a 46-year-old man for a killing in Amsterdam-Zuidoost.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Over 600 Amsterdam taxis were seized administratively; 26 legal entities allegedly formed an interconnected structure, with tax evasion, undeclared staff and fake driver cards. Owners can keep driving but remain liable for insurance, road tax and APK.

The Amsterdam council held its 2027 budget sessions on 11 to 12 November; no further plenary meetings were listed.

A Rotterdam fireworks seizure and the Houten case (see section 9) are the other Randstad security items.

11. Society and culture.

More Dutch primary school pupils are receiving higher secondary school recommendations.

Temperatures are plummeting for the weekend, with lows near -7°C expected.

The Kamer wants limits on cat shooting and an ADHD-drug pilot for crack addiction (section 3).

What to watch.

Saturday 28 November: Yeşilgöz speaks at a VVD party congress; watch for any change to her leadership role.

1 to 3 December: Tweede Kamer budget debates on Volkshuisvesting en Ruimtelijke Ordening and Sociale Zaken en Werkgelegenheid.

8 to 10 December: Votes on all budgets in the Tweede Kamer, plus a debate on the 17 to 18 December European summit.

15 December: Eerste Kamer vote on the Box 3 bill (37.053), if the committee majority holds.

Coming weeks: Buma's advice on cabinet-opposition relations, expected within about a month of mid-November.

Ongoing: the US MATCH Act and whether it is attached to the annual US defence bill; formal ECB appointment steps.
