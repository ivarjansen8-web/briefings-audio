Here is your Netherlands briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

Tax plan gamble: Jetten's minority cabinet courts Senate as Red Line march hits Amsterdam.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The minority cabinet-Jetten (D66, VVD, CDA) still lacks a secure majority for its 2027 tax plan (Belastingplan), with the Tweede Kamer vote expected Thursday 12 November. The plan is the first big test of whether a minority coalition can govern; coalition leaders are now meeting opposition parties separately.

CDA leader Henri Bontenbal said sending the plan on without a Senate majority would be "irresponsible" and that he wants "everything done" this coming week to secure one. He spoke at the CDA congress in Almere, signalling tension inside the coalition over sequencing.

The "Red Line" (Rode Lijn) march against Dutch complicity in Israel's occupation and Gaza war takes place today on Museumplein, Amsterdam, from 13:00. Organised by 13 groups including Amnesty, Oxfam Novib and Save the Children; the October 2025 edition drew about 250,000 by organisers' count. No turnout figure for today yet.

About 100 to 200 activists broke onto the site of a planned Microsoft data centre in Sloterdijk, Amsterdam, on 6 November; riot police (ME) cleared it and 150 to 200 arrests were reported. The action targets hyperscale data centres and Microsoft's Israel links.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The cabinet agreed on 3 November to drop the planned "freedom contribution" (vrijheidsbijdrage) for 2027 and add roughly EUR 1.5 billion of purchasing-power improvement compared with Prinsjesdag. Jetten says this is the third time the cabinet has met the opposition halfway.

The coalition is split on wealth taxation: PRO and Volt offered support if capital taxes rise by EUR 1.5 billion, which the VVD fiercely rejects. A possible way out is coalition parties voting differently on opposition amendments while backing the package together.

Jetten wants Senate support secured before the Tweede Kamer votes, while Finance Minister Heinen prefers to wait for the Kamer vote first. The visible disagreement on sequencing matters because failure in the Eerste Kamer could threaten the cabinet.

Jetten hosts Belgian PM Bart De Wever and Luxembourg's Luc Frieden for a Benelux meeting on Tuesday 10 November.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

Quiet this weekend: no plenary debates were scheduled on 7 or 8 November; the last motion votes (stemmingen) were on 3 November. Those covered the energy system, the VWS budget, aviation, and the budgets of the Crown and General Affairs.

With support from ChristenUnie, 50PLUS and Groep Markuszower, the cabinet appears to have enough votes for the tax plan in the Tweede Kamer. The ChristenUnie amendment was received positively by the cabinet.

In the Eerste Kamer the plan meets resistance from both left and right, and PRO and BBB appear unwilling to help it to a majority. The Senate is expected to vote late in the month, as the plan must pass before year-end.

On Tuesday 10 November the Eerste Kamer holds a roll-call vote on the Paternotte/Wendel bill on embryo creation (36.416), votes on the school-absenteeism bill (36.663), and debates the state of the rule of law (14:05 to 16:30). The agenda page carried odd metadata, so verify the final agenda (unconfirmed).

4. Parties and polls.

The latest Ipsos I&O seat poll (14 October) put PRO (Progressief Nederland) first on 28 seats, ten ahead of the runner-up. No November poll has been published yet.

D66 and VVD each fell to 15 seats, taking the coalition from 66 to about 43 to 44 seats; JA21 rose from 14 to 18. Roughly 74% of voters were dissatisfied with the cabinet, so a hung vote on the tax plan carries electoral risk.

Wikipedia's polling table lists PVV also on 18 and the CDA on 13 for the same fieldwork (12 to 14 October). I did not check this against Ipsos's release (unconfirmed).

The CDA held its party congress in Almere on 7 November, where Bontenbal framed the tax plan as a "crucial moment" for the minority coalition.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

After the 6 November Council of Ministers (ministerraad), Jetten said the Netherlands is exerting more political and diplomatic pressure on Israel, and that other countries are following the Dutch sanctions on settlement products. Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) is pushing in Brussels for a critical review of the EU-Israel Association Agreement.

The cabinet's ban on trade in goods from illegal Israeli settlements is set to run three years, alongside planned sanctions on violent settlers and on Hamas. Critics say the Gaza line is largely unchanged since cabinet-Schoof.

Israel withdrew diplomatic status from Dutch diplomats in Ramallah in September after the settlement measures, which Berendsen called "unnecessary".

Jetten has reportedly shifted from "genocide" to "humanitarian crisis" wording on Gaza and is not attending today's protest (unconfirmed, activist sources).

The Netherlands will join a Coalition of the Willing exercise in Poland for the planned Multinational Force Ukraine (MNF-U), reportedly 9 to 22 November. The force only activates after a ceasefire and Dutch deployment needs political approval.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence Minister Dilan Yeşilgöz-Zegerius (VVD) and State Secretary Derk Boswijk run a department that reaches the NATO 2% norm in 2026, at EUR 27.7 billion (2.02%).

The Defence Paper 2026 (Defensienota, 29 June) commits to deeper NATO and EU cooperation, and the minister reiterated in the October budget debate that NATO remains the basis of security.

Yeşilgöz presented medals to 24 service members on 6 November. Minor, but a sign of the ministry's public profile.

Critics (Dagelijkse Standaard, partisan) say the Poland exercise pulls the Netherlands toward an Ukraine deployment and that confidentiality was promised to the Kamer (unconfirmed).

7. Asylum and migration.

The cabinet wants to close a "back-door route" (sluiproute) for third-country labour migrants who work in another EU country and then are seconded (gedetacheerd) to the Netherlands. Migrants would first need three months of work elsewhere in the EU; seconded third-country nationals rose from about 18,000 in 2021 to over 29,000 in 2025. Letter from Minister Vijlbrief (SZW); still an intention, not law.

Landgraaf (Megaland site) opens temporary emergency shelter for 300 asylum seekers from Monday 9 November; both supporters and opponents demonstrated on 7 November.

About 2,050 people were sleeping in Ter Apel on 2 November, with controlled access since 20 May and only the most vulnerable getting a COA place.

COA counted 83,474 people entitled to reception on 2 November; 106 municipalities signalled willingness after the Oss example, and 18 durable municipal locations exist.

JenV published 173 serious incidents in the reception chain over January-September 2026. Moordplekken (unconfirmed, secondary source)

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

The 2027 tax plan raises purchasing power slightly, and the VAT increase on ornamental horticulture (sierteelt) is postponed. Cover for the extra spending is sought by cutting tax breaks for firms and individuals.

Existing-home prices were 3.3% higher year-on-year in August, down from 5% in March, as price growth keeps flattening.

ABN AMRO forecasts house prices up 3.8% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2027, and cites the CPB's expected purchasing-power decline, especially for higher incomes.

Hardt Hyperloop returned from bankruptcy with millions in US funding (NL Times headline only).

No new inflation or cost-of-living release was found for the last 72 hours.

9. Justice, courts and security.

The OM demanded six months in prison against a 28-year-old Rotterdam police trainee for leaking police information to criminals (computer trespass); the court rules in about two weeks.

A 20-year-old suspect in a Vlissingen murder stays in custody for two more weeks.

A Czech man was arrested at Schiphol over a false bomb threat against a Prague flight, and seven burglars were arrested on a Groningen company roof.

Mass arrests at the Microsoft site (see Top stories) will test how prosecutors handle large protest cases.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Police and traffic rules for today's Red Line march close the Stadhouderskade, Overtoom and Amstelveenseweg; trams are diverted until about 17:00.

Mayor Femke Halsema closed a party venue on Rhoneweg (Nieuw-West) for three months after shootings and violence.

Council members asked written questions on 4 November after Het Parool reported two restaurants closed over suspected human trafficking and labour exploitation.

No trains run to, from or through Rotterdam Centraal on 7 to 8 November because of signalling work.

11. Society and culture.

A cancelled Jewish exhibition is back on the table after a mayor intervened, and a Maastricht university decision on a Jewish students' meeting is under scrutiny (headline-level, unconfirmed).

The cabinet's AI plan seeks partners beyond the US and China, and KNMI launches a local weather warning system (headline-level).

The Netherlands passed 2,000 sunshine hours for only the sixth time since 1906, and had its first sub-zero morning of autumn.

Limburg is seeking UNESCO heritage status for the Pinkpop festival.
