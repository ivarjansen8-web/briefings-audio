Here is your Netherlands briefing for Friday, 13 November.

Jetten cabinet on the brink after VVD votes against tax plan; Raad van State sends Syria asylum policy back.

Compiled from sources dated 10 to 13 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 (tax plan) on the evening of 12 November by the narrowest possible margin, reportedly 76 to 74, with coalition partner VVD voting against. D66 and CDA had struck a deal with opposition parties PRO and Volt to amend the plan, which left the minority cabinet-Jetten in its deepest crisis yet.

Premier Rob Jetten (D66) meets vice-premiers Dilan Yeşilgöz (VVD) and Bart van den Brink (CDA) this morning to try to save the coalition. He cancelled a trip to the EU meeting in Dublin to stay in The Hague, and had said earlier that a cabinet without VVD ministers is not an option for him. No outcome was reported at the time of writing.

The Raad van State ruled on 11 November that asylum minister Van den Brink must reassess the security situation in Syria. The court found the policy used to reject Syrian applicants insufficiently reasoned, which may slow asylum decisions.

DNB warned on 12 November that around 1 million households face higher mortgage costs as fixed rates reset. Average gross housing costs rise by roughly 150 euros a month, which feeds the cost-of-living debate.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

VVD leader Yeşilgöz said her party does not know whether it can stay in the coalition and demanded that D66 and CDA withdraw their tax deal with PRO and Volt. The amendment adds a higher tax band on savings and asset income above 60,000 euros and shifts about 1 billion euro of burden from workers to the wealthy.

VVD Kamerfractie chair Ruben Brekelmans called the outcome "ontluisterend" but said there was no decision yet on the cabinet. The party will first discuss its position internally, so a formal VVD verdict could come this weekend or next week.

Jetten called for a "serieuze doorstart" (serious fresh start) of the minority cabinet, whatever the vote result. This implies a reset of coalition ground rules rather than a quick collapse.

VVD finance minister Eelco Heinen now has to defend a plan his own party voted against. One commentary outlet argues the conflict is partly staged because VVD ministers had approved the bill in the ministerraad (unconfirmed, opinion).

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Kamer adopted the Pro/Volt amendment first (reportedly 79 votes, unconfirmed), then the whole Belastingplan at 76 to 74. Supporters were D66, CDA, PRO, Volt, ChristenUnie, DENK, PvdD and 50Plus.

The Eerste Kamer (Senate) majority is out of reach for now: supporters count roughly 34 to 35 of the 38 seats needed after the SP said it would vote against. The Senate can only adopt or reject, not amend, and the debate is provisionally set for Monday 7 December.

On 11 November the Kamer held the first round of the Buitenlandse Zaken begroting (foreign affairs budget) with minister Tom Berendsen. The Gaza policy dominated; DENK and SP pressed for sanctions and an arms-trade stop.

The Kamer agreed on 10 November to vote immediately after the Belastingplan debate on a motion of no confidence (motie van wantrouwen) tabled by PVV's Markuszower. Its outcome was not found in the sources.

4. Parties and polls.

Maurice de Hond's poll of 6 November gives PRO 31 seats, PVV 21, JA21 18 and VVD 13. PRO is far ahead and the coalition parties trail; the polls pre-date the tax-plan crisis.

The last Ipsos I&O poll found (12 to 14 October) had PRO 28, PVV 18, JA21 18, VVD 15 and D66 15. D66 is down sharply from the 2025 election result.

NOS reports the coalition is tens of seats below its election result a year on. The NOS Peilingwijzer puts D66 at 13 to 17 and PRO at 25 to 31 seats.

Hard-right parties had threatened to vote the tax plan down in the Senate. That gives PVV, JA21 and FvD leverage after the VVD split.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

After the ministerraad of 6 November, Jetten said Berendsen is pushing in Europe for a critical review of the EU-Israel association agreement (associatieverdrag). The cabinet is also working with Jordan on medical evacuations of sick children from Gaza.

The Rights Forum argues Gaza policy under Jetten is unchanged from the Schoof cabinet. An advisory committee reportedly finds the Netherlands does not consistently name Israeli breaches of international law (unconfirmed, advocacy source).

Israel's caretaker government is still in place while coalition talks await confirmation of the 4 November election results. No Gaza policy shift has been announced.

The Netherlands announced a new winter package for Ukraine with air-defence weapons and drones in late October. Berendsen sees "no turning point yet" and prefers the normal accession route to Germany's "guest member" idea.

Jetten skipped the EU meeting in Dublin because of the crisis. It shows how the domestic crisis can crowd out Dutch EU diplomacy.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence minister Yeşilgöz spoke on 11 November at the European Defence Innovation Forum in The Hague, calling support for Ukraine in the Netherlands' own strategic interest. She is both VVD leader and defence minister, so the coalition crisis directly affects this portfolio.

The Kamer's defence committee discussed on 11 November the EU defence ministers' council (RBZ Defensie) of 17 November. The plenary follow-up is next week.

According to the Rijksfinanciën, the Netherlands meets the 2% NATO norm in 2026, and the Defensienota 2026 stresses readiness for large-scale conflict.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Raad van State ruled on 11 November that the asylum policy for a Syrian man was "onvoldoende gemotiveerd". The minister wrongly assumed a "completely new situation" after the fall of the Assad regime and must weigh the humanitarian consequences of the civil war.

The court did not itself judge the general safety of Syria. Lawyers warn the ruling may lengthen waiting times in the IND process.

The government's overnight shelter for undocumented people was reported overrun with mold and rot.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

DNB expects 16% of outstanding bank mortgage debt to face a rate reset before the end of 2028. New mortgage rates are near 4% versus around 1.6% five years ago.

House prices in Q3 were 3.4% higher than a year earlier, slowing from the previous quarter, with September at 2.9%. DNB expects the market to cool further.

Nearly 204,000 residents are months behind on health premiums, while insurers had to publish 2027 premiums by 12 November. The government estimates an average of 169 euros a month; DSW announced 173.25 euros.

The minister scrapped the goal of zero homelessness by 2030, and business bankruptcies rose 27% in October.

9. Justice, courts and security.

A 16-year-old was arrested on 10 November for the fatal shooting of 19-year-old Tico in a Schiedam front garden on 7 November. Two more suspected shooters are sought, and a mistaken-identity motive is being examined.

The Raad van State asylum ruling (see sections 1 and 7) is the main court story of the week.

NOS reported an appeal (hoger beroep) against the Erasmus shooter. Details were not available in my sources (unconfirmed).

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

A large majority of the Amsterdam gemeenteraad urged Tweede Kamer members to stop asking for mayor Femke Halsema's dismissal. This followed a PVV motion by Marjolein Faber; only JA21 and FvD did not back the council's call.

Halsema wrote an eight-page letter arguing that the influx of nuisance-causing homeless people from Europe is a national problem.

Halsema misses Sunday's Sinterklaas arrival because of a trade mission to India; deputy Esmah Lahlah takes over.

The council's licence extension for the Fabel Friet chip shop was upheld, and the Netherlands and Germany agreed on faster, more frequent trains.

11. Society and culture.

IDFA, the Amsterdam documentary festival, opened on 12 November with 253 films. It runs to 22 November, with White Tiger Column as opening film.

The Netherlands plays Greece in the Nations League tonight.

IDFA awards are announced on 19 November, and the IDFA Forum runs 15 to 18 November.

What to watch.

13 November: outcome of Jetten's talks with Yeşilgöz and Van den Brink, and whether the VVD fractie (parliamentary party) decides to stay or leave.

13 November (evening): Netherlands v Greece, Nations League.

17 November: EU defence ministers' council (RBZ Defensie); the Kamer follows up.

19 November: IDFA award ceremony in Amsterdam.

Coming days: the minister's response to the Raad van State Syria ruling and any pause in Syrian asylum decisions.
