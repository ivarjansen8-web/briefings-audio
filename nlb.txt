Here is your Netherlands briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Jetten cabinet survives seven-hour crisis talks as VVD demands reset after tax-plan row; 200,000 march on Gaza.

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 14 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten cabinet survived seven hours of crisis talks on Friday 13 November, but the deadlock continues and talks resume this weekend. The row began when D66 and CDA struck a tax-plan deal with PRO and Volt that left the VVD out, and the minority cabinet (minderheidskabinet) remains one bad decision from collapse.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 on Thursday 12 November against the VVD's wishes. The VVD was the only coalition party to vote against, and the plan must now be carried out by VVD Finance Minister Eelco Heinen.

Coalition leaders meet again on Saturday at landgoed De Zwaluwenberg, with the outcome uncertain. Jetten and deputy PMs Yeşilgöz (VVD) and Van den Brink (CDA) are joined by the fractievoorzitters.

Roughly 200,000 people (organisers' figure) joined the fourth "Red Line" Gaza march in Amsterdam on 8 November. AFP gave a far lower number, so treat the size as disputed.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

VVD leader Dilan Yeşilgöz called the minority cabinet a "mislukt experiment" (failed experiment) and said collapse is a possible solution. She wants a "correction on substance" and says adding another right-wing party should be considered.

PM Rob Jetten said he does not want to continue without the VVD and admitted Friday "could have gone wrong". A collapse could mean a fourth election in five years, though another minority cabinet is possible.

CDA minister Pieter Heerma wants to continue and says a "reset" is needed. It shows the CDA wants to keep the coalition alive.

The VVD reportedly would not continue in the minority cabinet "in this form" (unconfirmed, sources). The AD reported the same idea of bringing in a right-wing partner.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer adopted the Belastingplan 2027 package and the novelle expanding box 3 capital gains tax on 12 November. One adopted motion requires a bill for a full capital gains tax (including crypto and precious metals) by Prinsjesdag 2027.

A PVV motion of no confidence (motie van wantrouwen) by Wilders and Vlottes was reportedly rejected 34 to 116 (unconfirmed, date not stated in the source). The vote count comes from a partisan outlet, so check the official stemmingslijst.

The Eerste Kamer passed the embryo-creation initiative bill (Paternotte and Wendel) 38 to 35 on 10 November. SGP and CU voted against.

The Senate also passed the school-absenteeism bill (Wet terugdringen schoolverzuim), in force in phases from 1 August 2027. The Alcoholmeter vote is set for 17 November.

4. Parties and polls.

De Hond's 6 November poll had PRO at 31 seats, PVV 21, JA21 18, D66 14, VVD 13, CDA 11. The three coalition parties together reached only about 38.

A new De Hond poll was announced for the evening of 13 November; I found no figures yet. Earlier reports said only about 10% wanted the minority cabinet to continue (unconfirmed).

Ipsos I&O earlier had PRO on 28 seats and JA21 level with the PVV at 18. That poll predates the crisis.

I found no new Peilingwijzer projection for November. The latest in my results dates from 24 October.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen reaffirmed support for a viable Palestinian state after the 8 November march. Organisers demand an arms embargo and trade sanctions on Israel.

On 12 November Berendsen declined to rule out reported talks on curbing International Criminal Court powers. The reports concern possible shielding of Israeli and US officials.

Tension with Israel persists after the Dutch settlement-goods ban. Israel pulled Dutch officials from the Gaza coordination centre, and Berendsen called that "unnecessary".

Jetten said he would not attend the march, citing his role as prime minister. The government has not announced new arms-export steps.

6. Defence and NATO.

Dutch troops and F-35s are taking part in Multinational Force-Ukraine drills in Poland, about 2,800 troops from 11 countries. Any actual deployment would need Tweede Kamer approval.

Defence Minister Yeşilgöz-Zegerius said over 500 Dutch troops trained in Ukraine in October (secondhand report, unconfirmed).

Defence spending is 27.7 billion euros in 2026, about 2.02% of GDP by the Dutch method, against the NATO 3.5% core target. The next NATO summit is in Ankara.

7. Asylum and migration.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: Minister Bart van den Brink (CDA) is rebuilding national asylum measures after the Senate rejected the Asielnoodmaatregelenwet in April.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Health insurers' 2027 premiums rise by between 5.50 eurosand 17.50 eurosa month. DSW is at 173.25 euros, up 14.75 euros, and the average across 61 insurers is about 173.13 euros.

The mandatory excess (eigen risico) rises from 385 eurosto 400, euroswith the planned jump to 455 eurospushed to 2028. Maximum zorgtoeslag for a single person becomes 140 eurosa month.

The "millionaire's tax" dispute concerns roughly 1 billion euros in tax shifted towards the wealthy (earlier reports said 1.5 billion euros). It is the root of the coalition crisis.

CBS/Kadaster put existing-home price growth at 3.3% year on year in Q3, slightly negative after inflation (unconfirmed, single source).

9. Justice, courts and security.

Nine suspects in a large drugs investigation will stay in pre-trial detention longer, after a Rotterdam rechter-commissaris decision.

A 16-year-old was arrested over a fatal shooting in Schiedam (unconfirmed, single social-media report).

The Hoge Raad scheduled a batch of rulings for 13 November. Docket details were not visible in my results.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Most of the Amsterdam gemeenteraad, except JA21 and FvD, asked MPs to stop demanding Mayor Femke Halsema's dismissal. This followed a motion by PVV MP Marjolein Faber.

Halsema will miss the sint-intocht because she leads a trade mission to India.

JA21 raised questions about a Halsema meeting on Jewish life tied to a 25 million euros fund.

A court upheld the licence extension for TikTok-famous Fabel Friet.

11. Society and culture.

IDFA opens its 39th edition in Amsterdam with 253 films, including a documentary on Halsema.

A Netherlands-Germany deal adds more and faster trains (unconfirmed, single report).

CBS says people turning 65 in 2032 have about 21 years of life expectancy left.

What to watch.

Saturday 14 and Sunday 15 November: continued coalition talks at De Zwaluwenberg; watch for a "reset" deal, a right-wing partner, or collapse.

Sunday evening and Monday: new De Hond and other polls showing the crisis effect on D66, VVD and CDA.

Tuesday 17 November: Eerste Kamer votes, including the Alcoholmeter bill and moties.

Coming week: the VVD's internal verdict on staying in the minority cabinet, and how Minister Heinen implements the box 3 changes.

Ongoing: any Dutch position on the reported ICC curbs and the Ukraine deployment mandate debate.
