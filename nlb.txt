Here is your Netherlands briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Jetten cabinet at the brink over tax plan as VVD revolts; Raad van State rebukes Syria asylum policy.

Compiled from sources dated 10 to 12 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten minority cabinet faces a make-or-break vote on the 2027 tax plan (Belastingplan) this afternoon, after D66 and CDA struck a deal with opposition parties PRO and Volt that VVD calls a breach of trust. The amendment shifts about 1 eurosbillion from taxes on workers to the wealthy; without a majority the treasury loses roughly 2 euros-2.5 billion in 2027.

VVD leader Dilan Yeşilgöz says her party was "overvallen" (ambushed) and may not be able to stay in the coalition if D66 and CDA back the amendment. She spoke of a "fundamental reset"; coalition sources nevertheless do not expect VVD to quit.

The Raad van State ruled on 11 November that asylum minister Bart van den Brink's Syria country policy is insufficiently reasoned and must be reassessed. It does not declare Syria unsafe, but weakens the basis for rejecting Syrian applicants.

Amsterdam's council held an emergency debate (spoeddebat) on a new IT crisis around the ground-lease (erfpacht) system, which could cost the city 20 euros-40 million.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

D66 and CDA say they will not renegotiate the PRO/Volt deal. PRO says the agreement puts a majority in both chambers "in sight" and that PRO and Volt will back the tax plan if the watered-down amendment passes (originally they wanted 1.5 eurosbillion).

The coalition has not ruled out split voting, with VVD possibly voting against the wealth-tax element while D66 and CDA carry it. D66-VVD-CDA lack a majority alone and need at least two opposition parties to reach the 10 extra Tweede Kamer and 17 extra Eerste Kamer votes.

The Belastingdienst needs the plan passed before about 20 November to reflect it in 2027 provisional assessments (voorlopige aanslagen). That deadline is why a Senate (Eerste Kamer) majority matters as much as the Tweede Kamer vote.

Earlier this week CDA leader Bontenbal called sending the plan to the Senate without a majority "irresponsible" (7 November), explaining why CDA sought opposition support.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer reopens the tax-plan debate on Thursday with the vote that afternoon. The vote could slip to next week if majorities remain unclear.

A motion of no confidence (motie van wantrouwen) from Groep Markuszower was rejected on 10 November, reportedly backed only by PVV and FvD. One right-wing outlet counts the votes differently, so the tally is (unconfirmed).

The Kamer adopted a motion by ChristenUnie leader Mirjam Bikker saying that after a cabinet fall the House debates a new formation before heading to elections. D66, VVD and CDA reportedly voted in favour (unconfirmed, single outlet).

The government counts on ChristenUnie, 50PLUS and possibly Markuszower's group as alternative votes. Their support could carry the plan in the Tweede Kamer without PRO or JA21, but the Senate arithmetic remains tighter.

4. Parties and polls.

The latest Ipsos I&O seat poll I could find (fieldwork 12 to 14 October) had PRO at 28, PVV and JA21 at 18 each, D66 and VVD at 15 each, CDA at 13 and FvD at 11. It is a Wikipedia table summary, so check Ipsos I&O directly.

NOS reports the coalition has lost dozens of seats a year after the election, with Peilingwijzer ranges of PRO 25 to 31, VVD 14 to 18, D66 13 to 17, CDA 12 to 16.

JA21 leader Joost Eerdmans says talks with the cabinet and SGP on a full right-wing agreement are over, but JA21 wants issue-by-issue deals (deelakkoorden). Wilders warned this weakens the opposition.

No new poll dated within the last 72 hours was found.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) says the government backs suspending the trade part of the EU-Israel Association Agreement, but lacks EU support. He framed the aim as "gedragsverandering bij Israël"; reporting is from early October, not this week.

The Council of Ministers press conference of 6 November said the Netherlands seeks a more critical review of the association agreement and wants other states to follow its ban on settlement goods. It is also working with Jordan on evacuating sick and wounded children from Gaza.

Israel stripped the status of Dutch diplomats in Ramallah in September in response to the settlement-goods ban. Berendsen called it "unnecessary".

Critics say Gaza policy under Jetten is unchanged from the Schoof era.

Nothing new dated 10 to 12 November on the Middle East was found; the Foreign Affairs budget (begroting) debate appears on the Kamer agenda.

6. Defence and NATO.

The Kamer's Defence committee debated European defence cooperation on 11 November (16:00 to 19:00) ahead of the EU defence ministers' meeting on 17 November. Defence minister Yeşilgöz-Zegerius attended; the debate covers costs, dependence on others and who decides on deployment. No outcome report was found.

Yeşilgöz, who is both VVD leader and Defence minister, is thus at the centre of the coalition row while handling the defence file.

The Netherlands will fund training centres in Ukraine, and agreement exists on a 90 eurosbillion EU Ukraine Support Loan.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Raad van State found the June 2025 Syria country policy, which described a "lower level of arbitrary violence", insufficiently reasoned. Humanitarian conditions were not weighed and a "completely new situation" after Assad's fall could not be assumed.

The ruling concerned one man who applied in late 2023 and does not itself grant status. Van den Brink says he is studying it; VluchtelingenWerk stresses applications are not automatically granted.

Asylum inflow in week 45 was about 790.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Inflation rose to 3.7% in October from 3.4% in September (CBS). The jump raises the political weight of the purchasing-power package in the tax plan.

The 2027 tax plan carries 1.5 eurosbillion for purchasing power, and the cabinet postponed Box 3 reform by a year with 350 eurosmillion set aside for relief.

House prices rose 3.4% in Q3 and Rabobank sees growth slowing from 8.8% this year to 4.8% in 2027 as ex-rental homes add supply. The figures are reported secondhand; check CBS.

The Netherlands and Germany agreed more cross-border trains and shorter journey times, with a focus on Berlin.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Police arrested a 16-year-old after a 19-year-old was shot dead while gardening in Schiedam.

NL Times reports an explosion destroyed at least five apartments in The Hague (unconfirmed, headline only).

The Arnhem court sentenced a 52-year-old to 12 years plus tbs for killing his (ex-)partner, and Haarlem court gave a Hoofddorp tattoo-shop owner 5 years 8 months over a worker's death.

An Amsterdam court upheld the licence extension for the Fabel Friet chip shop despite resident complaints.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Wethouder Scholtes put the erfpacht IT crisis cost at 20 euros-40 million, after the VVD pushed to make confidential figures public. The old Hermes system (since 2004) has largely lost technical support since October.

Officials reportedly knew from March 2026, but Scholtes learned only on 19 October. The auditor also flagged unlawful procurement; opposition criticism was sharp but no motion of no confidence followed.

IDFA, the Amsterdam documentary festival, opens Thursday for its 39th edition with 253 films from 80 countries.

11. Society and culture.

Children across the country celebrated Sint-Maarten on 11 November with lanterns and door-to-door songs.

Education minister Rianne Letschert visits Sint Maarten, Curaçao and Aruba on 16 to 20 November, including the four-country ministerial meeting.

Health care: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.
