Here is your Netherlands briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Jetten cabinet survives crisis talks as VVD demands reset; tax plan passes 76 to 74; Gaza march pressure.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten minority cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA) survived a Friday crisis meeting but talks resumed on Sunday around 11:00 at De Zwaluwenberg estate in Hilversum with no outcome yet. The trigger was a tax-plan deal that D66 and CDA struck with PRO and Volt without the VVD; the cabinet is intact but badly damaged.

The Tweede Kamer passed the amended Belastingplan 2027 by 76 votes to 74 on 12 November, with the VVD voting against. The amendments shift about EUR 1.5 billion of tax burden from labour to wealth; the Eerste Kamer is the next hurdle.

A PVV motion of no confidence (motie van wantrouwen) was rejected 116 to 34. Parliament is not ready to topple the cabinet, even as the coalition itself wobbles.

More than 200,000 people joined the fourth Red Line (Rode Lijn) Gaza march in Amsterdam on 8 November. The cabinet's reply on 11 November announced no new measures against Israel.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

VVD leader and Deputy PM Dilan Yesilgoz called the cabinet a "failed experiment" and demanded a "fundamental reset". She wants D66 and CDA to stop building majorities with the left.

PM Rob Jetten wants a "serious fresh start" (doorstart) and cancelled a Sunday trip to Sint Eustatius. He says improvements are needed on substance, politics and personal relations.

Eight hours of talks at the Ministry of General Affairs on Friday 13 November ended without a breakthrough. Parties remained far apart; this is the first big test of a minority cabinet that needs ad hoc majorities.

CDA minister Pieter Heerma said he feels "a great responsibility" to continue, and CDA leader Bontenbal had earlier called sending the plan to the Senate without a majority a gamble. The CDA is positioning itself as the cabinet's glue.

Telegraaf sources said the VVD sees the minority cabinet "in this form" as no longer viable (unconfirmed). This reflects a single-source report relayed by NL Times.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Kamer also adopted the novelle expanding the box 3 wealth-gains tax (vermogenswinstbelasting) on 12 November. JA21, SGP and BBB backed it, which suggests a Senate majority for that part.

The coalition lacks a Senate majority for the Belastingplan on its own; the Eerste Kamer date is unclear. DeZaak cites 7 to 8 December while Van Lanschot Kempen cites 15 December; the cabinet needs adoption before 31 December.

A motion for a full wealth-gains tax covering precious metals and crypto by Prinsjesdag 2027 was adopted. It adds pressure for a broader capital-income regime.

On 10 November a request by Markuszower for a debate on a no-confidence motion was rejected 117 to 33. The hard right is forcing repeated confidence votes.

4. Parties and polls.

Peil.nl (6 November) has PRO at 31 seats, PVV 21, JA21 18, D66 14, VVD 13, FvD 12 and CDA 11. If accurate, the three coalition parties would hold roughly 38 seats, far below their current 150-seat-chamber strength.

Maurice de Hond reported the coalition sinking to 38 seats while PRO rises. The crisis may deepen this trend.

Poll averages put Progressief Nederland (PRO) at 28.5, PVV at 19.7 and JA21 at 17.1 seats. Poll sources differ; no fresh Peilingwijzer figure was found.

Current Kamer seats: D66 26, VVD 22, PRO 20, PVV 19. The gap between seats and polls explains why new elections are unattractive to the coalition.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen told parliament on 11 November that the cabinet announces no new measures against Israel after the Red Line march. He reaffirmed support for a Palestinian state and Gaza humanitarian access; DENK and SP called the reply inadequate.

Jetten said the Netherlands "certainly sticks its neck out" and prefers a broad European front on pressure. The line signals no unilateral sanctions.

Israel recalled its ambassador to the Netherlands after he criticised ministers Ben-Gvir and Smotrich in a Dutch newspaper. The Netherlands declared both men persona non grata in July 2025.

Berendsen declined to rule out reported talks with Washington on curbing ICC powers. He said that does not reflect the Dutch position (Middle East Eye, single source, unconfirmed).

The Kamer voted on a Van Baarle (DENK) motion on the Rapid Support Forces in Sudan. Outcome not found.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence Minister Yesilgoz said supporting Ukraine is "also in our own interest" on 11 November. This follows a EUR 500 million winter package for air defence announced in October.

The navy escorted four Russian ships in the North Sea in the week of 4 to 10 November, and a Russian convoy passed the Dutch coast on 10 to 11 November. Routine shadowing but a reminder of North Sea exposure.

Queen Maxima was sworn in as a reservist on 13 November. A symbolic boost to reserve recruitment.

The Netherlands meets the NATO 2% norm in 2026 even under the most conservative calculation.

7. Asylum and migration.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Last notable item: first asylum applications fell 13% in Q3 2026 while reception centres remain overcrowded, and Deputy PM and Asylum Minister Bart van den Brink is placing more municipalities under supervision.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Health insurance premiums for 2027 rose on average 9.4%, from EUR 158.30 to EUR 173.13 per month. The gap between cheapest and dearest basic policy is EUR 649 a year, and the deductible (eigen risico) rises to EUR 400.

Inflation reached 3.7% in October from 3.4% in September (CBS flash estimate). Rabobank expects HICP inflation of 3.0% this year.

Existing-home prices rose 3.4% year on year in Q3, but momentum is slowing. CBS says growth is strongest in the east and north.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Police found a suspected 3D-printed weapons workshop in an Eindhoven home: five firearms, six knuckledusters and two 3D printers. Two men (64 and 66) were reportedly arrested after a 9 November raid; one outlet says nobody is arrested yet.

Two people were arrested over sabotage of the Prinsjesdag train (unconfirmed, headline only).

A man was tried over assaults during pro-Palestinian UvA protests; the OM listed a ruling on 3 November.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Sinterklaas arrives in Amsterdam on Sunday 15 November, after the national arrival in Grave on 14 November. In Grave a bus of blackface Pieten appeared (single partisan source, unconfirmed).

Amsterdam announced a tougher approach to drug nuisance: more shelter places, more care and stricter enforcement.

GVB is testing new metro routes in Zuidoost on 14 to 15 November, and the city is preparing the first New Year with a fireworks ban.

11. Society and culture.

Hilversum and Wijdemeren held municipal council elections on Wednesday 11 November, seen as a mood test for The Hague. Results were not found.

72% of Dutch people still call the helper Zwarte Piet, per a PanelInzicht poll for AD cited by NieuwRechts (unconfirmed).

What to watch.

Sunday 15 November, from 11:00: outcome of the Zwaluwenberg coalition talks: relaunch, VVD exit or cabinet fall.

Coming days: whether the VVD pursues a "reset" of policy and personnel, and whether Jetten seeks formal new agreements.

Early December (7 to 8 or 15 December, unconfirmed): Eerste Kamer vote on Belastingplan 2027 and the box 3 novelle; deadline 31 December.

23 November: CBS publishes October existing-home prices; 1 December: flash inflation for November.
