Here is your Netherlands briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

Jetten cabinet survives tax crisis as Buma is called in; Syria 'safe' row and Rwanda return hub.

Compiled from sources dated 16 to 18 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten cabinet survived its tax-plan crisis, but the underlying split is unresolved. After four days of crisis talks the D66-VVD-CDA minority cabinet agreed to carry on, and NOS judged it "back to square one" because the so-called weeffout (structural flaw) was not repaired. The coalition has no majority of its own, so every budget vote needs outside support.

Sybrand Buma, former CDA leader and vice-president of the Raad van State, has one month to improve cabinet-Kamer relations. Sources describe him mainly as an adviser who helps agree working arrangements with opposition parties on the budgets, not as a deal-maker. Opposition parties mocked the move: Klaver said "you don't need Buma to make decisions."

Asylum minister Van den Brink says Syria is still safe for return despite a Raad van State ruling. The Council of State ruled on 11 November that the policy was insufficiently reasoned, and his letter of 17 November restates it with new justification. About 8,000 cases are pending in court.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

D66, VVD and CDA said on Monday evening that they will continue as a minority cabinet. The crisis began when D66 and CDA struck a deal with Pro and Volt that raised wealth taxation by about EUR 1 billion, and the VVD then voted against its own cabinet's tax plan.

Jetten admitted he was "too optimistic" about broad majorities and said he will now negotiate with left or right blocs. This ends the pretence of a single consensus approach. The source is a podcast-style summary, so treat it as (unconfirmed).

VVD parliamentary leader Ruben Brekelmans said his party wants to "leave the chaos behind" and restore political balance. The VVD's willingness to stay is conditional.

JA21 leader Dassen called the coalition's problem a "weeffout" from day one and urged Jetten and Bontenbal not to be "blackmailed" by the VVD. Right-wing opposition is positioning itself as the alternative partner.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 (tax plan) 76 to 74 on 12 November. The VVD voted against the whole package, and the margin shows how fragile the majority is.

The nine VVD senators announced on 17 November that they will back the amended plan in the Eerste Kamer. A vote is expected on 15 December, with debate on 7 to 8 December (one source), and the Senate can only accept or reject.

An adopted motie asks the cabinet to submit a full vermogenswinstbelasting (capital gains tax) bill by Prinsjesdag 2027, for introduction in 2030. From 2029 a 38.5% box 3 rate applies above EUR 60,000.

The Kamer held an eight-hour debate on the political situation that ran late into Tuesday night. Opposition MPs said the cabinet still has not chosen between left and right partners.

On 17 November the Kamer adopted moties on the Sahel region and on digitalisation and police budgets. One motie by Van der Plas passed unanimously.

4. Parties and polls.

Peil.nl (De Hond), fieldwork 13 November, gives Pro 32 seats, PVV 21, JA21 16, and VVD and D66 14 each. JA21 lost two seats since 6 November, and Pro's lead has grown during the crisis. This is a single pollster, and no newer poll turned up.

The Ipsos I&O poll of mid-October had Pro at 28, PVV and JA21 at 18 each, and VVD and D66 at 15 each. Both coalition leaders were down about 5 seats from the election.

Eerdmans (JA21) attacked CDA leader Bontenbal for dropping the VVD in favour of a deal with Pro and Volt. Bontenbal said Klaver "gave away a lot" to reach it.

Wilders asked why the prime minister is "downgraded to the second row, as an intern for Buma." The PVV remains a vocal opponent.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign minister Tom Berendsen met NGOs including Amnesty and MSF on 17 November about Israel and the Palestinian territories, and also spoke with CIDI, StandWithUs and Christians for Israel. He called Israel "still a friendly nation" while the Kamer pressed for more.

The Rights Forum says Gaza policy under Jetten is unchanged from the Schoof cabinet. This is an advocacy source, but it matches the August-September pattern in which Israel expelled Dutch officials over the Dutch settlement-goods ban.

More than 230 former ministers, diplomats and officials have urged an arms embargo and suspension of the EU-Israel association agreement. Their call frames the pressure on Berendsen.

The US reissued security warnings to Americans in the Gulf after Iranian strikes on 18 November (unconfirmed). No Dutch reaction was found, and the report is a single liveblog entry.

The European Commission's 17 November forecast sees Dutch GDP growth of 1.8% in 2026 and inflation of 3.0%.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence minister Dilan Yesilgoz said on 11 November that supporting Ukraine "is also in our own interest." She also looked to Ukraine for lessons in modern warfare.

The Netherlands has put EUR 250 million into drones for Ukraine, bringing its Drone Line contribution to EUR 1 billion. The figure dates from September, and no new pledge was found this week.

The Defensienota 2026 follows the coalition goal of 3.5% of GDP on defence by 2035.

Defensie answered questions on 18 November about the planned barracks in Zeewolde.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Netherlands, Germany, Austria, Greece and Denmark are close to a deal on a Rwanda return hub for rejected asylum seekers. Rwanda would offer about 100 places, first transfers could come in spring or summer 2027, and costs are unknown.

Pro MP Lisa Westerveld said at the 18 November budget debate that the plan opens the door to the far right and that "terugkeerhub" is misleading. Right-wing parties reacted indignantly. The debate outcome was not found.

The IND itself concluded Rwanda cannot be classified as a safe third country. The cabinet says the plan is in line with EU law.

The IND has paused decisions rejecting pending Syrian applications while policy is reviewed. The EU Court of Justice hears a similar question on 26 November.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

CBS flash estimate puts October inflation at 3.7%, up from 3.4% in September. Rents are a large driver.

Existing home prices rose 3.4% year on year in Q3 and fell in 13 municipalities. Rising interest rates and ex-rental stock are flattening prices.

New mid-market rent contracts fell 16% in Q3 (unconfirmed, headline only).

A Tweede Kamer debate on the Staat van de volkshuisvesting is scheduled for 19 November with housing minister Boekholt-O'Sullivan. The date was moved from 28 October.

More than half of Dutch students are struggling financially, per Armoedefonds.

9. Justice, courts and security.

A murder suspect was attacked by a victim's relative during a hearing in Amsterdam on 17 November. The 40-year-old is accused of a December 2024 stabbing near Muiderpoort; prosecutors seek 12 years plus psychiatric treatment, and two arrested men were released.

The suspected gunman in the 1 September Overasselt security-guard killing reportedly confessed (unconfirmed). He allegedly said he acted in "total panic."

The OM demanded six months in a case about leaking police information; the court rules in about two weeks.

About 8,000 Syrian asylum cases are in court after the Raad van State ruling.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Nearly the whole Amsterdam council asked the Tweede Kamer to stop requests to dismiss mayor Femke Halsema. Only JA21 and FvD did not sign, and the dispute follows a PVV motie.

Amsterdam opened a lying-down public toilet at the city hall on 18 November, a first in the Netherlands. The city also plans EUR 142 million for school buildings in 2027.

Het Parool reported via Woo documents that finding a new gemeentesecretaris was difficult.

11. Society and culture.

The PFAS list of polluted sites is riddled with errors, say the local authorities responsible for clean-up.

Cheap supermarket groceries from a budget-brand price war are expected not to last.

KNMI forecasts colder, drier weather toward the weekend with possible night frost.
