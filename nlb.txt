Here is your Netherlands briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

Jetten cabinet survives crisis, Buma called in to mend ties; Red Line fallout on Israel policy.

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 17 November 2026 (most items 15 to 17 November). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten minority cabinet survived a four-day crisis, with D66, VVD and CDA saying on Monday night that "trust is restored". The row began over the 2027 tax plan (Belastingplan), and the three parties agreed to carry on without bringing in any other party.

Former CDA leader Sybrand Buma is brought in to review the first months of the minority government and to repair relations with the opposition. Jetten says Buma will advise on how a minority cabinet works, not on policy content, which makes this a test of the cabinet's credibility.

NOS calls the crisis "defused with an empty story", saying the coalition continues on willpower and the left-right choice has been postponed again. The opposition remains sceptical.

Arriva scrapped its Schiphol-Zwolle night train after the trade and industry appeals tribunal (CBb) struck down municipal subsidies as contrary to EU law. It is a small story, but it shows how far state-aid rules reach into local transport policy.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

Jetten told a press conference on 17 November that the cabinet will work "with a different dynamic", including structural agreements on budget and tax decisions and more support for SMEs. He rejected the image of a cabinet running "like a headless chicken" ("kip zonder kop").

Finance Minister Heinen (CDA) will not appear in the Tweede Kamer on the crisis, although Jetten did not rule it out after the budget debates. The Kamer had asked about Heinen's messages (appjes) during the crisis.

VVD leader and deputy PM Dilan Yeşilgöz had called the minority cabinet a "failed experiment" and said it "cannot continue like this" before the deal. The VVD voted against the tax plan, and its voters are restless (see Parties and polls).

The cabinet was installed on 23 February 2026 as a D66-CDA-VVD minority government, so it must find a majority case by case. That is why a left-leaning amendment (PRO and Volt) could decide the tax plan.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer held a debate on 17 November with Jetten and deputy PMs Yeşilgöz and Van den Brink on the crisis outcome. PRO leader Jesse Klaver said Buma is not needed to make choices and voiced "great concern that little has changed".

The Belastingplan 2027 passed the Tweede Kamer 76 to 74 on 12 November with the help of small left parties; the VVD voted against. The Eerste Kamer finance committee discussed procedure on 17 November.

The Eerste Kamer passed the alcohol-meter (alcoholmeter) driving law on 17 November, with FVD and PvdD against (unconfirmed, single-source summary). A related drink-driving bill was adopted without a vote.

The question hour (vragenuur) on 17 November covered GPS disruption near Schiphol, students' money problems and errors in the government's PFAS-contamination list. D66's Dion Huidekooper asked about the PFAS list, which NOS reported is "riddled with errors".

A narrow Eerste Kamer majority (38 to 35) approved the embryo-culture initiative bill on 10 November. It shows how tight the Senate arithmetic is on ethical files.

4. Parties and polls.

Maurice de Hond's Peil.nl poll of 13 November puts PRO first on 32 seats, then PVV 21, JA21 16, VVD 14, D66 14 and CDA 12. The three coalition parties together hold roughly 40 seats, far from a majority.

In the same poll, 56% say the VVD should leave the cabinet, and 59% of VVD voters want that (sources differ on whether 56% wants new elections). This is the pressure behind Yeşilgöz's position. Dagelijkse Standaard, 13 November (unconfirmed)

Ipsos I&O's last seat poll (16 October) had D66 and VVD at 15 seats each, five fewer than in September, with Yeşilgöz's leader grade at 3.7 and 74% dissatisfied with the cabinet. No newer Ipsos I&O poll turned up.

NOS reported that the coalition is tens of seats below its 2025 election result one year on.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Organisers say about 200,000 people joined the Amsterdam "Red Line" (Rode Lijn) march on 8 November, demanding an arms embargo and a wider trade ban on Israel. Jetten said the Netherlands is "exerting greater political and diplomatic pressure" on Israel to respect its Gaza agreements.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) announced no new measures against Israel in a letter of 11 November. It answered a request from MP Kati Piri, and motions to recognise genocide or impose a full trade embargo were recently rejected.

A national ban on goods from illegal Israeli settlements has applied since September, and Israel responded by stripping Dutch diplomats in Ramallah of their status. Berendsen called the move "unnecessary" and said the ban "is not directed at Israel".

On Ukraine, Berendsen has pushed for keeping all funding options open, including frozen Russian assets, beyond the EU's EUR 90bn loan for 2026 to 27. The reports are months old, so treat them as background.

6. Defence and NATO.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background: the Netherlands backs the NATO 3.5% (+1.5%) spending path, and the NOS noted extra costs of EUR 16 to 19bn a year were not yet funded. The crisis over the tax plan keeps budget room uncertain.

7. Asylum and migration.

Asylum Minister Bart van den Brink (CDA) rejected a BBB proposal to give boa's and police more powers on COA (asylum reception) grounds. He sees limited added value, since registering the grounds as public space would not change their actual status.

COA figures at 9 November show 83,429 people entitled to reception, with the system reported at about 102% of capacity. Part of them are in emergency shelter.

A letter of 10 November says 1,400 resettled refugees will arrive in 2026 and 2027, while the minister calls reception "untenable". Resettled refugees receive a residence permit on arrival. NieuwRechts, November (unconfirmed)

The ministry published a list of serious incidents around COA locations on 3 November (about 175 incidents), the first since 1 January 2025. The PVV asked questions in the question hour.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Rents rose 4.4% on 1 July 2026 (3.8% for existing contracts), according to CBS. Amsterdam rose 4.3% and Rotterdam 4.7%.

Existing-home prices rose 3.3% year on year in Q3, the seventh quarter in a row of weaker growth, and fell slightly in real terms. Bouwen and Uitvoering (unconfirmed)

Groceries are cheaper because budget brands are in a price war, though experts expect this to be temporary.

The tax plan fight (see sections 1 to 3) shapes household finances for 2027, and the Eerste Kamer must still approve it.

9. Justice, courts and security.

The CBb ruled on 17 November that subsidies from Almere and Lelystad for Arriva's night train breach EU law, with up to EUR 176,923 per municipality for 2025 to 26 affected. Subsidy already paid must be repaid.

The Noord-Holland court sentenced a 33-year-old man to 12 years for killing his father in Heerhugowaard and disposing of the body. Rechtspraak, 17 November (unconfirmed)

The Eerste Kamer justice committee scheduled a letter on a draft VOG (certificate of conduct) police-data regulation for 17 November. It was an agenda item, not a decision.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Amsterdam approved a sharp tourist tax increase on 12 November despite objections from businesses.

A broad majority of the Amsterdam council urged MPs to stop asking for Mayor Femke Halsema's dismissal, after PVV MP Marjolein Faber's motion on 10 November. Only JA21 and FvD declined to join, and the councillors say mayors' independence is under pressure.

Protesters broke into a Microsoft data-centre site in Amsterdam on 6 November.

Amsterdam's Red Line march on 8 November started at the Museumplein.

11. Society and culture.

The KNMI issued code yellow for severe gusts in Zuid-Holland, Zeeland and parts of Utrecht, Limburg and Noord-Brabant, and rain worsened the Tuesday rush hour.

More students are asking for help with money problems, according to a NOS report that prompted ChristenUnie questions.

The Dutch men's football team reportedly lost heavily to Germany in the Nations League on 16 November (unconfirmed, single-source score).

The IDFA documentary "The Mayor: Halsema and Amsterdam" screened on 17 November.
