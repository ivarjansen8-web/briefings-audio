Here is your Netherlands briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Jetten cabinet limps on as Buma is called in; Syria policy and Destinus drone row.

Compiled from sources dated 11 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Prime Minister Rob Jetten asked Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Raad van State, to advise on how the minority cabinet can work with the Kamer on finances. Buma has a free hand, about a month to report, and reportedly starts Monday; Jetten says the role will not be political.

The Jetten cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA) survived a four-day crisis over the Belastingplan 2027 but with, in NOS's words, an "empty story" and little substance. The coalition says it has restored trust, yet the crisis atmosphere lingers.

The Raad van State ordered Asylum Minister Bart van den Brink to re-justify his June 2025 policy that Syria is safe for return; he kept his line in a 17 November letter. It matters because the IND has paused rejections of pending Syrian cases.

An NRC investigation says staff of Dutch arms maker Destinus operated interceptor drones in Ukraine, and Russia is demanding an explanation. The defence ministry reportedly regards such acts as direct participation in hostilities.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

VVD leader Dilan Yesilgöz called the minority cabinet a "failed experiment" before the deal to carry on. The row began when D66 and CDA backed a PRO/Volt amendment (a "millionaire tax" in box 3) that the VVD opposed.

The cabinet promised "structural agreements" on the financial framework for budget and tax decisions, plus procedural ground rules. It also announced extra support for small businesses, which looks like a VVD concession.

The cabinet wants to work more with a "blok van partijen" (bloc of parties) rather than ad hoc majorities. The opposition questioned the role of Buma and of leaked messages ("appjes") from Finance Minister Heinen.

Pro leader Klaver says Buma's help is not needed; PVV says the cabinet will "become nothing"; JA21 wants the cabinet to seek right-wing support. A Substack column argues Buma's role clashes with the Raad van State's statutory tasks (opinion).

The cabinet has governed as a minority since 23 February 2026. Its parliamentary base is thin, so every vote is a potential crisis.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer passed the Belastingplan 2027 on 12 November by 76 to 74, with the VVD voting against. The box 3 novelle (wealth-gains tax) also passed in amended form.

The package raises the top box 3 rate to 38.5% above a EUR 60,000 return and cuts the regular rate from 36% to 35.5%. The current forfait system with tegenbewijsregeling stays for 2027 to 2028.

The Eerste Kamer (Senate) must still decide, and sources conflict on timing (7 to 8 December versus 15 December). Check the Senate agenda; the Belastingdienst needs the rates settled for next year's assessments.

The Kamer's next plenary sessions are on 24 and 25 November, with the week 48 vote list (stemmingslijst) published. I could not confirm its contents.

4. Parties and polls.

Ipsos I&O (17 to 18 November): PRO is largest, JA21 and PVV tie at 19 seats each and VVD falls to 13 (from 15, not significant). JA21 has grown by 10 seats since the election while the PVV is down 7.

Ipsos I&O reports satisfaction with the cabinet at a new low. The crisis likely weighs on the coalition parties.

The Peilingwijzer (20 November), as listed by an aggregator, shows roughly PRO 28, PVV 20, JA21 18, D66 15, VVD 14, CDA 14, FvD 12. Maurice de Hond has PRO at 31 and PVV at 22 (unconfirmed; third-party listing).

Maurice de Hond's poll shows JA21 and PVV rising and the VVD slipping. The VVD's tension with its partners may be costing it.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen debated the 2027 foreign affairs budget on 11 November, saying Dutch demands on Israel, including on E1, are unchanged and that pressure should be a broad package. He gave an opinion (oordeel Kamer) on two Gaza motions.

The Rights Forum argues Gaza policy under Jetten is unchanged from the Schoof cabinet. This is an advocacy view, not the government's.

Australia, Canada, Italy and the UK urged Israel to allow "rapid and unimpeded" aid into Gaza, citing 150,000 tents held at the border. The Netherlands was not among the signatories, which is useful context for where Dutch policy sits.

Background: the Netherlands moved ahead nationally on a ban on trade with Israeli settlements, and Israel expelled two Dutch officials from a Gaza support centre (August). Berendsen has said member states will act nationally if the EU is too slow.

Five EU states including the Netherlands plan an asylum return hub in Rwanda. It was expected to be announced at a Vienna meeting on 16 November; 46 Dutch human rights groups object.

6. Defence and NATO.

Russia demands an explanation over Destinus staff in Ukraine, and Jetten says the Netherlands "won't be intimidated" after the Dutch embassy in Kyiv was hit. The NRC story raises questions about how far Dutch industry can go before the state is seen as a party to the war.

The defence minister declined to comment on Destinus, citing international humanitarian law. The minister is named as Yesilgöz in one report; verify (unconfirmed).

Dutch troops take part in the NATO exercise Freedom Shield in Lithuania (38,000 troops from 8 countries, to end of November). Ms. Den Helder has joined SNMG1.

Earlier commitments: EUR 250 million for 700 Ruta cruise missiles for Ukraine and EUR 248 million for drones. Both were agreed earlier in 2026.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Raad van State told the minister on 11 November that the Syria policy was insufficiently substantiated. He responded on 17 November that Syria remains safe, and the IND temporarily suspended rejections of pending Syrian cases.

The EU Court of Justice holds a hearing on 26 November on humanitarian conditions in assessing safe countries of origin. The minister is not waiting for its guidance.

Under the EU pact the Netherlands must take in asylum seekers from other states next year or pay to opt out. Details are not final.

Nearly 18,500 status holders still wait for housing, and COA urged municipalities to find emergency solutions.

The Uzbek Babayants family is leaving a Kampen church after two years of church asylum, having filed a new residence application. They can stay legally while it is processed.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

House prices in Q3 were 3.4% higher than a year earlier (2.9% in September), far below the 12% of November 2024. In 13 municipalities prices are falling, with Best at -4.6%.

Rabobank expects prices to be flat in Q4 and real prices to fall as inflation picks up; DNB sees 3 to 4% a year to 2028. Inflation of about 3.3% is expected for 2026.

The Belastingplan and the "millionaire tax" are the main fiscal story. See section 3; Eerste Kamer approval is pending.

The IND backlog has reportedly grown to about 75,000 cases despite EUR 374 million extra funding (third-party site, unconfirmed).

9. Justice, courts and security.

The Amsterdam court convicted a 28-year-old former police officer of computervredebreuk (unauthorised access) for two years of passing information from police systems to third parties (20 November). It is a corruption-adjacent case that goes to police integrity. Rechtspraak.nl (listing; unconfirmed)

In Utrecht the OM demanded up to 17 years in a case over deaths and serious injuries from beer bottles filled with liquid MDMA (hearing 19 to 20 November). A verdict date was not found.

Russian threats and the damage to the Dutch embassy in Kyiv raise the security-policy temperature at home.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Amsterdam plans to invest EUR 142 million in school buildings next year.

Construction has started on what AT5 calls Amsterdam's largest mosque.

A fire broke out on a ship in the Westelijk Havengebied, and a subsidence problem hit the C. van Eesterenlaan bridge.

A 64-year-old woman's car ended up in an Amsterdam canal (NL Times headline; details not checked).

11. Society and culture.

Nearly 60% of Dutch municipalities no longer require residents to separate waste at home.

Youth-care activist Jason Bhugwandass has died, and AT5 reports rising popper use among young people.

Nieuw-West runs a campaign against violence from 16 November to 10 December.
