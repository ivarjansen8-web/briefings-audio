Here is your Netherlands briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Tax-plan vote tests Jetten's minority cabinet as 200,000 march for Gaza in Amsterdam; inflation hits 3.7%.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 11 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Tweede Kamer closed the Belastingplan 2027 debate on Tuesday 10 November, and the vote is set for Thursday 12 November. The minority cabinet of D66, VVD and CDA (66 of 150 seats) has no guaranteed majority, and DutchNews reports the coalition parties were ready to vote against each other on amendments; NRC reported the cabinet would almost certainly fall if the plan failed.

Organisers say more than 200,000 people joined the fourth "Rode Lijn" (Red Line) march against Israeli policy in Gaza in Amsterdam on Sunday 8 November. Police said the march was largely calm, with four arrests, and the size of the turnout puts pressure on the cabinet's Israel policy.

Dutch inflation rose to 3.7% in October, the highest of 2026, driven by energy and fuel (+14.4%). It was the fourth monthly rise in a row, and euro-area inflation reached 4.0%.

A Maurice de Hond poll (8 November) puts the three coalition parties at just 38 seats, with PRO (the merged GroenLinks-PvdA) at 31. It is one pollster, but it shows the government's weak standing going into the tax vote.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The Jetten cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA; sworn in 23 February 2026) is a minority government and has reportedly been sounding out the ChristenUnie, 50PLUS and the Groep Markuszower for support. With them there would be a Tweede Kamer majority but not one in the Eerste Kamer.

The ministerraad (cabinet meeting) approved a national AI vision on 6 November, presented by state secretary Aerdts (Digital Economy and Sovereignty). It is the cabinet's first broad AI strategy.

Premier Rob Jetten hosted social partners on 4 November on the climate and energy agenda towards 2040. It signals the cabinet is seeking broad buy-in despite lacking a majority.

State secretary Jo-Annes de Bat (CDA, Climate and Green Growth) says keeping the village of Moerdijk is "feasible", with a smaller port expansion of about 200 net hectares instead of 450. No final decision yet; research on liveability must come first, and residents ask what happens if the cabinet falls.

The Netherlands formally nominated former DNB president Klaas Knot to succeed Christine Lagarde at the ECB (announced 4 November by finance minister Eelco Heinen). Spain's Pablo Hernandez de Cos is the main rival; a decision is expected mid-December.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Kamer debated the Belastingplan 2027 on Tuesday 10 November; motions were filed by Keijzer and Vermeer, Markuszower, Teunissen and Schenk. Votes on the package follow on 12 November, and the plan must pass to take effect in 2027.

Gidi Markuszower's request for a debate on a motie van wantrouwen (motion of no confidence) failed to reach the 30 supporting members required. The cabinet is not facing a formal confidence debate yet.

The box 3 novelle (amendment to the capital-gains-style box 3 law) was discussed on 9 November, with D66, VVD, CDA and JA21 reportedly willing to support it; the Tweede Kamer vote is tentatively 12 November. A majority in the Eerste Kamer is not assured and the Raad van State was sharply critical. Reports conflict on whether the 2028 start is delayed a year (NOS via NL Times, 3 November), so treat the timeline as (unconfirmed).

The Eerste Kamer's 10 November agenda included a vote on the bill to lift the ban on creating embryos for research (36.416) and the school-absenteeism bill (36.663). Outcomes were not found, and the embryo-vote date is unclear (unconfirmed).

The Eerste Kamer votes on the Alcoholmeter bill (36.585) and four motions on 17 November.

4. Parties and polls.

De Hond (8 November): PRO 31, PVV 21, JA21 18, D66 14, VVD 13, CDA 11; coalition total 38 versus 66 won in October 2025. The coalition stood at 47 on 1 August and 42 on 2 October.

The latest NOS Peilingwijzer found (24 October, so outside the 72-hour window) had PRO 26.8, PVV 18.9, JA21 16.2, VVD 15.9, D66 15.0, CDA 13.4. No newer Peilingwijzer turned up.

A Dwarsnieuws write-up of a poll claims only 10% want the minority cabinet to continue and 40% want new elections (unconfirmed, partisan outlet). Treat with caution.

CDA-SGP interest in a formatiemotie after a possible cabinet fall is reported by Dagelijkse Standaard (unconfirmed, opinionated source). It shows parties positioning for a collapse.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) said after the 8 November march that public concern about Gaza and the West Bank is "understandable" and reaffirmed support for a Palestinian state. Jetten said the cabinet has acted against illegal settlements and is working with EU partners to raise diplomatic pressure.

Israel revoked the accreditation of Dutch diplomats in Ramallah after the Dutch import ban on goods from illegal settlements (announced late September). Berendsen called the move "unnecessary"; the dispute is the core of current bilateral tension.

Critics argue Gaza policy is largely unchanged from the Schoof cabinet: the Netherlands joined the genocide case against Israel as intervener but not as co-plaintiff with South Africa. The cabinet says humanitarian aid must be scaled up since the ceasefire.

Jetten urged EU leaders at the October summit to do more for Ukraine; the Netherlands announced a roughly EUR 500 million winter package (air defence, drones).

Euro-area finance ministers were due to consider ECB presidency nominations on 9 November (outcome not found).

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence minister Dilan Yesilgoz (VVD) appears before the Kamer today, 11 November, for a debate on European defence cooperation and the coming EU Foreign Affairs Council (Defence). The Council meets on 17 November.

The Defensienota 2026 is the guiding document, aiming at readiness for large-scale conflict within NATO. The coalition agreement targets 3.5% of GDP on defence by 2035, to be anchored in law.

State secretary Derk Boswijk (CDA) supports Yesilgoz; no NATO-specific event surfaced in the last 72 hours.

7. Asylum and migration.

As of 2 November the COA housed 83,474 people entitled to reception, 34,126 of them in emergency shelter, plus 7,767 outside COA locations. Capacity pressure continues.

Weekly asylum intake was about 830 in week 44 (770 in week 43, 840 in week 42). These are rough estimates.

The EU Migration Pact applies since 12 June 2026, changing reception rules and giving children school access within two months.

The cabinet wants to close a loophole for cheap labour migrants and raise fines for labour-law violations by about 22%.

A non-official site reports 173 serious incidents in the reception chain in the first nine months of 2026 (unconfirmed).

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Inflation hit 3.7% in October (3.4% in September); gas was 11.7% dearer than a year ago. HICP rose from 3.0% to 3.3%.

Petrol prices fell to their lowest since March as oil prices dropped. This may ease the energy-driven inflation in coming months.

Existing homes were 3.4% dearer in Q3 than a year earlier (2.9% in September), with an average price of EUR 504,658 (CBS/Kadaster). ABN AMRO expects cooling in 2027.

The cabinet reportedly plans to delay box 3 reform by a year and set aside EUR 350 million for tax relief (NOS sources, via NL Times; disputed).

9. Justice, courts and security.

A 16-year-old from Schiedam was arrested on 10 November in Capelle aan den IJssel over the fatal shooting of 19-year-old Tico at the Weverpad on 7 November. Two more shooters are at large; Nu.nl floats mistaken identity (unconfirmed).

The Amsterdam court sentenced a 37-year-old to one month in prison on 3 November for hitting two men with a plank at a 2024 pro-Palestinian UvA protest.

A verdict is due today in Amsterdam against a 34-year-old suspected of robbing and raping two sex workers (22 January 2026).

Partisan outlets allege incidents at the Rode Lijn march (Overtoom baton use, a flag confiscation, an alleged attack on a house); police have not confirmed these (unconfirmed).

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Mayor Femke Halsema banned the Israeli flag at the Rode Lijn march (reported by Christenen voor Israel; unconfirmed). Mayoral public-order powers are again contested.

Amsterdam saw the largest Gaza march of its kind: over 200,000 by organisers' count across 130+ groups, with limited arrests.

Schiedam residents fear retaliation after the shooting; the mayor says several scenarios are being examined.
