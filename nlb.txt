Here is your Netherlands briefing for Monday, 16 November.

Jetten cabinet in crisis talks over millionaires' tax; Raad van State rebukes Syria asylum policy.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 16 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Jetten minority cabinet is in open crisis, and D66, VVD and CDA resume talks at the Catshuis at 11:00 today after a weekend at De Zwaluwenberg estate near Hilversum failed to settle it. The row is about the Belastingplan 2027 and a new "millionaires' tax"; a cabinet fall now would hit a government barely nine months old.

VVD leader Dilan Yeşilgöz called the minority coalition a "failed experiment" and said a collapse is a "possible solution". A VVD source says the party does not want to continue "in this form", and she floated adding a further right-wing party.

Business leaders warn a collapse would be "disastrous" for the Dutch economy. VNO-NCW's chair led the warning, and four administrators and legal scholars told Nieuwsuur a fall would serve nobody.

The Raad van State ruled the government's Syria asylum policy insufficiently reasoned. The minister must reassess Syria's security situation, which could slow the asylum chain.

2. Cabinet and coalition.

Premier Rob Jetten, vice-premiers Dilan Yeşilgöz (VVD) and Bart van den Brink (CDA), and faction leaders January Paternotte (D66) and Ruben Brekelmans (VVD) negotiated at De Zwaluwenberg on Saturday and Sunday without a result. The RVD said the coalition was "not yet out" and talks continue at the Catshuis.

Friday's crisis meeting lasted more than seven hours and ended in deadlock, with the cabinet surviving "for now". Jetten spoke of a "serious restart" (doorstart), a hint at a reset of the coalition agreement.

The trigger was a tax deal D66 and CDA struck with left-wing and centre opposition parties (PRO, Volt) behind the VVD's back. The VVD says this broke the coalition agreement (coalitieakkoord).

D66's Rob Dassen urged Jetten and CDA leader Bontenbal not to let themselves be "blackmailed" by the VVD. It shows the split inside the coalition over whether to bend to the VVD. RD, 14 November (unconfirmed, headline only)

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer adopted the Belastingplan 2027 including the millionaires' tax by 76 votes to 74 on Thursday 12 November. The amendment from PRO and Volt had D66 and CDA support; one source says the VVD voted against its own coalition's plan.

The amended box 3 rate would be 35.5% from 2029, with 38.5% on the return above EUR 60,000, costing roughly EUR 1 billion. Critics warn smaller investors may also be hit hard.

The Kamer holds a two-minute debate (tweeminutendebat) on the Foreign Affairs Council today with a vote on motions, including Grinwis/Van Brenk and two De Roon motions to end aid to Sahel countries. Results were not yet published at compilation.

The Eerste Kamer votes Tuesday 17 November on the Alcoholmeter bill (36.585) and four motions from the rule-of-law and Justice budget debates. On 10 November it passed the drink-driving bill (36.586) without a vote.

4. Parties and polls.

The last Ipsos I&O poll I could find (12 to 14 October) had PRO at 28 seats, JA21 and PVV 18 each, and D66 and VVD 15 each. That is a poor base for a three-party minority coalition now in crisis.

Peilingwijzer's cached update was from 27 to 28 October and no November poll turned up. Expect the first post-crisis polls this week.

A late-September EenVandaag/Verian poll gave PRO 28, PVV 20, JA21 15 and D66+VVD+CDA together 49 seats. The coalition has lost seats since the 55 recorded in May. NieuwRechts (unconfirmed, secondary summary)

PRO and Volt now hold the pivotal role in budget votes, having co-written the tax amendment with D66 and CDA. This tests the "alternative majority" model of a minority government.

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign minister Tom Berendsen takes Dutch positions to the EU Foreign Affairs Council (Raad Buitenlandse Zaken) in Brussels today. He told the Kamer on 11 November that The Hague wants EU measures on Israel to stay on the table, focused on settlement policy and the humanitarian situation in Gaza.

Jetten said after the 6 November cabinet meeting that the Netherlands is pushing for a reassessment of the EU-Israel Association Agreement. Berendsen is seeking an EU majority, but suspending the trade part has lacked support among member states.

Israel has reportedly withdrawn accreditation of Dutch diplomats in Ramallah and barred them from the West Bank. Berendsen still called Israel a "friendly state", which activists criticise. The Rights Forum (unconfirmed, advocacy source)

Berendsen declined to rule out talks with Washington on curbs to the ICC's powers. The ICC states' assembly meets in The Hague from 30 November to 7 December.

Critics say the Jetten cabinet's Gaza policy is essentially unchanged from the Schoof cabinet. That leaves a gap between Kamer motions and government action. The Rights Forum (unconfirmed, advocacy source)

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence minister Dilan Yeşilgöz said on 11 November that supporting Ukraine is "also in our own interest". She spoke at the European Defence Innovation Forum in The Hague.

The Netherlands is giving EUR 10 million to modernise two training centres in Ukraine; cumulative military aid is nearly EUR 12 billion. She also said paying out more, sooner is not financially possible in coming years.

The government wants to anchor the NATO path to 3.5% of GDP by 2035 in law via the Defensienota 2026. Her crisis role as VVD vice-premier makes defence continuity a coalition question.

A Dutch-led drone technology hub effort involving 28 countries was announced in Latvia in late October.

7. Asylum and migration.

The Raad van State (ECLI:NL:RVS:2026:6543) found the Syria country policy did not properly weigh humanitarian conditions and rejected the idea of a "completely new situation" after Assad's fall. The court gave no view of its own on Syria's security.

Asylum minister Bart van den Brink (CDA) says he is studying the ruling; an asylum lawyer warns of longer waits. A rewrite or better justification are the two options.

A NOS round-up says 42 municipalities are on the minister's list for taking too few asylum seekers, with a shortfall of 4,500 reception places. Councils say they feel misjudged. NOS (date unclear)

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Dutch inflation reportedly hit 3.7%, with analysts fearing more after the US midterms. DutchNews (unconfirmed, headline only)

Health insurance premiums will rise next year. The size of the increase was not visible in the results. DutchNews (unconfirmed)

Existing home prices were 3.3 to 3.4% higher in Q3 2026 than a year earlier, the seventh quarter of slowing growth, and slightly lower after inflation.

VNO-NCW warns that a cabinet collapse during budget season would damage confidence. The box 3 reform and its EUR 1 billion cost sit at the centre of this.

9. Justice, courts and security.

Two people were arrested over the Prinsjesdag train sabotage. Details were not available beyond the headline. DutchNews (unconfirmed)

The OM suspects a 26-year-old woman from Amsterdam of sending armed teenagers out to carry out contract killings (liquidaties). It appeared at a first court hearing. NOS (date unclear)

The Eerste Kamer completed debates on the Staat van de rechtsstaat and the Justice budget, with motions voted 17 November.

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

Sinterklaas arrived in Amsterdam on Sunday 15 November by boat from De Omval to the Scheepvaartmuseum, welcomed by wethouder Esmah Lahlah. The parade ran on to Leidseplein.

Cruyff Court Lily Yohannes opened in Holendrecht on 15 November.

The Hague is the stage for the crisis: Catshuis talks today and the ICC states' assembly from 30 November.

11. Society and culture.

Germany v Netherlands is tonight at 20:45 in Berlin, the last Nations League group match. The Dutch lead Group A2 with 11 points to Germany's 5.

Ajax's Marc ter Stegen is in Germany's squad, and three Ajax players are with Jong Oranje.
