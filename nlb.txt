Here is your Netherlands briefing for Monday, 9 November.

200,000 march for harder Israel line as Jetten stays away; tax plan still lacks majority.

Compiled from sources dated 3 to 9 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Organisers say at least 200,000 people joined the fourth Rode Lijn (Red Line) march in Amsterdam on Sunday 8 November, demanding maximum pressure on Israel. Police reported four arrests and no major incidents; the turnout figure is the organisers' own, with no independent count found (unconfirmed).

Prime Minister Rob Jetten skipped the march and said the Netherlands "does stick its neck out"; the cabinet is adding no new measures against Israel. The absence angered speakers who called on him directly, and it exposes the gap between D66's campaign rhetoric and a three-party coalition with the VVD.

The cabinet still has no majority for the Belastingplan 2027 (tax plan) ahead of the Tweede Kamer vote on 12 November. Without it, about EUR 2.5 billion in revenue is at risk next year, which would push the deficit past the EU's 3% limit.

A Maurice de Hond poll of 8 November puts the three coalition parties at 38 seats combined, with PRO (the GroenLinks-PvdA merger) on 31. It is a single pollster, so treat it as indicative (unconfirmed).

2. Cabinet and coalition.

The Jetten cabinet (D66, VVD, CDA, sworn in 23 February 2026) is a minority cabinet and must find opposition support for each major bill. This explains the repeated talks with seven opposition parties: PRO, JA21, BBB, SGP, ChristenUnie, 50Plus and Volt.

The latest round of talks on the tax plan produced no agreements, according to NOS. The coalition's fractievoorzitters (parliamentary leaders) were meeting the opposition one-on-one and hoped to see a route to support early this week.

PRO and Volt reportedly made an "ultimate" offer to shift EUR 1.5 billion from labour tax to wealth tax, which the VVD rejects. The cabinet also does not want to accept it, so the main route to a Senate majority remains blocked for now (unconfirmed, as reported via NOS summary).

The cabinet agreed an AI strategy in the ministerraad, with extra legislation and a plan for datacentres. State Secretary Aerdts (digital economy and sovereignty) leads the file.

The cabinet plans to cut about 25,000 rijksambtenaren (central-government jobs), roughly 16% of the workforce, by 2035, saving about EUR 3.5 billion. Education, Economic Affairs and Interior are hit hardest per NRC.

3. Tweede Kamer and Eerste Kamer.

The Tweede Kamer votes on the Belastingplan 2027 on Thursday 12 November. Whether it passes depends on opposition parties still negotiating with the coalition.

In the Eerste Kamer (Senate) the coalition is far short of the 38 seats needed, even with JA21 and SGP; the eleven BBB senators look near-indispensable. ChristenUnie, 50Plus and the Markuszower group say they would vote in favour, which still falls short. Sources disagree on the Senate vote date (7 December or 15 December) (unconfirmed).

The Kamer has a stemmingen (voting) block on Tuesday 10 November at 15:00 and a wetgevingsoverleg on the police budget that evening (18:30 to 21:30).

A stikstof (nitrogen) bill went to the Tweede Kamer on 3 November, and a wetgevingsoverleg on the nitrogen fund is set for Monday 16 November. The bill replaces nitrogen deposition targets with emission targets.

4. Parties and polls.

De Hond (8 November) has the coalition at 38 seats and PRO at 31, with D66, the biggest coalition party, on 14. An earlier edition had the coalition on 39 and PRO on 30.

The NOS Peilingwijzer of 24 October had PRO 26.8, PVV 18.9, JA21 16.2, VVD 15.9, D66 15.0 and CDA 13.4 seats. The Peilingwijzer smooths several pollsters, so it is steadier than De Hond's numbers.

BBB leader Henk Vermeer says the nitrogen plans mean "farmers are being governed into bankruptcy." BBB is also the key swing group in the Senate, so its stance matters well beyond farming.

Reporting on the latest De Hond poll says about half of D66 voters would like to continue with PRO (unconfirmed, single pollster).

5. Foreign policy, the Middle East and the EU.

Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen (CDA) said he understands the "powerlessness" of Rode Lijn marchers and speaks for coalition partners VVD and D66. The cabinet line is continued work on Middle East stability and a Palestinian state next to a secure Israel.

The Tweede Kamer has backed suspending the trade part of the EU-Israel Association Agreement; D66 and CDA voted in favour, the VVD against. The cabinet says it is building EU support but has not yet found a qualified majority, so national action is limited.

Jetten will not add new measures against Israel, calling the Dutch position already exposed. Analysts note joint EU steps would have the biggest effect, but there is no majority yet.

The Netherlands nominated Klaas Knot to lead the European Central Bank (unconfirmed, single outlet summary).

A KLM flight on the Amsterdam-Bucharest-Tel Aviv route was hit by a bomb threat on 5 November. Relevant for security around Israel-linked travel.

6. Defence and NATO.

Defence Minister Dilan Yesilgoz-Zegerius runs the Defensienota 2026, which anchors defence spending at 3.5% of GDP towards 2035 in law. It follows the NATO summit in Ankara on 7 to 8 July.

The Netherlands joins with the UK in new amphibious transport ships and with ten allies in replacing AWACS aircraft. These were the headline NATO summit deals.

Yesilgoz and Berendsen told parliament the Netherlands will join an exercise in Poland for a possible post-ceasefire Multinational Force Ukraine. The date of the letter was not clear in the results (unconfirmed).

Quiet: no new defence decisions dated in the last 72 hours were found.

7. Asylum and migration.

Minister Bart van den Brink (Asylum and Migration, second deputy PM) is implementing the EU Asylum and Migration Pact, in force at the IND since 12 June 2026. Screening takes place at the Ter Apel registration centre.

The Eerste Kamer has passed the Wet terugkeer en vreemdelingenbewaring (return and detention law), giving government more tools to enforce departure. The date of passage was not confirmed in the results.

The cabinet announced on 7 November that fines for labour-law violations rise by about 22% and that it will close a loophole for third-country nationals. Nieuws summary via search, 7 November (unconfirmed)

Quiet: no new asylum debates dated in the last 72 hours were found.

8. Economy, housing and cost of living.

Dutch house-price growth slowed to 3.3% a year in August, from 3.9% in July (CBS). Q3 was reported at +3.4% year on year, with registered sales down 1.7% (secondary source).

Rabobank expects 3.1% house-price growth for 2026. The slowdown fits the tight, expensive market for first-time buyers in Amsterdam.

A government-commissioned study found nearly 80% of rental agents go along with landlords' discriminatory requests. Discrimination against tenants with foreign roots is the central finding.

Reports say KLM plans to cut 5,000 jobs (unconfirmed, single outlet).

The cabinet's tax plan includes purchasing-power measures; the box 3 (wealth tax) novelle must pass the Senate by 31 December.

9. Justice, courts and security.

A 28-year-old Rotterdam woman faces a demand of six months in prison for misusing police systems over two years; the court rules in about two weeks.

A former Eindhoven police commissioner is due in court in Zwolle on 13 November over alleged bribes in 2010 to 2016.

A 54-year-old woman was arrested over a fatal stabbing in Zaandam on 7 November; police are also hunting three suspects after a fatal shooting in Schiedam.

Al Jazeera reports protesters broke into a Microsoft data-centre site in Amsterdam on 6 November (unconfirmed, single outlet).

10. Amsterdam and the Randstad.

The Museumplein was the starting point for Sunday's march, with traffic and tram diversions in the centre. AT5 reported baton use during an arrest on the Overtoom (unconfirmed).

Amsterdam's parking permits become more expensive in 2027.

Major maintenance on the Surinameplein starts Monday 16 November, with rerouting for nearly a year.

Rotterdam Centraal had no trains on 7 to 8 November because of signalling work.

11. Society and culture.

The national Kristallnacht commemoration is at the Portuguese Synagogue on Monday 9 November, 17:30 to 18:30. Jetten and Amsterdam officials were reportedly due to attend, set against rising antisemitism concerns (unconfirmed for 2026).

First frost hit three provinces; a chilly weekend was forecast.

FC Twente drew 2 to 2 with Ajax on 8 November, and PSV took the Eredivisie lead.
