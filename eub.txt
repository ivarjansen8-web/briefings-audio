Here is your EU news briefing for Tuesday, 10 November.

Kallas: Russia only 'pretending' to negotiate; Lagarde succession opens; Malta blocks tobacco tax.

Compiled from sources dated 5 to 10 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Kaja Kallas said on 9 November that Russia is "just pretending" to negotiate, speaking at a G7 ministerial in Paris ahead of US-Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. She urged Washington to add sanctions coordinated with the EU's, which she said would have a "significantly greater" effect.

Euro-area finance ministers opened the race to succeed Christine Lagarde on 9 November, with Klaas Knot (NL) and Pablo Hernández de Cos (ES) nominated. No recommendation was made; ministers are waiting for leaders to settle a package that also covers Isabel Schnabel's seat (she leaves 3 January 2027) and Philip Lane's (term ends May 2027). Lagarde leaves on 7 February.

Malta blocked the tobacco taxation reform at ECOFIN on 9 November, leaving the package at 26 of 27 states. Irish presidency minister Simon Harris hopes for a deal in December; until then nicotine pouches carry a zero EU minimum rate.

Parliament and Council face a midnight 16 November deadline on the 2027 annual budget, with a conciliation meeting on 10 November. Parliament wants 1.17 eurosbillion more than the Commission draft while the Council cut about 1 eurosbillion; failure would force a new Commission draft.

Dublin hosts the European Political Community summit on 12 November and an informal EU-27 leaders' meeting on AI on 13 November. Over 50 leaders are expected, with Canada's Mark Carney attending, and the next MFF is likely to feature in corridor talks.

2. European Commission.

The College is due on 11 November to discuss a consumer package including the Digital Fairness Act and a consumer enforcement initiative. It is the next big test of the digital-rulebook "simplification" debate.

Commission officials told Parliament on 5 November that the EU is considering new import-declaration requirements to identify goods from Israeli settlements. It stops short of the binding trade ban MEPs requested.

Commissioner Magnus Brunner told MEPs on 4 November that Frontex will be able to assist deportations to non-EU return hubs. This applies only where an international agreement with rights guarantees exists and Parliament has approved it. Ua.news citing Politico Europe, 4 November (unconfirmed)

Von der Leyen said the EU will take part in the Abu Dhabi talks alongside the UK, France and Germany, after speaking to Zelenskyy. (Date of the report unclear.) The European (unconfirmed)

3. European Parliament.

A mini-plenary sits in Brussels on 11 to 12 November; there is no sitting on 9 to 10 November. The next Strasbourg session is 23 to 26 November.

MEPs will debate on Wednesday with Kallas how to return forcibly deported Ukrainian children, and will vote Thursday on the civil protection mechanism for 2028 to 2034.

Plenary is also due to assess whether further Russia sanctions are needed and to discuss EU cyber resilience.

MEPs urged a binding EU-wide ban on trade with illegal Israeli settlements on 5 November, arguing labelling is insufficient. The call is non-binding.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

Malta's veto sank the tobacco tax deal at ECOFIN on 9 November, a reminder that fiscal files still need unanimity. Estonia reportedly accepted the Irish compromise on freezing the inflation adjustment.

The Eurogroup agenda on 9 November covered the digital euro, defence financing and banking union; no outcomes were reported in my sources.

Eurogroup president Kyriakos Pierrakakis and Harris want the next ECB president named by year-end. Under the procedure, finance ministers recommend, Parliament holds a hearing, then leaders appoint.

A Budget ECOFIN is held on 10 November, ahead of the conciliation meeting.

MFF 2028 to 2034 positions remain far apart: the frugal group wants cuts of "several hundred billion euros". Timing of the reports is unclear.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

Foreign ministers meet on 16 November, with Syria the Middle East focus, followed by a second ministerial on cross-regional security and connectivity. Iran and Lebanon are not listed for that session.

The EU adopted its third package of sanctions on extremist settlers and Hamas figures on 28 May, after Hungary's change of government lifted a veto. The broader suspension of Association Agreement trade provisions still lacks a majority.

Settler violence in the West Bank has reportedly risen since the Iran war began on 28 February, according to Palestinian officials and the UN. This is the pressure behind the settlement-goods debate. Eunews, 5 November (unconfirmed)

The EU created a support mission for Lebanon's armed and security forces (EUSM Lebanon) in October, while UNIFIL's mandate is reported to end on 31 December 2026. Consilium, 12 October; Wikipedia (unconfirmed)

Kallas earlier said the Gaza peace process has stalled and urged the stabilisation force and technocratic committee to move forward. I found no new EEAS Gaza statement dated 9 November.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The EU is negotiating a 22nd Russia sanctions package; a Ukrainian official suggested November adoption is realistic, but no date is confirmed. Ua.news (unconfirmed)

The 21st package took effect in July and the 12 October listings hit about 1,569 individuals and entities, the largest expansion since the invasion.

Ukraine has four of six accession clusters open after Hungary lifted its veto in October, and Kyiv's envoy said on 3 November Hungary raised no new objections to clusters 4 and 5, though it asked for more time.

Kallas dismissed expectations for Abu Dhabi and argued that future European security talks should be EU-led, not ad hoc.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

The return regulation allowing "return hubs" outside the EU was provisionally agreed in June; five governments aim for first third-country deals this year and operations in 2027.

Brunner stresses the EU supplies the legal basis but member states must negotiate the agreements, with each deal requiring Parliament approval. Ua.news citing Politico Europe, 4 November (unconfirmed)

Rights groups object to the hubs and France has questioned their legality and effectiveness.

Nothing new dated 8 to 10 November was found.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

Conciliation on the 2027 budget runs to 16 November, with Parliament at about 194.05 eurosbillion in commitments against the Council's 191.88 eurosbillion (Agence Europe figures; EPRS figures differ).

Parliament wants to protect Erasmus+ and research funding as debt-servicing costs mount.

Šefčovič has pressed Washington to restore the 15% all-inclusive tariff ceiling; the US has floated a March 2028 sunset clause. Sources are older (May).

Digital euro talks continue, with the ECB reporting technical preparations.

9. Defence and security.

A Defence Council meets on 17 November on security and defence aspects of EU-China relations and support for Ukraine.

Eurogroup ministers were due to discuss defence financing on 9 November; no outcome reported.

Dublin's summit faces a "substantial" disruption threat rating and hoax concerns, while terror risk is rated low.

Quiet on new drone-defence announcements: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Poland's constitutional court standoff is unresolved: police entered the building on 30 October to install judges chosen by the Tusk majority, and President Nawrocki has refused most nominees.

The Commission urged Nawrocki to accept the new judges, citing the CJEU finding that the Tribunal is not independent.

On 5 November, coalition partner PSL proposed ending the mandates of all sitting constitutional judges, and Tusk offered a one-on-one with Nawrocki.

No new CJEU ruling dated 8 to 10 November was found.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Poland's institutional clash is the main story, with parliamentary elections a year away.

Hungary under Péter Magyar's government has dropped the blanket vetoes on Ukraine and settler sanctions, though it still asks for time on clusters 4 and 5.

Ireland holds the Council presidency and is hosting the summits, with a civil-service strike possibly disrupting 12 November (unconfirmed).

Germany reportedly signalled it will not seek the ECB presidency; the post is being traded among France, Germany, Spain and the Netherlands.
