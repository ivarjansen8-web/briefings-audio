Here is your EU news briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

EU leaders face budget and Ukraine loan showdown as 22nd Russia sanctions package lands and Parliament debates ending Article 7 on Hungary.

Compiled from sources dated 22 to 25 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Leaders meet in Brussels on 26 to 27 November in a special European Council to try to settle the 2028 to 2034 budget and a new 45 eurosbillion Ukraine loan for 2027. Costa's invitation letter says he has tabled his own overall MFF proposal, but commentators judge a full deal unlikely because the budget and Ukraine aid are now linked.

The Commission proposed a 22nd Russia sanctions package on 23 November, hitting banks, crypto platforms, refineries, 70+ shadow-fleet vessels, and Russian athletes and ex-combatants. It needs unanimity, and Ireland's Aughinish Alumina plant was reportedly left out because Brussels did not sense enough Irish support.

The Parliament debated on 24 November whether to close Article 7 proceedings against Hungary, with a resolution expected in December. The EPP says Hungary under Péter Magyar no longer breaches EU values, while Greens and others say work is unfinished. QNC News, 24 November (unconfirmed, single aggregator)

MEPs vote today in Strasbourg on consent to the EU-Switzerland package of agreements. It follows a 53 to 3 committee vote; a Swiss popular vote is planned for 2027. EU Parliament agenda, 25 November; SWI swissinfo (outcome not yet reported)

2. European Commission.

The Commission proposed on 18 November a loan of up to 45 eurosbillion to Ukraine for 2027, covering roughly two thirds of a 70 eurosbillion gap. It would be funded by EU borrowing and repaid only once Russia pays reparations, replacing the stalled reparations-loan idea blocked by Belgium.

Reports say the package splits into up to 25 eurosbillion for military aid and 20 eurosbillion for budget support. That split shapes how much Ukraine can spend on weapons versus running the state. Al Jazeera, 19 November (unconfirmed)

The Commission said on 20 November that Hungary met 126 of 127 milestones to unlock nearly 10 eurosbillion in frozen recovery funds. Separately, 4.2 eurosbillion in cohesion money was released in October. QNC News, 24 November (unconfirmed)

The 2026 Enlargement Package of 4 November found Moldova has met the conditions to open all remaining clusters. It called closing talks by early 2028 "ambitious but achievable".

3. European Parliament.

Parliament's 23 to 26 November Strasbourg session includes the Hungary rule-of-law debate and the Swiss package consent vote. Nine votes were scheduled on Tuesday.

Green MEP Daniel Freund warned before the debate that the Article 7 procedure should stay open until all major problems are addressed. He cites Hungary's unfinished compliance with the 2024 asylum judgment (200 eurosmillion fine plus 1 eurosmillion per day).

76 MEPs from the Left, Greens, S&D, Renew and some EPP wrote to Kallas and Brunner on 23 to 24 November asking for an urgent human-rights assessment of Rwanda. They question the legality of five states' plan to deport migrants to Rwanda from 2027. Agence Europe; Euronews, 24 November (sources differ on date and count)

The Parliament's own MFF position seeks 1,789 eurosbillion for 2028 to 2034, excluding NGEU repayment. That is above what the frugal states would accept.

4. Council and European Council.

Costa's invitation letter sets the 26 to 27 November summit around the next MFF and 2027 Ukraine financing, with a Euro Summit on 26 November. Interfax adds leaders will spend time on ECB leadership.

EU ambassadors on 24 November agreed Ukraine needs funding but not how, per national notes seen by the Kyiv Independent. Hungary, Czechia and Slovakia stayed out of the 90 eurosbillion loan agreed in April, and Merz ties MFF progress to burden-sharing. PIQ Markets summary (unconfirmed, secondary report)

Merz and Macron reportedly rejected the latest MFF compromise, deepening the impasse. Both are domestically weak, and Macron wants a deal by Christmas before 2027 elections. World Today Journal (unconfirmed)

Euro-area leaders are reportedly set to endorse Pablo Hernández de Cos as ECB president, with Lagarde leaving on 7 February 2027. A reported Franco-German deal pairs him with Markus Brunnermeier as chief economist. Euro Perspectives; Bloomberg, 6 November (unconfirmed)

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

Kallas urged Israel on 14 November to reopen crossings and allow "immediate, unimpeded" aid access to Gaza as winter and flooding set in. She called the strike near Nasser Hospital "extremely alarming" and reaffirmed the two-state solution.

Israeli FM Gideon Sa'ar reportedly vowed to cut contact with Kallas. For a Middle East watcher this signals further strain on EU-Israel dialogue. Hot Air, 18 November (unconfirmed, opinion-leaning outlet)

Foreign ministers on 16 November backed moving ahead with the EU-Syria Association Agreement while modernising it. Kallas said recovery, reconciliation and refugee return remain "challenging".

Ministers also exchanged views on the wider Middle East, including Iran and the Gulf, with no detail published.

The EU sanctioned violent settlers and Hamas leaders in May, after Orbán's April defeat removed the Hungarian veto. Suspending the Israel Association Agreement still lacks unanimity, as Kallas said in April.

Speakers at the UN Security Council called the Gaza ceasefire "far from perfect" yet the basis for recovery, and pressed the US-led Board of Peace for a timeline. The EU has sought a role there.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The 22nd sanctions package proposes removing about 60 Russian banks from SWIFT, barring EU firms from servicing Russian oil refineries, and banning Russian flags at EU sporting events. The Commission wants adoption by late December; Ukraine welcomed it but notes the nuclear sector is omitted.

Merz said after the 19 November Berlin Coalition of the Willing meeting that Europe will be at any new peace talks, with the UAE floated as venue. Zelensky says there is a "huge chance" if the US presses Putin, and Macron wants Ukraine's NATO path reopened.

Moldova has opened four of six clusters (24 of 33 chapters) and aims to open the rest by year-end. Hungary withdrew its objections to Ukraine and Moldova talks.

Montenegro has opened all 33 chapters and provisionally closed 21, targeting completion of talks by end-2026 and membership by 2028.

7. Migration and asylum.

Return hubs are the live fight: 76 MEPs challenge the Rwanda plan, with first transfers by five member states planned for 2027.

The Return Regulation, politically agreed in early June, lets states set up return hubs in third countries and creates a voluntary European Return Order. Brunner noted only one in four people without a right to stay is returned.

The Migration and Asylum Pact has applied since 12 June 2026, and ministers pushed in October for faster returns. Uzbekistan, Rwanda and Uganda have been named as possible hub locations.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

The USTR is expected on 25 November to announce Section 301 "excess capacity" tariffs on 16 partners including the EU, excluding steel and aluminium. Rates are not confirmed; one report cites caps at 15% where trade deals exist. Wisevoter, 25 November; Babypips, 25 November (unconfirmed, no USTR text found)

Greer is due to join EU trade ministers by videoconference on 30 November as a deadline on steel and aluminium derivatives nears. If Washington does not align rates by end-December, the EU can suspend concessions to US exporters.

The MFF proposal totals almost 2 eurostrillion (about 1.26% of GNI). The frugal bloc wants cuts of "several hundred billion", and a deal before year-end would allow adoption in 2027 and funding from January 2028.

Šefčovič said China agreed to hold hybrid-car exports to the EU to half or less of projected levels over four years. He also called the EU deficit with China "a mountain of a challenge". What's up EU (undated, single source, unconfirmed)

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius says a Defence Readiness Report concludes the EU is "not rearming fast enough or effectively enough" to be ready against Russia by 2030. It remains unpublished; he called it "a warning signal".

Kubilius urged industry at a business summit to outspend and outproduce Russia and scale up output for 2027.

SAFE offers 150 eurosbillion in EU-backed defence loans, and the roadmap targets 40% joint procurement by end-2027. End-2026 milestones are the next test.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Parliament's Hungary debate and December vote will decide whether to press the Commission to close Article 7. Budget conditionality measures have reportedly been lifted. QNC News (unconfirmed)

No CJEU judgment dated 24 to 25 November surfaced. The most recent rulings found: on 22 October the Court held Hungary and Czechia breached EU law on access to a lawyer; on 15 October it ruled against Hungary on smuggling-sentence conversion.

In April the Full Court held Hungary's anti-LGBTI law breaches Article 2 TEU. It remains the key precedent for Article 7 arguments.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Merz is making German MFF support conditional on all states sharing Ukraine financing, targeting the Hungary, Czechia and Slovakia opt-outs.

Macron and Lecornu's government is weak, and France faces 2027 elections. France's 2026 budget passed only after two failed no-confidence motions.

Hungary under Magyar, who beat Orbán in April, is now a cooperative actor on sanctions, enlargement and funds. Slovakia's Fico remains a possible blocker of sanctions.

Ireland holds the Council presidency and is also the obstacle on the Aughinish alumina plant in the sanctions text.
