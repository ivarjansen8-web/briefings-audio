Here is your EU news briefing for Friday, 27 November.

EU summit fails on budget and 45 billion euros Ukraine loan as Hernández de Cos picked for ECB; US tariff threat.

Compiled from sources dated 24 to 27 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

EU leaders ended a marathon special summit without a deal on the 2028 to 2034 budget (MFF). Costa's compromise reportedly cut the Commission's near-2 trillion euros proposal by about 10% to roughly 1.78 trillion euros; he is expected to table a revised text, with the next attempt at the 17 to 18 December summit, and Macron reportedly threatened to end talks if there is no December deal.

The 45 billion euros Ukraine loan for 2027 was not approved. Costa said 24 member states back it, but Hungary, Czechia and Slovakia kept their opposition, so the decision slides to December. TASS, 26 November (Hungary's stance is described inconsistently across outlets: unconfirmed)

Leaders chose Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde at the ECB. Macron confirmed the result; formal appointment is expected after Parliament and ECB Council opinions, at the December summit.

Washington proposed new Section 301 tariffs of up to 15% on EU goods. Šefčovič told the US to honour the Turnberry deal; the duties would not apply before January at the earliest.

2. European Commission.

The Commission proposed the 22nd Russia sanctions package on 23 November. It extends a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and lists more than 70 further shadow-fleet vessels; member states must still agree it.

The Commission recommended stepping up Bulgaria's excessive deficit procedure, opening the way to a fine of up to 0.02% of GDP. Dombrovskis called the responses insufficient; a decision is expected 11 December, the first such fine since Bulgaria joined the euro.

The Commission partially cleared Hungary's 10 billion euros recovery fund payment after rule-of-law reforms. Member states must still approve it, and all payments have to be authorised by end-November.

The Commission presented a Quantum Act focused on supply-chain risk and quantum-resistant cryptography, dated 25 November. Quantum Zeitgeist (unconfirmed)

3. European Parliament.

MEPs backed the Horizon Europe 2028 to 2034 (FP10) mandate by 458 to 190 and the European Competitiveness Fund mandate by 386 to 252. The narrower second margin shows how contested the next-budget architecture is.

A final vote was scheduled on 26 November on a deal delaying methane reporting duties for energy importers until May 2030. The outcome was not found in reporting. Kiprinform (unconfirmed)

A vote on simplified environmental assessments was postponed to 25 November after disputes over species protection.

Parliament debated Hungary's rule-of-law progress, with MEPs split on ending the Article 7 procedure. A resolution is slated for the December plenary.

4. Council and European Council.

The special European Council (26 to 27 November) opened with a Euro Summit and ended without an MFF deal. Frugal and cohesion camps held firm positions.

Merz linked Ukraine support to the budget and backed Macron's push for a December deal; Germany reportedly threatened a veto over the Ukraine loan. World Today News (unconfirmed)

Bulgaria will join the Ukraine loan scheme only for its non-military component, and Fico said Slovakia will not fund Ukrainian military spending.

Costa's invitation letter set the agenda: MFF, Ukraine's 2027 financing and ECB leadership.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

Kallas said the EU is preparing further Iran sanctions after France blamed the IRGC for cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration systems on 11 to 12 October. Iran denies involvement; the IRGC is already EU-listed as a terrorist organisation.

The EU called on Israel to reopen all Gaza crossings and allow unimpeded aid as winter nears, and called the Nasser Hospital strike "deeply alarming". Israel denies blocking aid.

Gaza's phase two remains stalled over Hamas disarmament, while the EU seeks a seat on the Board of Peace and presses Palestinian Authority reform.

Foreign ministers backed moving toward an EU-Syria Association Agreement, with technical and legal preparation to precede signature. EU Perspectives (unconfirmed)

No EEAS statement on Gaza or the wider region dated 25 to 26 November turned up. Check the EEAS press page directly.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

Von der Leyen told Parliament on 25 November that a European team will join the next round of Russia talks. Zelensky said the E5 (UK, Germany, France, Italy, Poland) plus EU and NATO representatives would take part.

Moscow disputes this: US envoys said the Kremlin agreed, but Ushakov said the format is not being widened beyond Russian, Ukrainian and US delegations.

The proposed 45 billion euros loan would be repaid only if Russia pays reparations and would cover two-thirds of Ukraine's roughly 70 billion euros 2027 gap. Zelensky says Kyiv needs 115 billion euros.

The 22nd package would also bar Russian teams and athletes from EU sporting events.

Von der Leyen said the EU must prepare for enlargement while attending to decision-making. No new accession step was found this week.

7. Migration and asylum.

The Commission identified four Mediterranean states as under migratory pressure, opening the door to relocations under the Pact.

Interior ministers approved faster returns on 1 October; only 36,825 of 110,335 return orders were carried out this year.

Brunner says return hubs stay voluntary; Austria, Denmark, the Netherlands, Greece and Germany are moving ahead.

The Commission launched the second annual migration management cycle on 6 November, citing a 55% fall in irregular crossings over two years.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

The USTR's Section 301 overcapacity probe targets the EU and 13 others; EU goods already under sectoral tariffs are exempt. Trade ministers discuss it at the Foreign Affairs Council (Trade) on 30 November.

France 24 reports China, the EU and others called the proposed tariffs "unacceptable".

Euro area inflation rose to 4% in October, a three-year high, and Lagarde urged leaders to agree the digital euro by year-end.

Euronews reports the Commission is negotiating with Beijing to rebalance trade and fined Temu 200 million euros under the DSA. Euronews (undated, unconfirmed)

9. Defence and security.

The Commission reopened 21.3 billion euros in unspent SAFE loans, urging joint projects with Ukraine. Euronews (undated, unconfirmed)

Eight SAFE investment plans worth 74 billion euros have been approved; SAFE offers up to 150 billion euros in loans.

Kubilius warns the EU has only about half the capabilities needed by 2030. The roadmap targets 40% joint procurement by end-2027.

An AI-powered cyberattack hit Commission HR systems; China-backed hackers are suspected. Euronews (undated, unconfirmed)

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

The Court delivered judgments on 26 November including Amazon Italia (could count as postal services) and Mekanova (Brussels Ia jurisdiction over flight compensation).

Advocate General Medina delivered an opinion in the Russian legal-services ban appeals (C-865/24 P, C-866/24 P). Same source.

The new European Chief Prosecutor and six European Prosecutors took their solemn undertaking at the Court. Same source.

About 3.5 billion euros for Hungary remains blocked under a separate procedure over academic freedom, LGBTQ+ rights and asylum.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary under Prime Minister Péter Magyar is unlocking frozen funds (16.4 billion euros roadmap, 4.2 billion euros cohesion released in September).

Bulgaria faces its first euro-area fine amid a 5.0% forecast deficit for 2026; opposition leader Vasilev wants the budget laws withdrawn.

Austria, the Netherlands, Denmark, Finland, Sweden and Belgium want budget cuts; Meloni resists a deadline.

Lagarde's early exit is reportedly timed so Macron negotiates the ECB succession before France's 2027 election; the presidential first round is set for 18 April. Archyde (unconfirmed)
