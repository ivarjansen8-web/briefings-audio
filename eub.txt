Here is your EU news briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

EU proposes 45 billion euros Ukraine loan as Berlin summit and EU-UK summit loom.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 19 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Commission proposed a 45 eurosbillion joint-borrowing loan for Ukraine on 18 November, split 25 eurosbillion military and 20 eurosbillion civilian. It would cover about two-thirds of Kyiv's expected 70 eurosbillion gap in 2027, and Ukraine would repay principal only once Russia pays reparations.

Slovakia says it will not join the loan, and reports differ on whether three or four states will opt out. Von der Leyen says she expects all 27 to take part, so unanimity on the 2028 to 2034 budget link is the real fight. Mezha, 18 November; ASEAN Now, 18 November (unconfirmed on the count)

Ukraine's allies meet in Berlin today for a Coalition of the Willing summit, hours after a Trump-Putin-Xi meeting in Shenzhen. Merz warned Washington against surprise deals made without Europe, and US envoys are expected to meet Europe's new negotiating team.

The second EU-UK summit takes place tomorrow, 20 November, in Brussels. Costa and von der Leyen will host Prime Minister Andy Burnham, with a joint declaration expected.

Israel's foreign minister keeps his channel to Kallas closed. The EU's Middle East diplomacy is shifting toward the Commission as a result.

2. European Commission.

The Commission preliminarily found Temu in breach of the Digital Services Act on 17 November. It says Temu requires test reports for only a tiny share of products and often does not check them; a fine of up to 6% of turnover is possible if confirmed. This is separate from the 200 eurosmillion fine of May.

Dombrovskis said on 17 November that Italy could exit the excessive deficit procedure next spring. The Commission forecasts a 2.9% deficit in 2026, but Italy exceeds 3% in 2027 and 2028 because of defence and energy flexibility.

The Commission will rule on Bulgaria's budget plan on 24 November. Bulgaria is under an excessive deficit procedure six months after joining the euro, with a projected deficit of 4.1% in 2026.

The Commission backed a Ukrainian investigation blaming Russian forces for chemical attacks and called on the OPCW to act. It raises pressure for accountability alongside the sanctions track.

The Commission will examine a citizens' initiative to suspend the EU-Israel Association Agreement, with 1,117,104 validated signatures. It has six months to respond, so a decision is likely around May.

3. European Parliament.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The next Strasbourg plenary is 23 to 26 November, so this week sits between sittings.

The 23 to 26 November agenda lists 28 votes, including the emissions trading stability reserve and protection of EU financial interests in the post-2027 budget.

The Parliament's November I outlook flagged final votes on the retail investment framework and the ECHA governance reform. Both follow provisional deals with the Council.

Metsola and Hungary's Magyar discussed ending the Article 7 procedure, conditional on tangible progress on the ground.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

The General Affairs Council meets today, 19 November. It holds its annual rule-of-law country dialogues on Cyprus, Lithuania, Luxembourg and Hungary.

A special European Council is reported for 26 to 27 November. It is expected to take up Ukraine financing and the budget, but I could not confirm the date from a primary source (unconfirmed).

Merz refuses to accept the 2028 to 2034 budget without fresh Ukraine support, and Hungary, Czechia and Slovakia resist joint borrowing. The budget needs unanimity, which makes the Ukraine loan the hinge.

The Council has been adopting sector mandates for the 2028 to 2034 budget, most recently the farm policy position on 26 October. The Council wants a deal before the April 2027 French presidential election.

Dublin informal talks agreed that Europe will be represented in Ukraine talks by the EU institutions plus France, Germany, the UK, Italy and Poland. There is no single envoy.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

Foreign ministers agreed on 16 November to move ahead with the EU-Syria Association Agreement, initialled in 2008 and never signed. Kallas says the modernised text will carry human-rights conditionality; engagement is meant to stop others filling the vacuum.

The same Council voiced concern over Iranian threats to Europe, including cyberattacks, and said the EU is ready to respond.

Kallas said on 14 November that Gaza's humanitarian crisis is "devastating", called the Nasser Hospital airstrike "deeply alarming" and urged Israel to reopen all crossings. Israel denies blocking aid.

Israeli FM Sa'ar's spokesperson says his cut-off of contact with Kallas stays in place. The Commission is taking a larger role in Middle East diplomacy.

The suspension of the Israel Association Agreement lacks the unanimity it needs, and ministers earlier found no support for it. The citizens' initiative adds public pressure but no legal change yet.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The Council added 113 individuals and entities to its Russia list and 32 under hybrid and cyber regimes on 16 November. A 22nd package covering banks and crypto is still being drafted, with timing reported as late November or by Christmas.

Commissioner Kos said on 16 November the remaining accession clusters for Ukraine and Moldova could open as soon as December. The 4 November package judged both ready, and Hungary lifted its block on clusters 2 and 3 on 7 October.

Poland's Tusk says Warsaw will put no extra money into PURL for now. Burden-sharing for Ukraine remains uneven.

The Commission wants Norway, the UK and Canada to find 25 eurosbillion for Kyiv in 2027.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

The Return Regulation (EU) 2026/2280 entered into force on 16 October, allowing return hubs in third countries. Hubs are voluntary for states, and no host country has been named.

Greece is one of five member states in talks with African countries to host return hubs, according to Brunner's June remarks.

Rights groups, including more than 250 organisations, and Human Rights Watch warn hubs could become offshore detention.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours on new return deals.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

Šefčovič is pressing Washington on steel and aluminium duties of up to 50%. If the US does not align by end-December, the EU can suspend tariff concessions under the Turnberry deal; this is about two weeks old.

Commission officials put interest on the new Ukraine loan at 2 euros-2.5 billion a year. The EU would likely subsidise it. Yahoo News (unconfirmed)

The Commission also answered Italy's call for energy cost relief, per a Euronews headline. I could not see details (unconfirmed).

The Commission presented its Digital Fairness Act proposal last week against dark patterns. World Today Journal (unconfirmed)

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius launched "SAFE 2.0" on 18 November, a call for 21.3 eurosbillion of unspent SAFE money aimed at Ukraine procurement. He wants deliveries in 2027.

A non-public European Defence Agency report finds the EU is not rearming fast enough for 2030, which Kubilius called "a warning signal". EU Perspectives, October (unconfirmed)

A defence market package, including the procurement directive revision, is due by end-November or early December.

Intelligence assessments cited by Kubilius in August say Russia could be ready to attack EU states before 2030.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

The Court of Justice ruled on 17 November in C-698/23 P that the European Data Protection Supervisor cannot challenge EU laws. It dismissed the EDPS challenge to the Europol Regulation amendment.

On 5 November the Court set aside a General Court judgment on Dutch Covid liquidity support to KLM.

The Council ended the conditionality mechanism against Hungary in October, releasing 4.2 eurosbillion of a 16.4 eurosbillion package. Magyar says the Article 7 process is nearing conclusion.

Rule-of-law dialogue with Hungary takes place at today's General Affairs Council.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary's Magyar criticised linking Ukraine support to the budget talks during a visit to Germany. His government is friendlier on rule of law but still opposes military aid to Kyiv.

Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary are picking a fight with Berlin over Ukraine aid. Merz backs universal participation in the loan.

France and Germany are reported near a deal putting de Cos in the ECB top job. Only a headline was visible (unconfirmed).

Spain votes on 29 November, and France elects a president on 18 April and 2 May 2027. Both shape budget and Ukraine timelines.
