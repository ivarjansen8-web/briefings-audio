Here is your EU news briefing for Saturday, 21 November.

EU proposes 45 billion euros joint-debt Ukraine loan as Hungary lifts accession veto and EU-UK seal reset.

Compiled from sources dated 18 to 21 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Brussels proposed a new 45 billion euros Ukraine loan backed by joint debt on 18 November, split into up to 20 billion euros budget support and 25 billion euros for weapons such as drones and air defence. Together with the earlier 90 billion euros loan it brings EU borrowing for Ukraine to roughly 135 billion euros over 2026 to 27, and Slovakia, Hungary, Czechia and Bulgaria have said they will stay out.

Hungary has reportedly lifted its veto on opening Ukraine's and Moldova's last two accession clusters (4 and 5), with ambassadors due to approve screening results on 20 November. Formal opening still needs invitations to submit negotiating positions; reporting says all six clusters could be open in December.

The EU and UK sealed a "reset" deal at their Brussels summit on 20 November, including a Youth Experience Scheme for 100,000 young people each way per year from 2028. The package is contingent on a tuition-fees agreement within six months and also links the two carbon markets.

Russian missiles and a drone crashed in Poland on 20 November; Tusk called it an "intentional provocation" and NATO's Rutte a "grave violation" of airspace. No injuries were reported, but it lands days before the 26 to 27 November summit.

2. European Commission.

The Commission disbursed almost 8 billion euros to Ukraine on 20 November, its largest reform-linked payment so far. It shows the Ukraine Facility working as the bridge until the new loan is agreed.

The Commission partially cleared Hungary's 10 billion euros recovery-fund request after Budapest met 126 of 127 milestones. Member states must still approve the payment, and the missing milestone should bring only a small penalty.

Commissioner Kubilius told the European Business Summit on 18 November that EU industry must scale up so Ukraine's 2027 needs are met, and pointed to a SAFE 2.0 call over 21.3 billion euros of unspent funds. A defence market package with a revised procurement directive was previously expected around end-November or December (timing unconfirmed).

The Commission is weighing new import-declaration requirements for goods from Israeli settlements, a customs official told MEPs on 5 November. A proposal to suspend the Association Agreement remains on the Council agenda without a qualified majority.

3. European Parliament.

Parliament meets in Strasbourg 23 to 26 November, with 28 votes and 15 debates on the agenda.

MEPs vote on the 2027 annual budget (199.7 billion euros commitments, 211.5 billion euros payments) on 24 November, after the Council-Parliament deal of 11 November. It is the last annual budget under the current MFF.

The plenary will hold a debate on the rule of law in Hungary on Tuesday morning and, unusually, a debate on Gaza. For Middle East watchers, the Gaza slot is the first such debate in several sessions.

On Thursday MEPs take stock of COP31 in Antalya (9 to 20 November), and the week includes the retail investment framework and an emissions-trading market stability reserve vote.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

A special European Council on 26 to 27 November will focus on the 2028 to 2034 MFF and Ukraine's 2027 financing, followed by a regular summit on 17 to 18 December. Costa aims for a political deal on the budget by year-end.

Merz has written to Costa that Germany will not approve the next MFF unless every member state commits to financing Ukraine in 2027 and beyond. This targets the four opt-out states, which depend on cohesion money.

The Irish presidency's negotiating box (10 October) cuts 8% in real terms from the Commission proposal, with foreign aid down 17.4%. A 17-country group led by Madrid objected to the ambition falling to 1.02% of GNI.

The Council is expected to endorse the 2027 budget on 23 November (unconfirmed).

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

At the 16 November Foreign Affairs Council, the EU added one person and one entity to its Hamas/PIJ financing sanctions list, and Kallas said further Iran sanctions are in preparation. The listing is small, but it shows the Council can act on Hamas while stalling on settlers.

Kallas called on 14 November for unhindered UN access and the reopening of all Gaza crossings amid flooding and winter conditions.

Kallas condemned Iranian and Houthi attacks on Bahrain and Saudi Arabia and called for a fully reopened Strait of Hormuz (unconfirmed, secondary summary).

The UK and Australia imposed settlement-related sanctions on 20 November, while the EU remains split: Spain, Belgium and Ireland in favour, Germany and Italy opposed. A European citizens' initiative against the Association Agreement reportedly passed one million signatures.

A poll of 6,299 EU adults found 54% back a ban on trade with illegal settlements and 47% back suspending the trade parts of the Association Agreement.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

Kallas said on 16 November that the 22nd sanctions package will be presented "in the coming days", and von der Leyen said it would come before the 26 to 27 November summit. Likely targets are Russian banks, crypto providers, about 65 shadow-fleet tankers and a visa ban on former Russian fighters, which France and Italy have objected to.

A maritime services ban is unlikely because of opposition from Greece, Malta and Cyprus (single-source, Politico via aggregator) (unconfirmed).

On 16 November the Council listed 12 individuals and 101 entities over attacks on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure, plus 30 individuals and two entities under the hybrid-threats regime.

Hungary's shift on clusters 4 and 5 is tied to PM Péter Magyar and to Kyiv amending minority-language laws. Budapest set no public conditions this time, but formal steps are pending.

Russia launched over 400 drones and 110 missiles at Ukraine on 20 November, the same day missiles landed in Poland.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. The most recent step was the 7 October agreement on a Return Order with longer detention and return hubs outside the EU.

Background: Greece's migration minister says the first return hub could open in the second half of 2027 in an African country, and Brunner says hubs are voluntary. Rights groups warn of offshore detention.

The EU-UK summit created a Structured Dialogue on migration.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

The Commission estimates interest on the 45 billion euros loan at about 2 euros-2.5bn a year, with member states subsidising it, and around 6 billion euros a year across both Ukraine loans.

The EU-US framework caps most EU goods at 15% but excludes steel and aluminium derivatives at up to 50%, and the EU wants that fixed by 31 December. The Commission can suspend concessions after that date, with a compliance report due 1 December.

The EU-UK deal links carbon markets so British and EU goods such as steel are exempt from each other's border carbon levy.

The Commission's MFF proposal is close to 2 eurostrillion for 2028 to 2034, and failure to agree by year-end would roll over 2027 ceilings.

9. Defence and security.

NATO called the Russian missile landings in Poland a "grave violation" of airspace, and Tusk said Poland will share debris with the US. Reports differ on whether two or three objects fell, and Trump reportedly called earlier incidents mistakes (unconfirmed).

A non-public European Defence Agency report reportedly shows the EU is not rearming fast enough to be ready against Russia by 2030, per Reuters via Kubilius coverage.

The 28.3 billion euros SAFE loan tranche for 2026 is reportedly on track, with 12 billion euros disbursed (unconfirmed).

The EU-UK statement commits to enhanced security and defence cooperation but is thin on detail.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: no major CJEU ruling in the last 72 hours. The latest Hungary judgments were 15 October (C-521/25) and 22 October (C-660/24, with Czechia).

The Commission's partial clearance of Hungary's recovery funds after rule-of-law reforms will be debated in Parliament on Tuesday.

Pending: an Advocate General opinion in February 2026 found Hungary's sovereignty law infringes EU law, and a judgment is awaited.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary under Magyar is moving from veto player to bargaining partner, easing on Ukraine clusters while still opposing the new 45 billion euros loan. The Hungarian line on the loan is inconsistent across reports (unconfirmed).

Merz and Magyar agreed on 10 November to deepen cooperation on trade, energy and defence but still differ on the budget and Ukraine.

Germany's budget threat puts cohesion money for Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria in play.

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham used the Brussels summit to pursue a post-Brexit repair.

Slovenia's new government reversed its predecessor's measures on Israel in June, including the settlement-goods ban.
