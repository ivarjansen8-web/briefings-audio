Here is your EU news briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

EU readies 22nd Russia sanctions and 45 billion euros Ukraine loan as Syria deal advances.

Compiled from sources dated 14 to 17 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

EU foreign ministers adopted a fresh batch of Russia sanctions listings and said the 22nd package will be tabled "in the coming days". Sources differ on the count (113 pre-announced, 140 per Kallas, 145 per another report), and the targets include refineries, banks and shadow-fleet enablers in the Gulf and East Asia. The package is meant to be ready before the 26 to 27 November summit.

The Commission is expected on Wednesday 18 November to propose a new Ukraine support loan, reportedly about 45 billion euros. The FT says it would cover two-thirds of Ukraine's 2027 gap (about 70 billion euros), needs all 27 capitals, and may be paired with sanctions to shield it from a veto. Euronews, 16 November; UNN, citing FT (unconfirmed figure)

Foreign ministers agreed to advance the EU-Syria Association Agreement. Kallas called it a "major upgrade" of ties, with a parallel mandate to negotiate a modernised text with human-rights conditionality.

Von der Leyen said Europe will join Russia-Ukraine peace talks in an "E5 + EU" format. Kallas insists a ceasefire must come first and called Russia's demands "absurd".

2. European Commission.

The Commission proposed the Digital Fairness Act on 11 November. It bans ten manipulative design practices, sets rights for consumers using AI shopping agents, and allows fines up to 6% of global turnover in big cross-border cases. Industry group IAB puts business costs at 678 million euros-1.2bn a year.

The Commission disbursed 1.71 billion euros for Ukrainian drones, ammunition and air defence. The money counts towards the 90 billion euros loan.

The Commission is set to approve about 10 billion euros of frozen recovery funds for Hungary. This follows the May deal with Péter Magyar's government to release 16.4 billion euros, and payments must be authorised by end-2026.

Von der Leyen told the Enlargement Forum the EU must finish its own homework before admitting new members. She said existing treaties already allow more efficient decision-making and singled out Montenegro and Albania.

3. European Parliament.

Parliament is not in plenary this week. The last session was Brussels, 11 to 12 November, and the next Strasbourg session runs 23 to 26 November.

MEPs endorsed proxy voting for members during pregnancy and after childbirth in the 11 to 12 November session. The idea came from President Metsola.

Civil-society groups wrote to Metsola on 10 November over the 7 October "Islamist entryism" debate. They say some remarks breached the Rules of Procedure.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

The Council approved nearly 8 billion euros for Ukraine's reconstruction, described as its largest single disbursement.

A special European Council on the 2028 to 34 MFF and Ukraine's 2027 funding is set for 26 to 27 November. Reports differ on whether it lasts one day or two. Costa is expected to table a new "negotiating box" around 21 November.

Merz reportedly conditions German approval of the budget on all states backing future Ukraine loans. Hungary, Czechia, Slovakia and Bulgaria reject that.

Costa and von der Leyen met Zelensky in Dublin on 12 November, during the informal European Council.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

The Foreign Affairs Council on 16 November backed moving ahead with the Syria Association Agreement. The 2008 text was never signed over human-rights concerns, so the EU wants a modernised version with conditionality.

Kallas's 14 November statement demanded Israel reopen all Gaza crossings and allow unimpeded aid as winter floods worsen conditions. It called the 12 November strike on Nasser Hospital "deeply alarming" and said Hamas must have no role in governing Gaza. Israel denies blocking aid.

Kallas said the EU has imposed "sweeping sanctions" on Iran over its nuclear programme, human rights and freedom of navigation. The reports give no new Iran measures.

Ministers also met counterparts from the South Caucasus, Central Asia, Moldova, Türkiye and Ukraine on Black Sea and connectivity security.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The Council sanctioned 10 people and 17 organisations over the deportation of Ukrainian children. This sits alongside the larger Russia listings agreed on 16 November.

Kallas said the 22nd package could bar Russians who fought in Ukraine from entering the EU. Some capitals doubt it is workable, and the Commission starts consultations with member states on Tuesday.

Montenegro is set to close chapters 14, 15 and 22 on 19 November. That would take it to 26 of 33 chapters, and talks could end by year-end. Coreper is due to approve on 18 November.

Kyiv wants its roughly 70 billion euros 2027 budget hole filled before Europe's election-heavy 2027.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

The Return Regulation, politically agreed in June, is drawing fresh criticism. Statewatch says it greatly expands detention, with no maximum for people deemed a security risk, and return hubs in third countries apply at once.

Spain's intelligence service found no evidence Morocco orchestrated the July Ceuta entries. The report is dated 11 November, and the crisis is still politically live across the EU.

About 1,580 migrants reached the Canary Islands on 14 to 15 November.

Italy and Spain agreed in October to lift mutual border controls after the Ceuta tension. France and Germany have signalled they want to keep controls.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

Leaders want an MFF deal by end-2026. Costa says new EU-level taxes must be part of it. December looks the likelier landing date, with a summit from 17 December.

Šefčovič stresses the Turnberry deal after the US midterms. If Washington does not align tariffs with the 15% cap by end-December, the EU can suspend concessions.

A 2 eurosfee on all small parcels entering the EU applies from 1 November. It follows the 3 eurosduty from July.

The 9 October EU-China car deal aims to cut Chinese hybrid exports by more than half. Implementation details are open, and the sides meet by video in January.

9. Defence and security.

The Commission opened a second SAFE round worth 21.3 billion euros. Kubilius urges countries to fund joint projects with Ukraine, covering air defence, drones and ammunition.

A revision of the defence procurement directive is due in late November or early December.

Kubilius defended von der Leyen's push for an EU security council before MEPs.

Kallas said the Council wants a more active role in peace talks. She also floated obtaining lists of Russian combatants for entry bans.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. No CJEU rule-of-law ruling dated 17 November turned up. A 12 November judgment in Poland v Parliament and Council exists, but its outcome could not be read.

Context: the Court found in April that Hungary's anti-LGBTI law breached Article 2 TEU, a first. The Commission referred Hungary to the Court in two more cases in July.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary's Magyar met Merz in Berlin on 10 November. Both seek to rebuild ties, though they differ on Ukraine.

Police detained Áron Orbán, Viktor Orbán's brother, on 9 November over alleged people-smuggling. Authorities also seized over 6bn forints in a probe of a former minister.

Publication of communist-era files forced Magyar to apologise in parliament. The head of the national security archives offered to resign.

Ireland currently holds the Council presidency and hosted the Dublin informal summit. Consilium, 12 November (unconfirmed)
