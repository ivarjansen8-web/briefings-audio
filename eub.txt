Here is your EU news briefing for Tuesday, 24 November.

EU proposes 22nd Russia sanctions package as Costa's trimmed EUR 1.78tn budget and Ukraine funding head to summit.

Compiled from sources dated 21 to 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Commission proposed a 22nd Russia sanctions package on 23 November, targeting banks, crypto platforms, the shadow fleet, energy services and Russian athletes. It needs unanimity among the 27 and lands days before the 26 to 27 November summit.

European Council President Costa cut the draft 2028 to 2034 budget to about EUR 1.781 trillion on 21 November, removing EUR 44 billion and adding a digital levy on US tech firms. Merz calls it only a "first step" and France objects on farm support and its net contribution. EUobserver; Streamlinefeed (unconfirmed on some figures)

Denmark accuses Russian intelligence of plotting to kill a US-Ukrainian rapper on its soil and has expelled Russia's defence attache. Von der Leyen called it "an attack on EU soil", and Copenhagen wants tourist visas for Russians halted.

Ukraine's 2027 financing gap is the other summit headline, with a roughly EUR 45bn loan for Kyiv under discussion. Germany is pressing Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria to drop their opt-outs from Ukraine aid.

2. European Commission.

Von der Leyen unveiled the 22nd sanctions package on 23 November, including a ban on Russian ex-combatants entering the EU. She tied it to a rise in Russian "non-conventional kinetic activities" in Europe.

The Commission adopted guidelines for stricter checks on Russian travellers, including rejecting passports without biometric features. This answers part of Denmark's visa demands.

Von der Leyen said the EU will soon propose a "new European Security Strategy" to reinforce preparedness and deterrence. It is the framework for the hybrid-threat response.

The Commission promised a proposal covering Ukraine's 2027 funding shortfall before the summit. Contents were not public in the sources found.

A 4 November Commission communication says 12 omnibus simplification packages have been tabled so far and promises a mid-term review of the Savings and Investments Union.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs sit in Strasbourg 23 to 26 November, with 28 votes and 15 debates listed. Votes begin Tuesday at 12:00.

MEPs debate Costa's new budget proposal on Wednesday morning. Parliament wants a more ambitious budget than the frugal capitals.

A debate on the humanitarian situation in Gaza is on Tuesday's agenda. Eunews describes it as coming "at last", which signals how contested the topic has been.

Hungary rule of law is debated Tuesday morning, with the resolution vote deferred to December.

Votes expected include organic-logo rules, the critical medicines act and a 300-day cap on environmental impact assessments. Parliament also set a EUR 10.72bn mandate for AgoraEU 2028 to 2034.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

A special European Council meets 26 to 27 November in Brussels on the 2028 to 2034 budget and Ukraine's 2027 financing, with a Euro Summit in inclusive format. Costa told Merz the Ukraine question would be settled there.

Fico called Merz's linkage of the budget to all states backing Ukraine "blackmail". Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria resist burden-sharing.

The Council listed 113 individuals and entities under the Ukraine sanctions framework on 16 November. This is separate from the 22nd package.

No Slovak veto of the 22nd package was reported as of 24 November. Fico blocked the 18th package in 2025 until he won guarantees, so watch Coreper.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

Foreign ministers on 16 November backed moving ahead with an updated EU-Syria Association Agreement, with new conditions. Al Jazeera reports on 23 November that diplomats fear the talks will centre on repatriation of Syrians.

Kallas said on 16 November that the EU is preparing further Iran sanctions and is ready to engage Israel's next government after its elections. Ministers will have a "deep dive" on the election result at the next meeting.

Kallas's office on 14 November demanded immediate humanitarian access to Gaza and protection of civilian infrastructure.

The Commission is weighing new import-declaration requirements to identify goods from Israeli settlements. Suspending trade concessions under the Association Agreement still lacks a qualified majority.

The EU adopted a third package of sanctions on extremist settlers and Hamas figures on 28 May, after Hungary dropped its veto. That precedent shows how Hungary's change of government has shifted Council dynamics.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The 22nd package would remove 60 Russian banks from SWIFT, target crypto and payment networks and bar Russian-flag teams from EU sporting events.

The Commission's 4 November enlargement package set roadmaps for Montenegro, Albania, Ukraine and Moldova and says Ukraine has met the conditions to open all remaining clusters. Hungary's veto no longer blocks the path in the way it did.

Montenegro could conclude talks as early as December 2026 and has provisionally closed 23 of 33 chapters. Sources differ on the treaty signing date.

Kos warned on 16 November that Russia is trying to block enlargement. Informat (unconfirmed, single source)

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

The Pact on Migration and Asylum has applied since 12 June 2026.

Parliament and Council agreed the Return Regulation in June, including mutual recognition of return decisions and optional return hubs. Brunner says no state is obliged to set up hubs.

Brunner signed a readmission agreement with Nigeria in Abuja on 11 November. It follows the pattern of third-country returns deals. Democrata (unconfirmed, single source)

Syria talks may be driven by repatriation, according to diplomats. This links migration to the Middle East file.

Quiet otherwise: no new migration legislation in the last 72 hours.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

Costa's EUR 1.781tn MFF draft cuts EUR 44bn and adds a digital levy on US tech firms. Poorer states fear losing cohesion funds, richer ones want farm subsidies protected.

Merz says the budget growth of over 40 percent is too high and Paris opposes it over farm support. Reports on France's exact position differ. Streamlinefeed; Menafn (unconfirmed)

The Turnberry deal's steel deadline is the end of 2026, after which the EU can trigger a suspension clause if the US does not cut steel-derivative tariffs to 15 percent. Parliament has conditioned the deal on it.

The Eurogroup will review euro area fiscal policy in December, with the euro area deficit forecast at 3.5 percent of GDP in 2027.

9. Defence and security.

Von der Leyen framed the Danish plot as an attack on all member states and floated rapid-response teams and sanctions on those responsible.

Denmark will press ambassadors to refuse non-biometric Russian passports and halt tourist visas. Estonia says Europe cannot limit itself to condemnation and will close several border checkpoints from 1 December.

Kubilius told MEPs in autumn that the EU is not rearming fast enough for 2030, and the next MFF allots only about EUR 130bn for defence and space.

Quiet otherwise: no new Readiness 2030 or SAFE decisions found in the last 72 hours.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Parliament debates Hungary's rule-of-law record on Tuesday, with the vote in December.

Euronews Verify reports Hungary's emergency rule by decree has ended and the Sovereignty Protection Office has been abolished, though scrutiny continues.

The Court found in April a standalone breach of Article 2 TEU by Hungary and on 30 September dismissed six universities' challenges to Erasmus+ funding restrictions.

The Council released EUR 4.2bn in cohesion funds to Hungary in October, but little of the money has reached the Treasury.

Quiet otherwise: no major Court judgment found for 23 to 24 November.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Merz is linking approval of the next budget to all members supporting Ukraine. Fico calls it blackmail.

Macron and Merz face domestic budget debates this week in the French Senate and Bundestag. That limits their room to compromise in Brussels.

Spain's Sanchez is reported to be at risk of losing power. EUobserver (unconfirmed, headline only)

Hungary under Magyar has reversed Orban-era vetoes, as on settler sanctions.

Denmark is pressing partners for concrete steps against Moscow.
