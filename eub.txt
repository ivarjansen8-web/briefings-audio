Here is your EU news briefing for Friday, 13 November.

Russia sanctions package and Ukraine funding loom as Dublin summit ends; Hungary funds thaw.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 13 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Von der Leyen announced a 22nd Russia sanctions package and a Ukraine financing proposal for 2027, to land before the 26 to 27 November special European Council. Diplomats say it targets banks, crypto platforms and third-country circumvention, so Ukraine's 2027 funding gap and the next MFF will be fought out at the same summit.

The ninth European Political Community summit in Dublin drew about 50 leaders but produced few concrete results. Zelenskyy's meeting with US envoys overshadowed the agenda, and Macron said around 40 countries agreed "common principles for Europe's future security".

Europe and Russia are in a row over who sits at the Ukraine peace table. Moscow says the Abu Dhabi talks are Russia-Ukraine-US only, while the Commission and Council are sending a joint team. The two-day round ended 11 November with only an in-principle swap of 314 POWs.

The Commission is set to approve almost all of Hungary's roughly EUR 10bn in recovery money next week. Payments must be authorised by end-November, a sign of how far relations have thawed since Péter Magyar replaced Orbán in May.

2. European Commission.

The Commission proposed the Digital Fairness Act on 11 November, alongside a Simplification Directive. It targets dark patterns, addictive design and influencer marketing, and bans profiling-based ads aimed at minors. A revised consumer cooperation regulation would give Brussels direct enforcement powers with fines up to 6% of global turnover.

Preliminary findings that TikTok breached the Digital Services Act were issued on 6 November. It keeps platform enforcement on the agenda as the Commission handles the transatlantic trade dispute. EU news portal, November 2026 (unconfirmed)

The Commission published its 2026 enlargement reports on 4 November, including one saying Moldova aims to close talks by 2028 and join by 2030. Chisinau's timetable depends on unblocking negotiating clusters.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs elected Tilly Metz (Greens/EFA, Luxembourg) as a Vice-President at the 11 to 12 November mini-plenary. She fills the seat vacated by Nicolae Stefanuta. UNN, 12 November (unconfirmed)

Parliament adopted a resolution on deported Ukrainian children. It keeps the issue on the EU agenda while peace talks continue. UNN, 12 November (unconfirmed)

Parliament and Council negotiators agreed the 2027 annual budget on 11 November: EUR 199.7bn in commitments and EUR 211.5bn in payments. The final plenary vote is set for 24 November and closes out the current MFF.

The Franco-German push to soften car CO2 rules faces a plenary vote in November. The EPP is the largest group, so its position decides the outcome.

4. Council and European Council.

Leaders hold an informal meeting in Dublin today, 13 November, chaired by Costa. Costa is expected to table an updated MFF proposal afterwards.

Four governments (reportedly Hungary, Slovakia, Czechia and Bulgaria) resist more contributions and joint debt for Ukraine. That makes unanimity on the 26 to 27 November package hard. Rio Times (unconfirmed)

EU ambassadors have reportedly preliminarily agreed to list 145 individuals and entities, with approval planned for 16 November. It is separate from the 22nd package. IR-Press, 12 November (unconfirmed)

The Foreign Affairs Council meets 16 November and the General Affairs Council on 19 November. The GAC will cover simplification omnibus packages.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

EU foreign ministers will discuss the Middle East, with a focus on Syria, on 16 November. Ukraine is also on the agenda.

Commissioner Dubravka Suica and Syria's foreign minister co-chair the Syria Partnership Coordination Forum in Damascus. An internal EEAS document says the EU wants to limit Russian and other foreign influence in Syria; no funding pledges are expected.

G7 foreign ministers meeting in Paris on 9 to 10 November backed diplomacy on Iran's nuclear programme. They urged Iran to end attacks on Hormuz shipping, in the shadow of the war that began on 28 February.

The EU adopted its third settler sanctions package on 28 May, but member states still lack consensus on curbing trade with Israel. The Association Agreement remains unsuspended. No new Israel decision was found in the last 72 hours.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

Von der Leyen and Costa told Zelenskyy in Dublin that the EU will keep supporting Ukraine and that 2027 financing will be discussed at the 26 to 27 November summit.

Poland's Sikorski warned against "Western Europe dealing with Russia over the heads of East Europeans". It shows the split over how Europe is represented in US-brokered talks.

Russia's lead negotiator Medinsky said Moscow sees no reason to meet Europeans on ending the war.

The EUR 90bn interest-free loan for Ukraine for 2026 to 27 stays in place, while a push to use EUR 210bn of frozen Russian assets resurfaced in October. Belgium still opposes the latter.

7. Migration and asylum.

The Return Regulation (EU) 2026/2280 was signed 7 October and published 15 October, with return-hub provisions applying immediately. It allows return hubs outside the EU and detention up to 24 months.

Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands, Austria and Greece reportedly plan a pilot to transfer rejected asylum seekers outside the EU in 2027. The European Post (unconfirmed)

Quiet: no new Pact or third-country partnership decision found in the last 72 hours.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

The MFF 2028 to 2034 talks run on the Irish Presidency's negotiating box, which Parliament criticised. The Commission proposed EUR 100bn for post-2027 Ukraine support.

Sefcovic is pressing Washington to bring steel-linked tariffs back within the 15% Turnberry cap by end-December. The EU can otherwise suspend concessions to US exporters.

Berlin and Paris's deal to ease car emissions rules, including the 2035 combustion ban, is meeting opposition from other member states.

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius called a European Defence Agency finding that the EU is not rearming fast enough a "warning signal". A defence market package with a revised procurement directive is due by end-November or early December.

The EUR 28.3bn SAFE weapons loan for 2026 is on track, with EUR 12bn disbursed.

Parliament's mini-plenary also took up a defence-industry report. UNN, 12 November (unconfirmed)

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

Background: the Court's April ruling (C-769/22) found Hungary's anti-LGBTQ law breaches EU values. Hungary's parliament reportedly amended it in October.

The EU ended the conditionality mechanism against Hungary in mid-October, with about EUR 3.5bn still blocked.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary's Fidesz calls the funds release a "Brussels-Tisza pact". Magyar's government now sets the tone in Council.

Trump lost Congress in the US midterms, and Brussels is pressing for tariff cuts and Ukraine aid.

Ireland holds the Council presidency and hosted the EPC, giving Dublin agenda-setting weight on the MFF.

What to watch.

13 November: informal Dublin leaders' meeting; watch for Costa's revised MFF proposal.

16 November: Foreign Affairs Council (Syria, Middle East, Ukraine) and possible adoption of the 145 sanctions listings (unconfirmed).

Week of 16 November: Commission decision on Hungary's roughly EUR 10bn recovery payment.

19 November: General Affairs Council, simplification omnibus.

24 November: Parliament plenary vote on the 2027 budget.

26 to 27 November: special European Council on Ukraine financing and the MFF; the 22nd sanctions package is due before it.
