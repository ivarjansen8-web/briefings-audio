Here is your EU news briefing for Sunday, 8 November.

TikTok hit with preliminary DSA breach finding as von der Leyen heads to Black Sea; EU enlargement roadmaps published.

Compiled from sources dated 4 to 8 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Commission preliminarily found TikTok's "For You" feed in breach of the Digital Services Act on 6 November, over its "rabbit hole" effect. A confirmed breach could mean a fine of up to 6% of global turnover, and TikTok disputes the findings; no fine has been set.

Ursula von der Leyen is due in Romania on Monday 9 November, then Bulgaria, on a Black Sea tour after drone strikes on commercial ships. Talks are to cover Black Sea security, energy, enlargement and the next EU budget; she then goes to COP31 in Antalya. Reports on the itinerary are thin (unconfirmed).

The Commission published its 2026 Enlargement Package on 4 November with indicative roadmaps for Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine. Montenegro is on track to join in 2028, and the Commission says Ukraine has met the conditions to open all remaining clusters.

Democrats won Congress in the US midterms, and European officials are cautiously hopeful on Ukraine and tariffs. Commentators expect relief but no reset in transatlantic relations.

2. European Commission.

The Commission found TikTok's recommender system poses unassessed mental-health risks, and says the feed design must change. The case dates back to formal proceedings opened in February 2024.

Von der Leyen's week: Romania (Mon), Varna in Bulgaria (Tue), COP31 in Antalya (Wed), then Dublin (Thu-Fri). The Dublin leg covers the European Political Community and an informal European Council.

The Commission's Autumn 2026 Economic Forecast is listed for 17 November. The spring forecast saw EU inflation at 3.1% and flagged the Middle East conflict as the main uncertainty.

The College meets on 11 November. It also hosts research and innovation days on 10 to 11 November.

3. European Parliament.

Three Parliament committees (ENVI, IMCO, TRAN) backed the automotive simplification package 105 to 39 with 21 abstentions. The Commission estimates savings of about 706 eurosmillion a year.

A committee voted to ease the 2035 car CO2 target to an 87.5% cut, with a 7.5% top-up for EU-made low-carbon steel. Three mainstream groups struck the deal, and the full plenary vote is expected in December.

The clean corporate vehicles file passed committee 89 to 29 with four abstentions. It is separate from the main package, which is also headed for December.

A Brussels mini-plenary is scheduled for 11 to 12 November, with Strasbourg from 23 November. I could not confirm the mini-plenary agenda (unconfirmed).

MEPs rejected the Irish Presidency's MFF negotiating box, and more than 1,000 amendments were tabled to the "Performance" Regulation. That shows how divided Parliament is on the budget.

4. Council and European Council.

Leaders meet in Dublin: the European Political Community summit is on 12 November and an informal European Council on 13 November. Over 50 leaders attend the EPC, and no detailed agenda has been published.

A special European Council on 26 to 27 November is expected to focus on the 2028 to 2034 MFF. The Council's calendar still marks it tentative; the next regular summit is 17 to 18 December.

The EU and Montenegro provisionally closed two more negotiating chapters (goods, food safety and veterinary policy) on 6 November. That brings Podgorica closer to the 2028 target.

The Council adopted its position on simplifying energy and tyre labelling on 6 November. It is a negotiating position, not a final law.

The Eurogroup President issued a statement on nominations for ECB President on 6 November. Details were not in the snippet I saw (unconfirmed).

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

Quiet on formal EEAS action: I found no new Council decision on Israel or Gaza in the last 72 hours. The latest sanctions on violent settlers and Hamas figures were agreed in May after Hungary's new government dropped its veto.

ECFR is working on an effort to prevent another US-Iran war, and a 6 November podcast discusses what the midterms mean for the Iran war. It describes Europeans and regional powers building a diplomatic framework without US leadership.

The Iran war and Hormuz closure keep energy prices high: gas fell to about 85 euros/MWh on 5 November, over 15% below its four-year high, on deal hopes. Europe enters winter with its lowest gas storage in over a decade.

Euronews reports on a proposed EU-wide windfall tax on oil profits. Greenpeace estimates oil firms earned 36 eurosbillion extra on EU fuel sales since the war began.

EU Gaza pledges stand at nearly 900 eurosmillion, announced at a 13 July donor meeting, as the Board of Peace's reconstruction plan depends on Hamas disarming. Reporting from September says little has changed on the ground.

Lebanon: ministers set up an EU support mission for the Lebanese Armed Forces in October. Kallas also welcomed closer Beirut-Damascus ties.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The Commission says Ukraine has met the conditions to open all remaining negotiating clusters. Commissioner Kos says it is too early to set an end date, since the process depends on political will.

Hungary's Magyar government still conditions further steps for Ukraine on minority-rights guarantees. Budapest has opposed new clusters since September while lifting broader vetoes on arms funds.

The Council's 12 October package listed almost 1,600 individuals and entities, the largest ever. More than half the package relates to ballistic missile production.

At least seven commercial vessels were hit by drones in the Black Sea last month. Romania blames Russia, while Bulgaria cites drones from "an eastern direction".

The US floated unfreezing Russian assets in mid-October, and European officials made clear it would not be accepted. The EU runs a 90 eurosbillion budget-backed loan, and the reparations-loan idea is being pushed again by Sweden, the Netherlands, Spain and Poland (unconfirmed).

7. Migration and asylum.

The Commission launched its second Annual Migration Management Cycle on 6 November. It names Italy, Greece, Cyprus and Spain as under migratory pressure, though the Commission's own release lists only three of them.

Eurodac is now operational in all member states, and 15 more states are classed as at risk of pressure. The report covers 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026 and feeds the 2027 Solidarity Pool.

Crete arrivals rose about 105% while overall crossings into Greece fell about 23%. Brunner praised Athens' use of the new border procedures (Greek outlet, unconfirmed).

The Pact on Migration and Asylum has applied since 12 June 2026. The Return Regulation, politically agreed on 1 June and approved by interior ministers on 1 October, allows return hubs in third countries.

Civil society groups object to the Return Regulation's Article 23, which allows home raids to enforce deportations. I could not confirm the Official Journal publication date.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

Šefčovič is pressing Washington to honour the Turnberry deal's 15% cap. If US rates on steel-heavy goods stay up to 50% past end-December, the EU can suspend its concessions.

The special European Council on 26 to 27 November aims at the MFF, but a deal after 2026 is no longer ruled out. Some reports point to a conclusion by April 2027.

The Council secured its negotiating position on the post-2027 farm policy on 26 October. The size of the ringfenced farm envelope is left to the overall MFF talks.

France and Germany asked for a new trade tool allowing an "immediate cut off" from the single market. It targets China, and the Commission could act unless a qualified majority objects.

Gas near 85 euros/MWh and an energy-profits tax debate dominate the cost-of-living picture. The IEA says Europe has its lowest gas stocks in over a decade.

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius says the EU is not rearming fast enough for its 2030 readiness goal, citing a classified EDA report. He contrasts Russian and EU missile output: 1,100 versus 300 cruise missiles a year, and 900 versus zero ballistic.

A revision of the defence procurement directive is due in late November or early December. The Defence Readiness Omnibus is meant to be adopted by end-2026.

A Black Sea security hub is planned for Varna and Constanta, and NATO has stepped up surveillance. Von der Leyen called the attacks "reckless" and a gross violation of international law.

Kubilius is pushing a European Defence Union that includes the UK, Ukraine, Norway and Canada. He wants to drop unanimity in defence, and critics call it a distraction.

SAFE offers up to 150 eurosbillion in loans, and Hungary cut its request to 5.4 eurosbillion. The cut is about two-thirds.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. I found no CJEU judgment dated 5 to 7 November on rule of law.

Background: Hungary's parliament passed an amendment in October removing the LGBTQ-related provisions after the April CJEU ruling. The ruling found a breach of Article 2 TEU.

Background: the Commission's 2026 Rule of Law Report came out on 16 to 17 July. It includes a Hungary country chapter.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary: Magyar's government unlocked 16.4 eurosbillion in EU funds in May. He floats a binding referendum on Ukraine's final accession.

France: the minority Lecornu government survived no-confidence votes in January with Socialist support. France's April 2027 presidential election is a deadline for the MFF.

Romania: President Nicusor Dan hosts von der Leyen on 9 November. The Prime Minister is named as Luca Niculescu in one report (unconfirmed).

Spain, Italy and Poland all vote in 2027, which analysts say could stall the MFF. Meloni-Merz cooperation is also questioning the old Franco-German axis.
