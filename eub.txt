Here is your EU news briefing for Sunday, 15 November.

Europe picks E5 team for Ukraine talks as Merz ties EU budget to Ukraine funding.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

EU leaders agreed in Dublin on 13 November that the E5 (UK, France, Germany, Italy, Poland) plus the EU institutions will represent Europe in Ukraine peace talks. The format rejected calls from Washington, Kyiv and Moscow for one European envoy, and the Kremlin has already voiced scepticism.

Merz has made German backing for the 2028 to 2034 EU budget conditional on a Ukraine financing solution that all member states support. The gap is projected at about 70 billion euros for 2027, with an expected EU share of 45 billion euros, and Fico called the linkage "blackmail".

Von der Leyen said Europe needs at least 500 billion euros of AI investment and promised a proposal at the special summit. About 30 billion euros is being mobilised for seven AI Gigafactories, and an expert report is due within two weeks.

The Commission will present the 22nd Russia sanctions package and an option paper on Ukraine's 2027 funding before the special European Council on 26 to 27 November. This sets up the budget-for-Ukraine standoff as the month's main fight.

2. European Commission.

The Commission is expected to approve most of Hungary's 10 billion euros recovery-fund allocation this month. Officials aim to finish the remaining recovery-fund assessments by end-November so payments can go out before year-end.

Von der Leyen will table an AI investment proposal and an expert-led AI adoption plan within two weeks. It positions the Commission to lead on tech sovereignty ahead of the 26 to 27 November summit.

Šefčovič says the 'DiversifyEU' trade instrument will be presented by year-end, alongside work on a faster trade "kill switch". It signals a push to reduce trade dependencies.

The Commission is weighing new import-declaration requirements for goods from Israeli settlements. An official told MEPs that EU-Israel tariff preferences do not apply to goods from territories occupied since 1967.

3. European Parliament.

Parliament held a mini-plenary in Brussels on 11 to 12 November. Thursday's votes included final texts on the retail investment framework and ECHA governance.

MEPs debated cyber resilience for critical infrastructure and further sanctions on Russia. The sanctions debate feeds into the 22nd package due before the summit.

The next Strasbourg session runs 23 to 26 November. It is the first chance for MEPs to react to the Dublin outcomes and the budget row.

Parliament is challenging the Article 122 legal basis of the SAFE defence loans at the Court. Kubilius says an annulment would not automatically suspend SAFE.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

The ninth European Political Community summit in Dublin on 12 November set three security goals: a ceasefire, restored Black Sea navigation and conditions for a peace deal. Costa and von der Leyen met Zelensky there.

The informal European Council on 13 November focused on AI and Ukraine and ended without budget decisions. Merz's letter to Costa put the Ukraine funding gap on the agenda.

Hungary's Magyar and Czechia's Babiš have signalled unease about linking the budget to Ukraine. Babiš warned a blocked budget would also freeze Czech funds.

Costa's special summit on 26 to 27 November is expected to centre on the MFF. The next regular summit is 17 to 18 December.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

Kallas's office urged Israel to open all Gaza crossings after flooding hit at least 106,000 people (UNICEF figure). It called the strike on the Nasser Medical Complex "deeply alarming" and restated support for two states (unconfirmed: relayed by secondary outlets).

Foreign ministers meet on 16 November with Syria as the Middle East focus. HRW urged the EU on 12 November to tie its Syria relations to human-rights benchmarks and condemn Israeli abuses in the south.

The EU agreed sanctions on West Bank settlers in May after Hungary's veto lifted. Association Agreement suspension remains unlikely despite Irish backing.

The EU stands by Lebanon's sovereignty and is setting up a mission to train Lebanese forces. It also deplores renewed US-Iran hostilities and Iranian disruption of Hormuz shipping (undated EEAS statements).

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The 22nd sanctions package is expected to hit banks, crypto platforms and third-country circumvention. One report says ambassadors agreed 145 listings, with approval due 16 November (unconfirmed).

Trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on 11 November ended without a breakthrough. Both sides agreed in principle to a prisoner exchange.

The 90 billion euros EU loan agreed in December 2025 remains the backbone of Ukraine funding, but its continuation beyond 2027 is contested. Fico, Babiš and Magyar resist joint borrowing.

Two more accession clusters for Ukraine opened on 13 October. Remaining clusters now look likely by year-end, and Montenegro has closed 18 of 33 chapters.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

The Return Regulation was adopted in October, allowing "return hubs" outside the EU. Denmark is among five states working on a first hub.

Brunner says the return rate rose from 19% in 2023 to 38% in H1 2026, and irregular crossings fell 55% in two years. The Commission's second annual migration cycle launched on 6 November.

The Commission named Italy, Greece, Cyprus and Spain as states under migratory pressure. That triggers solidarity under the Pact, which applies since 12 June 2026.

Denmark, Italy and the UK issued a joint call for tougher returns. The EU also concluded a return agreement with Nigeria.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

The MFF 2028 to 2034 talks are in a decisive two-month window. Ukraine financing is now the lever in the dispute.

Šefčovič describes the EU-China deal as a "first phase" with a quota of 350,000 hybrid cars a year over four years. China would also drop tariffs on EU car parts, olive oil and footwear (undated, unconfirmed).

French 10-year yields are near 5%, with a spread over Bunds of about 152bp. Strikes on 5 November against the 2027 budget raise euro-area risk.

TEN-E regulation talks made progress and a deal is expected in early December. Fisheries statistics also got a framework deal.

9. Defence and security.

Defence ministers meet on 17 November under Kallas to discuss EU-China security, Ukrainian air defence and transatlantic ties. Ukraine's defence minister is invited.

Kubilius says unused SAFE money (about 20 billion euros) could fund joint EU-Ukraine defence projects. Nineteen states use SAFE.

Kubilius backs the E5 as launchpad for a European Defence Union and a possible EU security council. The Dublin E5 decision lends this weight.

Zelensky offered Ireland drone co-production. It shows Kyiv courting neutral states on defence industry.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. For context, the Court ruled on 21 April in C-769/22 that Hungary violated Article 2 TEU, a first.

The General Affairs Council on 19 November holds rule-of-law dialogue on Cyprus, Lithuania, Luxembourg and Hungary.

Recent Hungary rulings on 15 and 22 October concerned detention and lawyer access.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Merz's budget ultimatum is the leading German-EU story. He faces AfD pressure at home.

France's minority government faces a budget deadlock and union strikes. A failure would force emergency financing and rattle the euro area.

Hungary under Magyar (since 9 May) is regaining EU funds after over 100 law changes. He is still wary of the budget-Ukraine link.

Fico calls the budget linkage blackmail, and Babiš warns of Czech losses. Together with Magyar they form a sceptical bloc on Ukraine borrowing.
