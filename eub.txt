Here is your EU news briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

Dublin summits open as EU strikes 2027 budget deal and unveils Digital Fairness Act.

Compiled from sources dated 9 to 12 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Leaders from roughly 48 European states opened the 9th European Political Community summit in Dublin on 12 November, with Zelensky, Carney and NATO's Rutte attending. Costa and Taoiseach Micheál Martin co-chair under the theme "Europe - strong, secure and connected"; the largest Garda security operation ever surrounds it.

An informal meeting of EU heads of state or government follows on 13 November in Dublin, with a Costa-Martin-von der Leyen press conference in the afternoon. The 2028 to 2034 budget (MFF) is expected to dominate, and Costa is expected to table an amended "negotiating box" afterwards.

Parliament and Council struck a deal on the 2027 EU budget: EUR 199.7bn in commitments and EUR 211.5bn in payments. It is the last annual budget under the 2021 to 2027 framework; the plenary vote is pencilled in for 24 November. (Sources differ on whether the deal came on 10 or 11 November.)

The Commission proposed the Digital Fairness Act on 11 November, targeting dark patterns, addictive design and loot boxes for minors. Press reports cite fines up to 6% of turnover; the Commission-side figures are not yet confirmed from the primary text. JournalArta, 11 November (unconfirmed)

2. European Commission.

The Digital Fairness Act would bring one consumer-protection standard online and offline: transparent pricing (including in-app currencies), a ban on manipulative marketing, and user control over autoplay and infinite scroll. Reported savings for consumers are up to EUR 13.7bn a year; it will be negotiated alongside the planned Kids Act.

Von der Leyen pledged EUR 50m on 10 November to speed up a Black Sea maritime security hub in Bulgaria and Romania. The hub is planned for Constanta and Varna after drone attacks on cargo ships.

At COP31 in Antalya on 11 November, von der Leyen backed the "35 by 35" goal of electricity reaching 35% of global final energy use by 2035. It frames the EU's energy-transition pitch at the summit.

Šefčovič says a trade "kill-switch" against market disruption, aimed at China, should be presented by year-end. It would let the EU limit imports quickly; Beijing's ambassador threatened retaliation.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs meet in a Brussels mini-plenary on 11 to 12 November. The agenda includes the 2028 to 2034 civil protection mechanism (debate Wednesday, vote Thursday), where MEPs are expected to ask for more money than the Commission proposed.

MEPs debated with Kaja Kallas how to bring back Ukrainian children deported to Russia, with a resolution vote on Thursday. It also assesses whether more sanctions on Russia are needed.

Parliament must still ratify the 2027 budget deal; the plenary vote is expected 24 November. In October it had voted to reverse all Council cuts to the Commission's draft.

Final vote results from 12 November were not yet indexed at time of writing. Check the plenary page for tallies. (unconfirmed)

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

The EPC summit (12 November) and informal leaders' meeting (13 November) in Dublin are the week's main Council-side events. Informal meetings take no formal decisions.

The Irish Presidency's negotiating box for the 2028 to 2034 MFF has cuts of EUR 159bn in constant euros and draws fire from all sides. The Netherlands calls it unsatisfactory, Germany says cuts go the wrong way, Spain rules out further cuts, Lithuania is more positive.

Macron wants a long-term budget deal by Christmas and ahead of elections; Merz warns of "very, very difficult negotiations". The Irish Presidency aims for agreement by the end of 2026.

The Foreign Affairs Council meets 16 November and the General Affairs Council on 19 November.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

The EU is weighing new Iran sanctions after France attributed cyberattacks on European supermarket refrigeration systems to Iran. Kallas is open to it and ministers may discuss it on 16 November. Democrata, 10 November (unconfirmed)

A Commission official told Parliament on 5 November that the proposal to suspend the Israel Association Agreement stays on the Council's table without a qualified majority. The Commission is also considering new import-declaration requirements to separate settlement goods from Israeli goods.

Options to restrict settlement trade failed to be adopted at the 13 July Foreign Affairs Council. Kallas has said all 27 agree settlements are illegal; revoking the agreement needs unanimity, partial trade suspension a qualified majority.

In October the EU agreed a civilian-military mission to train Lebanon's armed forces, hoped to be ready by end-2026, as UNIFIL withdraws on 31 December. More than ten member states reportedly offered personnel; I found no November update.

US-Iran talks continue: Rubio says there is "no need" to restart combat operations, while Iran demands a halt on all fronts including Lebanon.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

EU ambassadors reportedly provisionally agreed a new Russia sanctions package on 11 November, with roughly 140 to 145 listings including about 50 banks. Final approval is expected at the 16 November Foreign Affairs Council; figures differ between reports. Nasha Niva (unconfirmed)

Zelensky, speaking in Dublin on 11 November, urged Europeans to name their negotiating representatives this month because talks will continue.

The EUR 90bn loan agreed in December 2025 was finalised in April; the frozen-assets reparations loan was shelved but the issue is back. 122 MEPs wrote to leaders in October urging renewed work; Belgium remains opposed.

Ukraine's accession talks have opened clusters on fundamentals (15 June), external relations (14 July) and internal market (13 October).

The 21st sanctions package (23 July) hit energy, finance and crypto; on 12 October the Council listed almost 1,600 individuals and entities in the Russian military-industrial complex.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

Regulation (EU) 2026/2280 on returns took effect 16 October and replaces the 2008 Return Directive. Return-hub provisions apply immediately; the rest applies about October 2027.

It gives member states a legal basis to send people to return hubs outside the EU. Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands, Austria and Greece reportedly plan a pilot in 2027; no third country is forced to accept one. European Post (unconfirmed)

NGOs report detention of up to 24 months and powers to search homes and devices. PICUM calls it the "Deportation Regulation".

Brunner frames it as restoring control over "who comes to the EU, but also who has to leave". No new asylum developments dated 11 to 12 November turned up.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

The 2027 budget deal sets EUR 199.7bn commitments and EUR 211.5bn payments, just under the Commission's EUR 199.9bn/212bn draft. The Council had proposed EUR 191.88bn/202.07bn.

US tariffs on most EU goods stay at 15% under the Turnberry deal, but steel and aluminium derivatives face up to 50%. If Washington does not align by end-December, the EU can suspend concessions.

After the Democratic midterm win, Brussels is pressing for tariff cuts, though Trump keeps control of trade policy.

France and Germany back a rapid-response trade tool and separate "Made in Europe" car rules. Both still need other member states' backing.

9. Defence and security.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours beyond the Black Sea hub pledge.

Background: Kubilius warned in October that rearmament pace is "a warning signal" citing a classified EDA report. The European Drone Defence Initiative, a Readiness 2030 flagship, is meant to be fully functional by end-2027.

The planned Lebanon training mission is the EU's main new crisis-management project.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours at the Court; no 11 to 12 November judgments were found. The most recent notable ruling was 5 November.

On 5 November the Court set aside a General Court ruling and restored the Commission's 2020 approval of Dutch liquidity aid to KLM. The case returns to the General Court on Ryanair's remaining pleas.

The Court rejected German Khan's appeal in Khan v Council (C-530/25 P) on 5 November.

Hungary: after the change of government, the Council ended rule-of-law conditionality and EUR 4.2bn was unlocked. About EUR 500m stays frozen over the child-protection law and the Commission is still assessing Hungary's request to withdraw EUR 10bn in recovery funds.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Ireland hosts both summits as holder of the Council presidency, putting Taoiseach Martin at the centre.

Netherlands: the prime minister called the MFF box not satisfactory enough, a signal of continued frugal-bloc resistance.

Franco-German friction over the next EU budget persists despite alignment on trade defence.

Hungary under Prime Minister Magyar is moving to join the European Public Prosecutor's Office.
