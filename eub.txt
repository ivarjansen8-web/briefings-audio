Here is your EU news briefing for Monday, 16 November.

Europe picks joint Ukraine talks team as Merz ties EU budget to Ukraine funding; Gaza access demand.

Compiled from sources dated 11 to 16 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

EU leaders at the Dublin informal summit (13 November) rejected a single envoy and chose a joint negotiating team for Ukraine peace talks, made up of the EU institutions plus the E5 (UK, France, Germany, Italy, Poland). Washington, Kyiv and Moscow had all pressed for one European voice; Kremlin spokesman Peskov said Europe must first show it contributes to peace.

Merz has threatened to block the 2028 to 2034 EU budget unless all 27 member states help fund a roughly 45 billion euros share of a 70 billion euros Ukraine gap for 2027. Fico and Czech PM Babiš called this "blackmail" on 10 November, and Slovakia and Hungary say they will not join a new loan.

Foreign ministers meet today under Kaja Kallas with Ukraine, Russia's hybrid campaign, Syria and Israel-Palestine on the agenda. A side meeting with South Caucasus, Central Asian, Moldovan, Turkish and Ukrainian counterparts follows.

The EU called on Israel on 14 November to open all Gaza crossings as flooding and winter worsen conditions. The statement also called an airstrike on Nasser Hospital "deeply alarming".

2. European Commission.

The Commission plans to present a 22nd Russia sanctions package and a Ukraine financing proposal before the 26 to 27 November summit. Von der Leyen said both would come "by year-end" at the latest; diplomats say banks, crypto platforms and third-country circumvention are in scope.

Dombrovskis described a second joint-borrowing loan modelled on the 90 billion euros facility as "one of the central options". The earlier 90 billion euros loan, agreed in December 2025, is paid in two 45 billion euros tranches.

The 2027 Commission work programme (COM(2026) 710) was published on 4 November under the title "standing up for one another". Leaked drafts pointed to a European Security Emergency Protocol, an AI push and further environmental deregulation.

Von der Leyen called the two-day Dublin summit "intense" and confirmed the EU will engage via an E5+EU format with "maximum pressure" on Russia.

3. European Parliament.

Parliament adopted its position on the 2028 to 2034 civil protection mechanism on 12 November (424 to 105 to 83), raising the envelope to 12.42 billion euros from 10.68 billion euros. It is one of the first post-2027 budget files to move.

MEPs backed a defence-capabilities report 411 to 133 with 41 abstentions at the 11 to 12 November Brussels mini-plenary. The same session passed a geographical-indications deal (613 to 3) and a proxy-voting amendment to the MEP Statute (578 to 25).

Parliament adopted a resolution on deported Ukrainian children calling for more pressure on Russia.

The next Strasbourg plenary runs 23 to 26 November, with 27 to 28 votes listed for Tuesday 24 November.

4. Council and European Council.

A special European Council on 26 to 27 November will take up Ukraine's 2027 financing and the MFF. Costa has kept both on the agenda after Dublin.

Leaders are far apart on the MFF; the October European Council confirmed that positions diverge and an end-2026 deal is in jeopardy. Swedish PM Kristersson said "we are not in a hurry", and the budget needs unanimity.

Hungary and Slovakia say they will not block a Ukraine package funded by others, but Fico says Slovakia has the same veto power as Germany over the budget.

A Foreign Affairs Council (Defence) follows tomorrow and a Trade FAC on 30 November.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

The EU statement of 14 November urged Israel to allow "immediate, unimpeded" aid access and full implementation of the aid commitments in the Gaza peace plan. It also said Hamas must have no role in Gaza's future governance; UNICEF counts at least 106,000 people hit by flooding.

Today's FAC will take stock of Syria's political transition and touch on Israel-Palestine. The EU earlier lifted sanctions on Syria's interior and defence ministers and agreed settler sanctions that had been blocked under Orbán.

Saudi FM Prince Faisal bin Farhan is in Brussels asking for Hormuz naval escorts, a wider Aspides mandate and a rescheduled EU-GCC summit (unconfirmed, single outlet). Kallas has said there is "no appetite" to extend Aspides to Hormuz and is pushing states to send pledged ships to the Red Sea.

The EU is planning defence talks with Qatar and Gulf states after the 24 October EU-GCC summit was postponed over Gulf tensions. Kallas has also accused Iran of arming the Houthis, which Tehran calls baseless.

Commentary argues Europe pays for Ukraine but has little weight on Iran, as the US reportedly pauses strikes and keeps its Hormuz blockade (opinion).

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

Ambassadors preliminarily agreed on 11 November to list 145 more individuals and entities, targeting oil refining and the shadow fleet; final approval is expected today.

The 4 November Enlargement Package says Ukraine has met the conditions to open all remaining clusters, but unanimity still blocks it. Moldova and Ukraine have each opened four clusters; the Commission targets early-2028 and end-2028 conclusions.

Montenegro has closed 21 chapters and could sign its accession treaty as early as June 2027.

Ukraine and the EU agreed to speed key reforms, aiming to meet rule-of-law and democracy benchmarks in H1 2027.

Abu Dhabi trilateral talks produced prisoner swaps but a settlement remains distant.

7. Migration and asylum.

The Return Regulation (EU) 2026/2280 entered into force on 16 October, replacing the 2008 Return Directive and allowing third-country "return hubs". Per ECRE it extends maximum detention to 24 months.

Denmark, Austria, Germany, the Netherlands and Greece agreed a common hub model in September and expect a first partner deal around the New Year. Uganda and Rwanda are named in press reports; partners are undisclosed.

The Pact on Migration and Asylum has applied since June 2026; the law is read together with the new return rules.

Quiet: no new migration deals found in the last 72 hours.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

The EU-US deal caps tariffs on most EU exports at 15%, and Šefčovič is pressing for cuts on steel-derived products before December. If Washington does not move by end-December, the EU can suspend its concessions.

Trump's loss of Congress in the midterms could ease pressure on trade; incoming House trade chair Neal criticised "price-hiking tariffs".

Merz urged Brussels to hold firm on carbon-market reform, especially ETS2 due in 2028, despite growing opposition.

Macron and Merz proposed in October new EU powers to exclude unfair competitors from the single market, which the Commission welcomed.

9. Defence and security.

An EDA finding, still classified, reportedly says the EU is not rearming fast enough for its 2030 readiness goal, and Kubilius called it a warning signal.

Only a third of EU counter-drone programmes are expected to deliver by 2030, per a leaked-draft analysis; the drone-wall deadline is end-2026.

Defence ministers meet 17 November on EU-China security, air defence for Ukraine and transatlantic issues, with US official Elbridge Colby at lunch.

Kubilius says military mobility needs an initial 70 billion euros.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

On 5 November the Court set aside the General Court's annulment of the Commission's approval of Dutch aid to KLM, sending Ryanair's case back.

The Court held that national data protection authorities are controllers for complaint data under GDPR Article 77 (Mettler-Toledo, C-237/25).

In April the Court's Full Court found Hungary's anti-LGBT law breached Article 2 TEU values, with 18 billion euros still frozen.

Mekanova (C-515/25) on flight-compensation jurisdiction is due 26 November.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary are in open confrontation with Berlin over Ukraine aid.

Hungary's PM Péter Magyar reportedly wants an extra 2 billion euros in the next budget period, and has not confirmed Fico's claim that they share a position.

Merz and Macron have invited the Coalition of the Willing to Berlin on 19 November (UK leader named as Andy Burnham in one source, unconfirmed).

Latvia's PM questioned why Europe does not tap Russian assets directly, while Belgium previously blocked that route.
