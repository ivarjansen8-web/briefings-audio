Here is your EU news briefing for Wednesday, 18 November.

Autumn forecast lifts eurozone growth; Europe joins Ukraine talks; Russia sanctions and Syria deal advance.

Compiled from sources dated 15 to 18 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Commission raised its eurozone growth forecast to 1.2% for 2026 and 2027 (EU: 1.4%) in its Autumn Forecast on 17 November. Dombrovskis said the economy is holding up better than expected, but twelve member states run deficits above 3% of GDP this year, rising to 16 by 2028.

Europe's "E5 + EU" team (France, Germany, Italy, Poland, UK plus the EU) joined US-Ukraine talks on 16 November, the first time Europe sat at the table as a bloc. Zelensky said a Berlin meeting follows this week, possibly 19 November (unconfirmed date); territory in eastern Ukraine remains the sticking point.

The Council added 12 individuals and 101 entities to Russia sanctions lists on 16 November, with a fuller 22nd package still being prepared. Sources disagree on whether the 16 November step already counts as the 22nd package, so the final shape is open.

EU foreign ministers agreed on 16 November to push ahead with an EU-Syria association agreement. It is the main Middle East decision of the week and carries a human-rights conditionality debate.

2. European Commission.

The Commission preliminarily found Temu in breach of the Digital Services Act on 17 November for failing to stop the sale of illegal products. A fine of up to 6% of global turnover is possible; Temu already paid 200 eurosmillion in May 2026 for a separate breach.

The Commission is due to present a proposal today for a loan of up to 45 eurosbillion to cover part of Ukraine's 2027 budget gap. It would cover roughly two-thirds of the shortfall and feeds directly into the 26 to 27 November summit.

Member states postponed a vote on lifting the ban on Brazilian chicken imports despite a positive audit. The Commission needs more time for member-state questions before a new meeting in early December.

The Commission published guidance urging citizens to prepare for 72 hours without outside help in a crisis. It is part of the broader preparedness agenda.

3. European Parliament.

Parliament's November I plenary outlook lists final votes on the retail investment framework and the ECHA basic regulation, plus a debate on the civil protection mechanism (UCPM+). The outlook gives no calendar dates and sources conflict on the session dates (17 to 20 versus 23 to 26 November), so treat timing as (unconfirmed).

MEPs negotiating the MFF's national and regional partnership plans will not vote before January 2027, per Agence Europe (13 November). The Council and Commission worry about the slow pace, and over 1,000 amendments to the Performance Regulation show deep divisions.

Parliament defends a 2028 to 2034 budget of 1,789 eurosbillion excluding NGEU repayment, well above the Irish Presidency's 1.02% of GNI box. This gap frames the coming trilogue-style fight.

4. Council and European Council.

A special European Council on 26 to 27 November will focus on the 2028 to 2034 MFF and Ukraine's 2027 financing. Costa aims for a "landing zone" and a deal before end-2026; Sweden's Kristersson says "we are not in a hurry".

Costa is expected to table a revised negotiating box with figures on 21 November. The earlier 13 November date was superseded; the previous Irish box was roundly rejected on 10 October.

Merz wrote to Costa on 9 November that Germany will not back the budget unless partners help cover Ukraine's roughly 70 eurosbillion 2027 gap (unconfirmed, single-source). Czechia, Hungary and Slovakia reportedly resist joint borrowing.

The Council's new European Economic Area meeting took place on 17 November in Brussels. Routine agenda on digitalisation and the EEA Agreement.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

Foreign ministers agreed on 16 November to keep working toward signing the 2008 EU-Syria agreement while negotiating a modernised one. Several ministers demanded clear human-rights conditionality; Damascus confirmed it is drafting a joint framework and a Syria Partnership Coordination Forum met in Damascus on 17 November.

Kallas called on 14 to 15 November for Israel to reopen all Gaza crossings and allow unhindered aid after flooding, and for full Israeli withdrawal. It shows the EU still limited to statements rather than leverage.

The Commission is reportedly taking a larger role in EU-Israel diplomacy as Israel's foreign minister has cut ties with Kallas since June (unconfirmed, single-source). EU officials deny she is sidelined.

The Council also adopted a sanctions listing targeting Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad on 16 November (unconfirmed detail). Only a linked reference was seen.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The Council's 16 November listings hit 12 individuals and 101 entities, plus 15 firms tied to shadow-fleet tankers. The EU has now listed over 690 shadow-fleet vessels.

A proposed ban on maritime services for Russian vessels is reportedly unlikely to pass because of Greece, Malta and Cyprus (unconfirmed, via Politico per Azernews). The 22nd package is expected to be finalised by December.

Von der Leyen told the 16 November Enlargement Forum that Montenegro is approaching the finish line and Albania shows impressive progress. Kos says Montenegro could close talks by end-2026 and sign a treaty from June 2027.

The Commission disbursed 1.71 eurosbillion to Ukraine on 16 November under the 90 eurosbillion support loan. About 19.2 eurosbillion remains available; Commission also reopened 21.3 eurosbillion in unspent SAFE loans for Ukraine joint projects.

7. Migration and asylum.

The Return Regulation, which replaces the 2008 Return Directive, has reportedly been adopted by Parliament and Council (unconfirmed final status). Parliament backed the deal 418 to 218 in June; it removes automatic suspensive effect of appeals and creates a European Return Order.

Return hubs in third countries are optional for member states, according to Commissioner Brunner. Critics such as the EESC call them detention camps, which matters for third-country partnership deals.

The Migration and Asylum Pact has applied since 12 June 2026. Implementation gaps are the story to track.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

The Autumn Forecast puts Bulgaria's 2026 growth at 2.7%, with deficit figures differing across reports. Sources conflict (4 to 5.5% of GDP), so check the Commission document.

Šefčovič is to present a "kill-switch" trade tool by year-end after an October understanding with China on cutting EV and plug-in hybrid imports by up to 50%. China threatened retaliation on 5 November.

France and Germany are pushing a rapid-response legal tool against trade coercion. A weighted majority of states could block it.

Paris and Berlin are in "intensive" talks on the next ECB president. France reportedly favours Hernández de Cos; Germany has not settled.

9. Defence and security.

The Commission reopened 21.3 eurosbillion in unspent SAFE loans for joint projects with Ukraine; the call closed 13 November. Allocations by Commissioner Kubilius should follow.

Five people were reportedly arrested in Athens and Skopje over a plot to assassinate someone in Greece on Iran's orders (unconfirmed, single-source). It touches EU-Iran security policy.

Ukraine struck the Crimea bridge, per Euronews's 17 November bulletin, while Russian winter strikes continue. Raises pressure for air-defence funding.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. No CJEU ruling dated 17 November was found. Background: the Court's April 2026 judgment against Hungary's anti-LGBTI law first applied Article 2 TEU, and Article 7 against Hungary remains formally unresolved.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary under Péter Magyar (in office since 9 May) has moved from confrontation to conditional cooperation. About 16.4 eurosbillion in frozen funds was agreed and partly released, but Budapest still rejects accelerated Ukrainian accession.

Germany's budget linkage on Ukraine and the Franco-German ECB talks set the tone for the summit. See the Council section.

France faces domestic strain, with a nationwide strike on 5 November against the 2027 budget and talk of a government fall (unconfirmed, undated). A weak Paris limits EU leadership.
