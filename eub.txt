Here is your EU news briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

Merz ties EU budget to Ukraine funding as Black Sea attacks prompt EUR 50m security pledge.

Compiled from sources dated 8 to 11 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Merz has threatened to block the 2028 to 2034 EU budget unless all 27 member states commit to financing Ukraine beyond 2027. He wrote to Council President Costa and wants Ukraine's gap on the agenda of the informal Dublin summit on 13 November, which makes the MFF a hostage to Ukraine policy.

Slovakia's Fico called Merz's link "classic blackmail" and hinted at a veto, while Hungary, Czechia and Bulgaria have ruled out joining another round of funding. The opposition of four governments makes the EU27 guarantee Merz wants hard to reach.

Von der Leyen pledged EUR 50 million on 10 November to speed up a Black Sea maritime security hub run by Bulgaria and Romania. Drones have hit at least nine merchant ships since early October, leaving 34 dead or missing by AFP's count, and later reports put EU pledges at EUR 70 million.

Parliament and Council negotiators tried on 10 November to settle the 2027 annual budget; the outcome is not yet reported (unconfirmed). The conciliation deadline is midnight on 16 November.

2. European Commission.

The Commission is exploring an extra EUR 45 billion loan for Ukraine in 2027 on top of the EUR 90 billion Ukraine Support Loan. It would cover about two-thirds of an estimated EUR 70 billion gap, with a formal proposal expected before the late-November summit.

Dombrovskis warned that Ukraine's IMF loan hinges on new EU funding and said the money must be backed by the EU27, not a subset. Without agreement on frozen Russian assets, 24 member states stay liable for the existing loan.

The Commission published its 2026 Enlargement Package on 4 November, saying Ukraine meets the conditions to open all remaining clusters. Commissioner Kos expects them to open by year-end.

The Commission said it is considering new import-declaration requirements for goods from Israeli settlements. It is a step short of a trade ban and signals pressure to act on the West Bank.

The Commission was due to reassess Bulgaria's excessive deficit procedure in its autumn forecast in November (unconfirmed). Bulgaria joined the euro in January and was put under the procedure in July.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs meet in a Brussels mini-plenary on 11 to 12 November. The agenda includes a debate on further sanctions against Russia and a vote to replace a Vice-President (unconfirmed, single monitoring source).

The LIBE committee pressed Commissioner Brunner on 4 November over fundamental-rights safeguards in the proposed Frontex reform. Brunner said Frontex work in third countries would need agreements requiring Parliament's consent.

Parliament opened budget talks with the Council, wanting Next Generation EU financing costs and the Ukraine loan kept off the 2027 budget. Paris, Berlin and Rome want lower contributions.

A Commission trade official told Parliament on 5 November that the suspension of trade provisions with Israel remains stuck in Council.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

Heads of government hold an informal meeting in Dublin on 13 November, after the European Political Community summit on 12 November. The EPC theme is "Europe - strong, secure and connected", with about 50 leaders expected.

A 24-hour Irish civil-service strike on 12 November could disrupt the summit (unconfirmed, reported on 8 November).

Finance ministers met on 9 November, aiming for political agreement on tobacco taxation, and the Budget Ecofin followed on 10 November. Outcomes were not found.

The Council approved conclusions on humanitarian action, endorsing a target of at least 25% of EU humanitarian funding going directly to local actors by 2027.

Merz also wants future Ukraine-funding decisions taken by qualified majority rather than unanimity. That would remove the veto that Hungary, Slovakia and others hold.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

The Board of Peace warned of an "immediate humanitarian emergency" in Gaza after severe flooding and blamed both Israel and Hamas. Israeli forces still hold about two-thirds of the Strip.

Trump reportedly urged Netanyahu to advance the stalled Gaza plan; Netanyahu says the army will not withdraw until Hamas is disarmed. The plan has stalled since Israel's late-October election.

EU settler and Hamas sanctions were adopted on 28 May after Hungary's new government lifted its veto; suspension of the Association Agreement's trade pillar remains blocked for lack of a qualified majority.

Kallas said at the G7 foreign ministers' meeting that activating the Blocking Statute to shield the ICC from US sanctions is "a last resort". She said the EU has "a little under six months" to find alternatives and urged firms not to over-comply.

Kallas has described new EU Iran measures targeting those responsible for breaches of freedom of navigation (undated, unconfirmed).

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

Ukraine opened "Internal Market" and "Competitiveness and Inclusive Growth" on 13 October, its fourth cluster opening this year, and aims to close talks by end-2028. Two clusters remain.

The 21st Russia sanctions package (23 July) added 216 listings and 33 banks. Bulgaria blocked listing Patriarch Kirill, and Parliament debates further sanctions this week.

Ukraine's 2027 financing gap is about EUR 70 billion, and the EU also wants frozen Russian assets on the table. Merz reportedly will push their use at the summit.

NATO will deploy maritime patrol aircraft, radars and drones in the Black Sea. Romania alleges "systematic" Russian strikes, Sofia has not blamed Moscow and Moscow denies involvement.

An EU Enlargement Forum takes place in Brussels on 15 to 16 November.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

The Commission proposed a new Frontex Regulation on 27 October making the agency central to returns. It would add a Return Coordination Centre and grow to about 30,000 staff by 2041, per one report.

Frontex would get an explicit mandate to monitor social media. Brunner says it will not target political opinions, but MEPs and rights groups warn of surveillance risks.

The legal status of the separate Return Regulation is unclear in the sources found (unconfirmed). One says it entered into force on 16 October, another that Parliament only backed negotiations in March.

Frontex operations in third countries would require agreements with those states, which need Parliament's consent. This is the main lever for MEPs.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

The Council's draft 2027 budget has EUR 191.88 billion in commitments and EUR 202.07 billion in payments. If the 2028 to 2034 MFF is not agreed in time, 2027 ceilings could roll over into 2028.

An extraordinary summit on the MFF is reported for 26 to 27 November (unconfirmed, single source).

The EU-US deal caps most tariffs at 15%, but steel- and aluminium-content goods still face up to 50%. The EU can suspend concessions if Washington does not align by end-December, and the US midterm result is reshaping talks.

Temu paid a EUR 200 million DSA fine in May, and the Commission has since accepted its action plan; three investigation strands remain open.

9. Defence and security.

The Black Sea hub is the EU's most concrete response to the shipping attacks. Von der Leyen wants clear completion timelines.

Analysts fear Russian escalation to test NATO's response. The attacks make Black Sea security an EU-NATO issue.

No Commissioner Kubilius statement or new EU defence-industry decision was found for the period. Quiet on the defence-industry front.

The Frontex reform ties border security to digital surveillance powers.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

No CJEU ruling dated 10 November on rule-of-law matters was found. Quiet in the last 72 hours.

The Court's April ruling in C-769/22 was the first separate violation of Article 2 TEU in infringement proceedings. It upheld the Commission on all counts against Hungary's anti-LGBTI law.

The Commission referred Hungary to the Court on 1 October over intra-EU investor-state arbitration involving MOL (unconfirmed, single source).

Hungary's post-Orban government appointed a new prosecutor to lead an anti-corruption drive (headline only, 3 November).

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Germany: Merz's Ukraine-budget ultimatum is the main domestic and EU story, alongside violent Gen Z protests in early November.

Slovakia and Czechia: Fico and Babis oppose guaranteeing more Ukraine money.

Hungary: Merz and Prime Minister Magyar clashed over Ukraine and the EU budget (unconfirmed, single source).

France: protests over the fiscal debate, wages and public services have moved to the streets.

Poland: Tusk is in a standoff with the top court (31 October, Bloomberg).
