Here is your EU news briefing for Friday, 20 November.

EU proposes 45 billion euros Ukraine loan as Merz ties budget to Kyiv aid and Trump meets Putin and Xi.

Compiled from sources dated 17 to 20 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

The Commission proposed a 45 billion euros Ukraine loan for 2027 on 18 November, financed by joint EU debt. It splits into 25 billion euros for military needs and 20 billion euros for budget support, on top of the 90 billion euros loan agreed last December, and needs unanimity on the financing side.

Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria have signalled they will stay out of the new scheme, yet von der Leyen says she is "very confident" all 27 will back it. Fico said "Slovakia will not participate... Period." The opt-outs set up a hard fight at the 26 to 27 November summit.

Merz has tied German support for the 2028 to 2034 EU budget to collective funding of Ukraine. Berlin wants a mechanism that stops a single state blocking Ukraine aid, which turns the MFF into leverage over the cohesion-fund recipients.

Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen on 19 November while Europeans gathered in Berlin. Reports say Ukraine was not central to the trilateral's four-point plan and that Trump urged Europe to stop funding Kyiv, which sharpens the sense of Europe being sidelined.

The UK-EU summit takes place in Brussels today, 20 November, with UK PM Andy Burnham. A food-standards (SPS) deal, an ETS link and a youth scheme are on the table; EU states reportedly cleared the package late Thursday (unconfirmed).

2. European Commission.

Health Commissioner Olivér Várhelyi was hit with a grey, ash-like substance by pesticide protesters on 19 November. Von der Leyen strongly condemned the attack, which targeted the Commission's proposed pesticide changes.

Startup groups asked von der Leyen to withdraw and re-table the EU Inc proposal. They argue member-state compromises would hollow out the 28th-regime reform.

The Commission puts the extra energy import bill from Hormuz disruption at 127 billion euros since the US-Israeli war on Iran began. The figure is von der Leyen's and underpins the energy-security argument.

The Commission will present the 22nd Russia sanctions package and further Ukraine funding measures before the November summit. Expected targets include banks, crypto platforms and third-country circumvention.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs' next Strasbourg session runs 23 to 26 November. EP Votes lists 28 votes and 15 debates, including the ETS market stability reserve and a debate on rule of law in Hungary after its 2026 elections.

The MFF co-rapporteurs threatened on 18 November to reject the 2028 to 2034 budget unless Parliament's priorities are reflected. They want a larger budget and new digital-tax revenue, a warning shot before Costa's figures.

Parliament and Council negotiators agreed a provisional deal on 19 November to delay methane rules for fossil-fuel imports by two years. It reflects energy-price pressure from the Gulf crisis; a review is due by January 2028.

The 11 to 12 November Brussels plenary voted on the civil protection mechanism budget and a resolution on Ukrainian children deported to Russia.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

A special European Council meets 26 to 27 November to weigh the new sanctions package and Ukraine financing. Unanimity is the obstacle for both.

Costa is due to table a negotiating box with figures for the 2028 to 2034 budget on Saturday 21 November. Officials want a deal by year-end so legislation can pass in 2027; the Irish Presidency's box sits at 1.02% of GNI.

The 16 November Council added sanctions on 12 individuals and 101 entities over attacks on Ukraine and approved nearly 8 billion euros for Ukraine. The release followed Kyiv meeting more than 20 reform targets.

The General Affairs Council on 19 November was due to take stock of omnibus simplification and the Commission's 2027 work programme. I found no outcome report (unconfirmed).

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

Kallas urged Israel on 14 November to reopen all Gaza crossings and allow "immediate, unimpeded" aid access after flooding. She called the strike on Nasser Hospital "extremely alarming"; Netanyahu said Israel supports winter preparations but will not let Hamas exploit supplies.

The Foreign Affairs Council on 16 November listed one more person and one entity for financing Hamas and Palestinian Islamic Jihad. Kallas added that the EU is ready to engage with Israel's next government.

Broader measures against Israel remain blocked. Settler sanctions passed only in May after Hungary dropped its veto, while trade measures on settlement goods and suspension of the Association Agreement's trade pillar lacked the needed majority or unanimity.

Energy chief Jørgensen warns the Gulf gas shock will outlast the Iran war. Even a reopened Hormuz would need years to restore gas output, and the EU keeps its ban on Russian gas.

Analysts say Brussels is a commentator on the Iran war. Carnegie Europe describes a "paralyzed" role, while ECFR urges a stabilisation coalition of the E4 and regional powers (undated pieces).

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The Berlin Coalition of the Willing met on 19 November with Zelensky, about 30 to 40 partners and US envoys Witkoff and Kushner. Leaders called for legally binding security guarantees after a ceasefire, backed by a European-led Multinational Force Ukraine, and said borders cannot change by force.

Germany, France, the UK and Ukraine urged Russia to accept a winter energy ceasefire and a Black Sea food-export truce. They also condemned Russian hybrid attacks in Europe.

Montenegro leads enlargement with 23 of 33 chapters provisionally closed, according to one report. Sources differ on the count, and Kos has floated membership in 2028.

The Commission says Ukraine and Moldova qualify to open all remaining clusters, but unanimity has blocked it. Kos expects Ukraine's remaining clusters to open by year-end; targets are Moldova early 2028, Ukraine end-2028.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

Irregular border crossings fell 29% in the first ten months of 2026 to almost 110,000. Central Mediterranean arrivals dropped 55% to about 26,600, and Frontex credits cooperation with departure states.

Libya is the departure hub for roughly three quarters of Central Mediterranean arrivals and for over 16,000 arrivals in Crete. Greece's migration minister says Libya may be using migration as political leverage.

The Return Regulation (2026/2280), with legal basis for third-country return hubs, was published in October. Statewatch says it expands detention and surveillance; most provisions apply 12 months after publication (sources differ on which apply at once).

An EU-funded Liminal study (3 November) says Frontex air surveillance led to over 33,000 returns to Libya and Tunisia in 2018 to 2024. It sharpens the pushback debate around partnership deals.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

EU governments approved signing a trade deal with Indonesia on 18 to 19 November, with provisional application allowed. It is framed as a hedge against Trump's tariffs.

The EU-Mercosur trade pillar has applied provisionally since 1 May 2026. The wider partnership agreement still needs Parliament and national parliaments.

The Commission will present three new trade instruments and a modernisation of existing tools on 9 December.

US tariffs on EU goods are capped at 15%, per the Commission.

The Hormuz closure has cost Europe an extra 142 billion dollars for the same energy, according to Fortune (3 November).

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius said on 18 November that the EU has opened a second SAFE call covering 21.3 billion euros of unspent funds. He pressed industry to scale up for 2027, including for Ukraine.

A non-public EDA report reportedly finds the EU is not rearming fast enough for its 2030 readiness goal (unconfirmed). A defence-market package, including a revised procurement directive, is due by end-November or early December.

The Council approved Hungary's revised 5.4 billion euros SAFE loan request. Signing is the next step, a sign of Budapest's thaw under Magyar.

Germany's defence budget is set to reach about 153 billion euros by 2029.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. I found no CJEU or rule-of-law ruling dated 18 to 20 November.

Background: the Court's 21 April ruling (C-769/22) found Hungary's anti-LGBTI law breached Article 2 TEU, the first such application.

A case on Hungary's sovereignty-protection law is pending after AG Kokott proposed finding an infringement in February.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary under PM Péter Magyar has regained access to 4.2 billion euros in frozen funds, approved by the Council in October. Programmes could resume in spring 2027, and the thaw changes Council dynamics on Israel and Ukraine.

Fico's refusal on the new Ukraine loan makes Slovakia the loudest holdout. Merz's budget threat targets the cohesion-fund dependence of this bloc.

Andy Burnham is UK PM and co-hosted the Berlin coalition meeting with Merz and Macron.

Both the Conservatives and Reform UK say they would scrap the UK-EU reset deal if elected.
