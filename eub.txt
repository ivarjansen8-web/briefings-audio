Here is your EU news briefing for Saturday, 28 November.

EU summit ends without MFF or Ukraine loan deal; Hernández de Cos picked for ECB; EU rejects new US tariffs.

Compiled from sources dated 25 to 28 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

EU leaders ended the 26 to 27 November special summit without a deal on the 2028 to 2034 budget (MFF), pushing talks to December. Hungary, Slovakia and the Czech Republic object, Parliament rejects the size of Costa's negotiating box, and Costa is expected to table a revised proposal ahead of the next leaders' meeting.

Leaders failed to agree on a roughly 45 eurosbillion (about 49 dollarsbillion) loan for Ukraine's 2027 needs after more than 12 hours of talks. Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia oppose using EU funds for military aid to Ukraine, and Merz has tied Ukraine funding to the budget.

Leaders picked Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde as ECB president. He is expected to take over when Lagarde leaves early on 7 February 2027 after the Netherlands dropped its own candidate, Klaas Knot.

Macron and Merz both want a budget deal by year-end. Macron says he cannot politically reopen talks after 31 December because the French presidential election is approaching (April).

The US trade representative proposed new tariffs on 14 economies including the EU, after a Section 301 probe. Šefčovič says Washington must respect the 15% ceiling agreed in the existing deal.

2. European Commission.

The Commission unconditionally approved Deutsche Börse's acquisition of Allfunds on 27 November. It is the only Commission press release dated that day that I could find.

Von der Leyen said the Ukraine loan proposal has "wide support." The sum is reported as 45 eurosbillion in euros and about 49 dollarsbillion in dollars, and summit talks showed it is far from unanimous.

The Commission proposed a 22nd Russia sanctions package on 23 November. It is the main fresh legislative item before the summit (details in section 6).

The Commission will examine a citizens' initiative to suspend the EU-Israel Association Agreement, which gathered more than 1.1 million signatures. Acceptance does not oblige it to act, but a Parliament hearing will follow.

Defence Commissioner Kubilius warned that proposed MFF cuts of about 30% (roughly 40 eurosbillion) could stall defence and space projects. The report is undated within the search, so treat the figure as (unconfirmed).

3. European Parliament.

MEPs approved their mandate on the Horizon Europe successor programme (FP10) by 458 votes to 190 on 25 November. The European Competitiveness Fund mandate passed by 386 to 252, which opens tough trilogues with the Council.

Parliament adopted the 199.7 eurosbillion 2027 annual budget, reportedly by 406 votes to 184. It is the last annual budget under the 2021 to 2027 framework, and the Council and Parliament had agreed it on 11 November.

MEPs debated Hungary's Article 7 procedure on 24 November and remain split on closing it. The EPP wants it closed, while Greens and S&D members say Hungary still does not comply with an EU asylum ruling. A resolution is due at the December plenary.

Parliament debated Gaza on 24 November without a vote on measures against Israel. MEPs called for suspending the Association Agreement and Israel's Horizon participation, but the obstacle is the Council.

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

The special European Council ended on 27 November with no MFF agreement. Leaders will return to it at their 17 to 18 December meeting.

Fico accused Merz of blackmail and said Slovakia will not back the 45 eurosbillion Ukraine loan. Babiš insisted Ukraine financing be negotiated separately from cohesion money.

Leaders agreed three senior ECB appointments, with Laurence Boone and Markus Brunnermeier put forward for Executive Board seats. This alongside the Hernández de Cos choice settles the post-Lagarde team at the political level, and formal steps are pending.

Digital ministers meet on 1 December, where the Irish Presidency hopes for a general approach on the mobile satellite services file. Some member states still hold reservations.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

Kallas announced more than 90 eurosmillion in extra EU humanitarian aid for Gaza on 26 November. This brings 2026 EU humanitarian funding for Gaza to over 217 eurosmillion, and she also named disarmament and border security as priorities in Lebanon.

The EU called on Israel on 14 November to allow unimpeded aid into flood-hit Gaza and reopen all crossings. The statement backs UN Security Council Resolution 2803, a two-state solution and no governing role for Hamas.

The Commission's July proposal to restrict imports from Israeli settlements is still awaiting approval from EU countries. Suspending the Association Agreement would need the Twenty-Seven to agree, which is the structural blocker.

Brussels has condemned Israel's invasion of Lebanon. Background on a US-Iran memorandum with a Lebanon ceasefire comes from a weak source, so treat it as (unconfirmed).

One commentary claims the Commission is taking a larger role in Middle East diplomacy while Kallas lacks a direct line to Israel (unconfirmed). It is an opinion piece and one to watch for the EU's institutional split on the region.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The 22nd sanctions package would cut 60 Russian banks from SWIFT and add more than 70 shadow-fleet vessels. It also widens the energy-services ban to refineries and electricity, targets crypto and payment networks, bars Russian flag teams from EU sporting events, and revives an entry ban on Russian soldiers.

The package is still a proposal, and I found no report of member-state approval. Euronews notes Brussels did not sense enough Irish support for some elements.

Donald Tusk urged Fico to show solidarity after Russian cruise missiles hit Polish territory. It raises the security stakes of the Ukraine-financing split.

The Commission says Ukraine has met conditions to open all remaining accession clusters, and Kallas wants that done before year-end. Montenegro could close talks by end-2026, with a treaty signing in June 2027 at the earliest.

A Bruegel footnote citing Euronews says Hungary will drop its veto on Ukraine's remaining clusters (unconfirmed). I could not open the underlying article.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background only.

The Pact on Migration and Asylum has applied since 12 June 2026. A provisional deal on the Return Regulation, which allows return hubs in third countries, was reached in June, and the formal adoption steps are still pending.

Interior ministers approved faster-return changes on 1 October, including a standard European Return Order form. Brunner says return hubs would be voluntary for member states and subject to EU safeguards.

Germany, Austria, Denmark, Greece and the Netherlands are negotiating hub facilities, while Albania has again rejected hosting one. No sites have been disclosed.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

The USTR's 25 November findings propose tariffs on 14 economies, and the EU has been reported as facing up to 20% (unconfirmed, sources differ). A senior US official told AFP they would likely start in January at the earliest.

Šefčovič said the EU strongly rejects the move and will speak directly to US trade officials on Monday. Trade ministers discuss EU-US relations on 30 November, and the EU can suspend concessions if Washington does not align rates by end-December.

The proposed seven-year budget is about 1.58 eurostrillion and needs unanimity. Reports give conflicting totals (1.58 eurostrillion versus 1.78 eurostrillion), and a proposed tech-firm levy is opposed by the Netherlands, Sweden, Estonia and Luxembourg.

The EU-Indonesia trade agreement was signed on 21 November, but Parliament says it cannot vote before 31 December. Provisional application is likely in early 2027.

The ETS review proposed in July is headed for trilogues after Parliament's September mandate. It would keep some free allocation for CBAM sectors and phase it out only by 2038.

9. Defence and security.

A second SAFE call worth about 21 eurosbillion in unclaimed loans is due, which could fund joint projects with Ukraine. Kubilius named the Freyja ballistic defence initiative.

Kubilius says a Court of Justice annulment of the SAFE Regulation would not automatically suspend its implementation. It matters because the legal basis of the flagship defence-loan tool is being challenged.

Kubilius backed von der Leyen's proposed European Security Council as linked to a European Defence Union.

Poland is the largest SAFE borrower at about 43 eurosbillion, and Russian missile strikes on Polish territory this week sharpen the urgency.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

The Court of Justice ruled on 26 November that Amazon Italia's outsourced delivery activities can count as postal services needing authorisation. The Court held that a company need not run the whole delivery chain to be a postal provider.

The Court held that uncapped variable mortgage rates are not inherently unfair (C-607/25).

The Court dismissed the EDPS appeal over the Europol Regulation amendment, ruling the EDPS cannot challenge EU laws this way. The date is from 17 November, so it falls outside the 72-hour window.

Hungary's Article 7 case remains open although 4.2 eurosbillion in cohesion funds was unlocked in September. A further 2.2 eurosbillion stays frozen over academic freedom.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Macron says he has until the end of the year to strike a budget deal, with his red lines on France's contribution and farm spending. Merz answered his "bridge builders" claim with open irony.

Merz is under domestic pressure after state election losses he called a "disaster" (unconfirmed, dates not verified).

Fico and Babiš are the loudest opponents of the Ukraine loan and the budget package. A weakened Merz and a deadline-bound Macron leave the frugal-cohesion split unresolved.

Italy's Meloni has signalled opposition to Costa's budget proposal.
