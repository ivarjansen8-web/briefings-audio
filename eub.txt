Here is your EU news briefing for Saturday, 14 November.

Dublin summit picks five-nation Ukraine talks team; 22nd sanctions package and Israel-pact petition loom.

Compiled from sources dated 11 to 14 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

EU leaders at the informal Dublin summit on 13 November chose a group, not a single envoy, to speak for Europe in Ukraine peace talks: the EU institutions plus France, Germany, the UK, Italy and Poland. Costa promised "one clear united voice", but the choice leaves Kaja Kallas's own seat unresolved, and the arrangement keeps Berlin and Paris at the table.

Von der Leyen said the Commission will soon present a 22nd Russia sanctions package and a proposal to cover Ukraine's 2027 funding gap before the 26 to 27 November European Council. Merz puts the 2027 shortfall at 70 billion euros, of which about 45 billion euros would fall to the EU.

The Commission accepted for examination a citizens' initiative with 1,117,104 validated signatures calling for full suspension of the EU-Israel Association Agreement. It has until 12 May 2027 to respond; acceptance does not itself change the agreement.

Paris and Berlin are in "intensive" talks over Lagarde's successor at the ECB, with Pablo Hernández de Cos the name in play. Germany denied a Le Point report that a deal was already agreed.

2. European Commission.

Von der Leyen said Europe needs at least 500 billion euros in computing investment and promised an "ambitious proposal" on tech-sovereignty funding. AI was the main theme of the Dublin leaders' meeting.

The Commission is due to approve nearly all of Hungary's roughly 10 billion euros recovery funds later this month, with payments needing authorisation by end-November (unconfirmed). This follows the October end of rule-of-law conditionality that freed 4.2 billion euros.

The Commission must report by 1 December on US tariffs on EU steel and aluminium derivatives. It can withdraw tariff preferences granted under the Turnberry deal if US duties above 15% persist after 31 December.

A defence market package, including a revised procurement directive, is due by end-November or early December. Kubilius has warned the EU is not rearming fast enough for 2030.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs approved rules allowing proxy voting for pregnant MEPs and new mothers on 12 November, by 578 votes to 25 with 4 abstentions. The Brussels mini-plenary ran 11 to 12 November.

Parliament was scheduled to vote on the budget and structure of the 2028 to 2034 civil protection mechanism; I found no result (unconfirmed).

A report says Parliament rejected one text in a retail investment package deal reached with the Council (unconfirmed, single headline). The details were not checked.

Parliament and Council agreed the 2027 annual EU budget on 11 November, with the plenary vote scheduled for 24 November.

4. Council and European Council.

EU leaders met informally in Dublin on 13 November, after the European Political Community summit on 12 November, with AI and Ukraine support on the agenda. The session featured Mistral AI's CEO and Geoffrey Hinton.

Merz made German backing for the 2028 to 2034 MFF conditional on all 27 sharing Ukraine's 2027 and later financing. Slovakia's PM called the letter "blackmail" and Czech PM Babiš rejected the precondition.

Costa is expected to unveil a new MFF proposal with figures on 21 November, ahead of the 26 to 27 November summit. Member-state positions remain far apart, putting an end-2026 deal at risk.

Four governments are reported to resist the 2028 to 2034 budget as net contributors seek "substantial cuts".

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

The Commission will examine the Israel Association Agreement suspension initiative, meeting organisers in coming weeks before a Parliament hearing. Any suspension would require a Council proposal and decision, where unanimity and majority hurdles remain.

Foreign ministers discuss an EEAS paper on deeper ties with Damascus on 16 November, including reviving a 2008 initialled association agreement. The paper also seeks to limit Russian influence in Syria.

The Syria Partnership Coordination Forum meets in Damascus on 17 November, co-chaired by Commissioner Šuica and Foreign Minister al-Shibani, with the EIB returning after 15 years. No financial pledges are planned; it prepares a possible 2027 investment conference. Reported EU funding figures conflict (280 million euros versus 620 million euros).

Saudi officials are reportedly pressing the EU to designate the Houthis as a terrorist organisation (unconfirmed).

Kallas earlier said the Gaza peace process has stalled and called the ceasefire one "in name only"; I found no new EEAS Gaza statement dated 12 to 14 November.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The 22nd sanctions package is reported to target banks, crypto platforms and third-country circumvention, and may include an athlete ban (unconfirmed); one report says approval could come 16 November, which conflicts with a Commission presentation by 26 November.

Zelensky was in Dublin but skipped the leaders' dinner to meet US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff. Kushner reportedly cited significant progress in negotiations.

Dombrovskis floated a new loan secured on frozen Russian assets; the existing 90 billion euros Ukraine Support Loan was agreed last December after Belgium blocked a reserves-backed loan. Hungary, Czechia and Slovakia are outside it.

The 4 November enlargement package set indicative roadmaps: Montenegro could sign a treaty as early as June 2027, Albania ends talks by end-2027, Moldova by early 2028. Kos calls them "living documents", not guarantees.

7. Migration and asylum.

Regulation (EU) 2026/2280 on returns, published 15 October, replaces the 2008 Return Directive and gives a legal basis for third-country "return hubs". Parts apply from 16 October, others after 12 months (sources differ).

ECRE says the regulation allows detention up to 24 months, extendable by six. Critics call it a "Deportation Regulation"; Brunner frames it as regaining control over who enters and who must leave.

Mutual recognition of return decisions between member states is a core element. I found no new migration deal dated 12 to 14 November.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

Parliament and Council agreed the 2027 annual budget on 11 November; the plenary vote is scheduled for 24 November.

Šefčovič says respecting the Turnberry deal is "of paramount importance" while pressing Washington on steel. The deal caps US tariffs on most EU goods at 15%, but some steel and aluminium derivatives face up to 50%.

Trump reportedly lost Congress in the US midterms, and Europe is pressing for tariff cuts and Ukraine aid.

Macron and Merz are said to be weighing a package that would give Berlin the ECB chief economist post and Paris Schnabel's seat (unconfirmed, Handelsblatt via Yahoo). Merz wants a few more days to consult his coalition.

9. Defence and security.

The 28.3 billion euros weapons loan for 2026 is on track, with 12 billion euros disbursed.

The Drone Defence Initiative and Eastern Flank Watch are due initial capacity by end-2026; one analysis says only a third of counter-drone programmes may deliver by 2030.

Kubilius cites intelligence warnings that Russia could be ready to attack EU states before 2030.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

The Court of Justice delivered judgments on 12 November, including Poland v Parliament (C-442/23), an annulment action against EU climate rules amending Regulation 2018/841; I could not confirm the outcome (unconfirmed).

Roughly 3.5 billion euros of Hungary's cohesion funds remain under separate conditions and 500 million euros is frozen over the child protection law. The Article 7 procedure is still open.

Under Magyar, von der Leyen said Hungary "strengthened rule of law", releasing 4.2 billion euros.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Merz and Macron are negotiating the ECB succession while Merz defends the five-state Ukraine talks format. He said "we always include Italy and Poland".

Israeli coalition-building is under way, with Haredi parties fielding overtures, per a 12 November liveblog. It shapes the backdrop to EU-Israel debates.

Slovakia and Czechia are resisting the Ukraine burden-sharing link to the MFF.

Hungary's Magyar government is trading reforms for EU money.
