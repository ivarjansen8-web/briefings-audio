Here is your EU news briefing for Monday, 23 November.

EU leaders face budget showdown as Costa tables revised MFF; Denmark pushes Russian visa curbs after plot; EUR 45bn Ukraine loan proposed.

Compiled from sources dated 19 to 23 November 2026 (older context flagged). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Costa tabled a revised 2028 to 2034 budget "negobox" on 21 November, and leaders meet in a special European Council on 26 to 27 November to try to close it. The box trims the Commission's proposal by roughly 10% and external action by about 21% (figures vary by outlet), so it is a fight over money for foreign policy.

Germany, the frugal states, France and the European Parliament all criticised Costa's compromise. Dutch finance minister Eelco Heinen said the budget must come down or the Dutch rebate must go up, so a year-end deal looks hard.

Von der Leyen condemned an alleged Russian plot to kill a U.S.-Ukrainian citizen in Denmark and promised a new European Security Strategy soon. Copenhagen wants EU-wide limits on Russian visas, which would turn a hybrid-threat case into a sanctions fight.

The EU and UK held their second summit in Brussels on 20 November and pledged to conclude open negotiations within six months. The joint statement builds on the 2025 Common Understanding and the Security and Defence Partnership.

The Commission is proposing a EUR 45 billion Ukraine loan for 2027, repayable only if Russia pays reparations. It covers about two-thirds of Kyiv's expected EUR 70 billion gap and still needs member-state approval.

2. European Commission.

The Commission published its November infringement package on 19 November, covering 26 member states and referring Poland, Austria and Greece to the Court of Justice. Referrals are the step before possible fines.

Commissioner Kubilius said on 18 November that Russia's military budget next year will roughly equal that of all EU states combined. He urged industry to outspend and outproduce Moscow.

The Commission presented a consumer package on 11 November led by the Digital Fairness Act. It targets dark patterns, addictive design and subscription traps, and gives Brussels direct enforcement powers (older than 72 hours, context).

Von der Leyen spoke at the first Eastern Border Regions high-level conference. She stressed support for frontline regions, tying cohesion money to the security debate.

3. European Parliament.

The Strasbourg plenary runs 23 to 26 November, with the 2028 to 2034 MFF debate the headline item. Votes are due on the 2027 annual budget, pharma-law reform and organic rules.

MEPs will hold a Gaza debate this week, which Eunews describes as long overdue. It sets up Parliament as a harder voice than the Council on the Middle East.

A Hungary rule-of-law debate is scheduled for Tuesday morning (24 November). It tests whether Parliament accepts the Commission's view that Budapest has repaired its record.

Monday's sitting is debates only, including the Audiovisual Media Services Directive revision and protecting EU financial interests in the post-2027 budget. Votes start Tuesday.

4. Council and European Council.

The special European Council on 26 to 27 November centres on the MFF and Ukraine's 2027 funding. Costa's stated aim is a deal by year-end.

Costa insists new EU-wide taxes must be part of any deal, raising about EUR 55 billion a year. The list leaves out digital platforms, crypto and online gambling, which Parliament prefers.

Macron and Merz both back a deal before end-2026, but a German source said no figures are agreed (Politico via Newsquawk, unconfirmed). Merz is reportedly open to bloc-wide taxes but rules out joint borrowing.

Ministers meet today in Agriculture and Fisheries and General Affairs (Cohesion) formats. No headline decisions were found.

On 20 November the Council agreed its negotiating position on a stronger civil protection mechanism under the next MFF. It is one of the sectoral files that must follow the overall budget deal.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

The Commission is examining a European Citizens' Initiative to suspend the EU-Israel Association Agreement. Reports cite 1.1 million verified signatures from 12 states (Middle East Monitor) or over 1.3 million from 13 (Palinfo, citing Reuters), and the Commission has six months to respond.

Foreign ministers agreed on 16 November to move toward signing the 2008 EU-Syria Association Agreement while modernising it in parallel. Kallas said the next steps are legal and technical preparation (older than 72 hours, context).

The same Council also discussed Iran and the Gulf, with no decisions reported. France was reported to want further measures against Iran over a cyberattack (unconfirmed).

G7 foreign ministers urged all parties to respect the Lebanon ceasefire and implement UNSC Resolution 1701 (9 to 10 November). Context for EU positioning on Lebanon.

Background: the EU sanctioned violent Israeli settlers in May after Hungary's new government lifted its block. A Commission proposal to restrict settlement imports is still awaiting member-state approval (older, context).

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

Denmark circulated a note on 20 November proposing a suspension of tourist visas for Russians and an end to recognising non-biometric Russian passports in Schengen. It is due to be presented to EU ambassadors today, 23 November. Russia denies the plot.

The EUR 45 billion Ukraine loan would sit on top of the EUR 90 billion agreed for 2026 to 27. It is meant to avoid a funding cliff in 2027.

The 4 November enlargement package says Ukraine has opened four clusters and meets the conditions for the rest, which Commissioner Kos expects by year-end. Montenegro could sign an accession treaty in June 2027 (older, context).

Peace talks look stalled, with US mediators reportedly preoccupied by the Iran conflict (unconfirmed). Zelensky wants talks restarted.

7. Migration and asylum.

The EU signed its first readmission agreement with Nigeria on 10 November. It sets fast procedures to identify and return people with no right to stay (older than 72 hours, context).

The Migration Pact applies since 12 June, and the return regulation was provisionally agreed on 1 June and approved by Parliament on 17 June. It legally permits return hubs in third countries (older, context).

Five states (Germany, Netherlands, Austria, Denmark, Greece) are talking to African countries about hosting return hubs, aiming for hubs in 2027. No new hub deal was found in the last 72 hours.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours on new asylum decisions.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

The MFF tops the economic agenda, with Horizon Europe reportedly kept at EUR 158 billion in the new box. Research money is shielded as external action is cut. CASRAI (unconfirmed, single source)

The EU-US deal caps tariffs at 15% on most goods, but steel- and aluminium-containing products still face up to 50%. The EU may suspend its concessions if Washington does not fix this by end-December.

Dutch minister Heinen demanded either a smaller EU budget or a larger Dutch rebate. It signals the frugal bloc will block any increase.

Parliament votes on the 2027 annual EU budget this week.

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius said the EU is not rearming fast enough for its 2030 readiness goal, citing a European Defence Agency assessment. SAFE loans have not yet had the hoped-for effect.

A second SAFE call worth EUR 21.3 billion is open.

A revised defence procurement directive is due in late November or early December, and the Defence Readiness Omnibus should be adopted by year-end.

Von der Leyen's promised European Security Strategy follows the Denmark plot.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

The Commission referred Poland, Austria and Greece to the Court of Justice in its 19 November package.

The Commission proposed in September to release EUR 4.2 billion to Hungary, saying rule of law had strengthened, and about EUR 500 million stays frozen over the child-protection law. Parliament debates Hungary on Tuesday (older, context).

The Court's Second Chamber ruled on 12 November in Poland v Parliament and Council (C-442/23, emissions allowances). It is not a rule-of-law case (older, context).

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Macron and Merz are both weakened at home, which limits their room on the budget. Macron's mandate is ending, and Merz is under pressure after poor results for his coalition. Streamlinefeed (unconfirmed)

Denmark is leading the push on Russian visas after the foiled plot. Copenhagen will expel a Russian diplomat and halt non-essential visas nationally.

Hungary under Prime Minister Magyar has had EUR 16.4 billion unlocked since May and a EUR 4.2 billion tranche proposed. Critics at the EPC warn that safeguards are thin.

UK Prime Minister Burnham is now an active EU counterpart. The six-month negotiating clock gives a deadline of May 2027.
