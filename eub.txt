Here is your EU news briefing for Sunday, 22 November.

Budget summit starts deadlocked as Merz and Macron reject Costa draft; Denmark expels Russian attaché over plot.

Compiled from sources dated 12 to 22 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Costa's revised 2028 to 2034 budget draft was rejected by Berlin and Paris within a day of its release on 21 November. The text cuts 204 eurosbillion from the Commission's plan to 1.781 eurostrillion (including inflation) and adds a 3% digital levy; Merz called the volume still too high and an Élysée source said it is not "a basis for an agreement", so the 26 to 27 November special summit starts in deadlock.

Denmark expelled Russia's defence attaché on 20 November over an alleged Russian plot to kill a US-Ukrainian rapper near Copenhagen. Von der Leyen called it "an attack on EU soil" and promised a new European Security Strategy, which puts hybrid threats back on the leaders' table.

The EU and Indonesia signed their trade (CEPA) and investment protection agreements in Brussels on 21 November after nearly ten years of talks. Duties fall on about 98.5% of EU tariff lines on the Indonesian side, with provisional application targeted for early 2027 and Parliament consent still needed.

Von der Leyen proposed a 49.4 dollarsbillion loan for Ukraine on 18 November (unconfirmed). This is single-source and the exact instrument is unclear; it feeds the 70 eurosbillion 2027 funding gap leaders must address next week.

2. European Commission.

The Commission partly cleared Hungary's 10 eurosbillion recovery-fund request on 20 November, finding 126 of 127 milestones met. A further 2.2 eurosbillion stays blocked over academic freedom and LGBTI+ rights, and member states must still approve payment, so this is a test of the Magyar government's reform roadmap.

Von der Leyen said the Commission will soon propose a new European Security Strategy. It follows her September counter-hybrid playbook, though the unanimity veto would still govern any joint response.

The Commission will present a 22nd Russia sanctions package and Ukraine financing options before the 26 to 27 November summit. Diplomats expect measures on banks, crypto platforms and third-country circumvention.

The Commission said it will examine the EU-Israel Association Agreement after a petition of more than 1.1 million signatures. Analysts expect little substantive change to the status quo.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs meet in Strasbourg from 23 to 26 November with 28 votes and 15 debates scheduled. The 2027 annual budget and the multiannual budget debate dominate the week.

Parliament's lead budget negotiator Siegfried Muresan called Costa's MFF draft "simply unacceptable". He says the Council is fixated on cuts, which signals Parliament will withhold consent for a bare-bones deal.

A Gaza debate is scheduled for Tuesday 24 November, the first in several sessions. It will cover flooding and restrictions on aid deliveries.

Plenary will also take stock of COP31 in Antalya and vote on the pharmaceutical reform and organic production rules. The pharma vote date is not firmly confirmed in the sources (unconfirmed).

4. Council and European Council (summits, ministers, vetoes)

A special European Council on 26 to 27 November has two items: the 2028 to 2034 MFF and Ukraine's 2027 financing. Both need unanimity, and Costa has asked leaders to find roughly 70 eurosbillion for Ukraine.

Costa's draft proposes a 3% levy on digital turnover for firms above 50 eurosmillion EU turnover and 750 eurosmillion global turnover. Ireland's Taoiseach rejected it and capitals fear US retaliation; Sweden says the budget is still too big.

The Council approved signature of the EU-Indonesia agreements on 18 November.

Merz has tied the budget deal to a Ukraine loan solution backed by all members, while Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria resist equal burden-sharing. France and Spain doubt a deal is needed this year.

The EU-UK summit on 20 November produced deals on food-trade red tape, linked emissions trading and a youth experience scheme. The UK was represented by Andy Burnham; SAFE participation and tuition fees remain unresolved.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East (EEAS, sanctions, statements)

On 14 November Kallas urged Israel to reopen all Gaza crossings and allow "immediate, unimpeded" aid access after flooding. The statement stressed Hamas must have no governing role and reaffirmed the two-state solution.

The Council listed the outlet Gaza Now and its founder under Hamas/PIJ sanctions on 16 November.

The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council on 17 November that Israel and Hamas are missing ceasefire commitments, delaying the Palestinian administration and international force. About 17 dollarsbillion in recovery pledges is reported; on 20 November the Commission urged Israel to honour the ceasefire after renewed operations near the Yellow Line.

Foreign ministers backed moving ahead with an EU-Syria Association Agreement on 16 November. Syria and the EU discussed lifting remaining sanctions on 21 November.

New Iran sanctions are being prepared, possibly for the 14 December Foreign Affairs Council. France blames Iran's Revolutionary Guards for cyberattacks on French retailers; Iran denies it.

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The Council approved nearly 8 eurosbillion for Ukraine under the Ukraine Facility on 16 November, and listed 12 individuals and 101 entities over attacks on Ukraine.

EU leaders agreed a Ukraine negotiating team of EU institutions plus France, Germany, Italy, Poland and the UK, which leaves Kallas out. She is pressing for a role.

Ukraine's FM Sybiha briefed ministers on the Abu Dhabi talks and winter needs.

Ukraine and Moldova opened new accession clusters in October; Moldova could finish talks in early 2028, Ukraine roughly a year later. Kos has hinted at decoupling them; Montenegro aims to sign its treaty as early as June 2027.

Carnegie argues Europe is struggling to sustain support for Ukraine.

7. Migration and asylum (Pact, return deals, third-country partnerships)

Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands announced on 16 November they are exploring a first return hub in Rwanda. Transfers are targeted for 2027, with Denmark ready by end-2027 and UNHCR advising.

Regulation (EU) 2026/2280 on returns, adopted in October, provides the legal basis for return hubs and mutual recognition of return decisions.

Statewatch says the rules allow detention up to 24 months, extendable by six. Rights groups call this disproportionate.

Brunner frames the rules as regaining control over who enters and who must leave.

8. Economy, trade and budget (MFF, tariffs, competitiveness)

The 2027 annual budget, the last of the current MFF, was reported agreed on 11 November. Parliament votes on it this week.

Costa's MFF cuts about 10% from the Commission's proposal and adds an e-waste levy, tobacco taxes and possible crypto and spectrum revenue. The Council estimates around 60 eurosbillion a year in own resources.

Berlin objects to the over-40% nominal growth in spending, while Paris objects to farm-support levels.

The EU-Indonesia CEPA removes duties on 98.5% of EU tariff lines on Indonesia's side and 90.4% of Indonesian lines into the EU.

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius warned on 18 November that Russia will spend as much on defence next year as all EU states combined. He cited a non-public EDA report saying the EU is not rearming fast enough for 2030.

A SAFE 2.0 call covers 21.3 eurosbillion in unspent funds. A procurement directive revision is due late November or early December.

Denmark is weighing suspending Schengen visas for Russians after the plot.

Costa called the alleged assassination attempt "unacceptable".

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

EU affairs ministers held the annual rule-of-law dialogue on 19 November. The Article 7 procedure against Hungary remains formally open but deadlocked.

Hungary's partial RRF clearance is the clearest rule-of-law consequence this week. A minimal penalty is expected for the missing milestone.

Court of Justice: Quiet. No significant ruling found in the last 72 hours.

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Merz and Macron both rejected the budget draft. Their positions will decide whether the summit ends in a deal.

Hungary's Magyar government is delivering reforms to unlock about 16.4 eurosbillion in withheld funds.

The UK's Burnham government led the EU-UK reset summit.

Denmark leads the response to the Russian plot.

What to watch.

23 to 26 November: Strasbourg plenary, including the 2027 budget vote and Gaza debate on 24 November.

26 to 27 November: Special European Council on the MFF and Ukraine financing.

Before the summit: Commission's 22nd sanctions package and Ukraine funding proposals.

Late November/early December: Defence procurement directive revision; European Security Strategy proposal.

By 31 December: Possible member-state sign-off and payment of Hungary's recovery funds.

14 December: Foreign Affairs Council, possible new Iran sanctions.
