Here is your EU news briefing for Monday, 9 November.

Kallas: no Ukraine deal without EU as Dublin summits and MFF showdown loom.

Compiled from sources dated 3 to 9 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

Kaja Kallas said on 6 November that the EU will not implement any Ukraine peace deal it is not party to. Speaking in Warsaw with Sikorski and Sybiha, she cited EU control over sanctions, frozen Russian assets, reconstruction and accession, and said the EU is "not the mediator" but on Ukraine's side; it sets up a fight over who speaks for Europe.

US, Russia and Ukraine have announced talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 to 11 November (unconfirmed, single report). Brussels is not at the table, which is why Meloni wants a single EU representative named.

Democrats won both chambers of Congress in the US midterms on 3 November. Tariff terms are expected to change little because the administration moved them under Section 301, but Kyiv hopes for more US aid from January.

EU leaders meet in Dublin on 12 to 13 November: the European Political Community summit with about 50 leaders, then an informal European Council on AI. Agence Europe says Ukraine talks representation will also come up.

Costa is preparing a budget compromise with figures ahead of a 26 November summit on the 2028 to 2034 MFF. He warns that failure to agree this year could push a deal to October 2027.

2. European Commission.

The Commission published its 2027 work programme on 4 November with 23 new initiatives, down from 38 in 2026 and 45 in 2025. The stress is on simplification and implementation over new rules.

It proposes withdrawing 18 pending legislative proposals according to its own Q&A (other outlets say 23). Named casualties reportedly include the deposit insurance scheme (EDIS), FIDA, the digital services tax and the Green Claims Directive; Parliament and Council can respond before a final decision.

The cross-border associations directive is among the proposals dropped after more than three years without Council-Parliament agreement. It shows the Commission pruning files it judges dead.

On 3 November the Commission announced new steps to prepare Europeans for growing crisis risks. It is part of the civil preparedness agenda.

3. European Parliament.

MEPs meet in a Brussels mini-plenary on 11 to 12 November (calendars conflict on venue; verify). The next Strasbourg session is 23 to 26 November.

On Thursday 12 November MEPs debate further sanctions against Russia, with no vote scheduled. The debate follows the record 22nd package of 12 October.

Kallas is due to discuss returning deported Ukrainian children with MEPs on Wednesday 11 November.

Parliament and Council aim to agree the 2027 annual budget this week.

Digital euro talks with the Irish Presidency aim for a political deal by 18 November. Merchant fee methodology and sanctioned persons' access remain open.

4. Council and European Council.

Informal EU summit in Dublin on 13 November will focus on AI, competitiveness and AI sovereignty. Costa says leaders will return to AI at the 2027 retreat in Alden Biesen.

Costa has taken over the MFF talks from the Irish Presidency after its 8% cut proposal (about EUR 141bn) won little support. His plan leans on new EU-wide own resources.

Costa says the EU must be "represented by its institutions" in Ukraine and security talks. The leaders' discussion in Dublin is expected to address it.

Meloni has asked for a single EU representative at Ukraine peace talks.

5. Foreign policy and the Middle East.

Quiet on formal EU output: I found no new Council or EEAS Gaza statement in the last 72 hours. Kallas's recent public focus was Ukraine.

The EU-Israel Association Agreement remains unsuspended. Germany and Italy blocked the push in April; the EEAS said on 12 August that Council discussions continue on other measures.

Late October: E3, Italy and regional ministers met in Istanbul urging US-Iran diplomacy rather than new attacks. The context is the US-Israel war on Iran; the EU has demanded a ceasefire since March.

Iran designated Britain's MI6 as a "hostile group" on 1 November, and London accused Tehran of trying to drag Lebanon into the war. Escalation risk is rising on Europe's doorstep. Wikipedia summary of reactions (unconfirmed)

6. Ukraine, Russia and enlargement.

The EU opened accession Clusters 2 and 3 for Ukraine on 13 October after Hungary lifted its veto, bringing the total to four of six. Kyiv passed an education-law change on minority schools to win it.

Hungary has since asked for more time on clusters 4 and 5, but Ukraine's envoy Kachka said on 3 November that Budapest raised no new objections. Kyiv expects them opened by end-2026.

The 22nd sanctions package of 12 October listed almost 1,600 individuals and entities, the largest ever. Reports of a further November package are pre-adoption (unconfirmed).

CEPA argues EU oil sanctions remain riddled with loopholes (6 November).

7. Migration and asylum.

Interior ministers agreed a tougher Return Regulation, with longer detention, indefinite entry bans and optional third-country return hubs. Euronews dates the agreement to 7 October; Parliament backing was reported earlier.

Brunner says hubs are voluntary for member states and subject to safeguards. Greece is among five states talking to African countries, with a first hub possibly in H2 2027.

Otherwise: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

8. Economy, trade and budget.

Šefčovič is pressing Washington on steel-content goods still facing duties up to 50%. If rates are not aligned by end-December, the EU can suspend concessions to US exporters.

France and Germany asked the Commission in October for a rapid-response trade tool, including an instrument to cut off market access. It is aimed mainly at China.

The MFF 2028 to 2034 summit is set for 26 November, and Lithuania's president has floated a "Plan B" for 4 February.

The Russian LNG ban applies from the start of 2027, but ACER reports Russian LNG volumes were up 17% year on year in spring.

9. Defence and security.

Kubilius said the EUR 150bn SAFE loans have not changed capitals' joint-procurement behaviour. He called a classified EDA readiness report "a warning signal" on the 2030 goal.

A defence market package, including a procurement directive revision, is due late November or early December.

The Defence Readiness Omnibus is meant to be adopted by end-2026.

10. Rule of law and the Court of Justice.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Searches found no new CJEU judgment this week.

For background, the Court ruled in April 2026 in Commission v Hungary (C-769/22) that Hungary's anti-LGBTI law breached EU values (unconfirmed, single source).

11. Member-state politics that matter for the EU.

Hungary under Magyar has traded its veto on Ukraine clusters for a minority-schools law, but he plans a referendum if Ukraine ever completes all chapters.

Paris and Berlin settled a car-rules compromise in mid-October: stricter "Made in Europe" rules plus looser emissions rules.

Meloni is pushing for one EU envoy for Ukraine talks.

Ireland holds the Council presidency until 31 December and hosts both Dublin summits.
