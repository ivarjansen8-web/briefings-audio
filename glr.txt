Masisi fighting flares as Kabila-M23 alliance and Rwanda sanctions shape stalled peace (thin-source run)

Compiled from sources dated 3 to 8 November 2026 where available, otherwise earlier (flagged). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

1. Top stories.

FARDC and Wazalendo launched simultaneous offensives against M23 positions in Masisi, North Kivu, on 3 November, reportedly retaking Mirindano; at least four civilians were reportedly killed (unconfirmed). Local sources cited by Radio Okapi say fighting hit Mirindano, Kazinga, Mahanga and Buhimba and that residents fled, which shows the front around Masisi is still active despite the peace tracks.

Former president Joseph Kabila reportedly launched a political-military coalition with M23/AFC figures on 12 October, with Corneille Nangaa as deputy and Sultani Makenga as army chief (unconfirmed, older item). If accurate, it recasts the rebellion as a challenge to Tshisekedi's legitimacy, not only a Rwanda-linked insurgency.

Tshisekedi has asked Russia for help in resolving the conflict with Rwanda and M23, per Africanews as relayed by CFR and the Rio Times (31 October; unconfirmed). It signals Kinshasa hedging beyond the US-led track.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

Radio Okapi reports the 3 November Masisi clashes involved the FARDC and Wazalendo against what it calls the FRN/M23 (unconfirmed, single source). Casualty figures come from local sources, not an independent count.

Similar heavy fighting around Masisi, including the Ngungu axis, was reported on 22 September, when government forces said they retook villages. The pattern is repeated local see-saw fighting with no decisive shift.

M23 captured Uvira in December 2025, its third major city after Goma and Bukavu, per AllAfrica; I could not confirm current control of Uvira. Context only, not a last-72-hours item.

Quiet: no verified reporting found for Ituri or FDLR in the last 72 hours.

3. Peace process.

Kinshasa and AFC/M23 agreed in Switzerland (Qatar-mediated, 13 to 17 April) to ease aid deliveries and release prisoners within 10 days. The talks also set up a ceasefire-monitoring mechanism and roadmap; I found no current reporting on implementation.

The Washington Accords were signed by Tshisekedi and Kagame on 4 December 2025; CFR says fighting has continued regardless. M23 is not a party to that deal, so Doha progress is the binding constraint.

The UN peacekeeping mandate in Congo runs to 20 December 2026, per the Rio Times. A renewal decision is the next hard date.

4. Rwanda.

Kagame attended the Commonwealth summit (CHOGM) in Antigua and Barbuda in early November, meeting Jamaica's PM and the WTO chief and arguing investors overstate risk in Global South markets. Kigali is pursuing normal diplomacy while under US sanctions pressure.

The US Treasury sanctioned the Rwanda Defence Force and four senior officers (reported as March 2026), citing breaches of the Washington Accords; Kagame called the sanctions "insults thrown in the face of my country". Rwanda denies backing M23 and says Kinshasa is not meeting its obligations.

Two US senators asked the NBA to reassess its Rwanda partnership in late October (unconfirmed, snippet only).

5. Kinshasa politics.

Opposition figure Delly Sesanga (ENVOL, C64 coalition) urged Tshisekedi in August to abandon constitutional change, saying a nation at war should not prioritise it. The opposition's stated goal is that Tshisekedi leaves office when his term ends in 2028.

Africanews reported in May that major opposition parties formed a coalition against revision after Tshisekedi reportedly opened the door to a third term.

Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

6. Burundi and Uganda.

The US Treasury warned that M23's presence near the Burundi border and continued RDF support risk a wider regional war; Burundian and Congolese troops withdrew into Burundi as M23 advanced on Uvira in December 2025. Context only.

A former Rwandan soldier and Kagame critic was reportedly found beheaded in Uganda (unconfirmed, single snippet, no date or name verified).

Quiet: no verified Burundi or Uganda developments in the last 72 hours.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

Treasury sanctioned the Gasabo Gold Refinery and its president, plus two individuals and four entities accused of trafficking Congolese minerals for M23 (dates unclear from snippets). The EU acted against the same refinery in March 2025.

Kagame disputes that Rwanda's mineral exports come from Congo, pointing to domestic tungsten, tin and tantalum. That is Kigali's claim.

Quiet: no new coltan, gold or cobalt deal news found in the last 72 hours.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

OCHA (February 2026) reported 74,000 new displacements in January 2026 and 6.47 million IDPs in total. No October or November figures found.

OCHA warned a drastic funding drop risks cutting aid to over four million people in the DRC.

Human Rights Watch has documented M23/AFC threats, detentions and attacks on journalists and activists in areas it controls. Older (March 2025) context.

9. International reactions and sanctions.

Reporting says the US Treasury sanctioned Rwanda's defence ministry and a credit society on 28 October, alleging troops, equipment, logistics and training for M23; Rwanda denies involvement (unconfirmed, single aggregated snippet, I could not verify the date).

Western policy toward Kigali has reportedly "shifted markedly", per US senators quoted by ESPN.
