Here is your Rwanda and Congo briefing for Wednesday, 11 November.

US hits Rwanda with sanctions and AGOA loss as Kabila joins M23 in new coalition.

Compiled from sources dated 18 October - 9 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Coverage note: Search surfaced little reporting dated within the last 24 to 72 hours, and direct article fetches were blocked in this session (network limits), so items below rest on search-result summaries of recent coverage. Sections are widened to roughly three weeks where needed and every claim should be checked against the linked source.

1. Top stories.

The US moved against Rwanda on two fronts in early November. Treasury sanctioned Rwanda's Defence Ministry and the Zigama Credit and Savings Society on 28 October, and on 2 November Washington said it intends to end Rwanda's AGOA trade preferences on 1 January 2027, per search summaries; Rwanda denies backing M23 and calls the sanctions unjust. This is the sharpest US pressure on Kigali yet and tests the Washington track.

Former president Joseph Kabila launched a political-military coalition with M23/AFC, the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN), signed in Goma on 13 October. Kabila leads it, with AFC chief Corneille Nangaa as deputy; Kinshasa has moved to suspend his party and he is wanted there. It turns a rebellion into a bid for national power.

At least 30 civilians were killed overnight in Beni Territory, North Kivu, according to local sources cited by OCHA, in a UN News report dated 6 November. The UN says the violence undermines efforts to contain cholera.

Tshisekedi reportedly said on 31 October that he is seeking Russian help against Rwanda and M23 (unconfirmed, single tracker entry, no detail found). If real, it would shift the diplomatic balance away from Washington.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

Kabila's FRN calls for resistance "by all means, including force" against Tshisekedi, per Al Jazeera's reporting. M23/AFC holds Goma and Bukavu, taken in early 2025, and large parts of the Kivus.

The ADF reportedly attacked Ebola clinics, killing at least 48 people (CFR headline dated 6 November; details unconfirmed). It points to ADF attacks in Beni and Ituri compounding an outbreak first reported in May.

The FARDC retook Uvira in South Kivu on 18 January after M23 withdrew, per a Wikipedia timeline; Uvira's status since then was not verified. Check current control before relying on it.

Ituri: CODECO has been blamed for mass killings, including at least 51 civilians killed in Djugu in February (Wikipedia timeline). No fresh Ituri reporting was found.

FDLR and Wazalendo: Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours.

3. Peace process.

The Doha track is stalled; DRC and M23 delegations were expected in Qatar in November (unconfirmed date). Analysts warn the Kabila coalition could undermine it.

The FRN says it will honour Doha commitments but warns that constitutional change in Kinshasa could destroy the talks, per Al Jazeera. This links the peace process directly to the third-term debate.

Government and M23/AFC delegates agreed a roadmap for talks on 23 August, and met again in Switzerland after months without dialogue (Kivu Morning Post, date metadata inconsistent). Includes a ceasefire verification mission at Minembwe with MONUSCO support.

AU, EAC and SADC: Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours.

4. Rwanda.

Kigali says the US sanctions "unjustly" target Rwanda and denies supporting M23, per search summaries. Earlier US rounds in March 2026 hit the Rwanda Defence Force and four commanders, which Rwanda called one-sided.

The US says Rwanda must halt M23 support and withdraw troops from eastern DRC (Treasury Secretary Bessent, as quoted in summaries). Estimates put Rwandan troops at 3,000 to 4,000 in the east; Kigali disputes this framing.

No Rwandan state-media (New Times, KT Press, IGIHE) items were retrieved in this run.

5. Kinshasa politics.

Kinshasa moved to suspend Kabila's party, and Kabila remains under a death sentence in absentia from a military court (October 2025), per search summaries.

The FRN frames Tshisekedi's constitutional-change plans as a path to a third term. Kinshasa's own position was not found.

South Africa: AllAfrica ran a piece on 9 November accusing Kabila of betraying South Africa over his rebel links. Content not read.

6. Burundi and Uganda.

Uganda and the DRC continue Operation Shujaa against the ADF, begun in November 2021; analysts say it has not stopped attacks on civilians.

Burundi fights for Kinshasa and its own security on the same ground, while neither Burundi nor Uganda sits at the Doha talks, per a September analysis.

Human Rights Watch counts nearly 122,500 people who fled to neighbouring countries by September, over 70,000 to Uganda and 40,000 to Burundi.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

The loss of AGOA preferences from 1 January 2027, if confirmed, would hit Rwanda's export sector. The US cited trade barriers and human rights alongside security.

Tshisekedi ordered an audit of copper and cobalt export revenues in April 2026; cobalt exports reached about 220,000 tonnes in 2025. Background only, nothing newer found.

Quiet: nothing significant found on coltan, gold or US/EU minerals deals in the last 72 hours.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

OCHA reported more than 55,000 suspected cholera cases nationwide since January (update of 27 October), with North and South Kivu the main eastern epicentres. Displacement and insecurity are driving it.

OCHA says 5.8 million people are displaced in 2026, with 1.9 million returnees; the 2026 plan seeks EUR 1.4 billion for 14.9 million people.

An Ebola (Bundibugyo) outbreak first reported in Ituri in May has spread, with new cases now mostly in North Kivu, per the Congolese health minister (secondary source).

9. International reactions and sanctions.

Washington's pressure is escalating from targeted sanctions (2025, March 2026, 28 October) to trade measures (2 November). Rwanda's earlier listed figures include Minister of State James Kabarebe.

Reaction from the EU, UN Security Council and Qatar to the Kabila coalition was not found.
