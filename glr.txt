Here is your Rwanda and Congo briefing for Tuesday, 17 November.

US moves to end Rwanda's AGOA status as DRC-Rwanda rift spills into Francophonie summit.

Compiled from sources dated 2 to 15 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Sourcing note: many outlets (Reuters, Al Jazeera, Africanews, AllAfrica and others) could not be opened from the routine's network, so this edition rests largely on search-result summaries. Dates are widened to roughly two weeks because little dated reporting surfaced for the last 24 hours. Verify before citing.

1. Top stories.

The US announced on 2 November that it intends to terminate Rwanda's AGOA participation from 1 January 2027. The USTR statement covers Rwanda and South Africa and cites rule of law, political pluralism and human and worker rights; it is the latest US pressure on Kigali.

The Francophonie summit opened in Cambodia on 15 November amid a Rwanda-DRC rift over who leads the organisation. Reporting says Kinshasa threatened to leave the OIF if the Rwandan-backed candidate is re-elected; Rwanda denies backing M23.

The UN peacekeeping chief met AFC/M23 leaders in eastern DRC around 3 November. A rare high-level UN contact with the group that holds Goma and other major cities. (Detail limited to a search snippet.)

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

Current battlefield reporting for 15 to 17 November was not retrievable. A Critical Threats Congo War Security Review with a data cutoff of 10 November exists, but its content could not be read.

A UN Group of Experts report (July) described AFC/M23's military strength and growing ties to former president Joseph Kabila. It remains the baseline for assessing the group.

A Wikipedia entry states the AFC was formally dissolved on 14 October 2026, its assets passing to a body called FRN (unconfirmed, single secondary source). Treat as unverified until confirmed by primary reporting.

3. Peace process.

Kinshasa and M23 agreed a shared roadmap in Geneva in late August, with the US State Department issuing a joint statement as mediator. A ceasefire monitoring mission launched around the same time, with Doha running in parallel to the US-mediated Congo-Rwanda track.

The November 2025 Doha framework had eight protocols, of which only prisoner exchange and ceasefire monitoring were partly under way, per US envoy Massad Boulos. It frames why implementation remains the sticking point.

No dated reporting on new Washington, Doha, AU, EAC or SADC rounds this week was found.

4. Rwanda.

Kigali faces the AGOA termination plus earlier US sanctions on the Rwanda Defence Forces and senior officers over alleged M23 support. Rwanda denies backing M23; in April Kagame said sanctions would not weaken the RDF. (Sanctions detail per search summaries.)

The New Times (government-aligned) published an opinion piece urging Rwanda to turn the AGOA threat into trade diversification. It reflects Kigali's likely line.

Analysis cited in search results says the US takes about 2% of Rwanda's merchandise exports and about 4% of those enter under AGOA, so economic damage looks limited and the cost is mainly reputational. (unconfirmed, single analysis.)

5. Kinshasa politics.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. No dated reporting on Tshisekedi, the opposition or the constitution debate was retrievable. The OIF dispute above is the main political thread.

6. Burundi and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours was found for Uganda. For Burundi, M23 claimed earlier to have captured hundreds of Burundian soldiers in an offensive; that claim is M23's and undated in the snippet.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

A French AllAfrica analysis dated 10 November says the AGOA move and US sanctions have revived debate over Washington's mix of diplomacy and minerals interest. (unconfirmed, single source, not opened.)

Congo offered the US a 44-project shortlist, including the M23-held Rubaya coltan area, under the US-DRC minerals pact (Reuters via Mining Weekly, February-March). Rubaya is about 15% of world coltan per the same coverage.

The US has struggled to de-risk "war zone minerals" despite the pact, sources told Reuters in March.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours could be confirmed. Background: the UN counted more than 200,000 displaced around Uvira after the December 2025 fighting.

9. International reactions and sanctions.

The US is using trade and sanctions leverage on Kigali (AGOA, RDF sanctions) while pursuing the minerals pact with Kinshasa. The USTR cited human rights and pluralism, not only the DRC.

The Francophonie vote is a diplomatic proxy for the Kigali-Kinshasa dispute.

What to watch.

Francophonie summit outcome (mid-November): the secretary-general vote and any DRC walkout.

Rwanda's formal response to the AGOA decision before the 1 January 2027 effective date.

Next Doha or Washington implementation meetings: no dates found; check State Department and Qatar statements.
