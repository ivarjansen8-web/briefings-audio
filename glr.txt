Here is your Rwanda and Congo briefing for Thursday, 19 November.

Francophonie vote deadlocked by Rwanda-DRC rift; Masisi fighting displaces hundreds at Nyabiondo.

Compiled from sources dated 13 to 18 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. Note: most outlet sites could not be opened in this session, so this briefing relies on search-result summaries; coverage is thinner than usual and several sections are widened to 72+ hours.

1. Top stories.

The Francophonie summit in Phnom Penh ended on 16 November without electing a secretary-general, and Louise Mushikiwabo stays on for another 12 months. The vote was postponed by a year after heads of state could not agree among four candidates, in a standoff between the DRC and Burundi on one side and Rwanda on the other; it is the first diplomatic arena where the Kinshasa-Kigali rift has visibly blocked a multilateral decision.

FARDC and Wazalendo fighters clashed with the FRN (the new coalition grouping M23/AFC and allied rebels) at Nyabiondo, Masisi, on 18 November, displacing hundreds of households. Radio Okapi reports the fighting began Wednesday morning; Kivu Morning Post says shooting started around 6 a.m. and a government offensive reportedly began Tuesday evening at Mahanga.

Nearly 6,000 Rwandan refugees have returned home from the DRC in 2026, according to a 18 November report. The same report says implementation of the Washington Accords remains fragile and limited.

Health access in the Bunyakiri area of South Kivu is deteriorating because of fighting between the FRN and the army since 6 November. A medical organisation told Actualite.cd that insecurity complicates care in a zone already hit by cholera and malaria.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

Fighting between FARDC/Wazalendo and the FRN (the rebel coalition formed in October that includes M23) continues in Masisi, North Kivu. Radio Okapi and Kivu Morning Post both report heavy combat at Nyabiondo on 18 November; the town's socio-economic activity was reported paralysed, and the Masisi-Walikale boundary is the apparent axis of the government push.

Clashes between FRN and the army have intensified in Bunyakiri (South Kivu) since 6 November. The fighting continues despite ceasefire commitments between Kinshasa and Kigali and between Kinshasa and the AFC/M23, which shows how little the paper agreements constrain ground operations.

Search summaries of the Critical Threats and CFR trackers describe fighting near Kambale on 9 November as pro-government forces tried to retake lost positions, and more than ten civilians killed in a week in the Kalima area of South Kivu, per Radio Okapi (unconfirmed, tracker summary only). Check the full trackers for dates and perpetrators before citing these figures.

MONUSCO's peacekeeping chief met leaders of the new rebel coalition in eastern DRC in early November. The meeting signals the UN is dealing directly with the structure that now includes M23; details of what was agreed were not retrievable.

FDLR, Ituri: Quiet in the sources I could access: no significant reporting found in the last 72 hours.

3. Peace process.

The Washington Accords (June 2025) remain the framework, but implementation is described as limited. A 18 November report says the Doha protocols have seen little implementation while fighting continues, and that the African Union has called for better coordination between the Washington and Doha tracks and for more inclusive dialogue.

The Regional Economic Integration Framework was initialled in Washington on 7 November 2025 and still awaits signature by Tshisekedi and Kagame, with implementation contingent on the wider peace agreement. A year on, the economic track remains the part of the architecture the US most wants to see move.

Kinshasa and M23 signed a non-binding Doha framework on 15 November 2025 containing protocols on ceasefire verification and prisoner exchange. A ceasefire monitoring mission launched in August 2026 and the parties agreed a roadmap for talks the same month, per search summaries.

No new Doha round or REIF signing was found for November 2026 (last 72 hours: quiet on formal talks).

4. Rwanda.

Kigali refused to withdraw Mushikiwabo's candidacy for a third term at the Francophonie, and denied reports that Macron asked Kagame to do so. Foreign Minister Olivier Nduhungirehe said Kigali remained committed to her re-election before the postponement; the outcome keeps her in post.

The US sanctioned Rwandan army officials and the Rwanda Defence Force in March 2026 over support for M23, including the December 2025 offensive on Uvira. Rwanda denies backing M23, although Congo, the US and UN experts say it does. 2026 in the DRC, Wikipedia (background; secondary source)

Rwanda's government-aligned New Times covered the Francophonie election as a contest to elect the OIF secretary-general; Kigali's framing emphasises Mushikiwabo's record. Treat that coverage as Kigali's line.

Kagame has repeatedly cited the FDLR as Rwanda's core security concern. The quote I found is older, so treat it as background.

5. Kinshasa politics.

The Francophonie standoff shows Kinshasa willing to escalate diplomatically: DR Congo threatened to quit the OIF if Mushikiwabo were re-elected, and on 14 November said it was considering withdrawing its own candidate. Burundi sided with Kinshasa.

The referendum law remains the central domestic issue. The National Assembly passed it on 9 June, the Constitutional Court validated it in late July, and Tshisekedi asked Parliament on 10 August for a fresh deliberation under Article 137, scheduled for September; I found no November update.

The opposition (including the C64 coalition) argues Article 220 bars changes to presidential term limits and fears a new constitution would reset Tshisekedi's term count. Tshisekedi says the revision is not about a third term. (Last 72 hours: no new developments found.)

6. Burundi and Uganda.

Burundi aligned with Kinshasa at the Francophonie, backing the DRC side of the dispute against Rwanda. Burundian troops remain deployed in South Kivu.

Uganda keeps forces in north-eastern Congo, and one commentary claims its gold exports more than doubled to 8.9 dollarsbillion in the year to June 2026 (unconfirmed, single commentary). It cites a UN Group of Experts report saying Ugandan refining is largely supplied by eastern DRC gold.

Kavimvira, the DRC-Burundi border crossing, reopened on 23 February after a two-month closure.

Quiet: nothing significant on Burundi or Uganda bilateral moves in the last 72 hours.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours on new minerals deals.

The US-DRC minerals agreement is criticised as imposing concrete obligations on Kinshasa (preferential fiscal terms, regulatory reform, privileged US access) with few explicit security guarantees.

Congo replaced its February 2025 cobalt export ban with quotas in October 2025: 18,125 tonnes for late 2025 and up to 96,600 tonnes a year in 2026 to 2027 (per a search summary).

The US sanctioned the Gasabo Gold Refinery in June 2026 over a mineral-smuggling network. M23 itself was excluded from the new sanctions to protect diplomacy.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

Hundreds of households fled Nyabiondo on 18 November as fighting reached the town. Numbers beyond "hundreds of households" were not available.

Cholera and malaria are compounding insecurity in Bunyakiri, where care access is hampered.

Rwandan refugee returns from the DRC reached nearly 6,000 in 2026.

Earlier OCHA reporting put displacement in North and South Kivu at roughly 4 million in early 2025, with over 3 million returns by July 2025. No fresh 2026 OCHA figures were found.

9. International reactions and sanctions.

Macron welcomed the Francophonie compromise as a diplomatic outcome after the unprecedented postponement of the election.

US sanctions on the RDF (March 2026) and the Gasabo refinery (June 2026) remain the main punitive measures; no new sanctions found in the last 72 hours.

The AU is calling for tighter coordination between the Washington and Doha processes.
