M23 claims jet shootdown in South Kivu as pilot capture and Ituri clashes hit Ebola response; UN sanctions Kabila's FRN.

1. Top stories.

A South African pilot has reportedly been captured by M23 rebels, raising concern about foreign personnel in the Congo war (unconfirmed). Reports dated 26 November say the capture has sharpened questions over the role of foreigners and contractors on the government side.

The FRN (the renamed AFC/M23) says it shot down a FARDC fighter jet near Minembwe, South Kivu, around 06:00 on 22 November (unconfirmed). The group supplied photos; this is a rebel claim and I found no independent confirmation.

Renewed fighting in Ituri forced civilians to flee towards Bunia and led to evacuation of Ebola response staff from a hospital in Akwe. WHO chief Tedros said WHO and Congolese health ministry personnel were evacuated safely; the violence directly undermines the response to the largest Ebola outbreak in DRC's history.

The UN added the FRN to its sanctions list on 11 November, largely a formality after Joseph Kabila's movement succeeded M23 and its political wing AFC in mid-October. The UK followed on 12 November by listing FRN as an alias of AFC; Kinshasa welcomed the move, per Congolese agency ACP.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

AFC/M23 took Kasheke and Bituna (Masisi, North Kivu) on 18 November, pushing Wazalendo back towards Kautu (unconfirmed). The date comes from a Wikipedia chronology whose metadata is inconsistent, so treat as provisional.

Between 21 and 23 November, villages in the Waloa Yungu grouping (Walikale), including Kaliki and Buhimba, reportedly fell to AFC/M23 (unconfirmed). It suggests continued M23 expansion westward from Masisi.

On 25 November AFC/M23 reportedly captured Kilungutwe (Mwenga, South Kivu), with FARDC falling back towards Kasika and Mwenga-centre (unconfirmed). South Kivu violence since 2025 has largely involved M23-allied groups under the AFC umbrella, according to ACLED.

Ituri: clashes displaced thousands towards Bunia since Monday (23 November). This overlaps with the Ebola epicentre, compounding the emergency.

Defections continue: Congolese outlets reported in early October that 29 AFC/M23 fighters surrendered in North Kivu. This is the government's account and older than the 72-hour window.

5. Kinshasa politics.

Tshisekedi signed a referendum law around 11 November that opens the way to constitutional change, fuelling third-term fears. His second term ends in 2028 under the current text.

The opposition (the C64 grouping including Martin Fayulu and Moise Katumbi) vowed on 12 November to resist, calling the process a constitutional coup. Main parties reportedly rejected Tshisekedi's dialogue offer and want an inclusive national dialogue instead.

Kabila, sentenced to death in absentia for treason last year, now fronts the FRN and has vowed to end "tyranny" by all means, including force. An Ebuteli researcher said holding elections in 2028 is hard to picture given insecurity.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

Quiet: nothing significant found in the last 72 hours. The most recent reporting is from February 2026.

Kinshasa put the rebel-held Rubaya coltan mine on a shortlist of assets offered to the US, according to a Reuters-reported document. Rubaya produces roughly 15% of world coltan and is controlled by sanctioned M23, so any deal depends on the war.

Scrutiny of the US-DRC minerals deal continues, with Congolese fearing they will lose out. The mining minister denies giving wealth away.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

Ebola (Bundibugyo strain) is the largest and deadliest outbreak in DRC history; by early October over 8,000 confirmed cases and about 4,000 deaths were reported. These are older figures; Ituri is the epicentre.

OCHA says more than 1.1 million people are displaced in Ituri, over 373,000 of them in sites, and its appeal for 7.3 million people is barely half funded. Insecurity has delayed sample transport and burned a Kigonze Ebola transit centre.

New displacement towards Bunia this week and the Akwe evacuation show the conflict is again blocking health access.

9. International reactions and sanctions.

UN and UK sanctions now cover the FRN name (11 to 12 November). It closes a loophole created by M23's rebrand, though enforcement remains the issue.

Kinshasa welcomed the UN listing as a win for its position that Kabila is tied to M23. This is the government's framing.

The captured South African pilot may draw Pretoria in, given its history in the region (unconfirmed).
