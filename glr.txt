Tshisekedi signs contested referendum law as rival peace tracks fail to curb eastern Congo violence.

1. Top stories.

President Tshisekedi promulgated a referendum law (Law No. 26/045) on 9 November, announced on state television on 10 November (unconfirmed). Critics fear it opens a path to scrapping the two-term limit before his mandate ends in 2028; no referendum date has been set.

Al Jazeera's 18 November piece is headlined "DR Congo's rival peace efforts fail to halt violence in the east" (unconfirmed; only the headline was visible). It points to the Washington and Doha tracks running in parallel without reducing fighting.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

Critical Threats' 11 November review reports AFC/M23-aligned fighters clashing with Wazalendo and FDLR fighters in western Rutshuru, including a 2 November clash in Bwito chiefdom (unconfirmed). It shows fighting continuing in North Kivu despite the ceasefire framework.

Earlier, AllAfrica reported M23 offensives on Wazalendo positions in southern Masisi from 24 July (unconfirmed). This is background for the Masisi front, not new.

3. Peace process.

MONUSCO's peacekeeping chief met AFC leaders around 3 November (unconfirmed). It signals UN engagement with the rebel coalition on ceasefire monitoring.

Kabila's FRN wrote to Qatar's mediators on 1 November arguing that constitutional change during talks undermines the legal basis of the agreements (unconfirmed). It links the referendum debate to the Doha track.

Background: an August ceasefire-monitoring mission and a Geneva roadmap between Kinshasa and AFC/M23.

5. Kinshasa politics.

The referendum law allows initiation by the president, government, half of each chamber, or citizen petition (unconfirmed). The Constitutional Court reportedly cleared it on 28 July with thirteen interpretive reservations.
