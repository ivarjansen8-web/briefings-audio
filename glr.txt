M23/FRN reported offensive in South Kivu as US midterms rattle Kinshasa; sanctions on Rwandan unit reported.

1. Top stories.

M23, now rebranded as the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation (FRN), reportedly launched an offensive in South Kivu on Friday 6 November and has advanced since (unconfirmed). Front lines had been fairly stable since the December US-brokered DRC-Rwanda deal, so a renewed push would be a significant break.

Washington has reportedly sanctioned a Rwandan defence ministry unit and a financial institution over alleged ties to the rebels, and the insurgency is said to have named former president Joseph Kabila as its leader (unconfirmed). Both points come from the same wire summary and need a second source.

Commentary in Camer.be (9 November) claims the US midterms of 3 November cost Tshisekedi his main ally in Washington and ended his third-term hopes (unconfirmed, opinion-led). Afrik.com (8 November) similarly calls him the biggest African loser of the vote, while noting nothing official confirms he has dropped the project.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

The M23/FRN is reported to be advancing in South Kivu after Friday's offensive (unconfirmed). I could not retrieve place names or casualty figures; check the News24 piece and Kivu Security Tracker.

Critical Threats' mid-September review described shifting fighting among Wazalendo, FARDC and M23 in North and South Kivu, including a contested village called Mahanga. This is background, not news from the last 72 hours.

Afrik.com reports M23 had to withdraw from Uvira (unconfirmed, date not given in the summary). An earlier withdrawal claim dates to December 2025.

Ituri and FDLR: nothing found. Quiet: nothing significant surfaced in the last 72 hours.

3. Peace process.

Tshisekedi said in late August that the planned national dialogue (up to three months, preceded by provincial consultations) would exclude M23, which he says belongs in the Doha track. A renewed offensive makes that split harder to sustain.

A UN Security Council session on 29 September found fighting continuing despite several peace agreements, and flagged the worst Ebola outbreak on record, with more than 3,900 dead (UN figures as summarised by search). The outbreak compounds the security crisis.

Background: Doha framework signed 15 November 2025; Washington accord 27 June 2025. The one-year mark of the Doha framework falls on 15 November.

4. Rwanda.

Forbes (9 November) reports a new Rwandan F1 Grand Prix adds to Qatari diplomacy in Africa (unconfirmed, headline only). Qatar is both Doha mediator and a Kigali partner, which complicates its neutrality.

Kigali's stance: Rwanda denies backing M23, while the DRC, UN experts and Western governments say it does. Nothing newer than April 2026 (Kagame saying US sanctions would not weaken the army) surfaced.

Quiet: no other dated Rwandan government news found in the last 72 hours.
