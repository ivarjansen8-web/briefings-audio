Here is your Rwanda and Congo briefing for Thursday, 12 November.

FRN pushes into Kalehe as US sanctions Rwanda's defence ministry and Kabila's coalition clouds Doha.

Compiled from sources dated 22 October - 9 November 2026 (nothing newer surfaced). Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports. Note: page fetching was blocked in this session, so every item rests on search-result summaries, not full-text reading; check links before citing.

1. Top stories.

The FRN (the renamed M23, now led by Joseph Kabila per one report) reportedly pushed about 50 km along the RN3 in South Kivu on 8 to 9 November and took Bunyakiri in Kalehe territory (unconfirmed). If it holds, this puts Kinshasa's forces on the back foot in the highlands west of Lake Kivu, with Kambali reportedly occupied and Kambegete still held by the FARDC.

The US sanctioned Rwanda's defence ministry and the Zigama Credit and Savings Society on 29 October over alleged support for M23. Washington says Zigama facilitated Rwandan support to the group; Rwanda denies backing M23.

Kabila joined M23 in a new political-military coalition, announced around 13 October. It reframes the war as a challenge to Tshisekedi's legitimacy, and analysts warn it could undermine the Qatar-mediated process.

A Walikale court sentenced 66 Congolese soldiers to death for abandoning positions in fighting with the rebels (date not shown in the result). It signals army morale problems as the front moves.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

The FRN offensive in Kalehe (South Kivu) is the newest front movement, reported 9 November by a single outlet citing unnamed sources (unconfirmed). I found no confirmation or official FARDC/Wazalendo response.

News24 also reported on 9 November that rebels advanced in an eastern offensive, citing local and security sources (headline only; details unread). It is a second signal of movement, though I could not read the text.

In Masisi (North Kivu), fighting between AFC/M23 and FARDC-Wazalendo continued around Ngungu and Katoyi as of 7 October. Military sources said the coalition retook Katoyi centre; these are claims from the Congolese side.

Critical Threats' August review described an M23 counteroffensive in Kalehe, so this axis has been contested for months.

FDLR, Ituri and Uvira: no fresh dated reporting found in the last 72 hours.

3. Peace process.

A November Doha round is reported but unconfirmed, with no date found. Treat it as a lead rather than a schedule.

In August, Kinshasa and M23 agreed a roadmap for talks under the Doha framework, but fighting continued. The roadmap is the baseline for any November round.

In an early-November letter to Qatari mediators obtained by RFI (as relayed in search results), the FRN tied the constitution to the Doha talks. It warned of "all necessary means" if Kinshasa breaks the constitutional order (unconfirmed; secondhand summary).

Both sides keep accusing each other of violating the Washington commitments. The accords have not stopped the fighting.

4. Rwanda.

Kigali has rejected US sanctions before: after the March sanctions on the RDF and four officers, its foreign ministry said they "misrepresent the reality" and blamed the DRC for ceasefire violations. These are Rwanda's claims; I did not find its response to the 29 October action.

Kagame called the earlier sanctions "insults" and later said they "hurt" but Rwanda would not give in. Expect similar rhetoric now.

Rwanda says it acts against the FDLR; the US Treasury and UN experts say the RDF fights alongside M23, which Kigali denies.

No New Times (government-aligned) or KT Press item from the last 72 hours was retrievable.

5. Kinshasa politics.

Tshisekedi told his Union Sacree coalition on 25 October he does not like the current constitution, opening the door to a referendum and a possible third term (unconfirmed detail from a secondary summary). His term ends in 2028.

The opposition C64 coalition (named for Article 64) opposes any change and rejected Tshisekedi's dialogue offer in August.

A Kinshasa opposition protest was dispersed on 16 September, with one reported dead. Street repression is a recurring pattern.

The Constitutional Court approved a referendum law that critics say could enable a third term; I found no referendum date.

6. Burundi and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours. Background only: in December 2025 Rwanda accused Burundi of deploying around 20,000 troops in South Kivu (Rwanda's claim). KT Press, December 2025 (Rwandan outlet) The Kalehe advance sits close to Burundi-backed lines, so watch for Bujumbura's reaction.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

Reports (no article found) say the US suspended Rwanda from a trade arrangement on 2 November (unconfirmed). This would extend the sanctions into trade; verify before relying on it.

Congo offered the M23-held Rubaya coltan deposit to the US in a minerals pact, per a February document. The UN estimates rebels collect at least 800,000 dollarsa month in taxes there.

A May analysis judged that most Western investors will avoid eastern Congo projects for the foreseeable future.

No new cobalt or gold data in the last 72 hours.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

The UN reported on 22 October that thousands more fled escalating hostilities in the southeast, joining about 14,500 who fled earlier that month. OCHA again called for civilian protection and access.

The UN's 2026 plan projects nearly 15 million people in need, with one source citing a 53% funding shortfall (unconfirmed).

The death sentences for 66 soldiers raise fair-trial concerns; no rights group reaction found.

Displacement from the Kalehe push is likely but not yet documented.

9. International reactions and sanctions.

US Treasury sanctions escalated in two steps: the RDF and four officers on 2 March, then the defence ministry and Zigama on 29 October. Washington cites violations of the Washington Accords.

One result says no decision has been made on sanctioning Kagame personally (unconfirmed).

Civil-society voices argue sanctions alone are symbolic and peace needs more.

No EU, AU or UN Security Council statements from the last 72 hours were found.

What to watch.

Bunyakiri and the RN3: whether FARDC/Wazalendo confirm or contest the 8 to 9 November advance, and whether Bukavu or Burundi-border areas are threatened in the coming days.

Doha: a date and venue for the November round, and whether the FRN's letter on the constitution becomes a precondition.

US follow-through: Rwandan responses to the 29 October sanctions, confirmation of the reported 2 November trade suspension, and any move toward Kagame personally.

Kinshasa: any announced referendum timetable or new opposition protest in the next week.

Humanitarian: an OCHA flash update on displacement from Kalehe.
