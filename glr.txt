Here is your Rwanda and Congo briefing for Wednesday, 25 November.

Kenya-DRC ambassadors return as cobalt curbs loom; Kabila's FRN and US sanctions on Rwanda frame the war.

Compiled from sources dated 28 October - 24 November 2026. Items marked (unconfirmed) are single-source or early reports.

Sourcing note: In this run, article pages for most outlets could not be opened (network restrictions), so this briefing relies on search-result summaries. Very little dated within the last 24 hours surfaced on the war itself; most sections are widened to 72 hours or to the past month, and are labelled as such. Treat details as provisional and verify against the linked pages.

1. Top stories.

Kinshasa opened the 12th Makutano mining forum on 23 November, with Tshisekedi pushing local processing of critical minerals. He called for faster industrialisation of the mining sector, including lithium, refining and battery-materials plants, which fits Kinshasa's strategy of selling minerals access as leverage with Washington.

Kenya and the DRC agreed to restore ambassadors, ending a near three-year freeze, after a mini-summit in Oyo. It signals Kinshasa repairing regional ties that broke down over Nairobi's hosting of the AFC/M23 alliance.

Congo is reported to be suspending cobalt exports again from 30 November (unconfirmed). The report is single-source and would extend the supply-management policy meant to support prices.

Joseph Kabila's new Front pour la refondation de la nation (FRN), announced in M23-held Goma on 12 October, remains the main political shock of the autumn. It absorbs the AFC/M23 alliance under a former president who has been sentenced to death in absentia by a military court in Kinshasa.

2. Eastern DRC front lines.

Quiet: nothing significant surfaced in the last 72 hours in the sources I could reach. I could not retrieve current Kivu Security Tracker, Radio Okapi or Actualite.cd battlefield reports, so absence here is a limit of this run, not evidence of calm.

Most recent detailed battlefield reporting found is older: heavy FARDC-Wazalendo versus AFC/M23 fighting in Masisi, North Kivu (September). The report headline says clashes were continuing; I could only see the headline.

Critical Threats reported in August that fighting returned to Walungu and Mwenga in South Kivu for the first time since May, with the FARDC claiming on 14 August to hold Chambombo and Lumbishi. That is the FARDC's claim; it shows front lines still moving in the highlands behind Uvira.

Friction inside the Kinshasa-aligned coalition is a recurring problem. In late 2025 FARDC and Wazalendo clashed in Uvira over WFP food distribution, killing several people, per regional reporting.

3. Peace process.

Quiet: no confirmed new Doha or Washington round found in the last 72 hours. One conflict summary dated 19 November describes talks as stalled, with M23 still holding territory around Goma (single-source, unconfirmed).

Al Jazeera reports Kinshasa and AFC/M23 have discussed a ceasefire, refugee returns and detainee releases in Doha, and the FRN says it will honour Doha commitments. Analysts quoted warn Kabila's entry could undermine the Qatar track.

The legal base remains the 27 June 2025 Washington agreement between the DRC and Rwanda and the Doha framework of 15 November 2025. An M23 representative said at the time the framework had no binding clauses and would not change the situation on the ground.

The Kenya-DRC ambassador deal came out of a quadripartite political summit in Oyo (details thin). It is a regional diplomacy signal rather than a ceasefire step.

4. Rwanda.

Quiet: nothing significant from Kigali found in the last 72 hours. I could not reach The New Times (government-aligned), KT Press or IGIHE this run.

Rwanda's line remains that the core issue is the FDLR and its presence within the Kinshasa-backed coalition. Kigali denies backing M23; the UN and Western governments say it commands and supports the rebels.

The US Treasury sanctioned Rwanda's Ministry of Defence and the Zigama Credit and Savings Society on 28 October, saying the ministry directed the RDF to deploy thousands of troops in North and South Kivu. I found no Rwandan government reply to this round in the results.

5. Kinshasa politics.

Tensions between the Catholic Church and the government, and a continuing debate on revising the constitution, are flagged in Congolese media (summary only, unconfirmed). A constitutional change would let Tshisekedi seek a further term, which opponents and the Church oppose.

Kinshasa's military court sentenced Kabila to death in absentia for treason and war crimes over alleged support for AFC/M23. Kabila rejects the case as political; the US sanctioned him in April, per Democracy in Africa.

Tshisekedi is using the Makutano forum to frame minerals as an economic transformation agenda.

6. Burundi and Uganda.

Quiet: nothing significant in the last 72 hours in the sources I could reach.

Burundi's bid to join the peace talks was rejected by AFC/M23 in November 2025, a reminder that Bujumbura, whose troops back Kinshasa, is a party the rebels will not accept at the table.

7. Minerals, trade and the economy.

Kinshasa hosts the Makutano mining forum 23 to 25 November, courting investment in processing, refining and battery-materials manufacturing.

A new cobalt export suspension is reported to take effect on 30 November (unconfirmed, single secondary source). It would be aimed at regulating supply and supporting prices.

An "Invest in DRC Energy 2026" hydrocarbons conference was held in Kinshasa on 24 November.

8. Humanitarian situation and human rights.

Quiet: no new OCHA or MONUSCO figures found within 72 hours. One summary puts internal displacement above seven million, mostly in North Kivu, South Kivu and Ituri; check OCHA and UNHCR for authoritative numbers.

Access in rebel-held areas depends on negotiations with M23 and on whether Goma airport, banks and supply routes are open.

9. International reactions and sanctions.

The US sanctioned Rwanda's defence ministry and Zigama on 28 October, after March sanctions on the Rwanda Defence Force and four senior officers including chief of staff Vincent Nyakarundi. Kinshasa welcomed the October move.

Critical Threats counts Rwandan or M23-linked targets as 15 of 16 US Treasury Great Lakes designations in 2026.

Rwanda's foreign ministry said the March sanctions "misrepresent the reality and distort the facts of the conflict."
